    Evaluation Engineering¶

    Evaluation engineering turns an AI product claim into versioned evidence and a release decision. It separates component quality from end-to-end outcomes, measures uncertainty and critical slices, and keeps evaluation useful after data, prompts, models, tools, and user behavior change.

    Evidence vocabulary¶

    • Invariant: a condition that must always hold; violation blocks release or triggers containment.
    • Assumption: an unverified input to a design or calculation.
    • Benchmark: a result tied to a frozen fixture, environment, and method.
    • Production outcome: a result observed after deployment with known attribution limits.
    • Gate: a deterministic rule over named metrics, slices, and operational evidence.

    Do not call a demo, anecdote, or provider leaderboard a product evaluation.

    Evaluation contract¶

    Start from the product decision, not from a generic model score.

    EvaluationContract(
  id, task, user_population, decision, consequence,
  answerability_policy, abstention_policy,
  dataset_manifest, slice_schema, metric_definitions,
  safety_invariants, operational_limits,
  baseline_bundle, candidate_bundle,
  acceptance_rules, owners, approved_at
)

    Invariants¶

    1. Every reported score names the exact dataset, code, model, prompt, retrieval, tool, and policy versions.
    2. Safety invariants and legal constraints are gates, not terms that can be averaged into one utility score.
    3. Retrieval, grounding, answer quality, tool behavior, latency, reliability, and cost are measured separately before any composite view.
    4. Candidate and baseline run on the same frozen examples and scoring policy for paired comparison.
    5. Evaluation examples do not silently enter training, prompt tuning, or judge calibration sets.
    6. A release exception has scope, approver, expiry, compensating control, and rollback trigger.
    7. Missing or immature labels remain explicit; they are not counted as success.

    Evidence architecture¶

    flowchart LR
    A[Product contract] --> B[Dataset manifest]
    B --> C[Evaluation runner]
    D[Candidate bundle] --> C
    E[Baseline bundle] --> C
    C --> F[Stage records]
    F --> G[Metric and slice reducer]
    G --> H[Confidence analysis]
    H --> I{Release gate}
    I -- pass --> J[Shadow or canary]
    I -- fail --> K[Failure analysis]
    J --> L[Mature production outcomes]
    L --> B

    The feedback from production updates future evaluation sets through review. It does not overwrite the evidence used for the original release decision.

    Dataset and judgment design¶

    An evaluation row needs enough state to replay scoring and diagnose failure:

    {
  "example_id": "eval-1042",
  "dataset_version": "support-v7",
  "input_ref": "object://eval/support-v7/1042",
  "expected": {"action": "ESCALATE", "required_facts": ["refund_window"]},
  "answerability": "ANSWERABLE",
  "slices": {"language": "en", "customer_tier": "regulated", "long_context": true},
  "provenance": {"source": "adjudicated-production", "event_time": "2026-05-11T00:00:00Z"},
  "judgment_policy": "jp-4",
  "retention_class": "restricted"
}

    Use immutable dataset manifests that list row IDs and content digests. Keep train, tune, judge-calibration, release, red-team, and audit sets separate. Deduplicate semantically related cases across splits where leakage would inflate results. Record exclusions and unresolved disagreement.

    Human judgment needs a rubric, qualification examples, blind ordering, and an adjudication policy. Measure agreement, but do not confuse agreement with correctness. For high-impact tasks, preserve the individual judgments and adjudication trail.

    Layered metrics¶

    Layer Examples Product question
    ingestion parse coverage, field accuracy, quarantine rate did usable evidence enter the system?
    retrieval Recall@k, MRR, filter violations was required evidence available?
    grounding claim support, citation correctness, unsupported-claim rate does the response follow retrieved evidence?
    task precision/recall, ranking utility, exact schema validity did the model perform the task?
    safety policy violations by threat slice did any non-negotiable control fail?
    tool/workflow valid call rate, effect correctness, unknown outcomes did execution preserve authority and state?
    operations p95/p99 latency, availability, queueing, recovery can it meet the service contract?
    economics cost per accepted result, review load, wasted work is the result sustainable?

    An end-to-end score without stage attribution cannot tell the owner what to fix. A retrieval miss cannot be repaired by a better answer prompt. A grounded answer can still be wrong because the source is wrong.

    Statistical decision design¶

    For a binary metric with x successes in n independent sampling units:

    p_hat = x / n
standard_error_approx = sqrt(p_hat * (1 - p_hat) / n)

    Use Wilson or exact intervals near 0 or 1 and for small samples. Define the independent sampling unit: repeated turns, generations, or documents from one user are not independent users. For a paired baseline/candidate comparison, compute each unit's difference and bootstrap those paired differences. Use a cluster bootstrap or matched analysis when observations share a user, conversation, document, or case. Do not compare two independent confidence intervals as a substitute for a paired test.

    Sample size for estimating a proportion within margin e at normal critical value z, using conservative p = 0.5:

    n ~= z^2 * 0.25 / e^2

    At 95% confidence (z = 1.96) and e = 0.02, n ~= 2,401. This estimates one aggregate proportion. Critical slices need their own effective sample sizes and uncertainty.

    Zero observed violations does not prove a zero violation probability. With n independent trials and no failures, a one-sided 95% binomial upper bound solves (1 - p)^n = 0.05, giving p_upper = 1 - 0.05^(1/n), approximately 3/n. For 1,000 clean trials, the upper bound is about 0.30%, which can still be unacceptable for a high-impact action. Enforce invariants through deterministic controls and adversarial tests as well as sampled quality evaluation; report the sample and its limits.

    Predeclare primary metrics and critical slices. Gating many metrics or slices at separate nominal 95% thresholds inflates the probability of at least one false finding. Use a declared family-wise or false-discovery procedure, simultaneous intervals, or a hierarchical testing plan; also report effect size and power or minimum detectable effect. Exploratory slices remain evidence for a later confirmatory run, not silent additional release gates.

    For two-stage systems:

    P(required_evidence_retrieved AND correct_output)
= P(required_evidence_retrieved)
* P(correct_output | required_evidence_retrieved)

    The factorization is diagnostic, not an independence claim. It equals end-to-end success only when the product contract requires retrieved evidence. For correctness alone, add P(no_required_evidence_retrieved) * P(correct_output | no_required_evidence_retrieved): a model may guess correctly without support, while a correctly abstaining unanswerable case may need no retrieved evidence. Measure these branches separately from joined per-example records.

    Judge models and nondeterminism¶

    An LLM judge is a measurement instrument. Version its model, prompt, examples, decoding, and rubric. Calibrate it against blinded human judgments, report disagreement by slice, and retain a human escalation path. Avoid using the same model family as both candidate and sole judge when correlated preferences could matter.

    Run repeated samples only when production behavior is stochastic. Report pass probability or metric distribution, not the best sample. Pin deterministic settings where the task requires repeatability. Preserve raw outputs and parser failures.

    Pinning temperature or a seed is not itself a determinism guarantee across kernels, batching, providers, or model revisions. Verify repeatability on the deployed implementation; persist accepted outputs for replay rather than regenerating them as evidence.

    Release state and evidence¶

    stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Proposed
    Proposed --> OfflineRunning
    OfflineRunning --> Rejected: gate fails
    OfflineRunning --> Shadow: gates pass
    Shadow --> Canary: operational gates pass
    Shadow --> Rejected
    Canary --> Approved: quality and SLO pass
    Canary --> RolledBack: trigger fires
    Approved --> Monitoring
    Monitoring --> RolledBack: production trigger fires
    Monitoring --> Retired
    EvaluationRun(id, contract_id, candidate_bundle, baseline_bundle,
              manifest_digest, runner_digest, start, end, status)
ExampleResult(run_id, example_id, attempt, stage_outputs,
              metric_inputs, errors, latency, usage)
MetricResult(run_id, metric_id, slice, value, interval, sample_count)
ReleaseDecision(id, run_id, outcome, gates, exceptions, approvers, time)

    The decision references immutable results. A recomputation creates a new run and decision.

    Online evaluation and delayed outcomes¶

    Shadow traffic validates production-shaped inputs without affecting users, provided duplicate processing is legal and tool effects are disabled. Canary traffic validates real behavior on a stable cohort. Keep assignment stable by user or entity when alternating versions would change the experience.

    Delayed labels require an outcome maturity rule. Compare cohorts only after the same observation window. Track censoring, interventions, reviewer overrides, and policy changes. Reserve a control slice when continuous optimization would otherwise erase the counterfactual.

    Sequential monitoring repeatedly examines data, so a naive fixed-sample threshold inflates false alarms. Predeclare decision cadence, minimum sample, stopping rule, and rollback authority.

    Failure, security, and testing¶

    Failure Discriminating evidence Control
    contamination similarity to training/tuning corpus quarantine and replace examples
    slice regression hidden by average metric and simultaneous interval per critical slice predeclared multiplicity-aware slice gate
    judge drift human/judge disagreement over time recalibration and dual scoring
    flaky tool or parser stage error by attempt and version deterministic fixtures and retries only where safe
    benchmark gaming metric gain without product outcome blind holdout and mature outcome check
    sensitive fixture leak access and export audit restricted storage and redacted reports
    canary harm invariant counter or complaint signal automatic containment and rollback

    Test metric reducers with hand-calculated fixtures. Property-test aggregation and slice membership. Replay archived runs after evaluator changes. Inject malformed outputs, missing citations, empty retrieval, tool timeout-after-commit, evaluator outage, and partial result upload. Verify that a failed or incomplete run cannot become release-approved.

    Migration and evolution¶

    1. Version metric definitions; never reinterpret an old metric ID.
    2. Dual-score old and new rubrics on frozen outputs and quantify changed decisions.
    3. Add new slices in observe-only mode, then set minimum sample and gates.
    4. Rebuild evaluation sets from reviewed production failures while retaining an untouched audit holdout.
    5. Run old and new judge models in parallel before cutover.
    6. Remove old evidence readers only after retention and audit windows close.

    Interview exercise¶

    Design evaluation and release for an enterprise support assistant that retrieves policy, drafts an answer, may propose a refund, and receives final resolution labels 30 days later. It serves five languages and regulated customers. Define answerability, stage metrics, critical slices, judgment policy, sample-size assumptions, shadow/canary design, delayed outcomes, safety gates, exception policy, and rollback evidence.

    L5 and L6 signals¶

    L5¶

    • Defines a versioned evaluation contract and immutable dataset manifests.
    • Separates retrieval, grounding, task, safety, operational, and cost metrics.
    • Uses paired comparisons and uncertainty rather than point estimates alone.
    • Calibrates model judges and retains raw evidence and human escalation.
    • Connects offline, shadow, canary, mature outcomes, and rollback.

    L6¶

    • Establishes cross-product metric definitions, critical-slice policy, and evidence ownership.
    • Governs contamination, evaluation-set renewal, release exceptions, and judge retirement.
    • Designs sequential decisions and delayed-outcome attribution across portfolios.
    • Connects capacity, risk, review economics, and release policy.
    • Names evidence that can falsify the evaluation program itself.

    Final design checklist¶

    • Every score resolves to exact inputs, versions, code, and judgments.
    • Invariants cannot be traded against aggregate utility.
    • Critical slices have sample counts, intervals, and owners.
    • Judge calibration and disagreement are visible.
    • Online experiments protect users, data, and downstream systems.
    • Delayed labels use a declared maturity and censoring policy.
    • Failed and incomplete runs cannot approve a release.
    • Migration preserves historical meaning and auditability.
