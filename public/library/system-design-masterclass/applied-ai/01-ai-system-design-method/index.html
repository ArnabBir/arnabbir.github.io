    Applied AI System Design Method¶

    Applied AI design starts with a product decision, not a model. The system must turn uncertain model behavior into a bounded, measurable, reversible service. This chapter gives a repeatable L5/L6 method for doing that without hiding ordinary distributed-systems concerns behind AI terminology.

    Use the Applied AI L5/L6 rubric to score a completed design. Use this chapter to construct one.

    1. Frame the decision before the architecture¶

    Write a one-paragraph problem contract:

    1. Actor: who requests or receives the result?
    2. Decision: what prediction, ranking, generation, or action occurs?
    3. Evidence: what data may influence it, and how fresh must that data be?
    4. Consequence: what happens when the result is wrong, late, unavailable, or refused?
    5. Authority: may the system advise, decide, or execute?
    6. Feedback: when does ground truth arrive, and can it be trusted?

    Principle: A model score or generated answer is evidence inside a product decision. It is not, by itself, the decision policy or authority to act.

    Decide whether AI is justified¶

    Compare the AI proposal with a deterministic baseline. Rules, search, templates, or an ordinary workflow are often cheaper to test and easier to reverse.

    Question Deterministic baseline favored Statistical model favored Generative model favored
    Output contract exact rule or lookup score, class, or rank open-ended synthesis
    Error definition fully specifiable measurable from labeled examples partly judgment-based
    Required evidence structured state patterns across features language, images, or mixed context
    Reversibility usually direct depends on downstream action generated text may already influence users
    Evaluation assertions labeled metrics and calibration task, grounding, safety, and human judgment

    Do not proceed until the team can state why the baseline fails and which measurable outcome the AI path should improve.

    2. Define scope, requirements, and invariants¶

    For a customer-support answer assistant, a useful contract might be:

    • return a cited draft within 3 seconds at p95;
    • answer only from documents authorized for the requesting agent;
    • abstain when evidence is missing or conflicting;
    • never send a reply or mutate an account;
    • retain a replayable decision record for 30 days;
    • support an immediate return to keyword search and templates.

    Important exclusions might include autonomous refunds, unrestricted web access, foundation-model training, and legal advice.

    Write invariants as conditions that remain true during retries, failover, migration, and overload:

    • policy and tenant checks happen before any provider route;
    • a logical request has one immutable input envelope and may have several attempts;
    • every result records model, prompt, policy, data, and index versions;
    • output and spend reserves cannot be silently consumed by retrieved context;
    • side effects use deterministic authorization and idempotency outside the model;
    • shadow output cannot affect users or learning labels;
    • deletion propagates to source, projection, cache, ledger, and future datasets under a stated policy.

    For write-capable systems, apply the stricter controls in RAG and Agent Security. For model lineage and delayed labels, use Production AI Lifecycle.

    3. Turn quality into an error contract¶

    "High quality" is not a requirement. Enumerate observable error classes and assign each a response.

    Error Example Detection evidence Product behavior
    false positive safe payment blocked mature outcome label review, appeal, policy cap
    false negative fraud approved delayed loss signal defense in depth, monitoring
    unsupported answer claim lacks evidence citation judgment abstain or label uncertainty
    stale answer superseded policy cited source/index version suppress stale generation
    unsafe content disallowed instruction safety suite and report block, transform, escalate
    latency miss answer after usefulness window stage spans degrade or time out
    unavailable dependency feature or model endpoint down health and attempt ledger tested fallback
    unknown side effect tool timed out after submit idempotency/status lookup reconcile before retry

    Define the cost of each error by user, slice, and action reversibility. Accuracy can be the wrong objective when classes are imbalanced, review capacity is constrained, or errors have unequal cost. Ranking requires top-K and downstream outcome measures. Generation requires task success, support, refusal, safety, latency, and cost.

    4. Quantify workload and uncertainty¶

    Separate request traffic from model work:

    input tokens/s  = requests/s * mean input tokens/request
output tokens/s = requests/s * mean generated tokens/request
concurrency     = arrival rate/s * mean service time/s
usage cost/day  = sum over attempts in the day and unit types (units/attempt * currency/unit)
capacity cost/day = reserved compute-hours/day * currency/compute-hour
daily cost      = usage cost/day + capacity cost/day + storage cost/day
                  + network cost/day + other resource cost/day

    Use distributions, not only means. Record p50, p95, and maximum prompt size; expected output caps; retrieval fan-out; feature fan-out; class mix; burst factor; retry rate; and deadline.

    Assumption (2026-09, illustrative): An answer service receives 120 requests/s at peak. Mean input is 3,200 tokens, p95 input is 9,000 tokens, mean output is 350 tokens, 5% of requests use a shadow model, and the permitted attempt rate is 1.03.

    Primary demand is 120 * 3,200 = 384,000 input tokens/s and 120 * 350 = 42,000 output tokens/s. Including shadows and attempts, the planning multiplier is 1.05 * 1.03 = 1.0815, giving about 415,296 input tokens/s and 45,423 output tokens/s. Capacity must still be tested against the p95 length mix because a mean-only model hides queue and memory pressure.

    Keep units and assumptions visible. The calculations workbook provides solved token, GPU, queue, reliability, retrieval, and cost exercises without prescribing vendor performance.

    The 1.05 * 1.03 estimate assumes shadow requests have the same mean token demand as primary requests and that 1.03 attempts applies to both streams. If either differs, sum arrival_rate * route_fraction * mean_attempts * mean_tokens_per_attempt by route and class. Avoid multiplying by retries again when measured token usage already includes all attempts. Provider usage charges and self-hosted capacity cost must cover disjoint resources rather than charging the same accelerator twice.

    5. Draw the decision and evidence loop¶

    flowchart LR
    U[User or producer] --> G[Policy and admission]
    G --> O[Orchestrator]
    O --> D[Data and retrieval]
    O --> M[Model route]
    D --> M
    M --> V[Validation and decision policy]
    V --> R[Result or bounded action]
    G --> L[(Decision ledger)]
    D --> L
    M --> L
    V --> L
    R --> F[Outcome and feedback]
    F --> E[Evaluation and monitoring]
    E --> C[Release control plane]
    C --> G
    C --> O

    This diagram separates four concerns:

    • the data plane serves requests under a fixed version bundle;
    • the evidence plane records what happened and joins later outcomes;
    • the evaluation plane determines whether quality and safety remain acceptable;
    • the control plane approves, deploys, limits, and retires versions.

    A request must never observe an accidental mixture such as prompt B with parser A, query embedding B with index A, or model B with feature schema A. Publish a compatibility bundle and bind each request to one generation.

    6. Define interfaces and durable state¶

    Keep a provider-neutral logical interface. Provider attempts are internal details.

    POST /v1/decisions
Idempotency-Key: 5f54...
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "task": "support.answer.v3",
  "tenant_id": "tenant-17",
  "subject": {"ticket_id": "t-902"},
  "input": {"question": "..."},
  "constraints": {
    "deadline_ms": 3000,
    "max_output_tokens": 500,
    "data_class": "internal"
  }
}

    {
  "request_id": "req-481",
  "status": "succeeded",
  "result": {"text": "...", "citations": ["chunk-31"]},
  "completion_reason": "stop",
  "version_bundle": "support-prod-2026-09-14.2"
}

    Core records:

    VersionBundle(id, model, tokenizer, prompt, policy, feature_view, index, evaluator)
LogicalRequest(id, principal, tenant, task, input_digest, budget, deadline, bundle, state)
Attempt(id, request_id, route, provider_request_id, start, end, usage, outcome)
Evidence(id, request_id, source_versions, chunk_spans, feature_versions, digests)
Decision(id, request_id, raw_output_digest, validated_result, action, reason)
Outcome(decision_id, label, observed_at, maturity, source, correction_version)

    Store sensitive payloads only when purpose, retention, access control, and deletion behavior are explicit. Metrics should use redacted dimensions; replay payloads belong in a narrower encrypted store.

    7. Allocate latency, capacity, and failure budgets¶

    Start from the end-to-end SLO and assign internal deadline allocations:

    Stage Illustrative deadline allocation Timeout behavior
    edge, auth, admission 80 ms reject before paid work
    data and retrieval 350 ms return bounded partial evidence or fail
    queue 300 ms shed by class
    model 1,800 ms cancel attempt
    validation and rendering 170 ms use validated fallback
    network reserve 300 ms preserve for variance
    total 3,000 ms product deadline

    These allocations sum to the product deadline; they are not independently measured p95 values. Percentiles of dependent stage latencies are not additive. Validate the caller-visible p95 from joint per-request traces under representative load, and use stage distributions to identify which allocation is burning the deadline.

    Principle: A timeout is a statement that the caller stopped waiting. It does not prove that the dependency stopped work or that a side effect did not occur.

    Retries must fit the original deadline and spend budget. Retry only errors that are transient and safe. Hedge only where duplicate work is acceptable, cancellation is effective, and the added load is part of capacity planning. Admission should consider tokens, accelerator memory, queue age, tenant quota, data class, model availability, and spend rather than request count alone.

    8. Design evaluation before rollout¶

    Create a versioned evaluation contract with:

    • representative tasks and traffic slices;
    • difficult, adversarial, and unanswerable cases;
    • expected evidence or labels and judgment instructions;
    • stage metrics for retrieval, model output, policy, and end-to-end behavior;
    • operational gates for latency, error, fallback, and cost;
    • confidence intervals or paired tests where statistical comparison matters;
    • contamination controls and a renewal owner;
    • explicit release, rollback, and exception criteria.

    Offline evidence does not establish production safety by itself. Use replay, shadowing, stable-entity canaries, bounded exposure, and mature outcome checkpoints. For detailed lifecycle mechanics, link to Production AI Lifecycle rather than duplicating its registry and delayed-label design.

    Benchmark (measurement requirement, 2026-09): Before launch, benchmark the exact model, tokenizer, prompt, hardware or provider class, input/output length distribution, concurrency, warm-up, duration, and success definition. Record p50/p95/p99 latency, token throughput, errors, quality gates, and cost. A catalog limit or another team's result is not this workload's benchmark.

    9. Make degradation explicit¶

    Define a fallback matrix before incidents:

    Failure Safe degradation Must not happen
    primary model saturated smaller approved model for eligible classes route restricted data to an unapproved region
    retrieval stale cited search or abstention answer against mismatched source/index epochs
    feature service unavailable compatible reduced feature view silently default safety-critical features
    evaluator unavailable freeze releases approve without evidence
    ledger unavailable reject high-risk decisions execute unaudited irreversible action
    cost budget exhausted reduce optional context or queue batch reset budget on every retry

    The failure cards contain incident discriminators and containment patterns. Use Reliability, Deadlines, Retries, and Pools, and Operational Readiness for the general distributed-systems mechanisms.

    10. Threat model data and authority¶

    Classify data before choosing a route. Define allowed regions, providers, retention, training use, logging, and human access for each class. Authenticate producers, authorize subjects and purpose, validate schemas, encrypt storage and transport, and log administrative changes.

    Treat prompts, documents, model output, and tool output as untrusted. The model never receives standing credentials. Side effects require a typed command, caller-bound authorization, current-state validation, idempotency, and reconciliation. High-impact actions need approval bound to exact arguments.

    Deletion is a workflow across source objects, feature stores, embeddings, caches, replay ledgers, snapshots, and future training filters. Define lawful exceptions for immutable audit evidence and prevent broad replay access from becoming a second data lake. See Privacy and Data Lifecycle.

    11. Compare alternatives and evolution paths¶

    State why the selected design wins under current evidence:

    Choice Prefer when Cost or risk
    managed model API variable demand, small platform team, approved data route provider limits, dependency, unit economics
    self-hosted model steady scale, specialized runtime, residency/control need accelerator capacity and serving expertise
    one model simple policy and evaluation correlated failure and capability ceiling
    routed model fleet distinct tasks, cost tiers, residency needs policy, compatibility, and evaluation complexity
    synchronous request result is immediately needed and bounded tail latency and client coupling
    asynchronous job large input, long runtime, resumable output state machine and delayed UX

    Use reversible phases: deterministic baseline, offline evaluation, shadow, small canary, staged expansion, and retirement. Keep the old compatible bundle warm through the rollback window. A model, prompt, index, feature, parser, threshold, or policy change is a release when it can change behavior.

    Vendor (Kubernetes documentation, accessed 2026-09-06): Kubernetes exposes vendor device plugins as schedulable extended resources, but its documentation does not define model-level batching, KV-cache admission, or AI quality gates. Schedule GPUs

    Decision: Use cluster scheduling for allocatable devices and build or adopt a separate model-serving control plane for model compatibility, warm capacity, request admission, and quality-bearing releases.

    Production report (Uber, published 2017-09-05): Uber described Michelangelo as an internal platform spanning data management, training, evaluation, deployment, prediction, and monitoring. This is evidence that lifecycle integration mattered in Uber's environment, not proof that every organization needs the same platform boundary. Introducing Michelangelo

    12. Interview exercise¶

    Prompt: Design an AI assistant that summarizes insurance claims, identifies missing documents, and recommends whether a claim should enter manual investigation. It processes 4 million claims/month. The recommendation must arrive within 10 seconds, documents contain sensitive data, investigation outcomes mature over 90 days, and the assistant may not deny a claim.

    Clarify:

    • claim types, document sizes and languages, arrival bursts, and retention;
    • whether summarization and investigation recommendation use the same evidence;
    • false-positive review cost versus false-negative loss;
    • citation and explanation obligations;
    • reviewer capacity and queue-age SLO;
    • regional and provider restrictions;
    • label maturity, corrections, and feedback-loop risk;
    • availability, cost, and fallback expectations.

    Worked design outline¶

    Use immutable document versions and a manifest per claim. Parse and classify asynchronously, quarantine failures, and retain offsets and parser versions. Build a point-in-time feature view for the recommendation and tenant-scoped retrieval for cited summaries. The synchronous decision request binds a compatible model, prompt, index, feature, and policy bundle. A deterministic policy prevents denial and routes only bounded recommendations to the review queue.

    Record the evidence bundle, raw score, recommendation, policy reason, citations, and later reviewer/outcome versions. Gate releases on parser coverage, retrieval support, summary factuality, calibrated ranking at review capacity, critical slices, 10-second latency, fallback, and cost. Shadow first; canary by stable claim hash; retain an exploration/control slice until 90-day labels mature. Degrade to deterministic completeness checks and document search. Never silently substitute a provider that violates the claim's data class.

    L5 and L6 signals¶

    L5¶

    • Converts the product request into users, decisions, errors, SLOs, authority, and exclusions.
    • Quantifies tokens, concurrency, storage, retries, and spend with labeled assumptions.
    • Defines versioned interfaces, evidence, evaluation, fallback, and rollback.
    • Separates model output from deterministic policy and authorization.
    • Handles overload, privacy, deletion, and delayed outcomes coherently.

    L6¶

    • Partitions risk and degradability across products, tenants, regions, and action classes.
    • Establishes compatibility bundles, release governance, exception ownership, and retirement evidence.
    • Models provider and capacity concentration, evacuation, fairness, and portfolio economics.
    • Designs cross-team provenance, deletion, evaluation-set stewardship, and incident accountability.
    • Names which assumptions must be retired by benchmark or production evidence and who owns that work.

    Final design checklist¶

    • The decision, evidence, consequence, authority, and feedback are explicit.
    • A deterministic baseline and important exclusions are documented.
    • Quality is an error contract with slices and product responses.
    • Workload estimates use distributions and separate input, output, memory, and attempts.
    • One immutable compatibility bundle binds every request.
    • Logical requests, attempts, evidence, decisions, and outcomes are distinguishable.
    • Deadlines, admission, retries, fallback, and unknown outcomes are bounded.
    • Offline, shadow, canary, mature-outcome, and rollback gates are defined.
    • Data-class routing, least privilege, retention, and deletion are enforced.
    • Operational metrics identify the causal stage, version, tenant, and route.
    • Alternatives and evolution triggers are tied to evidence.
    • The five-minute summary explains correctness, capacity, recovery, and governance.
