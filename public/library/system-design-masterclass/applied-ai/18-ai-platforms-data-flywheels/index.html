    AI Platforms and Data Flywheels¶

    An AI platform provides shared contracts for data, models, prompts, retrieval, evaluation, serving, tools, policy, and evidence without forcing every workload onto one runtime. A data flywheel is a governed learning loop in which product interactions produce reviewed evidence that improves future versions. It is not automatic self-training from user behavior.

    Platform thesis¶

    Centralize contracts and control points that reduce duplicated risk. Decentralize task ownership and product decisions that need domain context. The platform should make the safe path easier while preserving explicit escape hatches and ownership.

    flowchart LR
    T[Product teams] --> P[Platform APIs and templates]
    P --> D[Data and provenance]
    P --> E[Evaluation and registry]
    P --> S[Serving and gateways]
    P --> W[Workflow and tools]
    P --> G[Policy and audit]
    D --> F[Governed evidence loop]
    S --> F
    W --> F
    F --> E
    E --> P

    Platform invariants¶

    1. Shared control-plane nouns do not imply one shared data plane or queue.
    2. Every production decision resolves to immutable data, model, prompt, index, tool, policy, and runtime versions.
    3. Product teams own task quality and consequences; platform teams own shared contract correctness and service SLOs.
    4. Feedback enters training or evaluation only through declared purpose, provenance, consent/authority, review, and split assignment.
    5. Deletion and correction tombstone source and derived data, indexes, caches, and retained payloads; lineage marks affected models and adapters for restricted use, destruction, verified unlearning, or clean retraining. Propagation alone does not prove removal of learned influence.
    6. Exceptions have owner, scope, expiry, compensating control, and migration plan.
    7. Platform adoption and reuse are measured with outcome, reliability, and economics, not service count.

    Capability map and boundaries¶

    Capability Platform ownership Product ownership
    data contracts lineage schema, storage classes, deletion protocol source meaning, collection authority, labels
    model registry immutable artifacts, signatures, compatibility model choice and task suitability
    prompt registry versioning, review, rollout integration instruction content and domain rubric
    retrieval index contracts, tenancy, migration primitives corpus, relevance judgments, answerability
    evaluation runners, manifests, statistics, evidence store metrics, slices, gates, adjudication
    serving gateway, capacity, routing, telemetry fallback semantics and product SLO
    tools/workflows typed contracts, identity, durability action policy, compensation, approval
    governance policy engine, audit, retention enforcement risk acceptance and domain compliance

    Do not create a platform service merely because a capability appears in this table. Standardize when at least two teams share stable semantics and the control reduces material cost or risk.

    Project(id, owners, data_domains, budgets, risk_tier)

EvaluationContract(id, task, sets, metrics, slices, gates, owners)

    The governed data flywheel¶

    flowchart TD
    A[Production interaction] --> B[Decision and evidence record]
    B --> C[Outcome maturity]
    C --> D[Privacy and purpose filter]
    D --> E[Sampling and adjudication]
    E --> F{Destination}
    F --> G[Evaluation set candidate]
    F --> H[Training set candidate]
    F --> I[Failure and threat corpus]
    G --> J[Independent review and separate destination manifests]
    H --> J
    I --> J
    J --> K[Candidate version]
    K --> L[Evaluation and release]
    L --> A

    The loop has brakes. Negative user feedback may reflect preference, policy disagreement, abuse, or a product defect. Clicks are affected by ranking position. Reviewer decisions can encode workload pressure. Outcomes can be delayed or censored. Treat every signal as evidence with a generation process, not as a label by default.

    The joined review node does not merge training and holdout examples: each destination gets a separate immutable manifest, and held-out answers never enter candidate fitting. Log exposure and selection, not only feedback. If the old policy reviews only high-risk cases, observed labels cannot establish performance on never-reviewed cases. Use a legally and ethically permitted randomized audit/control sample or report that population as unidentified; weighting cannot recover outcomes where selection probability is zero. Hidden Technical Debt, verified 2026-09-08, explicitly identifies hidden feedback loops and data dependencies as system risks; these sampling and manifest controls are this chapter's design response, not guarantees from the paper.

    Feedback event and label maturity¶

    FeedbackEvent(
  id, logical_decision_id, principal_class, task,
  observed_at, event_time, outcome_type, value,
  source, intervention, maturity_at,
  data_policy, version_bundle, provenance
)

    Join feedback by stable decision ID, not by fuzzy text or timestamp. Preserve which model response, retrieved sources, policy, and tool effects the user saw. Record interventions such as human override, discount, escalation, or missing exposure.

    Define maturity per outcome. A support resolution may mature after reopen risk passes; fraud chargeback labels may take months. Unmatured events remain censored. Corrections append new facts and invalidate derived manifests through lineage.

    Flywheel calculations¶

    Evidence yield¶

    eligible_feedback
= interactions
 * P(consent_or_authority)
 * P(joined | consent_or_authority)
 * P(mature | joined, consent_or_authority)
 * P(quality | mature, joined, consent_or_authority)

    For 10 million monthly interactions with sequential conditional conversion rates 0.8, 0.95, 0.6, and 0.5:

    10,000,000 * 0.8 * 0.95 * 0.6 * 0.5
= 2,280,000 eligible examples/month

    This is a funnel volume estimate. Each rate is conditional on passing the preceding stages, so no independence assumption is needed. It is not proof of balance or correctness; estimate the rates from joined cohorts rather than multiplying unrelated marginal rates.

    Review capacity¶

    reviewer_hours
= sampled_examples * minutes_per_review / 60

reviewers_required
= reviewer_hours / productive_hours_per_reviewer

    At 30,000 reviews/month and four minutes each, work is 2,000 hours. At 120 productive review hours per reviewer-month, plan ceil(2,000 / 120) = 17 reviewers before quality audits and adjudication.

    Platform economics¶

    reuse_savings
= avoided_team_build_cost - platform_incremental_cost

cost_per_accepted_outcome
= total_platform_and_product_cost / accepted_outcomes

platform_adoption
= production_workloads_on_supported_path / eligible_production_workloads

    Avoided cost is counterfactual and easy to inflate. Validate it with migration effort, incident rate, lead time, and total ownership cost.

    Multi-tenant platform architecture¶

    Separate global metadata from regional or isolated execution. Projects, versions, evaluation evidence, and policy may share a control plane while regulated data, serving, and indexes remain in dedicated cells.

    flowchart TD
    G[Global catalog and policy metadata] --> R1[Regional control plane A]
    G --> R2[Regional control plane B]
    R1 --> C1[Shared low-risk cell]
    R1 --> C2[Restricted tenant cell]
    R2 --> C3[Sovereign cell]
    C1 --> E[Normalized evidence contracts]
    C2 --> E
    C3 --> E
    E --> G

    Normalize metadata without exporting restricted payloads. A sovereign cell can publish aggregate health or signed release evidence while keeping prompts and documents local.

    Golden paths and escape hatches¶

    A golden path includes templates, APIs, policy defaults, evaluation gates, dashboards, cost attribution, and runbooks. It should produce a deployable, observable, and reviewable service without hidden manual work.

    An escape hatch is valid when the supported path cannot meet a documented workload, security, or economics requirement. It needs an owner, threat model, telemetry contract, cost allocation, support boundary, expiry or review date, and convergence plan. Untracked bypasses become permanent shadow platforms.

    Platform state and governance¶

    stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Experimental
    Experimental --> Supported: contracts and SLO proven
    Supported --> Preferred: adoption and economics proven
    Preferred --> Deprecated: replacement approved
    Supported --> Deprecated
    Deprecated --> Retired: migrations complete
    Experimental --> Retired: evidence insufficient

    Publish support level, owners, SLO, compatible versions, region/data classes, cost model, and deprecation date. A component is not supported because a central team deployed it once.

    Failure, security, and testing¶

    Failure Control Evidence
    one platform outage affects all AI cells and bounded dependencies cell isolation game day
    bad shared prompt/policy signed versions and scoped rollout stable-cohort rollback
    feedback poisoning provenance, trust tiers, anomaly review poisoned-cohort evaluation
    privacy-purpose drift declared use and lineage enforcement denied reuse test
    deletion stops at source lineage-driven tombstones and artifact ineligibility payload-erasure proof plus destruction, clean retraining, or validated unlearning evidence
    platform forces wrong workload workload classes and escape path architecture review record
    evaluation monopoly product-owned contracts and audit set independent replay
    hidden cost transfer end-to-end allocation product/platform reconciliation

    Contract-test every shared resource and event. Maintain one prior compatible client and event reader. Run cell, identity, registry, gateway, and evidence-store outage drills. Test cross-tenant access, regional policy, poisoned feedback, duplicate outcomes, corrected labels, deletion, and replay.

    Security separates platform operators, product releasers, data stewards, evaluators, and exception approvers. Sensitive evidence uses purpose-bound access. Platform admins do not automatically receive customer payload access.

    Operating model and ownership¶

    Use a federated model:

    • platform teams own shared APIs, reliability, capacity, policy enforcement, and migrations;
    • product teams own task contracts, quality, user impact, and degraded behavior;
    • data owners own collection authority, lineage, retention, and correction;
    • evaluation owners own sets, rubrics, judge calibration, and release evidence;
    • security/risk owners define action classes, exception authority, and incident obligations;
    • FinOps owners reconcile unit cost, commitments, and allocation.

    Review platform roadmaps with measurable adoption friction, incident evidence, exception volume, unsupported demand, and total cost. Do not optimize for internal service count.

    Migration and evolution¶

    1. Inventory existing product paths, versions, data uses, and owners.
    2. Define shared contracts before replacing runtimes.
    3. Add adapters that emit common evidence while legacy systems remain authoritative.
    4. Migrate one workload class and risk tier at a time.
    5. Dual-run releases, telemetry, and cost allocation through a bounded overlap.
    6. Prove rollback, deletion, and incident response on the new path.
    7. Remove the old path only after traffic, retained evidence, paused workflows, and audit windows clear.

    Platform standardization is reversible at the implementation layer but durable at the contract layer. Prefer open protocols and portable artifacts where switching cost is not the differentiator.

    Interview exercise¶

    Design an AI platform for 40 product teams building classifiers, RAG assistants, batch pipelines, and tool-using workflows across three regions. Regulated teams require isolated cells. Outcomes mature from minutes to 90 days. Define shared and product-owned capabilities, resource contracts, evidence flow, data-flywheel controls, calculations, tenancy, reliability, cost allocation, golden paths, escape hatches, and a migration from five existing stacks.

    L5 and L6 signals¶

    L5¶

    • Centralizes stable contracts while preserving workload-specific execution.
    • Defines version bundles, feedback provenance, maturity, deletion, and release evidence.
    • Calculates evidence yield, review capacity, and outcome unit cost.
    • Separates platform, product, data, evaluation, security, and FinOps ownership.
    • Provides tested golden paths, cells, exceptions, and migrations.

    L6¶

    • Establishes organization-wide platform policy without erasing product accountability.
    • Governs cross-region/cell failure, provider concentration, evidence retention, and exception portfolios.
    • Connects adoption, developer lead time, reliability, capacity commitments, and economics.
    • Designs feedback loops against poisoning, bias amplification, censoring, and purpose drift.
    • Names evidence that would retire a platform component or reverse centralization.

    Final design checklist¶

    • Shared contracts do not force unlike workloads onto one queue or runtime.
    • Every decision is reconstructable from an immutable version bundle.
    • Feedback has provenance, authority, maturity, interventions, and destination.
    • Evaluation and training sets remain intentionally separated.
    • Deletion and correction reach derived data and artifact eligibility; learned-influence claims have destruction, retraining, or validated unlearning evidence.
    • Cells and escape hatches have explicit support boundaries.
    • Platform economics use accepted outcomes and total ownership cost.
    • Migration preserves rollback, audit, and paused-workflow compatibility.
