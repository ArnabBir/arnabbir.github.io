    Interviewer Packet¶

    Practice home | Rubric | Exemplars

    This file contains answer-bearing disclosures and probes. Keep it closed until the candidate's first pass is complete.

    Objective¶

    Assess whether the candidate can deliver useful retrieval without allowing generated behavior, stale indexes, caches, or regional failover to exceed a principal's current authority. Reward a coherent safe path over a catalog of AI products.

    Mode selection¶

    Mode Core evidence Optional branch
    15 minutes Boundaries, invariants, question contract, answer/no-answer, revocation race Citation mutation
    30 minutes Safe core plus records/events, hybrid retrieval, deletion, evaluation, telemetry Group expansion
    45 minutes Full core plus capacity, skew, threat model, regions, failure attribution Model outage
    60 minutes Full design plus zero-downtime migration, rollout, ownership, reversal Adversarial review

    Opening script¶

    Design a read-only enterprise knowledge assistant over 50 million documents and 20,000 tenants. Answers require citations and must respect current permission and residency. Start with scope and correctness invariants. Narrate authority, versions, and failure behavior as you draw.

    Do not suggest ACL-prefiltering, hybrid retrieval, reranking, tombstones, or dual indexes. Let the candidate discover the mechanisms.

    Facts available only on request¶

    Candidate asks about Disclosure Signal to observe
    Identity OIDC authenticates users. An entitlement service resolves nested groups and returns a signed, tenant-bound policy snapshot with an epoch. Does not trust claims copied from a client token forever
    ACL semantics Sources support user/group allows, explicit denies, inheritance, and tenant-approved cross-project sharing; deny wins. Establishes one normalized policy contract
    Source events Connectors provide a cursor but can duplicate, reorder, omit, and later repair events. Nightly source enumeration is available. Combines ordered application with anti-entropy
    Content versions Most sources expose immutable revision IDs; object storage versions require the connector to compute a content digest. Preserves citation identity
    Query mix 35% exact identifiers/names, 45% conceptual questions, 20% mixed. Four languages account for 95%. Justifies retrieval stages and slices
    Answer policy No answer is preferable to an unsupported answer. A qualification is acceptable when sources conflict and both are cited. Defines thresholds and user semantics
    Deletion Contract clock begins when the authoritative source accepts deletion or permission revocation, not when a connector notices it. Identifies source-to-serving measurement
    Caches Product wants semantic and answer caching, but correctness overrides hit rate. Keys and reauthorizes by tenant/policy/evidence generation
    Residency Tenants choose EU, US, or APAC. EU content and derived data may use only approved EU facilities; outages may reduce availability. Fails compliant rather than crossing region
    Model provider Regional endpoints exist. Provider contract disables training and has zero payload retention, but platform must still minimize sent text. Treats contract as one control, not the whole boundary
    Scale Average 12 chunks/document, 1,024-dimensional current embeddings, RF=2 inside a region. Performs derived capacity math
    Evaluation Product has thumbs-up logs but no curated benchmark. Builds versioned labels rather than treating clicks as truth
    Existing index Current lexical index is authoritative for search and supports current ACL filtering; migration introduces vector retrieval. Preserves safe authority during migration

    Checkpoints and non-leading probes¶

    Minute 5¶

    Look for authorities and non-disclosure. If absent:

    Which facts may be stale, and which may never be stale at disclosure time?

    Minute 15¶

    Look for an authorized query trace, immutable evidence, and no-answer. If absent:

    A useful paragraph exists but the user lost group membership 20 seconds ago. Trace it.

    Minute 30¶

    Look for end-to-end deletion measurement and evaluation decomposition. If absent:

    Queue lag is zero but a deletion canary still appears. Where can the old bytes survive?

    Minute 45¶

    Look for workload arithmetic, tenant skew, regional behavior, and attribution. Ask:

    Supported-answer rate fell three points after a release. What retained evidence identifies the responsible stage?

    Minute 55¶

    Look for generation migration, gates, rollback freshness, and owners. Ask:

    Shadow quality is better overall but two small-language tenants regress and migration storage has 12% headroom. Who may cut over?

    Branching follow-ups¶

    Choose the branch nearest the candidate's current design. Do not exhaust the list.

    Branch A: Global vector search then filtering¶

    1. Does the ANN service observe hidden vectors, tenant size, scores, or timing?
    2. What happens when authorized matches are crowded out of top K by inaccessible matches?
    3. Can an empty-result timing oracle reveal a document's existence?
    4. What physical or cryptographic boundary narrows the candidate universe?

    Strong evidence: tenant/security-domain routing before candidate generation, mandatory server-side filters within that boundary, current authorization before hydration, and negative isolation tests.

    Branch B: Authorization is strong¶

    1. Nested group expansion is unavailable. Which requests proceed?
    2. Membership changes after retrieval but before streaming token 500. What happens?
    3. A shared document changes from project A to project B while old chunks remain.
    4. How can support investigate without bypassing tenant policy?

    Strong evidence: bounded signed snapshots, fail-closed freshness rules, pre-context reauthorization, stream cancellation or policy binding, tombstones, and audited break-glass with customer-visible governance.

    Branch C: Retrieval is strong¶

    1. How do you fuse incomparable BM25 and cosine scores?
    2. Which metric identifies loss before reranking?
    3. The reranker times out. What safe quality mode remains?
    4. How do duplicate chunks from two revisions avoid crowding context?

    Strong evidence: rank-based or calibrated fusion, recall@K and NDCG separation, bounded fallback, version-aware dedupe, diversity, and token budgets.

    Branch D: Candidate says citations prevent hallucinations¶

    1. A citation is relevant to the topic but not to the attached claim. Is it valid?
    2. A sentence combines two claims supported by different spans.
    3. The title is visible but opening the span is now denied.
    4. How are citation labels produced and audited?

    Strong evidence: claim-span entailment/support labels, citation precision and recall, immutable versions, current render authorization, and no hidden metadata in denial.

    Branch E: Candidate relies on full reindex for deletion¶

    1. Can 600 million chunks be rebuilt inside 60 seconds?
    2. What about answer caches, replicas, reranker caches, and replay records?
    3. How is a missed source event found?
    4. What proves a tenant's deletion met its contract?

    Strong evidence: immediate policy/tombstone denial, versioned cache dependencies, asynchronous physical purge, anti-entropy, synthetic canaries, and an erasure evidence ledger.

    Branch F: Multi-region active-active¶

    1. EU is unavailable. May its query context go to the US?
    2. Two regions see different group epochs. Which can answer?
    3. How is tenant routing changed without split-brain ingestion?
    4. Which metadata is safe in a global control plane?

    Strong evidence: residency-pinned data plane, fail-closed restricted failover, single regional ingestion ownership or fenced leases, minimal global metadata, and audited routing epochs.

    Branch G: Candidate proposes dual index migration¶

    1. Which immutable snapshot seeds the new generation?
    2. How are concurrent updates replayed and gaps detected?
    3. Can a tenant query generations with different permission semantics?
    4. What remains current during rollback, and when may old data be deleted?

    Strong evidence: snapshot watermark plus CDC, idempotent version application, parity and isolation gates, tenant-atomic routing, old-generation catch-up, and rollback expiry.

    Branch H: Operationally strong candidate¶

    1. Which alarm pages Security rather than Search?
    2. How do metrics preserve tenant isolation and bounded cardinality?
    3. What is the first containment for one suspected cross-tenant citation?
    4. How do you distinguish a source outage from an embedding outage?

    Strong evidence: global kill/tenant quarantine, audit preservation, stage-specific SLIs and versioned traces, synthetic canaries, incident roles, and conservative re-enable criteria.

    Reference architecture for calibration¶

    This is one valid shape, not a required component list.

                             GLOBAL CONTROL (metadata only)
       tenant -> region | generation manifest | policy schema versions
                              | signed routing epoch
              +---------------+----------------+
              v                                v
     +---------------- EU DATA PLANE ----------------+
     |                                               |
user |  authn -> entitlement/policy -> query broker  |
---->|                   |              |            |
     |                   |scope         +--> lexical |
     |                   |              +--> vector  |
     |                   |                   |        |
     |                   +-----------> rank fusion    |
     |                                      |         |
     |        current policy recheck <--- hydrate     |
     |                                      |         |
     |    authorized rerank -> context/model           |
     |          citation map <--- model output         |
     |                |                     |         |
<----| answer/no-answer + final citation authorization|
     |                                               |
     | source -> connector -> version log -> builders |
     |                  |          |          |       |
     |                  +----> deny/tombstone  indexes|
     +-----------------------------------------------+

No content/context arrow crosses the regional box.

    Suggested records:

    TenantRoute(tenant_id, residency_policy, data_region, routing_epoch)
SourceVersion(tenant_id, source_id, version, digest, uri, observed_at, source_commit_at)
PolicyObject(tenant_id, object_id, policy_version, allow_refs, deny_refs, inheritance, region)
Chunk(chunk_id, source_id, source_version, offsets, digest, parser_version)
IndexManifest(tenant_id, generation, lexical_version, embedding_version, state, watermark)
DeletionIntent(tenant_id, object_id, source_commit_at, seq, state, purge_deadline)
Run(run_id, tenant_id, principal_ref, policy_epoch, region, query_digest, versions, status)
Retrieval(run_id, stage, candidate_ids, ranks, scores, authorization_decision_refs)
Answer(run_id, disposition, claim_spans, citation_ids, model_version, prompt_version)

    Suggested question API:

    POST /v1/tenants/{tenant}/questions
Authorization: Bearer <principal credential>
Idempotency-Key: <request retry identity>

{"question":"...","conversation_ref":"...","max_wait_ms":8000}

200 {"disposition":"ANSWERED|QUALIFIED|NO_ANSWER","answer":"...",
     "citations":[{"claim_ids":["c1"],"source_id":"...","source_version":"...",
                   "start":120,"end":244,"digest":"..."}],
     "evidence_as_of":"...","run_id":"..."}

    The server derives tenant authorization from authenticated routing, not the path alone. A retry may replay the stored result only after current authorization of every cited version; otherwise it recomputes or returns no answer.

    Use 400 for invalid bounded input, 401/403 for authentication/tenant access without revealing resource existence, 409 POLICY_CHANGED before response headers when a stream or replay cannot safely start under the required epoch, 429 with a retry hint for admission control, and 503 when current authorization or the compliant region is unavailable. NO_ANSWER is a successful product result, not a transport error. Once a stream has started, its HTTP status cannot change to 409: emit a defined terminal policy-change event and stop further output. Already delivered text cannot be retracted, so the streaming contract must specify the authorization freshness bound and recheck/cancellation behavior.

    One valid materialization lifecycle is:

    DISCOVERED -> BUILDING -> ACTIVE -> SUPERSEDED -> PURGE_PENDING -> PURGED
                    |          |             |
                    +----------+----deny-----+

An ACL revoke can make any non-purged state non-retrievable immediately.
An older object sequence cannot move PURGE_PENDING or PURGED back to ACTIVE.

    Capacity anchor¶

    Accept alternatives with explicit assumptions.

    chunks                         = 50M * 12 = 600M
raw float16 vectors            = 600M * 1,024 * 2 B = 1.2288 TB
assumed ANN graph + IDs        = 600M * 576 B = 345.6 GB
assumed lexical/metadata       = 600M * 1.5 KB = 900 GB
one generation                = 2.4744 TB decimal
RF=2 with 30% of capacity free = 2.4744 * 2 / 0.70 = 7.07 TB
two generations during change = 14.14 TB before snapshots/build scratch

average changes/s              = 5M / 86,400 = 57.9
20x burst                      = 1,157 changes/s
if 12 chunks and 2 indexes     = 27,768 chunk-index mutations/s at burst
six-hour outage backlog        = 57.9 * 21,600 = 1.25M source changes average

peak vector candidates         = 6,000 * 120 = 720K/s
peak lexical candidates        = 6,000 * 120 = 720K/s
reranker at 40/query           = 240K pairs/s
8 chunks * 600 tokens + 1,200 other = 6,000 input tokens/query
peak model input               = 36M tokens/s before cache or admission

    Numbers show why quotas, batching, tiering, and explicit model capacity are required. They are not benchmark claims.

    Sensitivity: if richer parsing doubles the average to 24 chunks/document, index storage, backfill work, and mutation fan-out roughly double; dual-generation allocation rises from about 14.14 TB to 28.28 TB under the same assumptions. A 30% uplift on demand (*1.3) leaves only 0.3/1.3 = 23.08% of provisioned capacity free; it does not meet a 30%-free gate. Reserve regional capacity before enabling the parser or use measured compression/tiering rather than consume incident headroom.

    Evaluation anchor¶

    Require source-span labels and authorized principal snapshots. A credible release suite includes answerable, unanswerable, contradictory, revoked, deleted, cross-tenant canary, exact identifier, multilingual, long-document, stale-source, and prompt-injected slices.

    Core metrics: recall@K, MRR or NDCG@K, authorization precision (must be 1.0 in tested cases), cross-tenant leakage count (must be zero), claim support precision/recall, citation precision/recall, supported-answer rate, no-answer precision/recall, freshness and revocation latency distributions, p95/p99 latency, tokens, and cost. Report slices and confidence intervals; aggregate gains cannot waive a safety regression.

    Evidence notes and calibration¶

    Capture exact candidate statements and whether independently offered or prompted. Apply the rubric, including mode normalization and caps.

    Topic Positive evidence Risk evidence
    Authority / invariants
    API / lifecycle
    Permission / isolation
    Retrieval / citations
    Capacity / latency
    Freshness / deletion
    Evaluation / attribution
    Threats / residency
    Operations
    Migration / leadership

    Strong L5: independently makes the bounded regional service safe, measurable, and implementable, including current authorization, evidence/no-answer, deletion, and incident behavior.

    Strong L6: additionally shapes policy contracts across teams, quantifies migration and operational risk, establishes decision rights, and makes generation and regional evolution reversible.

    Debrief script¶

    1. Cite one exact decision that protected users or evidence quality.
    2. Describe the highest-risk concrete failure sequence.
    3. State the normalized mode score and any cap.
    4. Name the artifact or experiment needed for the next level.

    Do not award points for "vector database," "zero trust," "hybrid," or "LLM guardrail" without a boundary and test. Do not penalize safe regional partitioning or no-answer as reduced availability. Do not reveal the reference design during the interview.

    A correct no-answer is a successful product outcome; a fail-closed 503 still consumes the availability error budget even when it is the safest decision. Evaluate the safety of that tradeoff without redefining availability. A 60-second propagation SLO is also not instantaneous revocation: state the allowed stale interval and fail-closed behavior when no sufficiently fresh authorization view can be established.
