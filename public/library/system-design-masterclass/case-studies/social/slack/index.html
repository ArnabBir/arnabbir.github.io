    Design Slack¶

    Interview roadmap¶

    Cover organizations, channels, threaded messages, real-time delivery, history, search indexing, and multi-device unread state. Exclude calls and app marketplace internals. Unlike consumer chat, workspace tenancy, retention, legal holds, enterprise authorization, and searchable history are central.

    Requirements, invariants, and capacity estimates¶

    Members send/edit/delete messages in public or private channels and DMs, receive real-time updates, page history, search permitted content, and track reads. Target 200 ms p99 sends, subsecond online delivery, and 99.99% history reads. Search may lag seconds. Tenant policy controls retention and exports.

    • A committed message has one channel-local sequence and stable ID.
    • Current membership and policy gate every history/search result.
    • Message versions are auditable according to tenant policy.
    • Event delivery is at least once; clients deduplicate by event ID.
    • Read cursors advance monotonically; unread counts are projections.
    • Tenant data, keys, quotas, and indexes remain isolated.
    • Delete/retention tombstones dominate stale index events.

    Assume 50 million daily users, 100 messages/user/day, or 5 billion messages/day: 5B / 86,400 = 58,000 writes/s average and roughly 290,000 writes/s at 5x peak. At 2 KB/message, canonical message metadata grows by 10 TB/day, or 3.65 PB/year before replication, revisions, attachments, and index expansion. With three zonal copies and a search projection budgeted at 1.5x canonical bytes, messages alone require about 16.4 PB/year; retention tiers and per-workspace deletion are therefore storage architecture requirements.

    Five million concurrent sockets dominate realtime capacity. If one gateway safely holds 40,000 connected devices, steady state needs 125 gateways; provisioning 40% reconnect and failure headroom raises the regional fleet target to about 175 gateway equivalents. At an illustrative average of two 700-byte delivered events/s per connected device, edge egress is 7 GB/s before TLS and WebSocket overhead. These estimates justify gateway-level multiplexing, bounded per-device queues, hierarchical topics for large channels, and cell-level capacity admission rather than per-user broker fanout. A 10x burst concentrated in one enterprise channel does not imply 10x canonical writes, but it can saturate fanout and must trigger channel-specific sampling of ephemeral events and resync behavior.

    Core requirements and deep dives¶

    Channel ordering and threads¶

    Each channel has one logical append leader assigning sequences. Database ranges or log partitions preserve order. Threads store parent_message_id but remain sequenced in the channel, enabling one event stream and consistent retention. Hot all-company channels can shard reactions and read traffic, but writes requiring strict channel order remain bounded by one sequencer; product limits or a relaxed broadcast mode handle the extreme.

    Real-time fanout¶

    The fanout service maps channels to gateway subscription shards. It sends one event per gateway, not per user, and the gateway multiplexes local sockets. Large channels use hierarchical topics. Slow devices have bounded buffers and receive a resync_required marker instead of consuming server memory indefinitely. Presence and typing are lossy ephemeral events with TTL and aggressive rate limits; messages are durable.

    Search and authorization¶

    Indexers consume versioned message events. Documents carry workspace, channel, message version, and tombstone state. Search first restricts tenant and candidate channels, then reauthorizes and hydrates current versions, because index membership can lag. Private-channel names must not leak through facets or timing. Tombstones use a priority lane, and periodic reconciliation compares index versions with canonical state.

    Unreads, edits, and retention¶

    Canonical read cursor is max(last_read_seq, incoming) after validating the caller, channel, current membership and that the requested sequence does not exceed the known channel head. Otherwise a forged future cursor could mark future messages read indefinitely. Unread count is estimated from sequence/counter projections and excludes inaccessible or deleted messages, so it can briefly disagree. Edits append revisions; tenant settings choose visible edit history. Retention jobs write tombstones before physical deletion, preserving legal-hold copies in a separately authorized vault. Delayed create/index events cannot outrank a higher deletion version.

    Partitioning and multi-region¶

    Place a workspace in a policy-selected home region. Partition message tables by workspace then channel hash and time range; this improves isolation and deletion. Very large tenants receive dedicated cells. Search indexes are tenant-routed, with large tenants isolated. Event gateway pools partition subscriptions by workspace/channel hash.

    Synchronously replicate channel writes across zones. Replicate encrypted logs to a disaster-recovery region subject to residency. Failover fences channel leaders by epoch. Global users connect to nearby gateways, which forward writes to the home region and consume replicated delivery streams. Read replicas may serve history only when authorization and retention versions are fresh enough.

    Consistency, idempotency, and failures¶

    Messages and membership changes are strongly ordered per channel shard; search, unreads, notifications, and realtime delivery are eventual. The send key maps to a request hash and committed ID. Outbox events and consumer uniqueness prevent dual-write loss and duplicate effects.

    Gateway failure reconnects clients and replays. Search outage does not affect messaging. Broker lag preserves history but delays live delivery and projections. A channel leader failure retries after epoch fencing. If authorization is unavailable, private reads fail closed; already open clients do not gain continued access merely because a socket subscription exists. Cell overload applies tenant quotas, sheds typing/presence/search first, and maintains message writes and history. Backups and logs support point-in-time restore; event replay rebuilds projections.

    Security, abuse, and privacy¶

    Enforce workspace SSO, MFA, SCIM deprovisioning, channel roles, app scopes, and policy versions centrally. Encrypt per tenant or cell, protect exports and legal holds with separate approvals, audit administrative reads, and redact message bodies from logs. Rate-limit spam, mentions, file uploads, search scraping, and bot tokens. Malware-scan files and rotate download URLs. Enterprise retention and deletion promises must explicitly describe backups, exports, and connected applications.

    Control plane, migrations, and enterprise recovery¶

    Separate the control plane that places workspaces, configures residency, rotates keys, and changes quotas from the data plane that sends and reads messages. A control-plane outage should freeze placement changes but leave existing cell routing functional through signed, cached maps. Routing records carry generations; gateways reject stale moves rather than write to both cells. Cell health, capacity, tenant size, and compliance constraints inform placement.

    Moving a workspace begins with a consistent source checkpoint. Copy immutable message ranges and snapshots, stream the outbox tail, build search in a shadow index, and validate message/revision counts plus ACL samples. Briefly fence source writes, apply the final tail, increment the routing generation, and retain the source read-only for rollback. Once the destination accepts writes, rollback must copy its acknowledged tail back, fence the destination and promote a complete source prefix; changing routing back to the old copy alone loses messages. External apps and exports use stable workspace IDs, so physical movement remains invisible. Large migrations are rate-limited to preserve customer traffic and have explicit abort criteria.

    A SCIM deprovision race illustrates authorization precedence. Once identity state marks a member disabled, new API calls and socket subscriptions fail regardless of stale channel membership. Gateways receive a high-priority session-revocation event and close existing sockets. Gate every new delivery under a current identity/policy epoch or a bounded freshness lease; a disconnected gateway stops delivery when that lease expires. Push alone does not prove a revocation deadline. Search and history still perform current authorization, so delayed gateway invalidation cannot leak new content. Legal-hold readers use a separate role and audited endpoint rather than weakening normal membership checks.

    Disaster recovery must be exercised at workspace granularity as well as cell granularity. Restoring a cell from backup establishes a log checkpoint, replays canonical mutations, rebuilds unreads and search, then compares sampled document hashes and membership versions before serving. Recovery objectives differ: message history and ACLs have strict durability, while presence, typing, cached unreads, and search ranking can be discarded or rebuilt. This classification controls replication cost and prevents teams from treating every projection as equally critical.

    Apps and bots introduce a second noisy-neighbor dimension. Events destined for integrations enter tenant-scoped queues after the message commit, and app acknowledgements never gate user-visible sends. Each installation has scopes, rate limits, retry budgets, and a revocation version. Duplicate webhook events carry stable IDs. If an app is slow, its queue is compacted or dead-lettered according to subscription semantics rather than retaining an unbounded copy of workspace history. Bot-authored mutations pass through the same channel sequencer and audit trail as human mutations.

    Operators expose per-installation lag and replay controls so workspace administrators can diagnose integrations without receiving access to another tenant's metadata.

    Observability and testing¶

    Measure send/commit latency, channel sequence gaps, outbox age, realtime event age, connected sockets, resync frequency, slow-consumer drops, history p99, search indexing/deletion lag, unauthorized candidate filtering, unread drift, tenant throttling, and hot channels. Attribute cost and SLOs by cell and tenant without exposing content.

    Test idempotent send/edit/delete races, membership boundary sequences, monotonic reads, index reorder, retention versus legal hold, gateway reconnect storms, and large-channel skew. Chaos-test cell dependencies and region failover. Authorization uses generated tenant/channel matrices and continuous synthetic former-member checks. Search upgrades use dual indexes, shadow queries, relevance comparison, and reversible alias switches.

    Tradeoffs and final architecture¶

    Per-workspace databases maximize isolation but waste resources for small tenants; multi-tenant cells with dedicated escape hatches balance cost. Direct per-user realtime fanout explodes work; gateway-level topic fanout exploits multiplexing. Serving search results directly from the index is fast but unsafe; reauthorization and canonical hydration cost latency but preserve policy. Channel-local ordering is useful, while workspace-global order is needless.

    Questions, levels, and five-minute summary¶

    1. How do large channels avoid per-user realtime fanout?
    2. How does search avoid leaking former private-channel membership?
    3. What happens to edit history under retention and legal hold?
    4. How are a noisy tenant and a hot channel isolated?
    5. How does regional failover fence old channel leaders?

    L5 candidates should define ordered channel storage, realtime reconnect, search projection, idempotency, tenant-aware partitioning, and failure fallbacks. L6 candidates should drive residency and legal-hold semantics, cell isolation, overload tiers, leader fencing, search migrations, authorization-version propagation, and enterprise operability.

    In five minutes: route each workspace to a regional cell and each channel to an ordered append shard. Commit message, idempotency key, and outbox together. Fan committed events hierarchically to subscribed gateways; reconnecting clients replay from cursors. Store history canonically, index versioned projections asynchronously, and reauthorize every history/search result against current membership. Track read cursors canonically while treating unread counts as projections. Shard by tenant, channel, and time; isolate huge tenants and degrade presence, typing, search, and notifications before messaging. Use tombstone versions for deletion and retention, fenced leaders for failover, and explicit enterprise controls for residency, audit, and legal hold.
