    Design a Fitness Tracking Platform¶

    Interview Roadmap¶

    Clarify which activities and devices are supported, whether recording must work without a network, how accurate distance must be, who can see routes, and whether live location is in scope. State the local-durability and activity-lifecycle invariants before choosing databases. Start with one phone recording an activity into a durable local journal and synchronizing it to a canonical backend. Then add GPS quality processing, resumable uploads, wearable handoff, social feeds, live sharing, regional scale, privacy controls, and operational recovery.

    A useful 45-minute interview allocation is five minutes for scope and invariants, five for estimates and APIs, ten for the device and upload protocol, ten for backend ingestion and GPS processing, five for feeds and live sharing, and ten for partitioning, reliability, privacy, and tradeoffs. The most important distinction is that recording and uploading are separate: losing connectivity must not lose the workout.

    Product Framing¶

    The product records runs, rides, walks, hikes, and indoor workouts from phones or wearables. It computes elapsed and moving time, distance, pace, elevation, heart-rate summaries, route maps, and personal records. A user may keep an activity private, share it with followers or friends, or publish it. Friends can view a feed and react. Optionally, a user can share a short-lived live location with explicitly selected viewers.

    The source evidence is the ordered sensor record captured on the device. The backend owns the canonical synchronized activity and immutable raw sample objects; summaries, route polylines, achievements, search indexes, and feeds are rebuildable projections. The system does not provide medical diagnosis, emergency dispatch, turn-by-turn navigation, or competition-grade timing. Payment, ad ranking, and detailed recommendation design are out of scope.

    Requirements¶

    Functional Requirements¶

    • Start, pause, resume, finish, discard, and recover an activity on a phone or wearable.
    • Continue recording for hours while offline or while the app is backgrounded.
    • Capture ordered GPS, altitude, heart rate, cadence, power, and device events when available.
    • Synchronize large activities with resumable, duplicate-safe uploads.
    • Compute distance, pace, moving time, splits, elevation, route, and quality indicators.
    • List a user's history and show a self feed with read-your-writes behavior.
    • Show authorized friend activities and support idempotent reactions and comments.
    • Apply activity visibility, blocks, route redaction, deletion, export, and retention policy.
    • Optionally share low-latency live progress with a small authorized audience.

    Non-Functional Requirements¶

    • A locally acknowledged sample survives process death and ordinary device restart.
    • Activity recording works without network access for at least 24 hours.
    • Backend activity reads target 99.99% availability and p99 under 300 ms.
    • Activity completion becomes visible to the owner within two seconds after finalization; friend-feed propagation targets under 30 seconds at p99.
    • Upload can resume after arbitrary interruption without resending the entire activity.
    • Battery, storage, and mobile data use are bounded and visible to the user.
    • Location data is encrypted, access-controlled, auditable, and minimized.

    Correctness Invariants¶

    1. Each activity has one stable client-generated activity_id; retries and device restarts never create another logical activity.
    2. Within a recording source, journal sequence numbers are unique and strictly increasing. An acknowledged journal record is never silently omitted from recovery.
    3. Local recording state follows legal transitions. FINISHED and DISCARDED are terminal for recording; no new sensor sample may be created after FINISHED, although bytes durably recorded by another source before finish may arrive later. Server lifecycle is separate, and DELETED is terminal there.
    4. A chunk identity binds activity, source, sequence range, encoding version, byte length, and checksum. The same identity cannot later represent different bytes.
    5. Finalization succeeds only against a complete upload manifest, or explicitly records known gaps. Summary generation references the exact manifest revision it consumed.
    6. Retries may redeliver commands and chunks, but stable keys create one canonical effect.
    7. Raw sensor evidence is immutable after acceptance. Corrections produce a new processing version, not an in-place rewrite of evidence.
    8. Visibility reduction, blocking, and deletion dominate stale feed, cache, notification, and live-session state.
    9. Derived distance never claims greater precision than input quality supports; rejected points remain explainable through quality metadata.
    10. Live sharing expires without a heartbeat and never broadens the audience beyond the current activity policy and explicit share grant.

    Capacity Estimates¶

    Assume 100 million registered users and 20 million daily active users. If 10% record one activity per day, the system receives 2 million activities/day, about 23 finalizations/s average and perhaps 20x peak, or 460/s. Activity metadata writes are not the dominant load; sensor bytes and social reads are.

    For a 60-minute outdoor workout sampled every second, there are 3,600 GPS samples. At 40 bytes/sample after delta encoding and compression, GPS consumes about 144 KB. Heart rate and motion metadata can add 100 KB, while indexes, manifests, and summaries add roughly 20 KB. Budget 300 KB/activity average:

    2,000,000 activities/day * 300 KB = 600 GB/day raw and derived ingress
600 GB/day * 365 days = 219 TB/year before replication
3 copies of this raw-and-derived budget = 657 TB/year
Additional indexes, versions and reserve may bring provisioned storage near 1 PB/year

    Sampling every five seconds lowers bytes but can destroy corner and interval fidelity. Instead, use adaptive sampling and compact delta encoding. A heavy five-hour ride at one-second sampling may be several MB, so protocols must not assume one request per activity.

    Assume each daily active user opens the feed eight times and reads two pages. That is 320 million page reads/day, about 3,700/s average and 40,000/s peak. Two million activities/day averaging 60 minutes imply 2,000,000 * 60 / 1,440 ~= 83,000 concurrent recordings on an average day. Applying a 3x time-of-day peak gives about 250,000 concurrent recordings. If 1% enable live sharing, 2,500 sessions at one update every five seconds produce 500 updates/s; ten viewers per session creates 5,000 deliveries/s. Regional pub/sub can handle that baseline, but celebrity fanout must still be capped because live sharing is designed for trusted small groups.

    At 256 KB chunks, a normal activity is roughly one or two chunks depending on sensor payload and sealing policy; the 300 KB estimate includes derived data that is not necessarily uploaded. A long activity may require tens of chunks. Upload bandwidth at a 20x activity peak is modest on average, but reconnect after a widespread mobile outage can release hours of backlog. Capacity planning therefore includes queued bytes and concurrent resumptions, not only normal QPS.

    Activity, accepted chunk metadata, immutable raw chunk objects, manifests, visibility, and relationship state are canonical. ActivitySummary, route segments, leaderboards, achievements, notification records, and feed items are projections. Raw samples need not be expanded into one database row each; compressed immutable chunk objects are cheaper, while sparse metadata and time-range indexes support processing and diagnosis.

    Partition keys and uniqueness constraints should be visible in the schema:

    CREATE TABLE activity_chunk (
  activity_id UUID NOT NULL,
  source_id UUID NOT NULL,
  first_seq BIGINT NOT NULL,
  last_seq BIGINT NOT NULL,
  object_key TEXT NOT NULL,
  byte_length INTEGER NOT NULL,
  sha256 CHAR(64) NOT NULL,
  encoding VARCHAR(32) NOT NULL,
  sample_count INTEGER NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (activity_id, source_id, first_seq),
  UNIQUE (activity_id, source_id, last_seq, sha256),
  CHECK (first_seq <= last_seq)
);

    The database transaction records object metadata only after object storage confirms durable bytes. Alternatively, upload to a temporary object key, verify it, transactionally install the chunk row, then promote or retain the content-addressed object. Orphan temporary objects are removed after their durable staging lease expires and a conditional deletion state fences descriptor publication. Age alone is insufficient if a delayed completion can still install the object.

    The mobile data plane records locally without waiting for the backend. The backend control path creates activities and upload sessions, while bulk chunk bytes go directly or through an upload edge to object storage. A canonical transaction finalizes the manifest, moves activity state, and writes an outbox event. At-least-once consumers derive summaries and social projections by activity and processing version.

    Requirement Authoritative flow Durable boundary Result
    Offline recording sensors -> local writer -> encrypted journal forced journal frame recoverable local activity
    Resumable upload sealed chunks -> coordinator -> object store and chunk metadata checksum-bound object plus canonical descriptor missing ranges can be queried and retried
    Finish and summarize /finalize -> manifest validation -> canonical DB/outbox -> processor manifest, server state, and outbox transaction versioned summary and route
    Feed visibility finalized event -> hybrid fanout -> authorization-first hydration canonical activity and current policy stale projections cannot grant access
    Live sharing coarse device updates -> authorized regional relay expiring grant, not each delivery bounded ephemeral location stream

    Offline-First Device Architecture¶

    The recorder owns the activity state machine and one append-only journal per active activity. Sensor adapters normalize platform callbacks but do not decide durability. A single writer assigns sequence numbers, validates the current state, frames records with length and CRC, encrypts them, appends them, and periodically forces data to stable storage. UI counters are projections from acknowledged records and may be recomputed.

    flowchart TD
    GPS[GPS adapter] --> Queue[Bounded sensor queue]
    HR[Heart-rate adapter] --> Queue
    Motion[Motion adapter] --> Queue
    Controls[Pause and finish commands] --> Writer[Single journal writer]
    Queue --> Writer
    Writer --> WAL[(Encrypted append journal)]
    WAL --> Index[(Local activity index)]
    WAL --> View[Live metrics projector]
    WAL --> Chunker[Immutable chunk builder]
    Chunker --> Outbox[(Local upload outbox)]
    Outbox --> Sync[Background sync worker]
    Sync --> Cloud[Backend upload API]

    The queue is bounded. Under overload, control events and coarse GPS fixes are retained before redundant high-frequency motion samples. The recorder emits an explicit SAMPLE_GAP record with sensor and interval rather than hiding loss. Platform background execution limits require foreground-service or workout-session APIs where available, wake locks used sparingly, and a user-visible warning when the operating system revokes recording capability.

    Phones and wearables can both record. Give each source its own sequence space and clock model; do not interleave samples by arrival time. On merge, estimate monotonic-time offset using synchronization exchanges and wall-clock anchors, then choose source precedence per sensor. If a watch transfers data after the phone is FINISHED, accept only samples the watch durably recorded before the finish cutoff, append a new server manifest revision, and reprocess rather than mutating accepted chunks. This extends synchronized evidence; it does not reopen local recording.

    Durable Local Journal and Crash Recovery¶

    Each journal frame contains (format_version, activity_id, source_id, seq, event_type, monotonic_time, wall_time, payload_length, payload, crc). Files use an authenticated encryption key held by the device keystore. A segment header records nonce material and previous-segment hash. Segment rotation bounds recovery scanning and lets completed prefixes become immutable upload chunks.

    Acknowledgement semantics must be honest. For a user command such as pause or finish, return success only after the record and required metadata are committed to SQLite WAL or forced to the journal file. Sensor callbacks may be batched for battery efficiency; document that the last small batch, for example up to five seconds, can be lost on sudden power failure. Process death after an OS callback but before local acknowledgement is not preventable.

    SQLite WAL alone is not a power-loss durability setting: SQLite's synchronous pragma states that WAL with NORMAL can lose a committed transaction after power/system failure, while FULL synchronizes after each commit. Use WAL plus synchronous=FULL and verified storage behavior for acknowledged control records; apply the documented lossy batch policy only to unacknowledged samples (accessed 2026-09-08).

    On startup, recovery performs these steps:

    1. Read the local activity index and locate every activity not terminally discarded or fully synchronized.
    2. Scan from the last verified segment boundary, checking frame length, sequence, authentication tag, and CRC.
    3. Truncate only an incomplete or corrupt trailing frame. Quarantine mid-file corruption and preserve later bytes for support tooling.
    4. Replay state transitions and metrics from the journal; never trust an independently updated UI-state row over the log.
    5. Resume an activity only if the platform still has sensor ownership and product policy allows it; otherwise mark INTERRUPTED and ask the user to finish or continue.
    6. Rebuild the local upload outbox from sealed segments and query server upload status before sending.

    Compaction is copy-on-write. After server finalization and a verified retention delay, create a compact summary record, atomically swap the local index to it, then delete old segments. A crash before the swap leaves old data authoritative; a crash after the swap leaves orphan files that a sweeper can safely remove. Disk pressure first drops regenerable map tiles and feed caches, then pauses new recordings with a clear error; it never silently deletes an active journal.

    Local recording, local sync progress, and server lifecycle are related but not identical. FINISHED is terminal for local sample creation; a separate sync status can remain PENDING, UPLOADING, or SYNCED. The server can be UPLOADING or PROCESSING while the device is locally FINISHED, and the API returns canonical server state plus upload completeness. Durable finish seals the ending timestamp and source high-watermarks. Evidence recorded by another source before that cutoff but transferred later requires a new manifest revision; post-cutoff samples are rejected.

    Pause is a durable control event, not absence of samples. Elapsed time spans start to finish minus policy-defined exclusions; moving time derives from quality-filtered motion and can differ from user pauses. Auto-pause creates separate inferred events so a later algorithm version can recompute moving time without rewriting user commands.

    Discard writes a local tombstone before deleting bytes and, if any server object exists, synchronizes a deletion command. Delete after publication immediately hides the activity in canonical policy, writes a tombstone to the outbox, evicts feeds and caches asynchronously, revokes live grants, and schedules physical erasure according to backup and legal-retention policy.

    Deep Dive 1: GPS Distance and Route Quality¶

    For consecutive valid points (lat1, lon1) and (lat2, lon2) in radians, a Haversine baseline estimates great-circle distance:

    dlat = lat2 - lat1
dlon = lon2 - lon1
a = sin(dlat/2)^2 + cos(lat1) * cos(lat2) * sin(dlon/2)^2
a = clamp(a, 0, 1)  # protect floating-point roundoff
c = 2 * atan2(sqrt(a), sqrt(1-a))
distance = R * c, where R ~= 6,371,000 meters

    Summing every raw pair overcounts distance when a stationary GPS fix jitters. The processor first validates time order and coordinate bounds, then associates each fix with horizontal accuracy, provider, speed, altitude quality, and motion evidence. It applies a quality pipeline rather than one magic smoothing constant.

    flowchart LR
    Raw[Immutable fixes] --> Validate[Bounds and time validation]
    Validate --> Accuracy[Accuracy and stale-fix gate]
    Accuracy --> Speed[Acceleration and impossible-speed gate]
    Speed --> Drift[Stationary drift and outlier filter]
    Drift --> Match{Map match useful?}
    Match -->|yes| Roads[Candidate roads and path scoring]
    Match -->|no| Free[Free-form route]
    Roads --> Distance[Distance and moving-time model]
    Free --> Distance
    Distance --> Simplify[Multi-resolution simplification]
    Simplify --> Publish[Versioned summary and route]

    Accuracy and Drift¶

    A reported 50-meter-accuracy point should not move a runner 35 meters merely because it arrived next. Reject fixes outside configured accuracy bounds or down-weight them in a Kalman-like state estimator. During likely stationary periods, require displacement to exceed a noise envelope derived from both fixes before adding distance. Keep raw points and rejection reasons so processing can be audited and improved.

    Tunnels, urban canyons, tree cover, and battery-saving modes create long gaps or biased fixes. Do not draw a confident straight line across every gap. Mark uncertain segments, cap interpolation by duration and plausible route, and expose a quality score. Barometric altitude, when calibrated, is usually better for elevation change than noisy GPS altitude. Elevation gain needs smoothing and minimum ascent thresholds; it is not the sum of every positive centimeter.

    Impossible Speed and Clock Errors¶

    Compute implied speed from distance divided by monotonic-time delta. Reject zero or negative deltas and quarantine clock jumps. Thresholds vary by sport: a 20 m/s point may be impossible for a run but ordinary for cycling downhill. Acceleration, neighboring points, motion sensors, and road constraints distinguish a single teleport from a real fast segment. Never use client wall time alone because users, networks, and daylight-saving changes can move it.

    Map Matching¶

    Map matching is optional and versioned. A hidden Markov model can score road or trail candidates by perpendicular distance, heading, road class, and path plausibility between fixes. It improves sparse road cycling tracks but can damage trail runs, open-water activities, or newly built roads. Apply it only when sport, map coverage, and confidence justify it. Retain both measured and matched geometry and show users when a route was corrected.

    Route Simplification¶

    Serving thousands of raw points to every feed card wastes bandwidth and leaks precision. Douglas-Peucker or a streaming equivalent creates multiple route levels with explicit error tolerances, for example 5 meters for activity detail and 50 meters for feed thumbnails. Preserve segment boundaries, redaction boundaries, pauses, and critical turns. Simplification runs after privacy clipping so it cannot recreate a removed endpoint by connecting across it.

    Distance algorithm upgrades write processing_version=N+1, compare old and new results in shadow, and atomically publish a complete new summary and route generation. Personal records and achievements reference the version used and are reconciled if a material correction changes eligibility.

    Deep Dive 2: Idempotent Resumable Upload¶

    The client seals journal segments into deterministic chunks. A chunk should contain one source and contiguous sequence range, use a pinned encoding version, and have a SHA-256 checksum over exact transmitted bytes. The client persists the chunk descriptor and checksum before enqueueing upload. It never regenerates different bytes under the same descriptor.

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
    participant D as Device sync engine
    participant U as Upload coordinator
    participant O as Object storage
    participant C as Canonical DB
    participant P as Processing pipeline
    D->>U: create session(activity, manifest revision, key)
    U->>C: upsert session and return accepted ranges
    C-->>D: session, accepted chunks, expiry
    loop only missing chunks
        D->>U: PUT chunk(range, length, checksum)
        U->>O: write temporary or content-addressed object
        O-->>U: durable object checksum
        U->>C: install descriptor if absent
        U-->>D: created or already present
    end
    D->>U: finalize(manifest, root checksum, high-watermarks)
    U->>C: verify coverage and atomically finalize plus outbox
    C-->>D: canonical activity revision
    C->>P: at-least-once ActivityFinalized

    The manifest lists all sources, each expected sequence high-watermark, ordered chunk descriptors, declared gaps, and a root checksum over canonical descriptor serialization. Finalization validates that accepted chunks exactly cover each required range without overlap, that checksums match, and that no unexpected source appears. Missing ranges return a machine-readable response; they do not produce a silently shorter workout.

    The sample SQL constraints alone do not reject overlapping ranges such as [1,100] and [50,150]. Serialize descriptor insertion and finalization under an activity/source lock, reject overlap with installed descriptors, and freeze the manifest revision while checking coverage and publishing it. Chunk uploads cannot race a completed coverage check to mutate the manifest being finalized.

    Session expiry does not delete installed content-addressed chunks immediately. A new session for the same activity and manifest revision discovers them through canonical chunk metadata. Concurrent retries race on the primary key; the winner installs the checksum, and identical losers receive Already Present. Different bytes for the same range produce a hard conflict requiring a new manifest revision or client repair.

    The critical crash windows are explicit:

    • Object write succeeds but metadata insert fails: the object is an orphan and later retried or swept.
    • Metadata commits but acknowledgement is lost: the retry finds the same checksum and succeeds without another effect.
    • Finalization commits but response is lost: the idempotency key returns the prior activity revision.
    • Processing publishes twice: projection keys include (activity_id, processing_version) and publication uses compare-and-set.
    • The device deletes local bytes too early: forbidden until it observes finalized manifest revision and persists that acknowledgement locally.

    Direct-to-object-storage presigned URLs reduce API bandwidth but complicate checksum enforcement and completion authorization. The coordinator can issue a URL bound to object key, maximum length, content hash, activity, and short expiry; the client must call complete, and the server independently verifies object metadata before installing the descriptor. For small normal activities, proxy upload may be simpler and safer.

    Deep Dive 3: Backend Storage and Processing Pipeline¶

    The metadata service performs strongly consistent per-activity transitions. Chunk bytes live in object storage, not a relational database. A transactional outbox emits ActivityFinalized, ActivityVisibilityChanged, and ActivityDeleted. Stream consumers are at least once and use source version checks.

    Processing stages decode and validate chunks, normalize source clocks, select sensor sources, run GPS quality logic, calculate summaries and splits, generate route resolutions, evaluate achievements, and publish a generation. Expensive map matching uses a separate bounded pool so its backlog cannot delay basic summaries. An activity can become READY with baseline geometry and receive an enhanced route later if the product allows; the API labels versions so readers never combine incompatible pieces.

    Pipeline records include input manifest revision, algorithm version, map-data version, stage status, output checksums, and error category. Deterministic stages can replay from raw chunks. Poisoned or unsupported chunks quarantine the activity as PARTIAL without blocking a stream partition. Operator repair selects a new processing version; it never edits output rows blindly.

    Use lifecycle-aware storage tiers. Recent metadata and summaries remain in a low-latency database. Raw chunks begin in standard object storage, transition to colder classes after the reprocessing window, and are physically deleted according to policy. Route thumbnails may be CDN-cached only under a signed authorization decision and short TTL; public activities are not assumed permanently public.

    Deep Dive 4: Self Feed and Friend Feed¶

    The self feed is an owner-indexed activity query ordered by (completed_at, activity_id). It reads canonical metadata or an owner projection with a fallback, so a newly finalized activity is visible immediately. This is cheaper and more correct than routing the owner's own activity through social fanout.

    For friends, use a hybrid feed:

    • Ordinary accounts fan out activity IDs on write into follower inboxes.
    • High-fanout accounts remain in an author outbox and are merged on read.
    • The feed row stores only a reference, author, rank key, and source version, not an authorization grant.
    • Hydration fetches current activity summary and rechecks visibility, friendship/follow state, blocks, and deletion.
    • Ranking may mix recency and affinity, but the cursor pins a query generation or stable rank boundary.

    Fanout events are duplicate-safe by (viewer_id, activity_id, activity_version). Visibility reduction emits high-priority invalidation, but correctness does not depend on deletion reaching every inbox before a read. Hydration filters stale rows. To avoid empty pages after filtering, fetch bounded extra candidates and continue until the page fills or scan budget is exhausted.

    Reactions are canonical idempotent upserts keyed by (activity_id, user_id). Aggregate counts are eventual and can be sharded for hot activities. A user sees their own reaction from canonical state. Notifications are retryable delivery based on durable reaction events; delayed notifications recheck that the activity remains visible and the reaction still exists.

    Optional Live Sharing¶

    Live sharing is a separate ephemeral path, not a reason to weaken durable recording. The owner creates a short-lived grant for named users, a trusted group, or an unguessable link according to product policy. The device sends coarser updates, for example every five seconds, containing activity ID, monotonic sequence, location, elapsed time, and optional heart rate. Every update is also recorded locally if it belongs in the final activity.

    Regional gateways authorize the grant at connection time and subscribe to an activity channel. They keep only the latest update plus a bounded replay window. Viewers tolerate gaps and discard lower sequences. No database write is required for every viewer delivery. The durable final route still arrives through normal upload and may differ after quality processing.

    sequenceDiagram

    Live route redaction can delay location by a configurable interval and withhold points inside privacy zones. Revocation increments a grant epoch distributed on a priority channel; gateways reject older tokens and close sockets. Every grant has a hard expiry, viewer cap, rate cap, and owner-visible audience. Under overload, reduce update frequency and history first; never bypass authorization or affect the durable recorder.

    Partitioning and Scaling¶

    Partition canonical activity metadata by owner_id so user history, privacy defaults, and ordinary writes are colocated; retain a globally unique activity ID lookup or encode shard routing in the ID. Chunk metadata can partition by activity_id, while object keys distribute by a hash prefix to avoid storage-listing hotspots. Processing streams partition by activity ID so one activity's manifest and policy events remain ordered.

    Owner partitioning creates skew for prolific or imported accounts but normal activities are small metadata records. Very large imports and bulk reprocessing use separate quotas and worker pools. Feed inboxes partition by viewer ID, author outboxes by author ID, and reactions by activity with hash-suffixed counter shards. Live relays partition by (region, activity_id) and enforce audience caps.

    Hot keys arise from celebrity posts, challenge leaderboards, and mass reconnect. Celebrity activities use read caches for public summaries, pull-on-read feed candidates, sharded reaction aggregation, and notification budgets. Leaderboards consume versioned summaries into a separate ranking system and are not transactionally updated with activity completion.

    Reshard canonical owners with an ownership epoch: snapshot owner data at sequence S, copy it, replay later changes, fence the old shard, and switch routing. Upload sessions include the current home shard but can be redirected without changing object identity. Processing and feed events carry ownership and source versions so stale workers cannot publish over newer state.

    Consistency and Idempotency¶

    Activity lifecycle, manifest installation/finalization, visibility, deletion, idempotency records, and live-grant state require strong consistency per activity or owner. Raw objects are immutable. Summaries, achievements, feed membership, reaction counts, search, and notifications are eventual projections. The owner gets read-your-writes through canonical metadata and a direct self-feed path.

    Use optimistic revisions for user edits. PATCH visibility with If-Match either installs the next version and outbox event or returns current state. A deletion tombstone has a version and retention greater than cache, event replay, backup-restore, and feed rebuild windows. Consumers apply only events newer than their stored source version; deletion and block policy still receive explicit precedence.

    Network delivery is at least once; the system does not claim exactly-once execution or delivery. Duplicate-safe business effects come from activity IDs, request idempotency keys with body hashes, chunk range and checksum uniqueness, conditional processing-version publication, feed-row uniqueness, and reaction upserts. Deduplication state must live at least as long as clients can retry or old events can replay.

    Reliability and Failure Modes¶

    Device failure is the first reliability domain. Process death recovers from the local journal. Battery death can lose only the documented unforced tail. Sensor permission revocation appends an interruption when possible and warns the user. Low disk blocks recording before corruption. Keystore loss makes encrypted data unrecoverable by design; the UI should distinguish it from an empty activity.

    Backend failures degrade independently. Metadata outage leaves devices recording and retrying with jitter. Object-store outage pauses chunk completion without losing journals. Stream outage delays processing and feeds but not finalization if the outbox remains durable. Processor failure replays immutable inputs. Feed-store loss rebuilds from canonical activities and graph snapshots. Authorization-store uncertainty fails closed for private or friends-only data.

    Reconnect Storms¶

    After a carrier outage or backend recovery, millions of devices may retry together. Clients use exponential backoff with full jitter, persist next-attempt time, honor Retry-After, and query upload status before retransmitting. The edge issues per-user upload concurrency limits and admission tokens. Separate metadata control requests from bulk-byte capacity so clients can learn what is missing even when data upload is throttled.

    The server publishes load tiers. At moderate pressure, it reduces presigned URL issuance and lowers per-device parallelism. Next it delays map matching, achievements, feed fanout, and notifications while preserving chunk acceptance and manifest finalization. At severe pressure, it prioritizes expiring active journals and final chunks over historical imports. Recovery ramps token issuance gradually to avoid a second wave.

    Corruption and Reconciliation¶

    End-to-end checksums detect in-transit and at-rest corruption. Periodic jobs compare manifest descriptors with object existence, length, and checksums; missing objects move activities to repair state and request retained device data where possible. Projection reconciliation compares the canonical finalized manifest and processing version with summary, route, feed, and achievement generations. Backups restore tombstones and privacy versions before serving reconstructed projections.

    Multi-Region Strategy¶

    Assign each user a home region with synchronous multi-zone canonical writes. Record and upload traffic routes home, while object storage replicates according to durability and residency policy. Authorized summaries and feed projections replicate near readers. Cross-region follows are asynchronous workflows; feed freshness can lag without changing visibility correctness.

    On regional loss, promote a replica under a higher ownership epoch. If replication has nonzero RPO, only devices that still retain journals can repair missing uploads; a client that already deleted finalized bytes cannot be assumed to do so. Bind permission to discard local bytes to the promised backend durability tier, and recover the acknowledged metadata/object prefix before claiming no activity loss. Object inventory may help recover candidate chunks but cannot by itself establish which manifest or privacy state was committed. Do not allow simultaneous activity owners in two regions. Privacy tombstones, blocks, and live-grant revocations use a priority replicated deny channel; when current deny state cannot be established, non-public reads fail closed.

    Privacy, Authorization, and Abuse Prevention¶

    Location is sensitive even when an activity is public. Visibility values such as PRIVATE, FOLLOWERS, FRIENDS, and PUBLIC are insufficient alone; evaluate owner blocks, viewer blocks, age and regional policy, club membership, live grant, and field-level sharing. Feed rows are hints, not authority. A signed CDN URL is a bearer capability until expiry unless every edge hit also checks a current authorization epoch. For immediate revocation of future reads, use a policy-enforcing gateway with authoritative checks and defined handling of in-flight responses; otherwise declare a bound covering token/cache lifetime and deny-state freshness. Already downloaded routes cannot be recalled. Service-to-service access uses workload identity, least privilege, encryption, audited break-glass, and purpose-limited raw-location roles.

    Route Redaction¶

    Users can define privacy zones around home, work, schools, or arbitrary places. Store zones encrypted and restrict which services can access centers. At publication time, clip raw or processed route points inside zones and optionally trim a configurable distance from start and end. Add randomized boundary treatment carefully: deterministic per activity avoids a mosaic of repeated outputs revealing the exact center, while excessive noise makes routes misleading.

    Redaction creates a new derived route with a policy version. Summary distance can still use the full private route, but public maps and segment achievements use policy-defined geometry. Simplify only after clipping and split the polyline at removed intervals; otherwise a straight connector can reveal the hidden path. Bounding boxes, thumbnails, start labels, timestamps, photos, and live updates must use the same or stricter redaction. Cache keys include activity and privacy version.

    Visibility reduction first updates canonical authorization and a deny epoch, then invalidates routes, CDN objects, feeds, search, notifications, and live grants. Stale projections may exist physically but must not be readable. Data export includes raw evidence and processing versions for the owner. Deletion has an immediate logical phase and an asynchronous physical phase across objects, replicas, caches, derived data, and backups with auditable completion.

    Abuse controls cover fake GPS, leaderboard manipulation, scraping, stalking, reaction spam, malicious compressed chunks, and denial-of-wallet uploads. Bound chunk sizes, decompression ratio, sample counts, coordinate rates, activity duration, and manifest revisions. Validate parsers in isolated workers. Fraud signals can label or exclude achievements, but should not delete personal workout history without policy and appeal.

    Observability and Testing¶

    Measure device journal append latency, force frequency, queue drops, recovered tails, active-journal age, local disk pressure, upload bytes pending, session retries, checksum conflicts, missing ranges, finalization latency, object orphans, and reconnect admission. Backend service-level indicators include activity-read latency and availability, processing lag by stage, PARTIAL rate, feed fanout/hydration latency, authorization denials, stale-policy cache blocks, live update age, socket count, and revocation propagation.

    GPS quality metrics include points by accuracy bucket, rejected-point reasons, implied-speed outliers, stationary drift, raw versus processed distance delta, map-match confidence, route simplification error, and processing-version change distribution. Avoid logging coordinates, zone centers, health values, or share tokens. Trace with salted activity identifiers and manifest/processing versions under controlled retention.

    Test the device state machine with deterministic simulated sensor and storage failures. Property-test sequence monotonicity, legal lifecycle transitions, journal prefix recovery, CRC truncation, idempotent replay, manifest range coverage, and root-checksum stability. Kill the process before and after append, force, segment seal, upload, finalization, and local deletion. Test full disk, permission loss, clock jumps, reboot, old app versions, watch handoff, and corrupt encrypted frames.

    Build GPS corpora for stationary jitter, straight runs, switchbacks, tunnels, urban canyons, out-and-back routes, dateline crossing, poles, bad accuracy, impossible teleports, and sparse samples. Compare Haversine and processing outputs against surveyed or simulator ground truth with sport-specific tolerances. Shadow algorithm and map-data upgrades before publishing.

    Integration tests inject duplicate, reordered, missing, conflicting, and partially uploaded chunks. Chaos tests cover object storage, metadata database, stream, processing stage, feed store, graph service, authorization service, and regional failure. Load tests include one celebrity, many long activities, a 24-hour outage followed by reconnect, and slow live viewers. Privacy tests verify the declared revocation bound for stale feed, direct URL, CDN, export, and live access, including policy-channel isolation and already-issued capabilities.

    Important Tradeoffs¶

    Recording directly to network simplifies the client but loses workouts offline; a durable local journal wins despite recovery complexity. One mutable activity blob is easy to upload but makes interruption expensive and conflict-prone; immutable checksummed chunks plus a manifest make retries bounded. One-second GPS captures shape but costs battery and amplifies jitter; adaptive sensing preserves important movement while reducing idle samples.

    Summing Haversine distances is transparent but noisy. Filtering improves quality but introduces algorithm-dependent results; retain raw evidence and version outputs. Map matching can repair road tracks but invents paths in weak map coverage, so it must be confidence-gated and optional. Fanout-on-write gives fast friend feeds but creates celebrity amplification; hybrid push/pull isolates skew. Live sharing adds safety and social value but materially expands privacy and operational risk, so keep it optional, short-lived, audience-capped, and separate from durable recording.

    Strong global consistency would make mobile and cross-region operation brittle. Strong per-activity state plus immutable bytes and eventual projections matches the actual invariants. Keeping raw location forever maximizes future reprocessing but increases cost and privacy exposure; tiered, policy-bound retention with explicit quality-version limits is preferable.

    L5 and L6 Expectations¶

    An L5 answer should separate recording from upload, define a durable local journal and legal lifecycle, estimate sensor storage, use stable activity IDs and chunk checksums, explain resumable finalization, give a defensible GPS filtering pipeline, partition by owner/activity, make feeds eventual but authorization current, and cover retries, deletion, observability, and major failures.

    An L6 answer should negotiate device acknowledgement and battery-loss semantics, formalize multi-source clocks and manifest coverage, identify every crash window, design reconnect admission and overload priorities, version GPS and map outputs, quantify feed and live-sharing amplification, separate control and data planes, define regional ownership and fencing, make privacy denial dominate stale distribution, plan algorithm and schema migrations, and connect raw-location retention to cost, regulation, abuse, and organizational access boundaries.

    Five-Minute Summary¶

    A fitness tracker must preserve the workout before it can synchronize it. A single device writer appends sequenced control and sensor records to an encrypted durable journal; crash recovery validates the journal prefix, reconstructs lifecycle state, and resumes a local outbox. Sealed source-specific ranges become immutable checksummed chunks. The backend accepts retries idempotently, reports missing ranges, and finalizes only a complete versioned manifest, then emits an outbox event. Raw chunks remain immutable while versioned workers validate clocks and accuracy, reject drift and impossible speed, calculate a Haversine baseline, optionally map-match confident routes, simplify geometry, and atomically publish summaries.

    Store canonical activity, manifest, visibility, and deletion state strongly consistently per owner/activity. Keep raw bytes in object storage and rebuild summaries, achievements, routes, feeds, and notifications from versioned events. Give owners a direct history path and friends a hybrid push/pull feed whose hydration always reauthorizes. Optional live sharing uses expiring grants, coarse updates, bounded regional relays, and fast revocation without entering the durable recording path. Partition metadata by owner, processing by activity, and feeds by viewer; isolate celebrity and import skew. During outages, devices continue journaling. During reconnect storms, admit uploads with jitter and tokens while delaying map matching and social work. Clip privacy zones before simplifying routes, version every redaction, fail closed on uncertain authorization, and make deletion and blocks dominate every cache and projection.
