    Design Tinder¶

    Interview roadmap¶

    Scope profile creation, preference/discovery, swipe decisions, reciprocal matches, and post-match messaging handoff. Exclude media transformation details, subscription billing, and chat internals. The core challenge is producing safe, fresh candidates under geography and preference constraints while creating exactly one match from concurrent reciprocal likes.

    Requirements and invariants¶

    Users maintain profiles and discovery preferences, receive ranked candidate cards, swipe left/right, and match when likes are mutual. Discovery targets 300 ms p99 and must not repeatedly show decided profiles unless product policy resets them. Location is approximate and highly sensitive. Blocks, bans, age gates, and visibility controls must apply promptly.

    • A swipe ID has one durable outcome under retry.
    • At most one active match exists for an unordered user pair.
    • A user never receives themselves, blocked users, ineligible ages, or current prior decisions.
    • Match creation is atomic with reciprocal-like verification.
    • Candidate pools and rank scores are projections, not eligibility truth.
    • Precise location is never returned to another user.
    • Delete/ban state dominates delayed pool and notification events.

    Assume 50 million daily users, 100 swipes/day: 5 billion/day, 58,000/s average and 300,000/s peak. Ten discovery pages/day produce 500 million ranking requests/day. Profiles average 10 KB metadata plus media references; media uses object storage/CDN. Swipe history grows by terabytes/day and needs lifecycle tiers. Dense cities and popular profiles create hot geographic cells and impression skew.

    Deep dives¶

    Candidate retrieval and geography¶

    Map recent locations to hierarchical cells such as S2/geohash. Query the user's cell and expanding neighbors until enough candidates exist, then calculate exact distance server-side. Sparse areas expand radius gradually; dense cells sample and diversify rather than scanning all profiles. Index only discoverable, moderated profiles with coarse attributes. Before response, canonical policy checks age, preferences in both directions where required, blocks, prior decisions, visibility, freshness, and rate limits.

    Ranking, fairness, and pool exhaustion¶

    Rankers combine preference fit, activity, distance bands, quality, and exploration. Apply exposure caps so popular profiles and dense neighborhoods do not absorb all impressions. Candidate pools cache IDs briefly but not authorization. A cursor includes pool generation and boundary; clients deduplicate across regenerated pools. When exhausted, the product may widen constraints with consent, wait for new users, or clearly return no candidates, never silently violate hard filters.

    Reciprocal match transaction¶

    Decision writes use (actor,target) plus swipe idempotency. A right swipe creates an active like; left may suppress future discovery. Match creation is unique by unordered pair. Unmatch transitions the match state and revokes chat access; whether future rematching is allowed is explicit policy. Notifications consume outbox events idempotently and recheck active match so delayed pushes do not announce an already-unmatched pair.

    Privacy and safety¶

    Store exact coordinates encrypted with tightly scoped access; retain only as long as needed. Return distance bands and add jitter where appropriate. Do not expose candidate-list membership, read-like status, or precise activity. Blocks are directional records but eligibility checks both directions. Safety systems detect spam swiping, automated scraping, underage accounts, impersonation, harassment, and coordinated abuse. Reports preserve evidence under restricted access.

    Partitioning, consistency, and regions¶

    Partition profiles and actor-history projections by actor, candidate indexes by region/cell and attribute buckets, and canonical directed decisions plus matches by unordered pair key. Both reciprocal decisions must share the match transaction described below; independently actor-sharded authoritative likes would reintroduce the cross-shard race. Popular profile versions are read-cacheable and impressions use sharded counters. Split hot cells, merge cold cells, and bound candidate generation and online filtering.

    Profiles have a home region; location updates go to the nearest compliant region and update geo projections asynchronously. Pair operations route deterministically to one owning region, avoiding active-active match races. During owner-region failure, queue swipes or fail over only after fencing. Discovery can serve stale pools but must recheck current blocks/bans; if that check is unavailable, fail closed.

    Swipe and match transaction state is strongly consistent per pair/decision keys. Geo indexes, candidate pools, rank features, impressions, and notifications are eventual. Outbox records make match notifications recoverable. Versioned events and tombstones prevent deleted profiles from re-entering pools.

    Failures include stale location, ranker outage, pool generation lag, and notification duplication. Use location TTL, chronological/simple-score fallback, on-demand bounded retrieval, and idempotent notification keys. Queue age and per-cell work budgets drive admission control. Rebuild projections from profiles, preferences, decisions, and blocks without modifying canonical matches.

    Observability and testing¶

    Measure discovery stage p99, eligible yield, empty pools, cache generation age, geo-cell skew, repeat-card rate, swipe and match transaction latency, duplicate match attempts, notification delay, block/ban propagation, profile exposure distribution, report rate, and precise-location access audits. Monitor quality by geography and cohort without optimizing only matches.

    Property-test pair uniqueness and symmetric eligibility. Race reciprocal likes, right/unmatch, block/match, and deletion/pool events. Replay duplicate/reordered events. Load-test dense and sparse geographies, popular profiles, and bots. Chaos-test pair-owner failover and policy dependency loss. Red-team enumeration, distance triangulation, screenshots/API scraping, and age-filter bypass.

    Product evolution, moderation, and recovery¶

    Discovery changes require more than an offline ranking score. New models first replay historical point-in-time features, then run in shadow without changing cards. A small canary receives the model under exposure, safety, latency, and geography guardrails. Holdbacks measure long-term match quality and reports, while dashboards detect whether a cohort or sparse region loses supply. Rollback switches a versioned routing rule; short-lived pools naturally expire instead of requiring a global cache purge.

    Profile review is a versioned workflow: draft, automated checks, visible, limited, suspended, and deleted. Every candidate carries the profile version used during generation. Online eligibility compares it with canonical state, preventing a pool entry from bypassing a later suspension. Media hashes and moderation decisions may be retained under restricted policy to stop repeat abuse, but deletion and appeal rules must distinguish safety evidence from ordinary profile data. Human actions include reason, evidence, policy version, and dual control for sensitive access.

    Account deletion writes a priority deny record, closes discovery eligibility, revokes active sessions, and emits work for geo indexes, pools, matches, notifications, media, analytics, and backups. Match/chat policy determines what counterparties retain. Completion is tracked as a saga with per-system checkpoints; retries are idempotent, and missed consumers are found by reconciliation. This is stronger than assuming one broadcast instantly erases every copy.

    Suppose a pair-owner shard fails after persisting a match but before responding. Both clients may retry their swipes. Unique swipe and pair keys return the existing outcome after the new leader is fenced in. The outbox eventually creates chat access and notifications once. If notification arrives after an immediate unmatch, its consumer checks match version and suppresses it. These races are useful interview probes because they connect database uniqueness, lifecycle versions, asynchronous effects, and user safety.

    Cost controls include bounded candidates per rank call, cache reuse of immutable profile versions, tiered swipe-history retention, and separate impression aggregation. They must not weaken block checks or secretly change hard preferences. Under overload, reduce expensive ranking features and pool refresh frequency, then return fewer cards; never widen age or safety constraints to improve availability.

    Pagination must also reserve impressions carefully. Returning a card records a deterministic impression token tied to user, candidate, pool generation, and expiry. A retry returns the same page when practical and does not repeatedly charge exposure caps. The swipe API accepts only a valid recent token, reducing arbitrary target enumeration without making the token an authorization grant. Expired tokens can still produce a safe rejection or a fresh eligibility check. Analytics deduplicates impressions by token, while ranking experiments retain the model and policy versions that selected each card.

    L6 mechanisms: geo retrieval, constrained exposure, and pair atomicity¶

    Concrete geo candidate algorithm¶

    Store discoverable users in S2 cells at level 14, roughly neighborhood-sized, with postings segmented by coarse age band, discovery mode, and activity bucket. Keep the encrypted exact point in a separate location service. For viewer u, compute the covering cells for concentric rings [0,5], (5,15], (15,30], and (30,R] km, intersecting the configured maximum radius. Query ring cells in deterministic pseudo-random order seeded by (u,pool_generation) so repeated generation is stable but cells do not receive positional bias.

    For each ring, request at most ceil(target_remaining / estimated_yield) postings, capped at 2,000 scanned IDs and 200 IDs per cell. Cell metadata maintains count and trailing eligible-yield estimates by coarse filter. Oversized cells subdivide to level 16; sparse adjacent cells are fetched in one batch. Merge postings, remove self and decisions with a Bloom filter followed by canonical lookup, batch-fetch profiles, then evaluate exact Haversine distance and mutual hard preferences server-side. Stop once 400 eligible IDs are found or the scan reaches 10,000 IDs/80 ms. Never infer supply by exposing cell IDs or exact distance to clients.

    Estimate-driven pools and fair exposure¶

    To return 20 cards with expected online eligibility yield y, rank acceptance a, and a 25% reserve, request N = min(400,ceil(20 * 1.25 / (y*a))). If canonical filtering historically yields y=0.55 and rank policy accepts a=0.8, request 57 candidates. Persist estimates as exponentially weighted rates by cell/ring/filter bucket, with a conservative floor of 0.1 for new buckets. Pool generation stops at 400 eligible IDs, 10,000 scanned postings, 80 ms geo work, or 2 MB intermediate data. Sparse markets may produce fewer cards; hard age, block, ban, visibility, and distance settings never relax automatically.

    Ranking first predicts compatibility/quality, then applies constrained selection. Give each candidate an exposure debt d = target_impressions(cohort,window) - actual_impressions, clipped to prevent low-quality promotion, and rank by base_score + alpha*d + exploration_bonus. Hard controls cap impressions per candidate per city/time window and at two cards from the same fine cell in any ten-card span. Reserve, for example, 10% of eligible slots for calibrated exploration among candidates above a safety and quality floor. For hard caps, reserve exposure capacity atomically before issuing a card token, settle it once on the deduplicated visible impression, and release only when the token can no longer become valid exposure under the defined window. Counting delayed impressions alone can overshoot a hard cap; approximate local counters provide only a declared soft cap. Audit exposure distributions by gender, age band, geography, account age, and subscription tier while withholding tiny cohorts; product fairness policy defines targets rather than the model inventing them.

    If the ranker fails, use a versioned deterministic score over activity band, distance band, profile completeness, and exposure debt. If the exposure authority is unavailable, spend only previously escrowed cap grants or return fewer cards; an arbitrary local cap is not a proof of a global bound. If canonical eligibility is unavailable, return no cards. Pool exhaustion returns exhausted_reason such as HARD_FILTERS, RECENT_DECISIONS, or SUPPLY_DELAY; widening requires an explicit user action.

    Pair-owned atomic match boundary¶

    Route canonical pair_key = H(min(A,B),max(A,B)) to one transactional shard and store both directed decisions plus the match row in that pair partition. The transaction for swipe (A,B,swipe_id) performs: insert idempotency result if absent; lock/read pair state; upsert A's decision; if it is RIGHT and B has an active RIGHT, insert the unique match; append MatchCreated to the same shard's outbox; commit the swipe result. The idempotency row stores the final response, including existing match_id, so timeout retries cannot return a contradictory outcome.

    Actor-keyed swipe history remains a projection fed from the pair transaction; it is not used to decide reciprocity. This avoids an impossible atomic write between independently actor-sharded likes and a pair-sharded match. Per-actor rate limiting can precede routing, but the pair owner is the correctness boundary. A monotonically increasing pair version orders right swipe, unmatch, block, and rematch transitions. Chat creation consumes (pair_key,match_version) and checks that exact active version. Chat access also checks current pair/block authority on every new operation or under a declared bounded freshness lease: a pre-creation check alone races unmatch after the check and before the grant is installed.

    Pair-owner failover requires a new routing epoch and quorum fencing before writes resume. Requests sent to an old epoch receive a redirect and do not queue locally. If commit succeeds but response fails, the new owner reads the persisted swipe_id. Concurrent reciprocal swipes serialize; the first commits a like and its accepted response, and the second creates the match. Retrying the first swipe returns that original response; the caller discovers the later match through event delivery or GET /v1/matches, not by changing a stored idempotency outcome.

    Cursor and impression-token contract¶

    The discovery cursor is signed and encrypted because even pool membership is sensitive: {viewer_id,pool_generation,offset,last_rank_key,policy_version,model_version,issued_at,expires_at,nonce}. It references a short-lived immutable ordered pool. Regeneration creates a new generation and subtracts durable decisions plus recent impression IDs; it never accepts an offset from another generation. The service caches (generation,offset,page_size) -> card IDs and tokens through the retry window so retries retain the same page identity and tokens, while current eligibility and prior decisions can remove cards. Cached payloads never bypass a new block or swipe.

    Each card carries a single-use-looking but idempotent signed token {token_id,viewer_id,candidate_id,pool_generation,rank_position,profile_version,policy_version,model_version,issued_at,expires_at}. Rendering records token_id with a unique constraint and charges exposure once. Swipe verifies signature, viewer, target, expiry, and profile version, then repeats current block/ban/age/visibility checks; the token proves the card was issued, not that it remains eligible. Reusing the same token with the same swipe_id returns the prior outcome; a different target or payload is rejected. Tokens reduce arbitrary-ID swipes and tie training data to selection provenance without becoming authorization capabilities.

    Tradeoffs and final architecture¶

    Precomputed pools reduce latency but stale quickly; short pools plus canonical read-time eligibility balance speed and safety. Exact distance improves ranking but leaks location; bands preserve utility. Global active-active pair writes maximize availability but complicate uniqueness; deterministic ownership with fenced failover preserves the core invariant. Pure engagement ranking causes concentration and unsafe incentives; constrained ranking makes policy and exposure first-class.

    Questions, levels, and five-minute summary¶

    1. How do simultaneous reciprocal likes create one match?
    2. How do you prevent distance triangulation?
    3. What happens when candidate pools are exhausted?
    4. How are popular profiles and dense cells kept from becoming hot keys?
    5. Can discovery continue if block state is unavailable?

    L5 candidates should model discovery, geo indexing, read-time filters, idempotent swipes, unique matches, partitioning, and abuse. L6 candidates should define pair ownership and fencing, rank constraints and exposure policy, privacy threat models, regional residency, overload behavior, projection rebuilds, and safe product behavior under uncertainty.

    In five minutes: generate short-lived pools from coarse geo and preference indexes, then recheck canonical mutual eligibility, decisions, blocks, bans, and profile versions before constrained ranking. Never return exact location. Persist swipes idempotently and route an unordered pair to one transactional owner that conditionally creates a unique match when reciprocal likes exist. Publish match events through an outbox and recheck active state before notifications or chat grants. Partition actor history, pair matches, and geographic cells separately; split dense cells and cap popular-profile exposure. Treat pools, rank scores, counters, and notifications as projections, while profile policy, decisions, blocks, and match state remain canonical.
