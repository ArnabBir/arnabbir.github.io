    Design Live Comments¶

    Interview roadmap¶

    Design comments attached to a live stream, sports event, or launch. Clarify ordering, audience size, moderation, replay, and whether every viewer must see every message. The key is separating durable comment acceptance from best-effort realtime distribution. At million-viewer scale, sampling and ranking are product requirements, not merely optimizations.

    Requirements, invariants, and estimates¶

    Users post comments, receive a continuously updating stream, react, report, and replay recent history. Moderators remove comments and mute users. Post acknowledgement targets 200 ms p99. For eligible comments selected into a viewer's subscribed distribution tier, delivery normally targets one second from selection; measure acceptance-to-eligibility delay separately. Acceptance does not promise broadcast to every viewer, and held comments have a separate moderation latency objective. The service remains available under celebrity-event bursts and protects the player from comment-system failures.

    • Accepted comments are durable before acknowledgement and have an event-local sequence.
    • A request key creates at most one canonical comment.
    • Authorization and moderation state override caches and delayed delivery.
    • Durable distribution consumers process at least once, but live socket delivery is best effort with bounded replay; clients deduplicate by comment ID.
    • A removal version dominates delayed create/index events.
    • Per-viewer ordering is monotonic by server sequence, with documented gaps.
    • Viewer backpressure never blocks canonical ingestion.

    Assume 10 million concurrent viewers for a top event, 2% commenting once/minute: 3,300 writes/s, with 20x bursts. Sending every comment to every viewer is billions of deliveries/s. Therefore channels produce one durable stream but viewer tiers receive ranked, sampled, localized subsets. At 500 bytes/comment, canonical storage is small compared with realtime egress and connections.

    Deep dives¶

    Massive fanout and selection¶

    The durable event stream is consumed once into regional relays. Gateways subscribe to topic variants such as moderator-approved highlights, locale, friends, or sampled general chat. A mixer enforces author diversity, rate caps, freshness, quality, and deterministic sampling so reconnects are less jarring. Hosts and moderators may receive an unsampled stream. Hierarchical relay prevents cross-region multiplication and sends one frame per gateway, which then multiplexes local sockets.

    Ordering, pagination, and replay¶

    An event shard assigns increasing sequences. Sampling creates intentional gaps, so clients order received items and use a server watermark rather than assume contiguity. History uses (event_id, seq) cursor pagination. During reconnect, gateways replay a bounded regional buffer; older gaps use durable history. Edits and removals are separate versioned control events at the same comment identity, not newly ordered comments, and must apply even when their original comment sequence is below the display watermark. A held comment released after the topic has advanced beyond its original sequence appears in authorized history but is not injected backward into the live ordered tier; alternatively the product must define a separate eligibility/delivery sequence.

    Moderation and viral abuse¶

    Admission applies authentication, per-account/device/IP token buckets, duplicate-text detection, URL policy, and lightweight classifiers. Async systems aggregate reports, reputation, and coordinated behavior. Moderation commands use a priority topic distributed to every gateway; gateways keep a compact tombstone cache and remove already-rendered items. Human tooling records actor, policy version, evidence, and appeal outcome. Slow moderation reduces distribution volume rather than allowing unchecked backlog.

    Backpressure¶

    Each socket has a bounded queue. Slow consumers lose typing/reaction updates first, then receive coarser comment samples, and finally a resync marker. Gateways never push back into canonical logs. Event-level admission budgets isolate hot events, while fair queues preserve small streams. If egress capacity falls, the mixer lowers sample rates transparently.

    Partitioning, regions, consistency, reliability, and recovery¶

    Partition canonical comments by event and time bucket. A hot event uses one logical sequencer with partitioned storage writers; any leased sequence ranges need a committed-prefix frontier and explicit sealed gaps before publication, since allocating disjoint ranges does not establish commit order. if strict total order is unnecessary, shard by lane and expose (lane,sequence) partial order. Reactions partition by comment plus hash suffix. Gateways and relays partition by event and geography.

    Write an event in its home region with synchronous zonal replicas. Replicate logs and moderation commands to viewing regions. Fenced failover prevents two sequencers; gaps are acceptable after lease loss. Distribution is regional and disposable. Comment append is strongly consistent per event; moderation, reactions, ranking, and delivery are eventual. Idempotency mappings and outbox commits close retry and dual-write gaps.

    Reliability objectives follow the product hierarchy: preserve video playback first, accepted-comment durability and moderation commands second, and complete realtime distribution last. Canonical comments use an RPO of zero within the home region because acknowledgement follows a synchronous zonal commit; regional disaster recovery targets an RPO under one minute from the replicated log and a 15-minute RTO for posting. Realtime relays are disposable and target a two-minute RTO by reconnecting viewers to healthy capacity with a lower initial sample rate.

    Bus failure delays realtime but history remains correct. Operators detect it from event-age and outbox-age SLO burn, pause nonessential reaction consumers, restore or reroute partitions, and replay from committed outbox offsets; idempotent comment IDs and tombstone versions make replay safe. Gateway loss reconnects clients with cursors, while admission jitter and replay budgets prevent a recovery storm. Regional relay loss shifts subscriptions to another relay at a lower sample rate. Sequencer loss acquires a higher fenced epoch before appends resume; ambiguous requests retry with their original idempotency keys. Cache loss invokes stricter load shedding. Poison messages are quarantined without blocking the partition. After regional recovery, reconciliation compares canonical sequence ranges, moderation-version watermarks, and sampled gateway delivery receipts before quality tiers are raised. Replays always honor current tombstones.

    Security, privacy, observability, and testing¶

    Do not expose viewer lists or raw IPs. Apply age/region policy, blocks, mutes, and streamer controls. Escape rendered text, scan links, restrict moderator APIs, encrypt and audit evidence, and define retention for chat replay. Protect against bot floods, Unicode impersonation, brigading, and report abuse.

    Measure accept and end-to-end latency, outbox age, moderation decision/removal propagation, accepted/held/rejected rates, comments per event, fanout amplification, relay/gateway saturation, socket drops, sample ratio, replay rate, duplicates, ordering regressions, and report outcomes. Correlate by comment ID and policy version.

    Property-test tombstone precedence and cursor ordering. Replay duplicates and version reorder. Load-test flash crowds, one hot event, many small events, slow sockets, and spam bursts. Chaos-test sequencer, relay, bus, and region loss. Safety changes run shadow evaluation and canaries; synthetic comments verify removals reach every regional gateway.

    Event lifecycle, replay, and capacity control¶

    An event moves through SCHEDULED, WARMING, LIVE, ENDED, and ARCHIVED. Warming preallocates sequencer capacity, regional topics, moderation staff/tools, and CDN-independent gateway pools based on expected audience. Live transition is idempotent. Ending rejects new ordinary comments, drains accepted work, records the final watermark, and converts hot storage into a replay index. Archival applies retention and legal policies to bodies, author mappings, reports, and moderation evidence separately.

    Replay is not simply the live stream saved forever. It may return a stable chronological subset, highlighted comments, and tombstones at the viewer's playback timestamp. Each record carries media time as well as ingestion sequence. Late moderation updates the replay projection and remains effective even though the original live packet was delivered. Rebuilders scan canonical comments plus versioned moderation actions up to a checkpoint, then atomically switch an index generation.

    Capacity planning uses connections, frames, and bytes rather than comment writes alone. If ten million viewers receive two 400-byte frames per second, edge egress is roughly 8 GB/s before protocol overhead. Batching several comments per frame cuts headers and CPU. Compression helps text but can increase latency and security risk if contexts mix principals; gateway policies choose bounded independent frames. Regional relays expose subscription counts so placement can add gateways before queues fill.

    A flash-crowd runbook establishes explicit thresholds. At moderate pressure, coalesce reactions and presence. Next, lower general-chat sample rates and reserve full feeds for moderators. Then disable expensive personalization, cap history replay, and reject comments from low-reputation unauthenticated sessions. At severe pressure, preserve the video product, canonical moderation commands, and accepted-comment durability while returning a clear degraded indicator. Recovery raises quality gradually to avoid a reconnect and replay storm.

    Privacy controls include per-viewer blocks and muted terms. Applying them at every regional relay would explode state, so gateways maintain compact viewer policy or clients apply convenience mutes; safety-critical blocks are checked during history hydration and distribution-token construction. The design must distinguish privacy authorization, which cannot be delegated to a client, from display preferences, which can.

    Reactions use a separate lossy aggregation path. A user reaction is an idempotent canonical upsert, but the animated count shown during the event is a windowed approximation produced from regional deltas. Periodic durable reconciliation corrects drift. This avoids turning a popular comment into a single counter hot key and prevents reaction storms from competing with moderation traffic. Clients render an approximate marker and fetch the user's own reaction state separately.

    Deployments drain gateways instead of dropping all sockets simultaneously. New connections move to the new version while old gateways stop accepting subscriptions and retain existing ones for a deadline. Protocol envelopes advertise supported versions, and unknown optional fields are ignored. Changes to ordering, cursor, or tombstone semantics require a negotiated protocol version and mixed-client tests. This operational detail prevents an otherwise harmless rollout from becoming the largest reconnect storm of the day.

    Tradeoffs and final architecture¶

    Broadcasting everything preserves chat completeness but collapses at large audiences and harms usability. Ranked deterministic sampling is scalable but creates different viewer experiences. Pre-moderation is safer but adds latency; risk-tiered synchronous gates plus fast async removal balance both. Strict total order simplifies replay but creates a sequencer bottleneck; retain it while rate is feasible, then explicitly move to lane ordering rather than pretending global order.

    Questions, levels, and five-minute summary¶

    1. Can every viewer see every comment at ten million concurrency?
    2. How do removals reach clients that already rendered content?
    3. What ordering contract survives sampling and failover?
    4. Which load is shed first during a flash crowd?
    5. How do moderator and ordinary streams differ?

    L5 candidates should separate ingest from fanout, quantify amplification, design bounded sockets, idempotency, cursor replay, and moderation. L6 candidates should negotiate completeness versus relevance, define overload tiers, sequence/failover semantics, regional relay topology, policy auditability, and a migration to partial ordering.

    In five minutes: durably append rate- and safety-gated comments to an event-local ordered log with an outbox. Consume that log into moderation and hierarchical regional distribution. Do not broadcast every item at huge scale; create policy-controlled, ranked, localized, or deterministically sampled topic variants and multiplex each once per gateway. Bound socket queues and degrade ephemeral reactions, sample density, then require resync without affecting ingestion. Use versioned tombstones that override stale events, opaque sequence cursors for history, fenced regional failover, and explicit gaps under sampling. Operate event skew, moderation latency, fanout amplification, queue drops, and replay correctness.
