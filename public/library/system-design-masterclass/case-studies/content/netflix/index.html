    Design Netflix¶

    1. Interview roadmap¶

    We design licensed-catalog ingest, entitlement-aware playback, adaptive delivery, profiles, progress, and regional availability. Unlike creator video, content arrives through trusted studio supply chains, is preprocessed before release, and is governed by territorial windows and DRM.

    This is a proposed architecture, not a reconstruction of Netflix's private control plane. Netflix Open Connect verifies ISP-embedded appliances, peering, content fill and appliance failover. The numerical workloads, account leases, DRM topology and ABR thresholds below are explicit design assumptions, not published Netflix guarantees (accessed 2026-09-08).

    2. Requirements, SLOs, and invariants¶

    Users browse a personalized catalog, start/resume episodes, switch devices, select audio/subtitles, and stream under account/device/concurrency rules. Studios deliver masters and timed metadata; operators schedule release and rights windows. Exclude ad auctions and recommendation-model internals.

    Targets: playback authorization 99.99%, p95 start under two seconds, rebuffer under 1%, progress visible on another device in five seconds, and no new license outside verified entitlement, with existing playback bounded by the explicit license and revocation window. Immediate revocation of already issued offline licenses is not promised. Invariants: manifests reference complete renditions; rights/parental/concurrency checks precede license issuance; assets are immutable/versioned; ingest and progress updates are idempotent; CDN loss cannot broaden access.

    3. Estimates¶

    Assume 250 million accounts, 100 million viewing hours/day, 3 Mbps mean delivered bitrate: about 135 PB/day egress. A 20,000-title catalog with episodes, 20 bitrate/device renditions, audio languages, subtitles, and regional replicas may consume tens of PB, modest beside egress. At 20 million concurrent streams and six-second segments, edges handle over 3 million segment requests/s. Browse traffic is cacheable; playback authorization and licenses scale with session starts, perhaps hundreds of thousands/s at premieres.

    Control plane owns catalog, rights, sessions, manifests, and CDN placement. Data plane serves encrypted segments from appliances near ISPs. They fail independently: existing bounded sessions can continue briefly during control-plane trouble without granting new rights.

    6. Ingest, transcoding, and release¶

    Studios upload encrypted masters through partner channels with checksums and delivery manifests. Ingest validates technical metadata, scans, performs quality control, and launches a DAG for per-shot/per-title encoding, HDR/SDR, codecs, audio normalization, subtitles, thumbnails, and device compatibility. Outputs use deterministic versioned keys. Human QC can gate premium releases.

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
  participant S as Studio
  participant I as Ingest
  participant O as Object store
  participant T as Transcoders
  participant C as Catalog
  S->>I: package manifest + checksums
  I->>O: verify durable master
  I->>T: versioned workflow
  T->>O: encrypted renditions
  T-->>I: checksums and QC
  I->>C: publish complete manifest + release state

    Release is a metadata transition after assets, DRM keys, rights, localization, and CDN prepositioning pass readiness. A missing subtitle may block only affected locales; a missing baseline codec blocks compatible devices. Rollback selects the previous manifest without rewriting media.

    7. Playback flow, ABR, and DRM¶

    The client sends profile, title, network hints, and signed device capabilities. Playback control authenticates account, evaluates territorial/time/plan/device/parental rules, acquires a concurrency lease, and returns a short-lived signed manifest. The DRM service verifies session binding and device trust before wrapping the content key. Keys are separate from CDN objects.

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
  participant U as Player
  participant P as Playback control
  participant R as Rights and session store
  participant D as DRM
  participant E as Edge CDN
  U->>P: start(title, profile, capabilities)
  P->>R: authorize + conditional concurrency lease
  P-->>U: signed filtered manifest
  U->>D: license challenge + session
  D-->>U: bounded content license
  U->>E: encrypted segments
  U-->>P: sequenced heartbeat/progress

    Manifests include only supported codec/HDR/audio/subtitle variants. ABR begins conservatively, uses throughput and buffer estimates, drops rapidly on congestion, and avoids oscillation. CDN serves immutable encrypted segments; regional shields and ISP appliances absorb premieres. Forecasts preposition likely title/language variants, while long-tail pulls through. Peer-to-peer delivery is rejected because privacy, ISP behavior, quality, and content-control costs outweigh origin savings.

    8. Progress and concurrency deep dives¶

    Heartbeats carry session identity, monotonic session sequence and media timestamp. Deduplicate within that session; never compare independent device sequence numbers. A versioned active-session ownership policy chooses which session updates (profile,title) resume state, allowing an intentional backward seek or rewatch. Greatest-ever media position is a completion/history signal, not always the desired resume position. Completion applies duration and credits policy, not simply the maximum timestamp.

    Concurrency uses expiring leases per account. Start conditionally reserves a slot; heartbeat renews it; missed heartbeats expire. During partition, strict global limits harm availability while local limits can briefly oversubscribe. Choose bounded oversubscription with signed regional allocations for consumer plans, stricter consistency for high-value events. Do not terminate healthy playback solely because progress storage fails.

    9. Rights, personalization, and CDN placement¶

    Rights are interval records by territory, plan, and device. Evaluate authoritative current versions on session start and bind rights_version plus expiration into tokens/licenses. A policy revocation publishes deny epochs to playback and DRM; maximum token/license duration bounds propagation. Browse catalog is a projection filtered by region/profile, but playback always rechecks canonical rights.

    Home rows are asynchronously materialized recommendation lists; cache keys include profile maturity and catalog epoch. Fallbacks are popular-in-region and continue-watching. Placement solves expected demand times origin cost under appliance capacity. Track hit rate by title/variant/ISP; avoid prepositioning every language or 4K asset.

    10. Partitioning, consistency, failures, and regions¶

    Partition catalog by title, sessions by account, progress by profile/title, and events by session. Hot premieres stress CDN and authorization caches, not one title row; signed versioned rights snapshots reduce reads. Salt QoE events. Reserve separate resources for browse and playback so recommendation overload cannot block starts.

    Catalog and assets replicate globally; account home region owns subscription writes, while playback runs active-active using replicated entitlement snapshots and account lease coordination. Rights/manifests need strongly ordered versions; progress and recommendations are eventual. License issuance fails closed when rights are uncertain. Existing licenses remain valid only to their short bound.

    DRM outage blocks new starts but not buffered media. CDN appliance failure reroutes within ISP then regional CDN with egress protection. Control-plane outage may renew existing session tokens only while the signed rights/revocation snapshot is fresh enough and within the original absolute rights deadline. Repeated local renewal cannot extend stale authorization indefinitely or create uncertain new entitlement. Queue failures delay future ingest only. Overload disables previews, reduces browse richness, and protects playback/license pools.

    11. Security, abuse, observability, and testing¶

    Encrypt media, separate keys, use hardware-backed DRM where available, rotate signing keys, watermark high-risk releases, attest devices, and detect credential sharing/account takeover without treating households as static IPs. Minimize viewing-history access, isolate child profiles, audit staff and studio operations, and honor regional privacy deletion. Rate-limit licenses and resist manifest/token replay by binding account, session, device, title, territory, and expiry.

    Measure playback success/start time/rebuffer/fatal rate/bitrate by device-ISP-CDN, rights denials, license latency, session oversubscription, progress lag, CDN hit/origin egress, ingest stage age, QC failure, preposition accuracy, and cost per viewing hour. Player telemetry is the truth for QoE.

    Test rights interval boundaries/time zones, device matrices, manifest completeness, heartbeat reorder/duplication, concurrency expiry, DRM binding, subtitle sync, and ABR with recorded network traces. Load premieres and household bursts. Chaos removes appliances, delays rights replicas, loses heartbeats, corrupts assets, and partitions account regions. Shadow new encoders and compare objective quality plus device playback before atomic manifest rollout.

    Release operations and capacity planning¶

    A release readiness record joins facts without making one giant distributed transaction: approved asset manifest, territory rights, localization coverage, DRM keys, policy rating, and CDN placement each publish a versioned attestation. The release controller checks a compatible set and conditionally moves the title from scheduled to available. If an attestation is revoked, playback policy immediately blocks the affected territory/device while operators repair or roll back. Scheduled releases use server time and are tested around daylight-saving boundaries; clients never decide eligibility from their own clocks.

    Capacity planning starts from expected concurrent streams by ISP and bitrate, not account totals. For a premiere, reserve origin shield and license-service headroom, prewarm only likely regional variants, and run a synthetic playback through each device/DRM combination. Admission control distinguishes a license retry from a new session so client storms do not multiply leases. Token-bucket limits are account-aware but avoid one shared global counter on every segment.

    L6 encoding optimization and playback controller¶

    Per-title analysis first encodes representative complexity samples across resolution, codec, bitrate, and preset, computes VMAF plus encode cost, and removes Pareto-dominated candidates. Per-shot optimization then places scene boundaries on segment boundaries and assigns each shot a quality target or constrained bitrate. Calm dialogue can use fewer bits while grain, camera motion, and visual effects receive more; a constrained optimizer minimizes total bytes subject to a VMAF floor, peak bitrate, decoder level, segment-size variance, and encode budget. Shots are reassembled only when timestamp, GOP, audio, subtitle, and encryption boundaries remain aligned across every rung, allowing seamless ABR switches.

    For example, fixed 1080p at 5.0 Mb/s uses about 4.5 GB for a two-hour film. Shot analysis may classify 70 quiet minutes at 2.2 Mb/s, 40 normal minutes at 3.5 Mb/s, and 10 action minutes at 7.0 Mb/s, averaging 3.03 Mb/s and about 2.73 GB, a 39% reduction. The optimizer rejects a cheaper plan if the fifth-percentile shot VMAF falls below 93 even when title-average VMAF passes. Encoder migrations shadow a reference corpus and a small title cohort; promotion requires byte saving, VMAF, decoder failures, startup, rebuffer, and manual golden-scene guardrails. Outputs carry source, optimizer, metric, encoder, and packaging versions for exact rollback.

    flowchart TD
  M[Studio mezzanine] --> S[Shot boundary and complexity analysis]
  S --> C[Candidate encodes by shot]
  C --> P[Pareto and constrained optimizer]
  P --> E[Parallel shot encodes]
  E --> A[Aligned segment assembly]
  A --> Q{Objective and device QC pass?}
  Q -->|yes| R[Release attestation]
  Q -->|no| F[Fallback preset or human review]
  F --> E

    The ABR controller combines conservative throughput and buffer occupancy rather than following throughput alone. It computes safe_bw = 0.85 * harmonic_mean(last 5 segment throughputs) and scores each candidate as quality - rebuffer_penalty - switch_penalty; any candidate whose predicted download time exceeds available buffer minus a two-second reserve is infeasible. Below 8 seconds the client steps down immediately, between 8 and 20 seconds it holds quality, and above 20 seconds it may step up one rung after two confirming downloads. Segment abandonment is allowed when the current segment would exhaust the buffer and a lower version can finish sooner. Device decode failures blacklist that codec for the session and retry the aligned baseline rung.

    If recent segment rates are 8, 6, 5, 4, and 3 Mb/s, their harmonic mean is about 4.65 Mb/s and safe bandwidth is 3.95 Mb/s. Given rungs at 0.8, 1.5, 3.0, 4.5, and 7.0 Mb/s, a client with 24 seconds buffered selects 3.0 Mb/s; it does not gamble on 4.5 Mb/s. For emergency low-buffer behavior, cap the rolling estimate by the latest measured segment throughput. If that measurement drops to 1.8 Mb/s with 7 seconds buffered, safe bandwidth is at most 1.53 Mb/s and the client immediately drops to 1.5 Mb/s. Rollout dashboards segment rebuffer, start time, average delivered VMAF, oscillations, abandonments, and decoder errors by device, ISP, CDN, and experiment cohort.

    DRM key lifecycle and outage containment¶

    Use a key-encryption-key hierarchy: an offline/root HSM protects regional key-encryption keys, which wrap randomly generated per-title or per-epoch content encryption keys. Packaging obtains a short-lived grant, encrypts segments, stores only wrapped keys and key IDs in metadata, and never places plaintext keys beside media. License service instances unwrap inside HSM-backed boundaries after entitlement, device, concurrency, territory, policy epoch, and anti-replay checks. Licenses bind title, account/session, device security level, allowed tracks, offline flag, and absolute expiry.

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
  participant K as Key authority and HSM
  participant P as Packager
  participant C as Playback control
  participant L as License service
  participant D as Device DRM
  K->>P: content key grant and key ID
  P->>P: encrypt immutable segments
  P-->>K: destroy plaintext working key
  D->>C: start request and capabilities
  C->>C: rights and concurrency checks
  C-->>D: manifest and license challenge
  D->>L: attested challenge and session token
  L->>K: authorized unwrap request
  K-->>L: key in protected boundary
  L-->>D: device-wrapped bounded license

    Keys transition through CREATED, PACKAGING, ACTIVE, RETIRED, REVOKED, and DESTROYED; transitions are audited and dual-controlled for premium releases. Rotation creates a new key epoch and manifest rather than mutating encrypted objects. Retirement stops new licenses but honors policy-approved existing offline licenses until their bound. Revocation increments a title/device/policy deny epoch distributed on a priority channel; license issuance fails closed on stale epoch, manifests stop refreshing, and device revocation lists address compromised DRM identities. Crypto-erasure happens only after contractual retention, rollback windows, and legal holds expire.

    During a license-service outage, already buffered encrypted media and cached valid licenses continue until expiry, but new licenses fail closed. A multi-zone regional quorum, preloaded wrapped-key cache, and local signed rights snapshots remove database dependencies without bypassing authorization. Never extend a license merely because the service is unhealthy. Runbooks distinguish HSM saturation, unwrap failure, stale revocation feed, and entitlement dependency failure; shed previews and retries, reserve capacity for renewals near expiry, and deduplicate retries by challenge ID. For one million starts/minute with two license calls per start and 30% headroom, plan about 43,400 license operations/s; if an HSM partition sustains 5,000 unwraps/s, at least nine active partitions plus zonal failover reserve are required.

    Appliance placement, capacity, and regional lease reconciliation¶

    For each ISP appliance, placement ranks an object bundle by forecast_requests * miss_cost - disk_bytes * storage_cost - fill_risk, subject to disk, NIC, codec/language demand, and minimum free-space constraints. Bundles include the popular audio/subtitle combinations but not every dub with every 4K codec. A two-hour title averaging 3 Mb/s is about 2.7 GB per video ladder path; ten video paths plus common audio/subtitles may consume 30 GB. A 100 TB usable appliance can hold roughly 3,300 such hot-title bundles before replication and reserve, so predicted title-ISP demand, not global popularity alone, drives placement.

    NIC and fill bandwidth are separate constraints. Forty thousand concurrent streams at 4 Mb/s require 160 Gb/s; an appliance pair with 2x100 Gb/s links has only 100 Gb/s after either appliance fails. A strict one-appliance-loss target permits at most 50% aggregate steady utilization before further reserve, so 160 Gb/s requires more appliances or proven upstream overflow capacity. Placement simulates one-appliance loss and a cold premiere: peer appliances, regional CDN, and origin shield receive explicit overflow budgets. Operators track disk working-set hit ratio, per-port saturation, eviction churn, fill completion before release, peer-fill amplification, origin egress, thermal/power alarms, and forecast error. A failed box is drained from mapping, repaired, securely re-enrolled, and repopulated gradually to avoid a cache-fill storm.

    Concurrency uses a home-account ledger plus signed regional lease budgets. The home region grants each playback region a short-lived allocation epoch and count; a region atomically spends a slot and creates (account_id, session_id, region, epoch, expiry). Heartbeats renew only the same session and never consume another slot. During partition, a region may spend only its remaining budget, bounding oversubscription; it cannot mint a new epoch. On recovery, regions submit active leases and spent counters, and the home ledger chooses the highest epoch, deduplicates session IDs, expires leases using server time, and issues corrected budgets only after prior regional grants can no longer authorize playback or their outstanding sessions have been counted against the new allowance. Selecting a higher epoch alone cannot revoke an isolated region's cached grant.

    For a two-stream plan, the home region might allocate one slot to us-east and one to us-west. If both regions partition after starting one stream, neither can start a third; if policy allows one emergency slot per region, maximum oversubscription is explicitly bounded at two extra streams, not unbounded. Reconciliation does not abruptly kill all conflicts: rank sessions by valid epoch, start time, recent heartbeat, and device trust, stop renewing losers, and let their short lease expire. Alerts cover oversubscription age, budget divergence, stale epochs, heartbeat replay, reconciliation backlog, and forced-expiry rate; synthetic partition tests verify the promised bound.

    Catalog evolution and experimentation¶

    Catalog metadata uses versioned schemas with tolerant readers. A new artwork or recommendation model is exposed through experiment assignment at profile level, but entitlement, maturity, and legal labels are never experimental. Assignment is sticky and logged with model/catalog versions so QoE and engagement results are reproducible. Guardrails include playback starts, rebuffering, title diversity, child safety, and cancellation, not only watch hours.

    Device capability declarations are untrusted hints. The server intersects them with an observed compatibility registry, and clients can report decoder failure to retry a baseline manifest. Deprecating a codec requires usage measurement, a long device migration window, and retention of fallback assets until no supported client requests them.

    12. Tradeoffs and final design¶

    Long licenses improve outage tolerance but weaken revocation; bound by rights risk and allow shorter windows for live/premium content. Strict global concurrency prevents sharing but makes partitions user-visible; expiring bounded leases balance both. Prepositioning everything minimizes misses but wastes appliance disks; demand-aware placement wins.

    Final design pre-ingests trusted masters into immutable, encrypted, versioned renditions; atomically releases complete manifests after rights/QC/CDN readiness; authorizes each playback against canonical policy; issues bound DRM licenses; and serves segments from ISP-adjacent CDN. Sequenced events update eventual progress and QoE. Control and media planes degrade independently.

    13. Interview questions and levels¶

    • How do rights revocations beat cached manifests?
    • What is the concurrency behavior during regional partition?
    • Which variants should be prepositioned before a premiere?
    • How can an encoder migration be rolled back safely?
    • Why is browse eligibility insufficient for playback authorization?

    L5 should cover ingest/transcode, manifests, ABR/CDN, entitlement/DRM, progress, scaling, and failure. L6 should quantify egress economics, define rights/token bounds, regional concurrency semantics, release readiness, device/codec evolution, QoE experimentation, graceful control-plane isolation, and privacy/anti-sharing tradeoffs.

    14. Five-minute summary¶

    Netflix is a rights-governed, preprocessed media system. Studio packages are checksum-verified, transformed by idempotent workflows into encrypted versioned ladders/audio/subtitles, quality-checked, prepositioned, then exposed by an atomic manifest release. Playback control rechecks account, profile, territory, plan, device, time window, and concurrency; DRM separately issues a short-lived session-bound content license. ISP-adjacent CDN appliances serve immutable segments, while ABR adapts to throughput and buffer.

    Partition sessions by account and progress by profile/title; sequence heartbeats so duplicates and reordering are harmless. Strong versions govern rights/manifests; progress, browse lists, and QoE are eventual. Bound stale authorization through token/license duration and deny epochs. Protect playback resources before browse richness, and optimize from client QoE and egress cost. The key choices are revocation versus offline/outage tolerance, strict concurrency versus partition availability, and preposition hit rate versus disk cost.
