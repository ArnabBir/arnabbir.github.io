    Design Maps and Navigation¶

    1. Interview roadmap¶

    We design map browsing, place search, route planning, turn-by-turn navigation, and live traffic. We separate slowly published geographic truth from fast traffic overlays, estimate tile and routing scale, and deep-dive into graph partitioning, shortest paths, map matching, ETA, offline behavior, failures, privacy, and operations.

    2. Requirements and invariants¶

    Users pan/zoom maps, search places, request driving/walking routes with alternatives, start navigation, receive reroutes, and download offline regions. Producers ingest road geometry, restrictions, closures, places, and anonymized traffic observations. Exclude street imagery and transit scheduling.

    Targets: cached tile p95 under 100 ms, route p95 under one second for regional trips, reroute under two seconds, traffic age under two minutes on major roads, and 99.99% navigation availability. Invariants: published map generations are internally compatible; routes obey hard access/turn/closure constraints; coordinates use declared versions/projections; stale traffic may worsen ETA but must not bypass closures; telemetry is bounded, consented, and de-identified.

    3. Estimates¶

    Assume 100 million daily users, 50 tile requests/session and five route requests: 5 billion tiles/day (58,000/s average, 500,000/s peak) and 500 million routes/day. CDN hit above 95% is essential. A continental road graph might contain 500 million vertices/edges and hundreds of GB compressed per generation, replicated in RAM/SSD across routing clusters. Ten million active navigators reporting every five seconds produce 2 million observations/s; ingest must aggregate early and enforce privacy thresholds.

    Routing capacity must be derived from measured CPU per query, not request count alone. If peak traffic is 60,000 route requests/s, 70% are regional routes costing 12 ms CPU, 25% are metro routes costing 35 ms, and 5% are long routes costing 120 ms, the weighted demand is 0.70 * 12 + 0.25 * 35 + 0.05 * 120 = 23.15 ms, or 1,389 CPU-seconds/s. At 60% target utilization and 32 usable cores/host, the steady-state fleet is 1,389 / (32 * 0.60) = 73 hosts. Provisioning 30% failover and rollout headroom gives 95 hosts, before replicas required for graph locality. Cache hit rate, K alternatives, vehicle constraints, and partition fanout must be included in the measured workload mix.

    6. Map publication and tiles¶

    Edits pass schema, topology, geometry, restriction, and referential checks in a staging generation. Builders create vector tiles and routing partitions against the same immutable map generation; a manifest lists checksums and compatibility. Regions can publish independently only where boundary contracts are versioned. Serving atomically switches manifests after replicas warm; old generations remain for active routes/offline clients.

    Vector tiles contain clipped geometry and style attributes, allowing client rendering and language/style changes without new raster images. Key by generation/z/x/y, compress, and cache immutably at CDN. Low zoom tiles are globally hot; high zoom is long-tail. Delta packages update offline regions, but clients verify base/new generation and checksums.

    7. Route computation¶

    Model directed road segments as weighted edges, with turn restrictions represented in an edge-based graph. Baseline Dijkstra is correct but too slow. A* uses admissible straight-line/time lower bounds. At continental scale, contraction hierarchies or multi-level Dijkstra precompute shortcuts; customizable contraction hierarchies separate topology preprocessing from periodically changing weights. Partition graphs by geography with border nodes and overlay shortcuts.

    The request snaps origin/destination candidates, filters by mode/vehicle restrictions, chooses a graph generation, applies closure and traffic weights, computes K diverse paths, validates hard constraints, generates maneuvers, and stores a short-lived route reference. Alternatives need overlap penalties, not simply second-shortest tiny deviations.

    autonumber

  participant T as Traffic/closure overlay

  A->>T: snapshot epoch
  A->>G: endpoints, mode, epoch, deadline

  G-->>A: candidate edge paths
  A->>A: validate, diversify, maneuvers, ETA
  A-->>C: route + generations

    Contraction and customization preprocessing¶

    A conventional contraction hierarchy orders vertices and inserts shortcuts using metric-dependent witness searches. When contracting v, add (u,w) if no witness path avoiding v is at most as cheap as (u,v,w); retain child edges for unpacking. Consequently conventional shortcut topology can depend on the weights used during preprocessing. The customizable variant below instead constructs a metric-independent elimination topology, then customizes its weights. Do not assume a traffic change preserves the correctness of an ordinary witness-pruned hierarchy.

    Customizable contraction hierarchies first build nested-dissection separators and topology-only triangles. A customization pass assigns metric-specific shortcut weights bottom-up: driving, truck, bicycle, and avoid-toll metrics can share topology. Full customization follows a map or policy release; incremental customization marks shortcuts whose dependency triangles contain changed edges and propagates dirtiness upward. Minute-level traffic remains a query-time overlay or triggers regional partial customization. Every weight snapshot records (map_generation, metric_version, traffic_epoch), and a route is rejected if hard restrictions cannot be validated against that tuple.

    flowchart LR
  G[Immutable edge graph] --> O[Separator and contraction order]
  O --> T[Topology-only shortcuts]
  M[Mode and policy metric] --> C[Bottom-up customization]
  T --> C
  C --> W[Versioned shortcut weights]
  D[Changed edge weights] --> I[Dirty triangle propagation]
  I --> C
  W --> Q[Bidirectional upward query]
  Q --> U[Unpack and validate original edges]

    Worked preprocessing example. Suppose roads A-B-C cost 4 and 6 seconds, while the cheapest witness path from A to C avoiding B costs 15 seconds. Contracting B creates shortcut A-C with cost 10 and children A-B, B-C. A traffic customization changes B-C to 11, so in a customizable topology the dependent triangles are recomputed and the shortcut becomes 15. The original witness-pruned hierarchy cannot in general be updated by changing this one shortcut alone. A hard closure on B-C is not represented as a merely large traffic cost: the query either uses a closure-aware customized metric or validates and rejects the unpacked shortcut.

    Cross-partition route algorithm and bounds¶

    Each balanced geographic partition owns interior vertices and exposes replicated boundary vertices. Offline preprocessing computes exact intra-partition boundary-to-boundary distances for the base metric and forms a much smaller overlay graph. A request runs local A from each endpoint to reachable boundaries, bidirectional Dijkstra/A over the overlay, then corridor refinement inside only the partitions touched by the selected overlay path. The coordinator pins one generation, sends bounded parallel RPCs, and carries the best complete route cost U; a shard can stop expanding when its queue lower bound is at least U.

    For exact static weights, exact boundary matrices plus admissible local heuristics preserve optimality. A global multiplicative stretch bound additionally requires the overlay to represent every feasible boundary crossing on the true optimal route, each arc weight to upper-bound a realizable path by at most 1 + epsilon, and exact optimization over that overlay. Then substituting the optimal route's crossings proves cost_returned <= (1 + max epsilon) * optimal_cost. Bounds only along an already selected corridor do not prove global optimality: a better omitted corridor may exist. Dynamic traffic can invalidate the certificate; return an approximation flag unless current lower bounds prove a new guarantee. Hard closures disable affected arcs, trigger base-edge refinement, or fail safely.

    flowchart LR
  S[Origin in partition A] --> LA[Local search to A boundaries]
  LA --> OA[Boundary overlay]
  T[Destination in partition D] --> LD[Reverse local search to D boundaries]
  LD --> OA
  OA --> K[Best overlay corridor A-B-D]
  K --> PA[Refine partition A]
  K --> PB[Refine partition B]
  K --> PD[Refine partition D]
  PA --> V[Unpack and validate]
  PB --> V
  PD --> V
  V --> R[Route with bound and epochs]

    Worked cross-partition example. Exact endpoint connectors cost 7 and 8 seconds. The chosen crossing costs 72 seconds with a 2% certificate, so the candidate costs 87 seconds and the optimum within that crossing is at least 72 / 1.02 + 15 = 85.59 seconds. If every alternative corridor has been exhausted or has an admissible lower bound of at least 90, 85.59 is also a global lower bound and the candidate is within 87 / 85.59 = 1.0165, about 1.65%. One competing queue with bound 90 alone would not establish that all alternatives were covered.

    8. Traffic, map matching, and ETA¶

    Noisy GPS observations are batched, sequence-numbered, and assigned coarse ephemeral identifiers. A hidden Markov map matcher generates nearby edge candidates using distance/bearing and uses transition likelihood along the graph to avoid jumping between parallel roads. Aggregate speeds into time buckets only after minimum contributor thresholds, reject impossible motion, and weight by freshness/device quality. Raw traces have short retention.

    Fuse live distributions with historical speed profiles, incidents, weather/event signals, and road priors. Low-confidence live data decays toward history. ETA sums edge travel-time distributions with correlation/calibration correction; machine-learned residuals account for intersections, time, road class, and route context. Continuously compare predicted arrival against actual, sliced by city/time/mode.

    Closures are a high-priority strongly versioned overlay and fail closed when their required freshness cannot be established; traffic is a soft eventually consistent weight. Rebuilding all shortcuts per observation is impossible: use base/customized weights plus dynamic local overlays, labeling results approximate unless a current bound is proved, and validating routes.

    HMM map matching with Viterbi¶

    For observation z_t, retrieve at most K directed-edge candidates within an accuracy-dependent radius. The emission log-probability combines perpendicular distance under a Gaussian noise model, heading agreement under a circular distribution, and optional road-access priors. For candidates i at t-1 and j at t, the transition log-probability penalizes the difference between shortest-path road distance and observed displacement, impossible speed, illegal turns, and reversal. Viterbi computes V_t(j) = emission_t(j) + max_i(V_{t-1}(i) + transition(i,j)) and stores the winning predecessor. A sliding window commits old states only after enough future evidence; beam pruning keeps the best B states, making work O(T * B * K) rather than unconstrained O(T * K^2) when transition candidates are bounded.

    flowchart TD
  Z1[GPS z1] --> C1[Edge candidates at t1]
  Z2[GPS z2] --> C2[Edge candidates at t2]
  Z3[GPS z3] --> C3[Edge candidates at t3]
  C1 -->|road-distance transition| C2
  C2 -->|road-distance transition| C3
  C1 --> V[Viterbi scores and backpointers]
  C2 --> V
  C3 --> V
  V --> B[Best edge sequence]
  B --> A[Privacy-thresholded aggregates]

    Worked map-match example. Three observations lie between a frontage road F and parallel highway H. At the middle sample, emissions favor H by 0.8 log units because it is closer. The previous and next samples match F; entering and leaving H would require 1.4 km of graph travel versus 220 m observed, adding a transition penalty of -6 twice. The Viterbi path remains F-F-F, while nearest-edge snapping incorrectly jumps to H.

    ETA model operations¶

    The ETA service computes a deterministic baseline from calibrated edge or segment travel-time distributions, then applies a versioned residual model to features available at request time. Training labels derive from completed, map-matched traversals; examples are weighted to prevent dense-city and long-trip dominance. Training uses time-based splits and reports median absolute error, p90 absolute error, signed bias, and interval coverage by city, trip length, mode, road class, and incident state. A prediction includes model, feature, map, and traffic versions for replay.

    Candidate models shadow production first. Promotion requires global improvement without violating slice guardrails, then geographic canaries ramp from 1% to 10% to 50%. Monitor feature nulls, drift, residual bias, calibration, late arrivals, and reroute churn. If a feature stream stalls, use bounded-stale values or a baseline-only model; if a model breaches bias or latency thresholds, atomically return to the previous artifact. Labels arrive after trips finish, so dashboards distinguish prediction-time cohorts from label-complete cohorts.

    Worked ETA example. A 20-edge route has a baseline sum of 31 minutes. The residual model adds 2.5 minutes for six signalized turns and an event feature, producing 33.5 minutes with an 80% interval of 29-39. In a city canary, p90 error improves from 9.0 to 8.2 minutes, but trips under 3 km develop +1.8 minute bias against a +0.5 guardrail. Promotion stops even though aggregate error improved; the old model remains active while that slice is corrected.

    9. Navigation and rerouting¶

    Client map-matches locally for responsive maneuvers and sends bounded updates. Off-route detection uses distance, heading, confidence, and consecutive samples to avoid GPS-jitter reroutes. If confidently off route, request from current matched position; preserve destination and user avoids. Server pushes closure/major-delay hints by route corridor, but client periodically refreshes because push is lossy.

    stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> ON_ROUTE
  ON_ROUTE --> UNCERTAIN: low match confidence
  UNCERTAIN --> ON_ROUTE: recovered
  UNCERTAIN --> OFF_ROUTE: repeated divergence
  ON_ROUTE --> REROUTE: closure or material delay
  OFF_ROUTE --> REROUTE
  REROUTE --> ON_ROUTE: new route accepted
  ON_ROUTE --> ARRIVED

    Offline packs include tiles, compact graph, places, and signing metadata. Offline routing uses historical weights and cannot promise live closures; UI clearly marks freshness. Active route pins a compatible generation, while controlled migration remaps remaining edges or reroutes.

    10. Partitioning, scaling, consistency, and regions¶

    Tiles partition naturally by spatial cell/generation and CDN. Place search uses S2/H3 cells plus text index; dense cells subdivide, sparse cells combine. Routing graph shards use balanced geographic partitions, border overlays, and replicas; long routes query coarse overlays then detailed corridor partitions. Avoid latitude-longitude rectangles near poles and uneven cities.

    Traffic streams partition by coarse cell then edge; metropolitan hotspots split by edge hash. Aggregate locally before global fusion. Route APIs run active-active with locally replicated immutable graph generations. Map publication is manifest-atomic, closures are rapidly replicated/versioned, and traffic is eventual with age/confidence. Idempotency keys cover route creation if billed; probe sequence IDs deduplicate bounded windows.

    Under overload, protect active-navigation reroutes over browse alternatives, reduce K alternatives, use historical traffic, and shed low-priority probe processing. Deadline-aware routing returns a validated approximate route, never one violating hard constraints. If a graph partition is absent, route around only if correctness is provable; otherwise return degraded failure.

    11. Reliability and security¶

    Graph replica loss reroutes requests; old verified generations continue through control-plane outage only while a compatible closure overlay satisfies its freshness bound. Map rollback must retain current emergency restrictions, remapping them to the older graph or refusing affected routes. Traffic outage decays to historical estimates. Closure-feed conflicts choose safest effective restriction and alert operators. Bad map releases roll back manifest pointers; route IDs record generation for diagnosis. CDN loss uses another provider/origin shield with capacity controls. GPS loss keeps local dead reckoning briefly and announces degraded guidance.

    Location is highly sensitive: explicit consent, minimization, transport/storage encryption, rotating identifiers, short raw retention, aggregation thresholds, access audit, and deletion controls are required. Never expose individual traces to traffic consumers. Prevent route API scraping/DoS with keys, quotas, cost-based limits, and bounded waypoint counts. Secure edit provenance and use anomaly/human review against malicious roads/closures and place spam. Signed offline packages prevent graph tampering.

    12. Observability and testing¶

    Measure tile latency/hit/bytes, route p50-p99, expanded nodes, partition fanout, no-route/timeout/approximation rates, graph-generation skew, closure propagation, traffic age/confidence/coverage, map-match accuracy, ETA error percentiles, reroute frequency, navigation completion, offline freshness, and cost/route. Slice by geography, mode, app generation, and road class; global averages hide dangerous regions.

    Property-test geometry/projection and route constraint satisfaction. Golden routes cover turn bans, one-way roads, ferries, borders, time restrictions, and dateline/poles. Replay anonymized traces for map matching/ETA; simulate GPS noise and tunnels. Differential-test optimized paths against Dijkstra on sampled subgraphs. Chaos removes partitions, delays closures, publishes malformed candidates, and rolls generations. Canary map releases by region and automatically compare route/ETA regressions before expansion.

    Geocoding and place search deep dive¶

    Forward geocoding combines a language-aware text index with spatial priors. Normalize abbreviations without discarding original language, retrieve name/address candidates, then rank by textual match, distance or viewport, administrative containment, category, and quality. Reverse geocoding searches containing polygons and nearby address points from finest to coarsest geography. Every result carries stable place ID and version; coordinates alone are not identity because entrances and road access points differ from centroids.

    Place edits from businesses and users enter a provenance-weighted moderation pipeline. Conflicting sources do not overwrite canonical state directly. Merge candidates, retain evidence, detect duplicate places through spatial/name/category similarity, and support appeals. Popular queries can cache by language and coarse cell, but opening-hours and closures need shorter overlays. Pagination signs query, viewport, index generation, score, and place-ID tie-breaker.

    Route quality and safety controls¶

    Fastest-path objective is not simply summed observed seconds. Edge costs include expected time plus configurable penalties for unsafe turns, unpaved roads, difficult intersections, tolls, and uncertainty; hard vehicle dimensions and legal access remain constraints. The system must prevent tiny ETA gains from causing unstable reroutes, so reroute only when improvement exceeds an absolute and relative threshold after maneuver disruption cost.

    Route explanation records which constraints and major cost factors selected a path without exposing proprietary weights. Safety incidents trigger a versioned regional rule or closure, then retrospective replay finds affected routes. Launching a new weight model uses shadow computation on live requests, golden-route review, geographic canaries, and guardrails for illegal maneuvers, ETA error, route churn, and concentrated neighborhood traffic.

    13. Tradeoffs and final design¶

    Server routing has current global data but needs connectivity; on-device routing improves resilience/privacy but ships large stale graphs. Use both. Aggressive shortcut preprocessing improves latency but resists traffic updates; customizable hierarchy plus overlays balances them. Raw probes maximize model flexibility but create privacy risk; early aggregation and short retention win.

    Final design publishes compatible immutable map/tiles/graph generations, distributes vector tiles through CDN, runs hierarchical routing on geographic replicas, and overlays strongly versioned closures plus confidence-weighted live traffic. HMM map matching feeds privacy-thresholded aggregation; clients combine local navigation/offline graph with server reroutes.

    14. Interview questions and levels¶

    • Why is A* alone insufficient for continental routing?
    • How can shortcuts coexist with minute-level traffic?
    • How do you prevent parallel-road GPS jumps?
    • What happens to an active route during map-generation rollout?
    • Which telemetry can produce traffic without retaining journeys?

    L5 should cover tiles/CDN, graph model, shortest paths, traffic, GPS matching, partitioning, and failures. L6 should define generation compatibility, hard versus soft overlays, hierarchy customization, privacy budgets, regional release/rollback, metro skew, approximation bounds, multimodal evolution, and measurable route/ETA quality.

    15. Five-minute summary¶

    Separate immutable geographic generations from dynamic overlays. Validate edits, build compatible vector tiles, place indexes, routing graphs, and checksummed manifests, then atomically warm/switch replicas. CDN serves generation-keyed tiles. Routing snaps endpoints and uses A* plus contraction/multi-level shortcuts across geographic partitions, applies hard closures and soft traffic weights, validates constraints, diversifies alternatives, and emits maneuvers.

    Privacy-bounded GPS probes are HMM map-matched to edges, aggregated above contributor thresholds, fused with historical profiles, and decayed when stale. Active navigation handles jitter locally, rerouting only after confident divergence or meaningful closure/delay. Run active-active on replicated graphs, pin route generations, retain rollback, and degrade traffic to history rather than bypassing restrictions. Optimize not just latency but valid-route rate, ETA calibration, closure age, reroutes, geography slices, and location privacy.
