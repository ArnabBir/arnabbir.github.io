    Design a Price Tracking System¶

    Interview roadmap¶

    Design a service that watches products across merchants, records price history, and alerts users when conditions are met. Clarify acquisition methods, product matching, currency/tax semantics, freshness, alert guarantees, and merchant policy. Start with scheduled URL fetches and user alerts, then add adaptive crawling, canonical product identity, offer normalization, change detection, anti-abuse, regional compliance, and reconciliation.

    Product framing and requirements¶

    Users submit supported product URLs, view normalized price history, and create threshold or percentage-drop alerts. Merchant integrations may be official APIs, feeds, or permitted web fetches. Prices include amount, currency, availability, seller, shipping, tax basis, membership requirement, and variant. We track facts and provenance; we do not purchase items or promise merchant accuracy.

    Target p99 API reads below 200 ms, 99.9% watch creation, popular-offer freshness below five minutes, long-tail freshness below six hours, and 95% of eligible alerts emitted within two minutes of observing a qualifying price. Legal removals and merchant crawl rules override freshness. History can be eventual; an alert must be based on a durable observation.

    Correctness invariants¶

    • Every observation retains source, fetch time, parser version, currency, and price semantics.
    • Price comparisons occur only between compatible product variant and normalized total-price basis.
    • One observation causes at most one logical transition per alert version, despite redelivery.
    • Alert notification requires a committed qualifying observation and persisted trigger record.
    • A failed or suspicious parse never silently becomes a zero price.
    • Merchant robots, rate limits, authentication, and removal policy are enforced before fetch.
    • Canonical observations are immutable; corrections append superseding records.

    Capacity estimates¶

    Assume 100 million users, 500 million active watches, 200 million distinct offers, and an average refresh every two hours: 200M * 12 / 86,400 = 27,778 fetches/s average, perhaps 100,000/s peak. Popular URLs are shared, so watches do not multiply fetches. This schedule produces 2.4 billion fetches/day. If every fetch yields one stored 2 KB observation, storage is 4.8 TB/day before compression; one billion stored observations/day and 2 TB/day instead require an explicit 41.7% retention/success fraction. At ten meaningful changes per thousand observations, the full-observation model produces 24 million changes/day, before alert fanout. Raw pages are retained briefly and sampled except for parser investigations. Alert fanout may spike around major sales, so queues need campaign-like smoothing.

    Core flows and algorithms¶

    URL resolution and product matching¶

    Normalize URLs using merchant-specific rules: allowlisted hosts, canonical parameters, redirect limits, and stable merchant product IDs. Never remove arbitrary query parameters globally because variant or seller identity may be encoded there. Resolve the offer, then match it to a product using GTIN/UPC where available; otherwise use normalized brand/model/attributes plus category-specific similarity. Auto-link only above a high threshold and keep match evidence/version. Ambiguous offers remain separate or enter review.

    Product identity and offer identity are distinct. A laptop with different RAM is a different variant; several sellers can offer that variant. Product-level alerts define whether the cheapest eligible seller, shipping, condition, and membership prices count. This prevents a refurbished accessory from appearing as a dramatic price drop for a new device.

    Optional browser-extension acquisition plane¶

    An opt-in extension can broaden discovery where contracts permit it, but its reports are untrusted evidence, never canonical observations. Local code extracts an allowlisted merchant product identifier, normalized variant, displayed amount/currency, availability, coarse event time, extension/parser version, and a hash of the evidence fields. It does not upload page bodies, browsing history, unrelated URLs, account identifiers, cookies, or merchant session tokens. A clear per-site permission, local preview, deletion control, short report retention, and regional privacy policy are product requirements. Private, logged-in, cart-only, negotiated, or membership prices are excluded unless the product explicitly models that audience and obtains consent.

    The extension sends reports through a separately rate-limited collector. Rotating installation attestations and coarse source cohorts support abuse controls without becoming a durable cross-site identity. The collector strips network identifiers after short fraud retention, deduplicates (evidence_hash,time_bucket,source_cohort), and writes only the minimum evidence record. The normalizer cannot promote a report directly into Observation or trigger an alert.

    flowchart LR
    X[Opt-in browser extension] --> C[Privacy and rate-limit collector]
    C --> E[(Untrusted evidence store)]
    E --> D[Discovery candidates]
    E --> K[Corroboration engine]
    D --> R[Canonical URL resolver]
    K --> V[Verification scheduler]
    F[Official feed or permitted fetch] --> V
    V --> Q{Evidence verified?}
    Q -->|Yes| O[(Canonical observation)]
    Q -->|No| Z[Reject or expire]
    O --> A[Alert evaluation]

    Evidence follows an explicit state machine. REPORTED means syntactically valid but untrusted. CORROBORATING has matching reports from independent cohorts but still cannot alert. VERIFYING schedules an official API/feed lookup or permitted server fetch. Only that authoritative acquisition creates VERIFIED canonical observation provenance. Conflicts become REJECTED; unsupported or old evidence becomes EXPIRED. If server verification is impossible, corroborated reports may power a clearly labeled discovery hint, never price history, charts, or notifications.

    stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> REPORTED
    REPORTED --> CORROBORATING: plausible evidence
    REPORTED --> REJECTED: invalid or abusive
    CORROBORATING --> VERIFYING: independent threshold met
    CORROBORATING --> EXPIRED: evidence window closes

    VERIFIED --> [*]
    REJECTED --> [*]

    Sybil resistance uses risk-weighted independence, not a raw report count. Reports sharing installation attestation, account, network prefix, device signals, identical timing, or evidence hash count as one cohort; per-installation, network, merchant, and offer quotas bound floods. New cohorts earn little weight, repeated agreement with later authoritative fetches increases bounded reputation, and no cohort can verify itself. Never expose exact reporter counts that make threshold gaming easy.

    Extreme changes receive stricter treatment. A drop beyond both an absolute category bound and a relative threshold, such as 50 percent below the 30-day median, requires authoritative verification and a second fetch separated in time or an official feed. Cross-catalog simultaneous drops, parser-version changes, implausible decimal shifts, and prices below known shipping or tax floors quarantine the case. Reports can increase verification priority but cannot bypass these gates.

    Discovery and monitoring are separate budgets and promises. Extension evidence may propose a new merchant URL or raise an unwatched offer's discovery score; discovery is sampled, aggressively expired, and paused first under load. Monitoring begins only after URL resolution, policy approval, product/variant matching, and one authoritative observation. Watched offers then use the normal adaptive scheduler and freshness SLO. Thus a report storm can create bounded verification work, not 500 million new scheduled watches or false alerts.

    If 10 million installations send 20 sampled reports/day, the plane receives 200 million reports/day, about 2,300/s average and perhaps 12,000/s peak. At 500 bytes each that is 100 GB/day before replication. Deduplication, seven-day expiry for unverified evidence, and a per-merchant verification budget keep this optional signal materially smaller than canonical history. Sampling must happen on device or before durable write; collecting everything and deleting later is not equivalent privacy protection.

    Fetch scheduling and politeness¶

    The scheduler maintains one next-due task per offer, not per watch. Priority approximates expected user value:

    priority = watch_weight * change_probability * staleness_cost / expected_fetch_cost

    Change probability derives from historical volatility, sale calendar, feed hints, and recent HTTP validators. Bound intervals by merchant policy. Apply hierarchical token buckets for merchant, host, credential, and IP, plus randomized timing. Conditional requests use ETag/Last-Modified. 429 and Retry-After multiplicatively reduce concurrency; repeated blocks open a circuit. Official feeds supersede crawling when their semantics and freshness are sufficient.

    Parsing and validation¶

    Prefer structured merchant APIs or JSON-LD, then merchant-specific selectors; generic extraction is fallback only. Parsers run in a networkless sandbox with CPU, memory, and output limits. They emit typed candidates and evidence, not database writes. The validator checks currency, realistic category bounds, decimal scale, availability, variant, shipping, tax mode, and discontinuity against recent history.

    A huge price drop with parser-layout change, low confidence, or many simultaneous zero-like values enters quarantine. Compare a canary parser against production using stored permitted fixtures and a small live shadow sample. Parser versions deploy per merchant and roll back independently. Raw response hashes support deduplication; encrypted raw bodies have short retention and strict access.

    Change detection and alert evaluation¶

    Normalize money in integer minor units. Preserve original currency and never compare using today's exchange rate to historical values without labeling the converted series. Define total as item plus known mandatory shipping and tax when available; unknown components make totals incomparable under strict rules.

    Append every accepted observation, but emit a change only when normalized semantic fields differ or freshness requires it. Collapse repeated identical observations into run metadata for storage efficiency. Alert evaluator reads current alert version and applies threshold, percentage from a defined baseline, availability, seller, and cooldown. Percentage baseline may be previous stable price, rolling median, or user-created price; name it explicitly.

    stateDiagram-v2
 [*] --> ARMED
 ARMED --> QUALIFYING: rule true
 QUALIFYING --> TRIGGERED: trigger committed
 TRIGGERED --> COOLDOWN: notification enqueued
 COOLDOWN --> ARMED: price resets or cooldown expires
 ARMED --> PAUSED
 PAUSED --> ARMED
 ARMED --> DELETED
 COOLDOWN --> DELETED

    Trigger insertion has unique (alert_id, alert_version, observation_id, transition_type). The transaction checks the current alert version, advances its armed/cooldown state with a conditional update, and writes trigger and outbox together. Serialize evaluation per alert and retain its last applied source position so delayed observations cannot roll the state backward. Per-observation uniqueness alone would still allow two different qualifying observations to race into the same cooldown window. Queue redelivery therefore cannot create another logical notification, though downstream providers may still duplicate. A hysteresis band prevents repeated alerts around the threshold. A user can choose one-shot or re-arm behavior.

    Deep dives¶

    Popular offers and fetch deduplication¶

    Millions may watch one console. All watches reference one offer and observation stream. A coalescing cache allows one active fetch lease per URL hash; concurrent demand attaches waiters rather than causing a stampede. Sale events prewarm known popular offers but merchant quotas remain absolute. Freshness SLO is tiered by watch count and volatility so scarce requests maximize value fairly rather than favoring only the largest merchant.

    Price history compaction¶

    Canonical immutable observations support audit, while a derived time-series projection stores change points and daily min/max/last. Recent detailed observations remain hot; older raw repeats compact into intervals. Queries merge tiers using stable cursors. Compaction records source ranges and checksum, and deletion occurs only after validation. Corrections append supersession links and rebuild projections instead of rewriting facts.

    Chart serving chooses a resolution from requested range and pixel width rather than returning every observation. Keep raw change points for 48 hours, hourly open/high/low/close/availability buckets for 90 days, and daily buckets thereafter as an example policy. GET /history?from=&to=&resolution=auto&max_points=800 selects the finest tier producing at most 800 buckets; callers may request raw, 1h, or 1d within retention. A 365-day chart therefore returns at most 365 daily buckets instead of scanning millions of repeated fetches.

    Buckets carry source count, first/last observation IDs, confidence floor, currency/tax basis, and completeness. The API never averages prices across variants, currencies, or incompatible total-price semantics. It returns min and max for the chart envelope and last for the line, labels gaps and stale intervals, and overlays superseding corrections by projection version. Cache keys include offer, range boundary, resolution, and projection version; stable cursors are needed for raw audit pages, while bounded chart windows return one immutable projection response.

    Feed and crawl reconciliation¶

    Feeds can be delayed, duplicated, or use different tax semantics. Assign source precedence per field and merchant contract; do not blindly let the newest timestamp win. Periodic sampled crawl compares feed values and raises drift alerts. Event IDs or content hashes deduplicate feed replay. Watermarks expose lag. If sources conflict beyond tolerance, lower confidence, pause alerts for the affected field, and retain both facts for operators.

    Partitioning, scaling, and regions¶

    Partition offers and latest observation by offer ID; partition append-only history by offer and month. Shard watches/alerts by user for management APIs, while an inverted index maps offer/product to alert partitions. Change events include offer ID and observation ID; evaluator fanout batches IDs and reads only potentially relevant rule buckets, such as currency plus threshold ranges, instead of all watches.

    Merchant queues are isolated because one host's outage or legal pause must not consume global workers. Hot merchants receive dedicated partitions and credentials. Scheduler time wheels bucket due tasks, lease buckets with epochs, and checkpoint scans. Duplicate scheduling is safe; unique observation hashes and fetch leases reduce waste. Backpressure lengthens low-value intervals, pauses unwatched discovery, then delays low-priority alerts while preserving observation durability.

    Fetches occur from permitted regions and IP ranges. User and alert data remain in a home region; public offer observations can replicate globally. Regional evaluators consume local copies and write triggers in the user's home region. The same observation may be evaluated in many regions, but alert-version uniqueness is authoritative. Merchant policy and parser configurations are signed control-plane artifacts with staged rollout.

    Consistency, failures, and recovery¶

    Watches, alert versions, and triggers require transactional consistency. Catalog matching, history projections, exchange-rate views, and search are eventual. Observation commit plus event publication uses outbox. Fetching is at least once. Lease epochs fence slow schedulers. Notification effects are idempotent by trigger ID but not claimed exactly once.

    Merchant outage serves stale data labeled with observation time and backs off. Scheduler loss rebuilds due tasks from canonical offers and checkpoints. Parser regression quarantines data and activates last good parser. History store failure buffers only bounded validated results in a durable log, then reduces fetch rate. Extension collector or corroboration failure drops or expires optional evidence and never blocks established monitoring; verification backlog is capped per merchant so it cannot starve watched-offer fetches. A chart projection failure falls back to a coarser verified tier or returns an explicit unavailable response rather than inventing buckets. Event lag delays alerts but cannot alert without committed observations. Notification outage retains trigger outbox. Region loss replays from replicated offsets; promote a writer only after fencing the old owner and recovering the acknowledged alert-state, trigger, and outbox prefix. Uniqueness collapses reevaluation only while that deduplication state survives. If recovery loses acknowledged triggers, pause effects and reconcile downstream trigger IDs before replay.

    Reconciliation scans offers whose latest observation is older than policy, trigger rows lacking outbox delivery, alerts whose qualifying changes were skipped, feed sequence gaps, and evidence cases stuck in verification. Replay requires merchant/time/parser scope and dry-run counts. User deletion removes watches, alerts, notification addresses, extension installation state, and attributable evidence; public canonical observations remain because they were independently verified merchant facts, subject to source policy.

    Security, abuse, observability, and testing¶

    Fetch only allowlisted public merchant destinations to prevent SSRF; resolve DNS safely, block private ranges after redirects, restrict methods, and sandbox content. Encrypt user data, authenticate APIs, sign feed webhooks, isolate merchant credentials, and audit parser/config changes. Prevent watch spam, competitor scraping through the read API, alert bombing, affiliate manipulation, and malicious pages. Honor robots, contracts, deletion, copyright, and geographic restrictions.

    Measure freshness by watch tier and merchant, fetch success/status/latency, quota utilization, queue age, conditional-hit rate, bytes, parser confidence and field missingness, quarantine rate, price discontinuities, observation-to-trigger latency, duplicate suppression, alert eligibility and delivery, feed watermark, catalog-match uncertainty, and cost per active watch. For extension evidence, measure consented active installations, reports accepted/dropped, cohort concentration, discovery yield, authoritative agreement, verification queue age/budget, extreme-change quarantine, deletion completion, and reports per verified offer without logging browsing detail. A sudden cross-catalog price collapse is a parser incident signal.

    Property-test money comparison, threshold boundaries, hysteresis, alert-version fencing, URL normalization idempotence, and immutable observations. Golden merchant fixtures test parsers. Contract-test feeds. Fuzz HTML, redirects, compression bombs, and malformed currency. Forge many reports from one cohort, rotate apparent identities, coordinate extreme-price claims, replay evidence after expiry, and assert no unverified report reaches history or alerts. Privacy tests verify field allowlists, on-device sampling, consent revocation, and deletion. Compare raw, hourly, and daily chart buckets at tier boundaries and after corrections, including gaps and incompatible price semantics. Replay historical pages against parser releases. Load-test popular-product stampedes, evidence floods within verification budgets, and sale-wide alert fanout. Chaos-test queue duplication, scheduler clock skew, merchant throttling, history lag, and region failover. Synthetic known pages verify end to end.

    Tradeoffs and final architecture¶

    Fetching per watch is simple but wasteful; shared offer observations win. Fixed refresh intervals are predictable but ignore volatility and value; adaptive scheduling improves freshness per dollar but needs fairness bounds. Generic parsers expand coverage but create false prices; merchant adapters and quarantine favor correctness. Extension reports expand discovery cheaply but add consent, privacy, and Sybil risk, so they remain expiring evidence and never alert without independent verification. Mutable latest-price rows are cheap but unauditable; immutable facts plus multi-resolution projections support alerts, efficient charts, and repair. Product-level matching improves comparison but false merges are dangerous, so uncertain identity remains separate.

    The final design shares acquisition by canonical offer, enforces merchant limits before network work, validates typed parser output, and stores immutable provenance. A durable change stream evaluates versioned alerts into unique triggers. History, latest price, and catalog search remain rebuildable projections. Isolated merchant queues, adaptive schedules, quarantine, and reconciliation make the system operable.

    Interview deep-dive questions¶

    1. How do 10 million users watching one product affect fetch load?
    2. What exactly is a comparable price when shipping or tax is unknown?
    3. How do you prevent a parser bug from sending zero-price alerts?
    4. How are repeated threshold crossings deduplicated and re-armed?
    5. How does adaptive scheduling remain fair to long-tail products?
    6. How are feed and crawled observations reconciled?
    7. What does user deletion remove versus preserve?

    L5 and L6 expectations¶

    An L5 candidate should distinguish watch, offer, observation, and alert; estimate fetch/storage load; deduplicate popular URL work; explain scheduling, parsing, price comparison, trigger idempotency, partitioning, and main failures.

    An L6 candidate should define price semantics and matching uncertainty, merchant governance, quarantine and parser rollout, adaptive cost/fairness policy, replay/reconciliation, regional data ownership, and overload stages. They should connect freshness tiers and raw-page retention to cost, contracts, privacy, and incident response.

    Five-minute summary¶

    Resolve each submitted URL into a canonical merchant offer and, only with strong evidence, a product variant. Many watches share one observation stream. An adaptive scheduler prioritizes watched, volatile offers but obeys hierarchical merchant quotas and backs off on throttling. Prefer feeds/APIs; sandbox versioned parsers for permitted pages and validate currency, variant, availability, shipping, tax, confidence, and discontinuities. Store immutable observations with provenance, then publish changes through an outbox. Evaluate current versioned alert rules into uniquely keyed triggers and notify idempotently, using cooldown and hysteresis. Partition offers/history by offer, alerts by user, and acquisition by merchant; isolate hot merchants and coalesce popular fetches. Quarantine suspicious parses, label stale data, reconcile feed/crawl drift and missing triggers, and measure freshness, quota, confidence, quarantine, trigger latency, and cost per watch.
