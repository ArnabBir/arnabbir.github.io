    Design a Hotel Booking Platform¶

    Interview Roadmap¶

    Clarify whether inventory represents exact rooms or room-type capacity, and whether the platform is merchant-of-record. This design searches hotels, prices date ranges, temporarily holds room-type inventory, confirms payment, and manages cancellation. State the per-night no-oversell invariant, estimate search-to-book skew, show a single SQL database first, then discuss date partitions, channel-manager races, and regional ownership.

    Product Framing¶

    Travelers search by destination and dates, compare properties, select a room and rate plan, and book. Hotels distribute the same rooms through direct and external channels, so inventory updates can be delayed. Search favors availability and speed; final booking must revalidate price, policy, and every night.

    Assume one property and room type per reservation, though multiple rooms are allowed. Flights, loyalty accounting, reviews, and hotel property-management software are out of scope.

    Requirements¶

    • Search properties by destination, date range, guests, amenities, and price.
    • Return room offers with taxes, cancellation policy, and expiry.
    • Hold all requested room-nights, pay or guarantee, and confirm a reservation.
    • Modify or cancel according to policy; synchronize hotel/channel inventory.
    • Never exceed configured sellable capacity for any property, room type, and night.
    • Search p99 under 800 ms; booking p99 under three seconds excluding customer challenge.

    Correctness Invariants¶

    1. For every inventory key, confirmed plus active held plus non-overlapping external-reserved quantity never exceeds sellable capacity plus an explicit overbooking allowance. External reservations already represented in confirmed counts must not be counted twice.
    2. An accepted complete hold or confirmed reservation owns all required nights and rooms. Any explicitly modeled chunked preparation is incomplete, cannot be sold as a complete hold, and has durable cleanup for partial allocations.
    3. One guest-scoped idempotency key creates at most one reservation and intended charge.
    4. Confirmed price, taxes, currency, occupancy, and cancellation terms are immutable snapshots.
    5. Cancellation releases only inventory owned by that reservation and cannot refund beyond paid amount.
    6. Search and availability indexes are projections; booking inventory is canonical.

    Capacity Estimates¶

    Assume 5 million properties, 100 room types each, two years of sellable nights, 100 million searches/day, and 2 million bookings/day. Search averages 1,150 QPS and peaks around 15,000 QPS; booking peaks near 1,000 QPS. Materializing every room-type-night produces 365 billion rows, too many for sparse properties, so generate a rolling horizon only for active inventory and compress default availability ranges. A reservation averages four nights and roughly 5 KB including snapshots, about 10 GB/day.

    Search fan-out and destination skew dominate. A holiday query for Paris touches thousands of properties, while booking contention concentrates on one room type and night.

    Entities and Data Model¶

    Property(property_id, owner_id, region_id, timezone, status, attributes)

      nightly_prices, taxes, policy_snapshot, total, expires_at)

    NightInventory has primary key (property_id, room_type_id, local_date). Hotel-local dates are explicit; UTC conversion must not shift nights around daylight-saving changes. Mutation source references are unique to deduplicate channel updates.

    Initially inventory and reservations share one SQL database, allowing room-night hold and reservation creation in one transaction. Separation comes later if ownership or throughput requires a saga.

    Requirement Flows¶

    Search and Offer¶

    Search queries a geospatial/text property index and a coarse availability projection such as minimum capacity over date buckets. It ranks candidates, then fans out in bounded batches to offer service for accurate prices on the top set. Slow properties are omitted or marked unavailable rather than delaying the page. The response carries as_of; final offer creation reads newer inventory and pricing.

    Offer service evaluates occupancy, nightly rate rules, promotions, taxes, fees, and cancellation policy in property local time. It returns a snapshot but does not hold inventory. Caching keys include property, room type, dates, occupancy, currency, and rule versions to avoid price leakage between contexts.

    Book a Stay¶

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
    participant T as Traveler
    participant B as Booking
    participant D as SQL
    participant P as Payment
    T->>B: Reserve(offer, rooms, key)
    B->>D: Lock each night in date order
    B->>D: Check capacity and create hold and reservation
    B->>D: COMMIT hold, reservation, idempotency and outbox then release locks
    B->>P: Authorize/reserve payment(order ID)
    alt payment succeeds
        B->>D: Convert held to confirmed plus outbox
        B-->>T: CONFIRMED
    else decline
        B->>D: Release hold and mark failed
        B-->>T: payment failed
    else timeout
        B->>D: Mark PAYMENT_UNKNOWN
        B-->>T: 202 processing
    end

    Lock nights in ascending date order to avoid deadlocks. Check confirmed + held + external_reserved + rooms <= sellable + allowance for every night. One transaction holds all nights or none. Payment ambiguity is reconciled using the same provider operation; expiration is logically enforced even if the sweeper is delayed.

    Cancel or Modify¶

    Cancellation reads the stored policy snapshot and current time in property timezone, computes penalty, conditionally changes state, releases confirmed inventory, and starts refund or collection. A modification is safer as an atomic inventory exchange: hold new dates, calculate difference, adjust payment, then release old dates. If cross-partition atomicity is unavailable, create the new reservation first and expose MODIFICATION_PENDING until compensation completes.

    Deep Dive 1: Date-Range Inventory¶

    A booking touches every night in [check_in, check_out). Per-night rows make the invariant explicit and support variable capacity, stop-sell flags, and maintenance. For long stays, transaction size grows; cap stay length or reserve in chunks under one coordinator while withholding confirmation until all succeed. A range representation saves rows but makes overlapping updates and constraints much harder.

    Availability projection can store the minimum remaining rooms for common windows or use per-night bitmaps/counts. It may produce false positives that booking rejects, but should avoid false negatives where practical. Search index updates coalesce many inventory mutations to prevent a popular property from flooding the stream.

    [*] --> PENDING_PAYMENT
    PENDING_PAYMENT --> CONFIRMED: payment verified and all holds still owned
    PENDING_PAYMENT --> PAYMENT_UNKNOWN

    Deep Dive 2: External Channels¶

    Hotels may sell through several OTAs and send delayed absolute counts or deltas. Assign each source event a unique ID and version. Prefer reservations and allocations over blind count replacement. A hotel can allocate bounded inventory pools per channel, reducing oversell risk at the cost of stranded capacity. A central channel manager can rebalance pools and publish stop-sell when remaining capacity is low.

    When external truth conflicts, never silently delete a confirmed guest. Flag overbooking, stop further sales, and invoke property operations to relocate or honor based on policy. Reconciliation compares platform reservations, hotel acknowledgments, and channel reports per room-night.

    Deep Dive 3: Pricing and Policy Snapshots¶

    Rates vary by night, occupancy, refundable status, meal plan, taxes, and promotions. Use integer minor units, explicit tax inclusion, and deterministic rounding. The offer records every nightly component and rule version. At booking, verify expiry and optionally tolerate only customer-favorable changes. A later policy edit cannot alter an existing reservation's cancellation charge.

    For hotel-collected payment, the platform stores a guarantee token and commission claim rather than pretending capture occurred. Data and state names must distinguish platform-paid, hotel-paid, and pay-at-property flows.

    Deep Dive 4: Hold Expiry and Overbooking¶

    Logical expiry lets a new transaction reclaim a hold at expires_at <= database_time by atomically changing its status and releasing its held counters before reusing that capacity. Sweepers release rows in batches and emit projection updates. Payment challenges may extend holds up to a strict cap. Attackers who repeatedly hold rooms face per-account, device, and payment-instrument limits.

    Confirmation must conditionally consume the still-owned hold for every night under the same locks as expiry. If expiry wins, retain the external payment as unknown until resolved; late success triggers void/refund rather than confirming a stay against reallocated rooms. A modification reserves only the incremental per-night quantity beyond its existing allocation, so unchanged nights do not require double capacity. Never hold the room-night SQL transaction open during the payment call.

    Overbooking is an explicit property-controlled allowance derived from expected cancellation/no-show rates, never an accidental race. Store the allowance and policy version per night, cap it operationally, and display exposure to hotels.

    Partitioning and Scaling¶

    Partition inventory by property ID so all nights and room types for one property remain local. Partition reservations by property when property operations dominate, or by reservation ID with a lookup if guest history dominates. A giant chain is not one property key. Search indexes partition by geography and replicate popular destinations.

    For destination bursts, cache property documents and fan out only to ranked candidates with a strict budget. Inventory shard backpressure rejects expensive broad offer requests before booking writes. Repartition properties between shards by snapshot, change log, verification, and a routing epoch; avoid moving during peak booking windows.

    Consistency, Idempotency, and Failures¶

    Room-night counters, holds, reservation state, and policy snapshots are strongly consistent. Search, price sorting, notifications, and hotel dashboards are eventual. Idempotency records include request hash. Payment, inventory, channel, and refund commands use stable business references. Outbox events are delivered at least once and projections apply only newer aggregate versions.

    Database failure rejects new bookings but cached search continues with an availability warning. Search outage falls back to curated destinations or no results without affecting reservations. Payment timeout creates processing state. Channel outage queues bounded updates and lowers or stops channel allocation before stale inventory becomes unsafe. Reconciliation finds paid-unconfirmed reservations, expired holds, counter mismatches, and unacknowledged hotel bookings.

    Multi-Region Strategy¶

    Assign each property a home region close to its inventory owner and legal requirements. Global search and offer reads use replicated projections; booking routes to the property home. Synchronous multi-zone writes provide normal resilience. Disaster recovery uses asynchronous replication and requires fencing the old region and advancing a property routing epoch before sale resumes. The promoted inventory must also include the required acknowledged reservation and mutation prefix; an epoch cannot recover bookings lost under asynchronous RPO. Reconcile or recover missing evidence before reallocating potentially sold nights. If fencing is uncertain, pause that property's booking rather than risk oversell. Control-plane policy can be global, but signed last-known versions let regional booking continue safely.

    Security and Abuse Prevention¶

    Tokenize payment cards and restrict guarantee-token access. Encrypt guest identity, addresses, and stay details; those records create physical-safety risk. Authenticate hotels and sign channel callbacks. Rate limit scraping, credential stuffing, fake reservations, hold hoarding, promotion abuse, and card testing. Property content requires malware and fraud checks. Support access is purpose-bound and audited; privacy deletion pseudonymizes guest profiles while retaining required reservation and tax evidence.

    Observability and Testing¶

    Track search freshness, offer fan-out and timeout, price-change rate, booking conflict, lock wait, hold age, payment unknowns, channel lag, room-night reconciliation variance, overbooking exposure, and reservation acknowledgment. Slice by destination, property, room type, arrival date, channel, and shard. Alert on value and guest impact, especially arrivals soon.

    Property-test date boundaries, capacity conservation, modifications, time zones, and cancellation calculations. Concurrency-test overlapping stays and lock ordering. Inject duplicate channel events, payment callbacks, stale projection, delayed sweeper, DST transitions, shard failover, and region fencing. Replay pricing changes in shadow mode and restore inventory from reservations plus mutation history.

    Important Tradeoffs¶

    Per-night rows consume storage but make overlap correctness tractable. Channel pools prevent oversell while stranding inventory. Global search improves discovery but final revalidation causes some disappointment. Property-home writes add cross-region latency but avoid global room-night consensus. Holds improve checkout completion but reduce availability and invite hoarding. Overbooking improves utilization only when explicitly governed.

    Final Architecture¶

    flowchart TD
    Edge[Global search edge] --> Index[(Geo property and availability projections)]
    Edge --> Home[Property home region]
    Home --> Offer[Versioned offer service]
    Home --> Book[Reservation state machine]
    Book --> SQL[(Property-owned inventory and reservations)]
    Book --> Pay[Payment adapter]
    SQL --> Events[(Outbox and stream)]
    Events --> Index
    Events --> Notify[Guest and hotel notifications]
    Channels[Channel and hotel systems] --> Sync[Versioned channel sync]
    Sync --> SQL
    Recon[Room-night reconciliation] --> SQL
    SQL -. fenced DR .-> DR[(Recovery region)]

    Interview Deep-Dive Questions¶

    1. How do you atomically reserve a fourteen-night stay?
    2. What happens when a channel update conflicts with a confirmed reservation?
    3. Should inventory partition by property, room type, or date?
    4. How do property-local dates affect storage and expiry?
    5. How would multi-room or multi-property itineraries change the saga?
    6. Which overbooking policy belongs in product versus infrastructure?

    L5 and L6 Expectations¶

    An L5 answer models per-night capacity, atomic range holds, immutable offers, idempotent payment, search staleness, and common failures. An L6 answer also handles channel disagreement, explicit overbooking policy, property routing and migration, modification sagas, legal/payment variants, reconciliation operations, and the cost and guest-experience effects of stale global discovery.

    Five-Minute Summary¶

    Hotel booking is date-range inventory allocation. Keep canonical property + room type + local date counters in SQL and atomically lock all requested nights in date order. Search and coarse availability are scalable projections and may be stale; a signed expiring offer snapshots nightly prices, taxes, occupancy, and cancellation policy, and booking revalidates it. Create a bounded hold, invoke payment idempotently, then convert every held night to confirmed. Expiry is logical, with sweepers only cleaning up. Partition by property and assign each property one fenced home region. External channels use deduplicated versioned updates or bounded allocations; disagreements trigger stop-sell and reconciliation, not deletion of confirmed guests. Monitor upcoming-arrival exposure, test overlapping stays and timezone edges, and treat overbooking as explicit governed product policy.
