    Design an E-commerce Checkout System¶

    Interview Roadmap¶

    Clarify whether checkout owns cart, inventory, pricing, payment, and order creation. Define the invariants, estimate peak order rate, then present the smallest correct saga: one order record coordinates reservations and side effects. Trace happy path and retries before adding queues, regional cells, or workflow engines. Reserve time for price snapshots, inventory races, payment ambiguity, and reconciliation.

    Product Framing¶

    Checkout converts a mutable cart into a durable order. It validates products and addresses, freezes the commercial terms, reserves stock, authorizes or captures payment, and initiates fulfillment. The system must explain partial failure to customers without double charging or silently overselling.

    Assume physical goods, one currency per order, multiple warehouses, cards and wallets, and guest or registered shoppers. Catalog browsing, recommendation, warehouse picking, and returns are adjacent but out of scope.

    Requirements¶

    Functional requirements

    • Preview totals, shipping options, taxes, promotions, and stock.
    • Submit checkout once and retrieve its status after disconnects.
    • Reserve inventory, pay, create an order, and start fulfillment.
    • Cancel safely before fulfillment and release inventory or refund payment.

    Non-functional requirements

    • Never create two orders or charges for one submitted checkout key.
    • A confirmed order has immutable line, price, tax, and address snapshots.
    • Target 99.95% submit availability and p99 under three seconds excluding step-up authentication.
    • Isolate slow providers, apply backpressure, and recover every intermediate state.

    Correctness Invariants¶

    1. One shopper-scoped idempotency key maps to one checkout and request hash.
    2. An order is confirmed only after required stock is reserved and payment policy succeeds.
    3. Reserved plus available inventory never exceeds physical sellable stock for a SKU-location.
    4. The amount sent to payment equals the server-generated order total and currency.
    5. Every intended external effect has a durable identity before dispatch; unknown outcomes remain discoverable and are reconciled. Failed orders retain owned compensation work until resolved or explicitly escalated, since compensation is not guaranteed to succeed immediately or be fully reversible.
    6. Cart, quote caches, and event projections are not canonical order state.

    Capacity Estimates¶

    Assume 100 million monthly active shoppers, 20 million orders per day, an average of three lines, and a ten-times peak. Orders average 230 QPS and peak near 2,500 QPS; preview traffic may peak at 25,000 QPS. At about 5 KB for order snapshots and workflow history, a year is roughly 36 TB before indexes and replicas. Events add similar magnitude. Payment and tax calls dominate latency; the write volume fits sharded relational storage comfortably.

    Popular SKUs create contention far above average. If one promotion drives 5,000 reservation attempts per second to one SKU-location, that key needs atomic counters, admission limits, or preallocated inventory buckets rather than indiscriminate database scaling.

    Core Entities and Data Model¶

    Checkout(checkout_id, shopper_id, cart_version, request_hash, status,
         idempotency_key, quote_id, order_id, expires_at, version)
Quote(quote_id, currency, subtotal, discounts, tax, shipping, total, expires_at)
QuoteLine(quote_id, sku_id, quantity, unit_price, adjustments_json)
Order(order_id, shopper_id, status, amount, currency, address_snapshot,
      quote_snapshot, created_at, version)
OrderLine(order_id, sku_id, seller_id, quantity, fulfillment_status)
InventoryReservation(reservation_id, order_id, sku_id, location_id,
                     quantity, status, expires_at)
PaymentAttempt(attempt_id, order_id, provider, provider_ref, amount, status)
WorkflowStep(order_id, step, status, attempt, last_error)
Outbox(event_id, aggregate_id, type, payload, published_at)

    Unique constraints cover (shopper_id, idempotency_key), provider references, and one active reservation per order-line allocation. The order stores snapshots rather than foreign keys to mutable prices or addresses. A relational database is the initial source of truth; inventory may be a separate service with its own transactional store.

    APIs¶

    Preview returns a signed, expiring quote and stock warnings, not a reservation. Submit returns 201 CONFIRMED, 202 PROCESSING with a polling URL, or a stable business error. Reusing a key with a different body returns 422. Authentication challenges carry a resumable checkout token rather than requiring a fresh order.

    High-Level Architecture¶

    flowchart LR
    Client --> Gateway[API gateway]
    Gateway --> Checkout[Checkout orchestrator]
    Checkout --> Orders[(Order SQL)]
    Checkout --> Pricing[Pricing and promotion]
    Checkout --> Tax[Tax provider]
    Checkout --> Inventory[Inventory service]
    Checkout --> Payment[Payment service]
    Orders --> Relay[Outbox relay]
    Relay --> Bus[(Event stream)]
    Bus --> Fulfillment[Fulfillment]
    Bus --> Notify[Notifications]
    Bus --> Analytics[Projections]
    Recon[Reconciler] --> Orders
    Recon --> Payment
    Recon --> Inventory

    Start with the orchestrator and order tables in one database. Direct synchronous calls are acceptable at modest scale if every intent is persisted and bounded. Move long-running retries to durable workers when provider latency or operational complexity justifies it.

    Requirement Flows¶

    Preview Checkout¶

    The service reads a specific cart version, batches catalog and pricing lookups, computes promotions, requests tax and shipping estimates, and returns a quote with a short expiry. The quote captures inputs and computed totals. Cache product and tax metadata, but reject checkout if the signed quote expired or regulated price changed. Preview can degrade by hiding unavailable shipping options; it must not invent totals.

    Submit Checkout¶

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
    participant C as Client
    participant O as Orchestrator
    participant D as Order SQL
    participant I as Inventory
    participant P as Payment
    C->>O: Submit(quote, key)
    O->>D: Create CHECKOUT_STARTED once
    O->>I: Reserve(order, lines)
    I-->>O: reservation IDs
    O->>P: Authorize(order ID, amount)
    alt authorized
        O->>I: Idempotently confirm still-owned reservations against expiry
        O->>D: Confirm order plus outbox
        O-->>C: CONFIRMED
    else declined
        O->>I: Release reservations
        O->>D: Mark PAYMENT_FAILED
        O-->>C: stable decline
    else timeout
        O->>D: Mark PAYMENT_UNKNOWN
        O-->>C: 202 PROCESSING
    end

    The orchestrator re-enters from durable state after crashes. Each step checks whether its effect already completed before retrying. Authorize-first is an alternative when stock is abundant; reserving first avoids charging for unavailable items. Capture can occur at confirmation or fulfillment depending on payment rules and shipping duration.

    Before order confirmation, inventory must turn each live reservation into a non-expiring fulfillment allocation or an explicitly renewed guarantee under its own authority. A remembered reservation ID is not proof that stock survived a long payment challenge. If expiry already won, void/refund the resolved payment and fail the checkout. Persist progress for each inventory confirmation so a crash between that step and order commit resumes or compensates rather than releasing a confirmed order's stock.

    Cancel and Fulfill¶

    Cancellation conditionally transitions the order only if fulfillment has not crossed its cutoff. It then voids authorization or refunds capture and releases unconsumed reservations. These effects may finish asynchronously, so the customer sees CANCELLATION_PENDING. Fulfillment consumes reservations idempotently by order-line ID and emits shipment events. Split shipments do not mutate the original commercial snapshot.

    Deep Dive 1: Saga Boundaries¶

    Two-phase commit across inventory, payment, and fulfillment is unavailable and undesirable. The order database stores the saga state and next action. A worker leases due workflows, invokes one dependency with a stable operation ID, records the response, and advances state. Lease expiry permits another worker to continue. Compensation is business logic, not rollback: release stock, void or refund money, and explain any delay.

    Ordering matters. Reserve then authorize minimizes refunds but temporarily locks inventory while payment challenges run. Authorize then reserve improves stock utilization but creates voids and customer confusion. A practical flow reserves for a short period, authorizes, confirms, and releases on decline. For scarce items, serialize or limit admissions upstream.

    Deep Dive 2: Inventory Reservation¶

    Inventory owns (sku_id, location_id) counters: on_hand, reserved, safety_stock, and version. A transaction checks on_hand - reserved - safety_stock >= quantity, increments reserved, and inserts reservation rows. Reservation expiry is logically evaluated during allocation; a sweeper performs cleanup. Warehouse selection considers stock, delivery promise, split cost, and capacity, but persist the chosen allocation so retries do not rerun a changing optimizer.

    For hot SKUs, partitioning by SKU cannot divide one key. Preallocate bounded stock tokens to regional or channel buckets. Each bucket sells only its allocation; a rebalancer moves unused capacity conservatively. This may show sold out while global stock remains, but it prevents oversell without global consensus on every purchase.

    Deep Dive 3: Payment Ambiguity¶

    The dangerous result is timeout, not decline. The provider may have authorized the card while the caller lost the response. Never issue a second provider operation with a new key. Mark the attempt UNKNOWN, query by merchant order ID, consume signed webhooks idempotently, and reconcile until terminal. Provider-reference uniqueness deduplicates one provider resource; it does not stop two different references from charging the same order. A conditional order-level payment winner and stable provider-operation identity prevent local double acceptance, while reconciliation detects any extra external charge. If duplicate captures occur despite controls, automatically refund the extra and alert.

    Step-up authentication pauses the saga. Store a short-lived continuation linked to the existing attempt. The browser callback cannot assert payment success; the backend verifies provider state before advancing.

    Deep Dive 4: Price and Promotion Correctness¶

    Catalog price is mutable, but an order total is immutable. The quote includes line prices, promotion rule versions, tax basis, currency, and expiry. At submit, verify its signature, ownership, cart version, and policy freshness. Server-side arithmetic uses integer minor units and explicit rounding by jurisdiction. Promotion usage is reserved transactionally when globally limited; otherwise eventual counters can exceed a campaign cap. The product owner must choose which guarantee applies.

    Partitioning and Scaling¶

    Partition orders by shopper_id or order_id; choose shopper if order history is dominant and mitigate celebrity-account hot keys. Keep workflow and payment attempts colocated with order. Inventory partitions by SKU hash and optionally location. Queries by provider reference use a global lookup index containing only the owning shard. Analytics consumes events rather than scanning transactional shards.

    Apply bounded concurrency separately to tax, inventory, and payment. Shed previews before submits, serve cached catalog data within freshness policy, and stop admitting new checkouts if durable workflow backlog breaches the reservation window. Autoscaling cannot rescue an exhausted database or provider quota; load shedding must occur at the gateway.

    Consistency and Idempotency¶

    Order transitions, quote acceptance, and each inventory counter update are strongly consistent within their owners. Search, shipment views, email, and analytics are eventual. The checkout key is stored before external calls. Every downstream command uses (order_id, operation, logical_step_id) as a stable key. Repeated refunds, split allocations, and shipments have distinct durable step identities; retries preserve the identity of the original step. Outbox events are atomic with order changes and delivered at least once; consumers keep inbox records or make naturally idempotent upserts.

    Optimistic versions reject competing cancellation and fulfillment transitions. Reconciliation joins canonical local intent with provider APIs and inventory reservations. It detects paid orders not confirmed, confirmed orders lacking reservations, expired workflows, and orphaned reservations. Operators repair by replaying named transitions, never editing totals ad hoc.

    Reliability and Failure Modes¶

    stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> STARTED
    STARTED --> STOCK_RESERVED
    STOCK_RESERVED --> PAYMENT_PENDING
    PAYMENT_PENDING --> ALLOCATION_CONFIRMING: authorized
    PAYMENT_PENDING --> PAYMENT_UNKNOWN: timeout
    PAYMENT_UNKNOWN --> ALLOCATION_CONFIRMING: reconciled success
    ALLOCATION_CONFIRMING --> CONFIRMED: all inventory allocations durable
    ALLOCATION_CONFIRMING --> COMPENSATING: stock no longer owned
    COMPENSATING --> FAILED: release and verified void/refund
    PAYMENT_PENDING --> FAILED: declined
    PAYMENT_UNKNOWN --> FAILED: reconciled failure
    STOCK_RESERVED --> FAILED: reservation expires
    CONFIRMED --> CANCEL_PENDING: cancellation accepted
    CANCEL_PENDING --> CANCELLED: release and void/refund

    An orchestrator crash is harmless because state precedes effects. Database unavailability rejects new submits rather than accepting memory-only orders. Inventory timeout leaves the step retryable. Payment outage holds a bounded workflow queue and reservation lifetime; beyond policy, cancel safely. Event-stream outage accumulates outbox rows while confirmed orders remain correct. Poison events move to a quarantine queue with alerts and replay tooling.

    Multi-Region Strategy¶

    Deploy regional checkout cells near shoppers, but assign an order to one home cell. Its database is synchronously replicated across zones and asynchronously to a disaster-recovery region. Inventory may be globally allocated into regional buckets; payment routing follows regulatory and provider constraints. Failover fences the prior writer with a database epoch before accepting workflow commands. In-flight orders resume only from a verified recovery prefix that preserves operation identities and reservations. If asynchronous replication lost acknowledged steps, hold affected orders for authoritative reconciliation instead of inventing new effect keys. Strong global stock without buckets would require consensus and adds latency, so reserve it for genuinely scarce goods.

    Security and Abuse Prevention¶

    Use hosted fields or provider tokens so checkout does not receive raw card data. Encrypt addresses, minimize log fields, and separate PCI and general workloads. Authenticate webhooks with signatures and replay windows. Validate all price and amount inputs server-side. Rate limit by account, device, IP, address, and payment instrument; detect card testing, promotion farming, inventory hoarding, and address manipulation. Admin cancellation and manual repair require least privilege, dual approval for high-value cases, and immutable audit records.

    Observability and Testing¶

    Measure conversion by step, preview and submit latency, inventory conflict, reservation age, payment declines versus unknowns, saga age, compensation failures, outbox lag, dependency saturation, and confirmed-to-fulfillment delay. Trace by checkout, order, and provider reference while redacting personal and payment data. Alert on invariant symptoms such as paid-unconfirmed age rather than only HTTP errors.

    Use state-machine property tests, contract tests with provider simulators, and concurrency tests against hot inventory keys. Inject duplicate callbacks, reordered events, crashes before and after each durable write, long authentication, expired quotes, and region fencing. Shadow price calculations during rule migrations and compare totals. Periodically restore backups and rebuild projections from snapshots plus events.

    Important Tradeoffs¶

    Orchestration centralizes workflow visibility but can become a coupling hub; choreography reduces central knowledge but obscures state and compensation. SQL costs more per write than an append-only workflow log but gives simple constraints and operator queries at this scale. Reserving before payment protects customer experience but can be abused to hoard inventory. Regional inventory buckets trade utilization for availability and bounded oversell risk. Synchronous fulfillment would reduce apparent lag but places warehouse availability on checkout's critical path.

    Final Architecture¶

    flowchart TD
    Edge[Global edge] --> Cell[Regional checkout cell]
    Cell --> Orch[Durable orchestrator]
    subgraph State[Order state and disaster recovery]
        DB[(Orders, steps, outbox)] -. fenced async DR .-> DR[(Recovery region)]
    end
    Orch --> DB
    Orch --> Inv[Inventory shards and regional buckets]
    Orch --> Pay[PCI payment service]
    Orch --> Quote[Pricing, tax, shipping]
    subgraph Events[Asynchronous order events]
        Stream[(Event stream)] --> Fulfill[Fulfillment]
        Stream --> Read[Order read models]
        Stream --> Notice[Notifications]
    end
    DB --> Stream
    Recon[Reconciliation workers] --> DB
    Recon --> Inv
    Recon --> Pay

    The final system is a durable, idempotent saga whose order database records truth. It adds partitioned inventory, queues, and regional cells only where contention, latency, and provider behavior require them.

    Interview Deep-Dive Questions¶

    1. Would you reserve stock before or after payment authorization, and why?
    2. How do you recover when the provider times out after charging?
    3. How are globally limited promotions enforced?
    4. How do you prevent a hot SKU from overloading one partition?
    5. What changes for marketplace orders with multiple sellers?
    6. How do you migrate orchestration logic while workflows are active?

    L5 and L6 Expectations¶

    An L5 answer produces immutable order snapshots, a clear saga, inventory conditional writes, payment idempotency, and concrete failure handling. An L6 answer additionally sets product policy for ambiguity and compensation, isolates provider and regional failures, handles hot inventory through bounded allocations, designs migration and repair tooling, and reasons about conversion, fraud, compliance, and operating cost.

    Five-Minute Summary¶

    Checkout turns a cart into an immutable order through a recoverable saga. Start with one multi-AZ relational order database and persist checkout identity and workflow state before calling anything external. Validate a signed, expiring quote; reserve inventory atomically; authorize payment with the order ID as idempotency key; then confirm the order and write an outbox event in one transaction. Timeouts become PROCESSING, not blind retries. Compensation releases stock and voids or refunds payment. Inventory owns canonical counters and uses regional buckets only for hot or globally distributed stock. Partition orders independently from inventory, isolate each dependency with bounded concurrency, and keep one home region per order. Reconciliation resolves payment ambiguity and orphaned reservations. Security centers on tokenized payment, server-generated totals, webhook verification, and defenses against card testing and inventory hoarding. Test every crash boundary, duplicate event, stale quote, and hot-key race.
