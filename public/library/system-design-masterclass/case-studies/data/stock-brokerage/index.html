    Stock Brokerage¶

    1. Interview roadmap¶

    Design a retail brokerage for cash equities: fund an account, view quotes, place/cancel orders, receive executions, maintain positions and cash, and reconcile with exchange, clearing broker, bank, and depository. We will separate order truth, execution truth, and accounting truth, then cover risk, event ordering, market-data fanout, settlement, failures, and regulation.

    2. Product framing¶

    The brokerage is an agent between customers and venues. An accepted order is not a trade; a venue execution creates an obligation; settlement later exchanges securities and cash. Quotes may be stale without changing ownership. Orders can partially fill, and cancel acknowledgement can race a fill.

    Scope includes limit/market orders and T+1 settlement in one market. Options, margin lending, matching-engine design, and tax-lot optimization are extensions. A broker may route through an exchange gateway and clear through an external clearing member.

    The venue boundary is a protocol contract, not a reliable RPC. Assume each trading session has one fenced sender, durable outbound sequence numbers, replay or gap-fill support, stable client order IDs, venue-assigned order IDs, execution IDs unique within a venue, and a status or drop-copy channel independent of the order-entry session. An acknowledgement confirms venue acceptance, not execution. A timeout is an unknown outcome unless the venue explicitly guarantees that an unacknowledged request was not processed. If a real venue lacks any of these capabilities, the adapter must document the weaker guarantee and compensate with tighter reconciliation or manual review.

    3. Requirements¶

    Functional¶

    • Open funded brokerage accounts and expose buying power.
    • Stream quotes and account updates.
    • Place, cancel, and query orders with partial fills.
    • Apply pre-trade limits and reserve cash or quantity.
    • Book executions into immutable cash and security ledgers.
    • Settle and reconcile against venue, clearer, bank, and depository files.

    Non-functional¶

    • 100,000 order requests/second at market open; p99 broker acceptance under 100 ms excluding venue.
    • No accepted execution lost or booked twice.
    • Account views available at 99.99%; trading can fail safe under uncertainty.
    • Full auditability with synchronized clocks and retained raw external messages.

    4. Correctness invariants¶

    1. Client order ID is unique per account; retry returns the existing order.
    2. Order filled quantity equals the sum of unique venue executions and never exceeds order quantity.
    3. Cancellation never erases a fill received before or after cancel acknowledgement.
    4. Every accounting transaction balances debits and credits per currency or security unit.
    5. Ledger entries are immutable; correction is a linked reversal and replacement.
    6. Available cash equals settled cash plus approved credit minus holds and obligations, according to policy.
    7. Internal books reconcile to independently obtained venue, clearing, bank, and depository records.

    5. Capacity estimates¶

    Ten million active accounts placing ten orders/day gives 100 million orders/day, but open and news bursts dominate. At 100,000 requests/second and 1 KB audit payloads, the order ingress is 100 MB/s. Executions may exceed orders due to partial fills.

    For market data, one million active symbols at 10 normalized updates/second produce 10 million updates/second internally. Suppose two million concurrent clients each watch 20 symbols: that is 40 million active subscriptions. Delivering every source update at the same 10 Hz would require 400 million client messages/second. At 200 bytes/message before TLS and WebSocket overhead, payload egress alone is 80 GB/s. Conflating each symbol to at most one update every 250 ms caps a continuously changing subscription at 4 messages/second, or 160 million messages/second and 32 GB/s in the worst case. Actual load is lower because many symbols update less often, but gateways must be sized from the measured subscription-weighted update rate, not symbol count. At a measured safe limit of 40,000 outbound messages/second per gateway, the 160 million messages/second bound requires 160,000,000 / 40,000 = 4,000 gateways before failover and burst headroom. At the worst-case 4 Hz this output limit admits only 10,000 continuously changing subscriptions per gateway; a larger subscription count is safe only when measurement proves a lower subscription-weighted update rate.

    Ledger volume includes holds, releases, trades, fees, settlement, deposits, and withdrawals. At 20 postings/order lifecycle and 200 bytes/posting, 100 million orders imply 400 GB/day before replication and indexes. Keep primary account indexes narrow and archive journal payloads.

    VenueOrderAttempt owns routing and transport state: QUEUED, SENT, UNKNOWN, ACKNOWLEDGED, or REJECTED. Order.status is the customer-visible lifecycle derived from attempt evidence and executions; it does not overwrite the attempt history. Posting uses integer minor currency units or integer security units; never floating point. A journal transaction must sum to zero independently for each asset. Orders, attempts, and projections are mutable state machines, while executions, journal transactions, and postings are append-only.

    Acceptance means the broker durably recorded and queued the order, not that the venue accepted it. Responses distinguish BROKER_ACCEPTED, ROUTE_UNKNOWN, VENUE_ACCEPTED, PARTIALLY_FILLED, FILLED, CANCEL_PENDING, CANCELLED, and REJECTED. ROUTE_UNKNOWN means the current VenueOrderAttempt is UNKNOWN: the venue may have accepted the order, the hold remains, and the order is neither rejected nor safe to route under a new economic identity. WebSocket account events carry sequence numbers; clients detect gaps and resnapshot.

    A quote GET returns a point-in-time value with source stream, sequence, exchange time, receive time, and staleness. A subscription begins with a snapshot and then deltas whose sequence is scoped to the returned retail stream, not necessarily the raw venue feed. The gateway buffers deltas while taking the snapshot, sends the snapshot at sequence S, then releases only deltas greater than S. A client seeing a gap or reconnecting without a retained resume window requests a new snapshot; replaying arbitrary old deltas onto a fresh snapshot is invalid. Duplicate sequences are ignored. This contract preserves convergence without pretending that quotes are transactionally current.

    9. Order lifecycle and pre-trade risk¶

    The API authenticates the account, validates market rules, and executes account-level risk serially or with fenced optimistic versions. A buy places a cash hold for bounded principal, fees, and slippage; a market order needs an enforceable price collar, protected-limit conversion, or explicitly authorized credit because its execution price otherwise has no finite worst case. A sell reserves available quantity. The transaction inserts the order, hold, first VenueOrderAttempt, and an outbox route command atomically. The router is the sole writer of attempt transport state and sends its stable broker_client_order_id according to venue protocol. On acknowledgement it atomically binds the attempt and reverse mapping from (venue, session, venue_order_id) to broker_order_id. Execution and cancel reports often carry only venue identifiers, so adapters resolve this map before touching order state. Reports that precede the acknowledgement remain durably parked by venue key and are replayed after mapping appears; they are never discarded as unknown orders.

    States do not assume message order. UNKNOWN belongs to the attempt; ROUTE_UNKNOWN is the corresponding derived order status exposed while recovery is in progress. An execution is accepted by unique (venue,execution_id) even if the venue acknowledgement is delayed. A cancel means "cancel remaining quantity" and can race fills. The derived order status follows filled quantity and venue facts, not arrival order. A cancel acknowledgement releases only the confirmed unfilled remainder using venue cumulative quantity and a reconciled execution prefix; reserve any known-but-not-yet-booked fills until their executions arrive. A late report of an already executed fill must not spend cash that cancellation incorrectly released.

    10. Deep dive: immutable brokerage ledger¶

    Use a double-entry, multi-asset journal. When reserving INR 1,260.00, debit customer:available:INR and credit customer:held:INR as balance-layer transfers. On a fill, release the proportional hold, recognize unsettled cash payable, security receivable, broker fee revenue, and taxes through balanced transactions. At settlement, move unsettled to settled accounts. Security postings balance units just as cash postings balance currency.

    The ledger accepts a business idempotency key such as execution:venue:123. It validates all accounts and balanced sums, appends transaction and postings in one database transaction, then emits via transactional outbox. No update or delete exists. A correction posts a reversal referencing the original and a corrected transaction. Materialized balances are caches; authoritative balance is the sum of postings through a journal sequence.

    autonumber

  participant E as Execution service

    11. Deep dive: settlement and external reconciliation¶

    At trade date, the clearing broker confirms obligations. At T+1, cash moves through a bank and securities through a depository. Internal settlement batches reference immutable execution sets. External transfer IDs are recorded before submission; retries query status before resending. Unknown outcomes freeze affected withdrawals or quantity rather than guessing.

    Reconciliation has independent inputs: venue drop copy versus OMS executions, clearer contract notes versus trade ledger, bank statement versus cash settlement accounts, and depository position file versus security ledger. Match by external ID, date, asset, amount, and account mapping. Breaks become owned cases with aging and severity. A balancing "reconciliation adjustment" without evidence is prohibited.

    12. Market data and event time¶

    Adapters normalize redundant feeds and retain source sequence numbers, exchange event time, receive time, and quality. A per-symbol order book requires sequence continuity; gaps trigger snapshot recovery. The broker publishes conflated latest quotes to retail clients rather than every tick. Trading risk uses a feed-age threshold and can reject market orders when prices are stale.

    The normalizer partitions by symbol and emits compact latest-value records. A subscription gateway authenticates the account, enforces symbol and connection quotas, groups requested symbols by stream partition, and maintains one shared upstream subscription per partition rather than one per client. It builds each connection's snapshot from a sequence-consistent latest-value store, then fans out changed symbols. Conflation happens before per-client serialization so a burst replaces an unsent quote for the same symbol instead of growing a queue.

    Each connection has byte and message limits plus a write deadline. A slow consumer first receives more aggressive conflation, then a RESYNC_REQUIRED control message; if it still cannot drain, the gateway closes it with the last delivered sequence. It never drops an arbitrary delta while claiming sequence continuity. Resume tokens are short-lived and bind account, subscription set, stream generation, and last sequence. Gateways shed unauthenticated connections and oversized subscriptions before healthy streams, while a separate non-conflated product uses dedicated capacity and a different contract.

    Trade and execution accounting uses venue trade time plus recorded receipt time. Reporting can order by event time; recovery and audit use receipt sequence. Late corrections never overwrite executions: bust and correct messages create reversal/replacement journal activity.

    13. Partitioning, consistency, and scaling¶

    OMS ownership partitions by account so holds and buying power serialize locally. Venue sessions partition by protocol session and preserve outbound sequence. Ledger partitions may use account affinity, but each balanced transaction must remain within one shard; use broker clearing accounts per shard and periodic balanced consolidation. Cross-shard transfers use paired pending transactions and a coordinator, not unbalanced writes.

    Orders and ledger commits require strong consistency in their ownership shard. Quotes and account projections are eventually consistent and sequence-tagged. Execution ingestion, ledger booking, and outboxes are idempotent. Avoid distributed transactions with venues: durable intent, stable external IDs, status queries, and reconciliation handle the boundary.

    14. Reliability and multi-region operation¶

    • API retry finds the order by client key.
    • Router crash recovers unsent outbox rows and venue sequence.
    • Unknown venue send status queries/drop-copy reconciles before resend.
    • Duplicate execution is ignored by venue execution ID only after its durable booking workflow is complete; otherwise resume that workflow.
    • Projection lag shows asOfSequence; trading risk reads authoritative holds.
    • Market-data gaps mark quotes stale and trigger feed failover.
    • Ledger failure stops new risk reservations; it never accepts unbookable trades deliberately.

    An unknown venue outcome follows a stateful recovery protocol:

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
  participant R as Venue router
  participant O as OMS
  participant V as Venue order session
  participant D as Status or drop copy
  R->>O: mark attempt SENT with stable client ID and sequence
  R->>V: submit order
  Note over R,V: connection fails before acknowledgement
  R->>O: mark attempt UNKNOWN and expose ROUTE_UNKNOWN
  Note over R,O: hold remains reserved
  R->>D: query stable client order ID
  alt venue proves accepted
    D-->>R: venue order ID and current state
    R->>O: bind reverse map and apply venue facts
  else venue proves absent
    D-->>R: NOT_FOUND at reconciled session watermark
    R->>V: resend same stable identity if contract permits
  else still ambiguous
    D-->>R: unavailable or incomplete
    R->>O: retain attempt UNKNOWN and escalate by age
  end

    NOT_FOUND is sufficient only after the venue has reconciled through the original outbound sequence; an immediate lookup miss can race ingestion. While ambiguous, the attempt remains UNKNOWN, the order reports ROUTE_UNKNOWN, and the router must not release the hold, send a new client order ID, or report rejection. Only authoritative absence can return the order to BROKER_ACCEPTED for a protocol-safe resend of the same identity; a different venue or identity requires a new attempt only after non-acceptance is proved. Executions from drop copy resolve acceptance even if the order-session acknowledgement never arrives. Recovery workers repeatedly compare outbound sequence, reverse mappings, open-order snapshots, and executions, with bounded retries and an operator queue for aged unknowns.

    Each regulated market has an active trading region near its venue and a hot standby with fenced session ownership. Stateless reads can be active-active. Synchronous ledger replication covers only the failure domains that acknowledge the commit. A regional standby with asynchronous copies can lose acknowledged orders, holds, venue keys and postings; recover that complete prefix from surviving logs and external evidence before resuming trading, or require synchronous cross-region durability for a regional zero-RPO contract. Failover requires venue session recovery and sequence reconciliation before order flow resumes.

    15. Security and controls¶

    Use strong customer authentication, device binding, withdrawal step-up, mTLS to venues, encryption, HSM-backed keys, least privilege, and immutable operator audits. Separate trading, ledger administration, and reconciliation duties. Apply account takeover, wash-trading, market-abuse, and sanctions controls. Mask personal data from engineering logs. Time sources are monitored and signed external messages retained.

    16. Observability and testing¶

    Track order acceptance and venue latency, state-transition anomalies, duplicate executions, outstanding holds, ledger imbalance attempts, projection lag, market-data gaps/age, unsettled obligations, reconciliation breaks and age, and bank/depository transfer unknowns. Trace by client order, broker order, venue order, execution, journal transaction, and settlement batch IDs.

    Tests model every order/cancel/fill interleaving, duplicate and reordered venue messages, balanced-posting properties, integer rounding, partial-fill hold release, bust/correct, venue disconnect, database failover, and settlement-file corruption. Contract tests run each venue adapter against acknowledgements delayed past executions, duplicate IDs, sequence gaps, session reset, status-query races, and an order accepted immediately before disconnect. Property tests assert that an unknown attempt never causes a second economic order and that every execution eventually resolves through the reverse map or an owned exception.

    Quote tests join snapshot and live updates while injecting a delta at the handoff boundary, duplicates, gaps, feed failover, gateway restart, and expired resume tokens. Load tests use measured symbol popularity and burst rates, then throttle clients to prove bounded gateway memory, deterministic resync, and isolation of healthy consumers. Daily proof sums all posting assets to zero and compares projection balances to journal recomputation. Disaster drills recover venue sequences and open orders.

    17. Tradeoffs¶

    • Account partitioning simplifies buying power but can hotspot institutional accounts; isolate them or serialize through dedicated owners.
    • Immediate cross-region active-active order writes improve availability but risk duplicate venue actions; one fenced market owner is safer.
    • Mutable balances are fast but unauditable alone; immutable journal plus projections provides speed and proof.
    • Reserving worst-case cash reduces overspend but lowers buying power; product policy may permit controlled credit, explicitly ledgered.
    • Conflated quotes scale retail fanout but are unsuitable for professional tick consumers, which need a separate tier.
    • A long quote replay window improves reconnect success but consumes gateway or stream storage; a short window plus snapshot is simpler for retail clients.
    • Retaining holds during unknown venue outcomes reduces buying power, but releasing them can fund a duplicate order; resolution safety wins over temporary availability.

    20. L5 and L6 expectations¶

    An L5 answer should distinguish orders, fills, and settlement; model partial fills and cancellation races; reserve funds; book immutable balanced entries; and handle venue idempotency and reconciliation. An L6 answer should define shard accounting, external unknown outcomes, drop-copy controls, market-data quality, regulated failover, settlement close, and segregation of duties.

    21. Five-minute summary¶

    Serialize order risk by account, atomically record an order, hold, and route outbox, then send a stable broker ID through a fenced venue session. Treat venue executions as immutable facts keyed by venue execution ID; accept them despite reordered acknowledgements. Partial fills consume proportional holds, and cancellation affects only remaining quantity.

    Book every execution, fee, hold, release, and settlement in a balanced append-only multi-asset journal using integer units. Cash and position screens are sequence-tagged projections, never authority. T+1 settlement moves explicit unsettled obligations through bank and depository. Independently reconcile OMS to venue drop copy, ledger to clearer, cash to bank, and units to depository; breaks create cases, not invented adjustments. Keep market trading single-owner with a fenced standby, and prefer safe rejection when authoritative risk or market-data quality is unknown.
