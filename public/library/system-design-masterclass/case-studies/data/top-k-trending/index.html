    Top-K Trending System¶

    1. Interview roadmap¶

    We will rank the most rapidly trending topics, videos, or products for a region and category over 5-minute, 1-hour, and 24-hour windows. We first define "trending," then separate exact event accounting from approximate candidate discovery. We will cover event-time windows, hot keys, ranking, serving, replay, abuse, and multi-region operation.

    2. Product framing¶

    Trending is not simply the largest lifetime count; that would permanently favor old popular items. It measures recent momentum, optionally normalized against an item's baseline and diversity rules. The product accepts engagement events and serves a fresh ordered list by scope. It need not expose a perfectly exact global order, but it must be stable enough not to flicker and resistant to manipulation.

    Out of scope are personalized ranking and content search. Assume eligibility, safety status, category, and creator metadata come from upstream systems. Deletes and safety blocks must remove an item quickly even if historical events remain.

    3. Requirements¶

    Functional¶

    • Consume views, clicks, likes, shares, purchases, and negative feedback.
    • Compute top 100 by country, category, and configured window.
    • Return ranked items with score explanation and update time.
    • Apply eligibility, moderation, diversity, and minimum-volume policies.
    • Support deterministic replay and offline quality evaluation.

    Non-functional¶

    • 2 million events/second average, 5x bursts.
    • New legitimate trends visible within 30 seconds; read p99 below 100 ms.
    • 99.99% serving availability and no loss of accepted source events.
    • Bounded cost as items and dimensions grow; one viral item cannot stall a partition.
    • Event-time semantics for delayed mobile events.

    4. Correctness invariants and SLOs¶

    1. An engagement ID contributes at most once per scoring version.
    2. A score uses the policy and metadata effective for the intended computation version.
    3. Blocked or ineligible content is absent within 10 seconds, independent of ranking freshness.
    4. Every served list identifies its scope, window end, score version, and generation.
    5. Approximation may miss an item only within a documented error budget; final candidate scores are computed exactly from retained window buckets.

    The freshness SLO is measured from event time for on-time events and from receipt for late events. "Top K" is therefore a versioned projection, not canonical business state.

    5. Capacity estimates¶

    At 2 million events/second and 150 compressed bytes, the log receives 300 MB/s or 26 TB/day. Ninety percent are views. Ten million items may be active daily, but only a small fraction can enter any top-100 list. With 200 countries, 100 categories, three windows, and 100 results, published lists are only six million entries; intermediate cardinality is the challenge.

    One-second raw counters for every item and dimension would create excessive state. Use 10-second buckets for the 5-minute window and one-minute buckets for longer windows. Retaining 24 hours of compact counters for ten million active scope-item pairs can still require hundreds of GB, distributed across workers. Candidate sketches sharply reduce global shuffle.

    6. Entities and model¶

    Engagement(event_id, item_id, actor_hash, event_type, event_time,
           received_time, country, source, metadata_version)
ScorePolicy(version, event_weights, decay, baseline_formula,
            eligibility_rules, diversity_rules, effective_at)
Bucket(scope, item_id, bucket_start, weighted_count, unique_sketch,
       negative_count, revision)
TrendList(scope, window, generation, window_end, policy_version,
          items[{item_id, score, rank, reason}], expires_at)
Eligibility(item_id, allowed_scopes, status, version, effective_at)

    Scopes are bounded product configurations such as country=IN,category=sports, not user-defined arbitrary dimensions. Raw events are immutable. Buckets and lists are replaceable projections. Actor hashes have short retention and are used for abuse controls and approximate uniques, not displayed.

    7. Interfaces¶

    GET /v1/trending?country=IN&category=sports&window=1h&limit=50
If-None-Match: "generation-8492"

200 { "asOf":"...", "policyVersion":17, "items":[...] }

POST /internal/eligibility-events
POST /internal/recompute { "from":"...", "to":"...", "policyVersion":18 }

    Engagement arrives through a durable event bus rather than a public synchronous API. Reads use a bounded limit, return an ETag, and fall back from category-country to country only when the product explicitly allows it. Opaque generation IDs prevent clients mixing pages from different lists; normally top 100 needs no pagination.

    8. High-level architecture¶

    flowchart LR
  Apps[Product services] --> Log[(Engagement log)]
  Log --> Clean[Validate and dedupe]
  Clean --> Local[Local bucket counters]
  Local --> Cand[Candidate merge]
  Cand --> Rank[Exact scoring and policy]
  Meta[Eligibility changelog] --> Rank
  Rank --> Lists[(Versioned trend lists)]
  Lists --> Cache[Regional cache]
  Cache --> API[Trending API]
  Clean --> Raw[(Raw archive)]

    The data plane is append, local aggregation, candidate generation, exact scoring, and atomic list publication. The serving plane reads immutable list generations from regional caches. A separate control plane owns policy versions, scope definitions, moderation, and rollout.

    9. Core requirement: counting recent engagement¶

    Partition input by a salted item key. First-stage tasks deduplicate IDs and update fixed event-time buckets. A deterministic salt from actor or event ID spreads a viral item. At bucket close, each task sends compact partials rather than individual events. Second-stage workers merge salts by (scope,item,bucket).

    Weights should not invite opaque arithmetic in an interview. A sample score is:

    raw = max(0, 1*views + 3*likes + 6*shares + 10*purchases - 4*negative)
momentum = raw_recent / max(expected_baseline(item, scope), floor)
score = log1p(raw_recent) * momentum^0.3 * freshness_decay

    The baseline prevents a huge established item from always winning, while the volume term prevents one event on a tiny baseline from dominating. Policy versions capture coefficients and thresholds.

    10. Deep dive: approximate candidates, exact final ranking¶

    Sending every active item to one global sorter is untenable. Each partition maintains a heavy-hitter structure, such as Space-Saving, for each active scope/window and emits top M, where M might be 500 for final K=100. The merger unions local candidates, retrieves their exact bucket totals, computes scores, filters eligibility, and sorts.

    This is approximate only at candidate discovery. Set M using replay experiments so recall@100 exceeds 99.9%. Count-Min Sketch alone is unsuitable for final ranking because collisions overestimate counts. For small scopes, exact heaps are simpler. The system records candidate cutoff and error bounds, allowing operators to detect when skew invalidates assumptions. See Probabilistic Data Structures for the CMS/Space-Saving selection boundary, merge constraints, and validation gates.

    Count-heavy-hitter guarantees do not automatically apply to the final baseline-normalized, decayed score. An item with fewer views but a much lower expected baseline can win this score while missing every count-based candidate list; exact rescoring cannot recover an absent candidate. Maintain separate positive-engagement candidates, momentum/baseline-stratified candidates, and recent entrants, then measure recall against an exhaustive final-score oracle on bounded scopes. Ordinary insertion-only sketches cannot ingest negative feedback as arbitrary decrements with unchanged guarantees: keep negative counts separately and apply them during exact scoring. The clamp above also keeps log1p(raw_recent) and fractional powers defined when negative feedback dominates.

    flowchart TD
  P1[Partition top M] --> U[Union candidates]
  P2[Partition top M] --> U
  P3[Partition top M] --> U
  U --> E[Fetch exact window buckets]
  E --> S[Score and filter]
  S --> D[Diversity rerank]
  D --> Pub[Atomic generation publish]

    11. Deep dive: event time, windows, and stability¶

    Workers advance watermarks from source partitions. An event updates the bucket containing its event time. Publish provisional rolling lists at most every 10 seconds using open-bucket contributions; waiting for a full one-minute bucket and then another 20 seconds would violate the 30-second freshness target. A 20-second watermark delay controls initial bucket closure, not whether open-window trends can be shown; events arriving up to six hours late revise affected buckets and may produce a new list generation. Older events remain in raw storage for offline training but do not rewrite the live trend.

    Sliding windows are implemented as sums of fixed buckets, subtracting the bucket leaving the window. This bounds state and avoids scanning raw events. Bucket IDs are UTC instants, avoiding daylight-saving ambiguity. When a source partition is idle, explicit idle markers keep its watermark from freezing all output.

    Pure ranks flicker around equal scores. Add hysteresis: an entrant must exceed the current item by a small margin, and retain a previous item for a minimum dwell unless blocked. This stability rule is part of policy and measured against freshness; it must never override urgent moderation.

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
  participant E as Event log
  participant W as Window worker
  participant R as Ranker
  participant S as List store
  E->>W: event at t, delivered at t+12s
  W->>W: dedupe and update bucket(t)
  W->>R: revised candidate partial
  R->>R: exact score + eligibility + hysteresis
  R->>S: compare-and-set generation N+1
  S-->>R: published

    12. Deep dive: eligibility, diversity, and abuse¶

    Ranking and eligibility are separate. A moderation tombstone is pushed to regional serving caches and checked on every read, including cached generation and conditional-GET responses, so removal does not wait for recomputation. A policy freshness lease bounded by the 10-second removal SLO makes isolated replicas fail closed; rollback and stale-list fallback retain the latest safety generation. Rankers also consume the ordered eligibility changelog for complete future generations.

    After scoring, a deterministic reranker enforces limits such as at most two items per creator and minimum category diversity. It records whether an item was omitted by score, eligibility, or diversity. This provides explainability and prevents debugging a policy outcome as a data outage.

    Bot defense uses trusted-source weights, rate limits, account age, repeated-device patterns, and unique-actor sketches. Suspicious activity enters a separate feature stream and can contribute zero or reduced weight. Never delete raw evidence. A policy update triggers shadow ranking and comparison before promotion.

    13. Partitioning, skew, and scaling¶

    Input is salted by item; aggregation state is partitioned by hash of (scope,item). Scope fanout is bounded: enrich an event only into a small configured hierarchy. A global event may update global, country, and one category-country scope, not every possible combination.

    Rankers own groups of scopes. Very large global scopes receive dedicated workers; tiny categories share workers. Consistent hashing reduces movement during scaling. Backpressure first delays ranking generations while durable counting continues. If state stores approach limits, spill closed buckets and reduce optional scope frequency rather than discard accepted events.

    14. Consistency and idempotency¶

    Event transport is at least once. A state-store put-if-absent(event_id) with retention beyond maximum live lateness removes duplicates; bucket updates and consumed offsets checkpoint atomically within the processor. Downstream partials carry (computation_epoch, partition, scope, item, bucket, revision) and a complete replacement value; mergers retain only the highest revision for that identity and adjust totals by the replacement difference. A newer delta cannot simply replace an older delta. List publication writes a complete immutable generation then atomically updates a scope pointer, so readers never see half a list.

    Serving is eventually consistent across regions. Strong global ordering is unnecessary; clients receive asOf and generation. Eligibility blocks are stronger and distributed on a priority path.

    15. Reliability and multi-region operation¶

    • Processor failure restores state and offsets, replaying idempotently.
    • Cache failure falls back to the regional list store or the last unexpired generation.
    • Ranker overload slows publication but does not lose buckets.
    • Metadata lag freezes promotion for affected scopes; unknown safety state fails closed.
    • A bad score policy rolls back by repointing to the prior version.
    • Region loss serves a nearby region's slightly older lists while logs replicate and recover.

    Each region computes local scopes. A designated region merges global candidates asynchronously, avoiding cross-region coordination per event. Raw archives and policy histories replicate. Recovery drills rebuild a chosen hour using pinned input watermarks, clock values, metadata/policy versions and prior hysteresis state, then compare generation hashes. Raw events and policy alone do not determine a list whose dwell rules depend on its previous generation.

    16. Security and privacy¶

    Authenticate event producers with workload identity and authorize list management separately. Encrypt data and logs, rotate keys, validate schemas, and reject dimensions that could leak sensitive attributes. Actor hashes are rotating, purpose-limited, and access-controlled. Public APIs enforce quotas and only expose approved metadata. Policy changes, moderation overrides, and replays require audit trails and least-privilege roles.

    17. Observability and testing¶

    Track source-to-list event-time freshness, watermark lag, duplicate rate, bucket revision count, candidate recall on sampled exact computations, score distributions, rank churn, eligibility-removal latency, cache age, hot-key skew, and per-scope generation failures. Alerts should distinguish empty because no events, all content filtered, and pipeline failure.

    Property tests cover bucket add/subtract, dedupe, deterministic reranking, and monotonic revisions. Replay tests compare known event sets and policy versions. Load tests inject a single viral item, 5x bursts, late events, and region isolation. Abuse tests generate coordinated actors. Shadow policies measure overlap@K, rank correlation, freshness, diversity, and safety violations before rollout.

    18. Tradeoffs¶

    • Exact distributed sorting maximizes precision but wastes shuffle on millions of losers; approximate candidates plus exact rescoring meets a measurable recall target.
    • Processing-time windows are simple but punish delayed mobile traffic; event time gives meaningful trends at the cost of revisions.
    • Updating a mutable sorted set per event offers low latency but creates hot keys and poor replay semantics; bucketed stream state is safer.
    • Active-active global ranking is fresher locally but can diverge; regional counting with one asynchronous global merge is operationally clearer.
    • More hysteresis reduces flicker but hides fast changes. Treat it as a policy with an SLO, not an implementation accident.

    19. Final architecture¶

    flowchart TB
  L[(Regional logs)] --> D[Dedupe + enrich]
  D --> B[Salted event-time buckets]
  B --> H[Per-partition heavy hitters]
  H --> M[Scope candidate union]
  B --> X[Exact candidate counts]
  M --> X
  X --> P[Policy, safety, diversity]
  P --> G[(Immutable generations)]
  G --> C[Regional caches]
  C --> API[Read API]
  T[Priority tombstones] --> C
  D --> A[(Raw archive)]

    This design spends precision where users see it: candidate discovery is bounded and approximate, final scores are exact over retained buckets, and list publication is atomic. Immutable inputs and versioned policy make rankings reproducible; a separate priority safety path prevents freshness mechanics from delaying removal.

    20. Interview questions¶

    1. How do you choose candidate count M and prove recall?
    2. What happens when one item receives half of all traffic?
    3. How would you add personalized trends without leaking user data?
    4. Can a late event remove an item from a previously served list?
    5. How do you distinguish organic virality from coordinated abuse?
    6. Which components require strong consistency?
    7. How would you handle a worldwide event during a regional partition?

    21. L5 and L6 expectations¶

    An L5 answer should define a score, estimate event volume, use logs and windows, handle dedupe and hot items, publish cached lists, and discuss late data and failure recovery. It should separate ingestion from serving.

    An L6 answer should quantify approximation quality, bound scope fanout, define watermark and policy-version behavior, separate moderation from rank freshness, reason about overload and multi-region ownership, and propose replay-based quality gates. It connects ranking correctness to product metrics and abuse controls.

    22. Five-minute summary¶

    Consume immutable engagement events into regional logs. Deduplicate and aggregate them in fixed event-time buckets, salted so viral items cannot hot-spot one worker. Each partition emits a bounded heavy-hitter candidate set. Union those candidates, retrieve exact bucket totals, apply a versioned momentum score, safety and diversity rules, then atomically publish an immutable top-K generation. Regional caches make reads fast; priority tombstones remove unsafe content independently.

    Watermarks provide timely initial output and bounded revisions for late events. Revision IDs make retries and replay idempotent. Small scopes can rank exactly; large scopes use approximate discovery with measured recall. During overload, preserve counting and delay optional list generations. Regional scopes compute locally, while one region asynchronously merges global candidates. The central tradeoff is controlled approximation for scale without making correctness unmeasurable.
