    Ad Click Aggregator¶

    1. Interview roadmap¶

    We will design a platform that accepts ad impressions and clicks, produces advertiser reports within one minute, and later publishes billable totals. The key distinction is between a fast, revisable event-time view and a slower authoritative billing view. We will establish event identity and attribution rules first, estimate load, design ingestion and aggregation, then cover late data, hot campaigns, replay, fraud, and reconciliation.

    2. Product framing¶

    An ad exchange emits an impression when an ad is rendered and a click when a user interacts. Advertisers ask for counts, click-through rate (CTR), and spend grouped by campaign, creative, publisher, geography, and time bucket. The dashboard can tolerate recent corrections; invoices cannot silently change.

    Assume this system starts after ad selection. Auction and creative serving are out of scope. We retain raw events for audit and reprocessing, provide near-real-time aggregates for 90 days, and finalize billing daily after a lateness window. Attribution is last eligible impression for the same ad opportunity or signed impression token, not arbitrary user-level multi-touch attribution.

    3. Requirements¶

    Functional¶

    • Ingest impression, click, and optional conversion events.
    • Validate tenant, signature, schema, and event identity.
    • Attribute clicks to impressions and reject impossible or duplicate events.
    • Query counts, CTR, and estimated spend over dimensions and time ranges.
    • Produce daily finalized advertiser and publisher statements.
    • Replay raw data after bugs without double charging.

    Non-functional¶

    • Sustain 1 million events/second globally and 3x bursts.
    • Acknowledge valid ingestion in 100 ms p99; expose 99% of accepted events in 60 seconds.
    • Dashboard availability is 99.95%; acknowledged ingestion survives one zone failure under the configured quorum policy. Regional destruction has the separately stated replication RPO, not an undefined percentage durability promise.
    • Billing totals must be reproducible and traceable to accepted events.
    • Isolate advertisers and degrade queries before dropping accepted events.

    4. Correctness invariants¶

    1. An event identity contributes at most once within the streaming deduplication horizon and at most once to finalized billing. Reports outside that horizon are provisional until canonical reconciliation.
    2. A billable click references one valid impression and satisfies the campaign's attribution and fraud policy version.
    3. Money uses integer micros and an explicit currency; currencies are never summed together.
    4. Finalized statements are immutable. Corrections create adjustment records linked to the prior statement.
    5. Every aggregate is derivable from immutable raw events plus versioned policy and reference data.
    6. Event time determines reporting buckets; processing time determines operational lag only.

    These invariants do not require exactly-once transport. They require durable identities, deterministic computation, and idempotent sinks.

    5. Capacity estimates¶

    At 1 million events/second and 350 bytes compressed per event, ingress is about 350 MB/s and 30 TB/day. Replicated raw storage at 3x is 90 TB/day, so lifecycle it: 30 days hot object storage and 13 months compressed archive. If impressions are 95%, clicks 4%, and conversions 1%, attribution state is driven mostly by impressions.

    One-minute aggregates with 5 million distinct active combinations per hour produce between 5 million and 300 million occupied combination-minute rows per hour, depending on temporal occupancy, before rollups. Storing every possible dimension cube is explosive. We precompute common combinations and query uncommon combinations from a columnar analytics store. A 60-second visibility SLO implies stream capacity above peak, bounded state, and explicit late-event handling rather than hourly batch jobs.

    6. Core entities and data model¶

    AdEvent(event_id, type, event_time, received_time, advertiser_id,
        campaign_id, creative_id, publisher_id, impression_id,
        user_token, country, cost_micros, currency, schema_version,
        signature, source_region)
Aggregate(key_dimensions, window_start, window_size, impressions,
          clicks, conversions, spend_micros, revision, updated_at)
Attribution(impression_id, click_event_id, policy_version, decision, reason)
Statement(statement_id, account_id, date, currency, amount_micros,
          input_manifest_hash, policy_version, status)
Adjustment(adjustment_id, statement_id, amount_micros, reason, evidence_uri)

    event_id is generated at the edge from source ID plus event type, or accepted from a trusted producer. A signed impression token carries immutable campaign, creative, publisher, price, expiry, and nonce. It prevents a compromised client from inventing a high-value click. Raw objects are partitioned by received date/hour/region, with event-time metadata in manifests so late partitions remain discoverable.

    7. Interfaces¶

    The event endpoint returns 202 Accepted after durable log replication, not after aggregation. Invalid signatures return 401; syntactic errors return 400; overload returns 429 before acceptance. Reports use opaque pagination tokens containing the query sort key and snapshot revision. Producers send batches with per-item outcomes to amortize network overhead.

    Collectors authenticate producers, apply admission control, add received_time, and append to a regional replicated log. Stream processors validate tokens, deduplicate, attribute, and update minute windows. Raw accepted and quarantined records land in object storage. A daily batch recomputes canonical totals independently; it never trusts a sum copied from the streaming projection.

    9. Ingestion and attribution¶

    Partition the log primarily by hash(advertiser_id, campaign_id) so campaign-relative order is usually preserved. A click can arrive before its impression due to separate client queues. The attribution operator keeps a bounded pending-click store and impression lookup keyed by impression_id. Signed token validation often permits attribution without waiting for the impression event, but the impression must eventually exist for billing.

    The processor emits explicit decisions: ATTRIBUTED, PENDING, EXPIRED, DUPLICATE, or INVALID. Pending clicks expire after the allowed lateness plus attribution window. Reference data such as campaign status and price is consumed as a versioned changelog. Event-time evaluation uses the policy effective at the event, avoiding today's campaign configuration changing yesterday's bill.

    10. Deep dive: event time and revisions¶

    The dedupe marker, attribution/bucket changes, and durable output intent commit together. Marking an event seen before recording its output loses the effect if the processor crashes between the two. Revision sinks receive full replacement aggregates, or deltas with unique effect IDs; skipping older delta revisions is not equivalent to applying their contributions.

    Mobile clients can be offline, clocks can be wrong, and inter-region links can lag. Each source partition advances a monotonic watermark from its observed progress and lateness policy, for example maximum valid observed event time minus an out-of-order allowance. A downstream operator generally takes the minimum watermark of active inputs. Idle-input exclusion must be explicit; a resumed source can then produce late events. A minute window emits when the watermark passes its end. Events up to 24 hours late revise the dashboard aggregate; later records go to a correction stream for batch handling.

    Never drop merely because a producer timestamp is old. First validate it against token issuance and receipt bounds. A wildly future timestamp is quarantined. Each aggregate upsert carries (window_key, computation_version, revision); the sink applies only a newer revision. Consumers can show a provisional badge until the daily close.

    Session windows are unnecessary: reporting uses fixed UTC minute/hour/day windows, while an advertiser's local day is a query or statement-calendar concern. Daylight-saving transitions therefore do not create ambiguous storage buckets.

    11. Deep dive: aggregation and hot keys¶

    A celebrity campaign can dominate traffic. Directly partitioning only by campaign creates a hot task. Use two-stage aggregation: edge or first-stage processors aggregate by (campaign, minute, random_shard); a second stage merges shards. The random shard derives from event_id, making replay deterministic. Very low-volume dimensions bypass excessive sharding.

    State stores retain dedupe IDs for at least maximum retry plus lateness. A compacted durable topic checkpoints aggregate updates; local RocksDB-like state is rebuildable. Backpressure propagates to collectors, which spool durably and shed untrusted debug events before paid traffic. Autoscaling reacts to input bytes, consumer lag, state write latency, and hot-partition skew, not CPU alone.

    Common report keys are written to a distributed OLAP store sorted by tenant and time. High-cardinality fields such as raw user token are forbidden report dimensions. Approximate sketches can estimate unique reach, but billing counts remain exact after dedupe.

    12. Deep dive: canonical billing and reconciliation¶

    After a 24-hour soft close, a batch job reads immutable manifests, deduplicates by event ID, joins versioned campaign data, runs the approved fraud model, and writes a candidate statement. It compares totals with streaming results by tenant, campaign, and hour. Differences over thresholds block finalization; differences under thresholds remain explained metrics, not silently ignored.

    Maintain a durable billed-event identity registry across statement dates, not just a per-day dedupe set: a retry received tomorrow must not become tomorrow's new charge. The registry and finalization commit atomically or through a recoverable staged protocol with one authoritative owner. The statement stores input manifest hashes, code and policy versions, row counts, and amount. Finalization is a transactional state change from CANDIDATE to FINAL. Later valid events or fraud discoveries create debit or credit adjustments in the next cycle. Reprocessing first targets a shadow namespace; promotion swaps a version pointer rather than adding deltas into production and risking double count.

  Raw[Immutable daily manifests] --> Recompute[Deterministic recompute]
  Recompute --> Candidate[Candidate totals]
  Live[Streaming totals] --> Compare{Within explained tolerance?}
  Candidate --> Compare
  Compare -->|yes| Final[Immutable statement]
  Compare -->|no| Hold[Hold and investigate]

  Final --> Adj

    13. Partitioning and scaling¶

    Log partitions are spread by campaign plus shard; raw data is time/region partitioned and compacted into healthy object sizes. OLAP data is tenant/time distributed, with campaign clustering for pruning. Tenant quotas stop one advertiser's broad query from exhausting the cluster. A query planner caps date range, dimensions, and estimated scanned bytes.

    Regional collectors continue during WAN isolation. Their logs replicate asynchronously to a home analytics region. Globally duplicated events retain the same ID and collapse downstream. We avoid synchronous global writes on ingestion because availability and latency matter more than instant global dashboards. Statement finalization occurs in one elected control region with fenced leases.

    14. Consistency and idempotency¶

    The dashboard is eventually consistent and revision-aware. Statement metadata and finalization are strongly consistent. Dedupe is layered: collector batch IDs reduce retries, stream state removes event duplicates, and sink revision checks prevent duplicate effects. Retention expiry means extremely late duplicate events may reappear in streaming views, but canonical batch dedupe over the billing horizon corrects them before charging.

    15. Reliability and failure modes¶

    • Collector crash before acknowledgement causes producer retry; event ID dedupes it.
    • Processor crash restores checkpointed offsets and state, then re-emits revisions safely.
    • OLAP outage accumulates a durable output topic; freshness degrades while ingestion continues.
    • Poison records enter a bounded quarantine with reason and encrypted payload reference.
    • Reference-data lag pauses affected campaign partitions rather than evaluating with unknown policy.
    • Object-store manifest corruption blocks billing and is detected through checksums and independent counts.
    • Replay cannot write the live namespace until validation and an audited promotion.

    Disaster recovery continuously copies raw objects and log segments cross-region. Recovery objectives are RPO under five minutes for dashboards, a separately measured cross-region accepted-event RPO of up to five minutes under normal replication lag, and RTO under one hour. A WAN outage can exceed that RPO; enforce an acknowledgement lag bound or explicitly widen the objective. Regional acknowledgement alone does not guarantee that every accepted event survives regional destruction. Restore drills prove checkpoints can rebuild, not merely that backups exist.

    16. Security and abuse prevention¶

    Use mTLS for server producers, rotating SDK credentials, signed short-lived impression tokens, tenant-scoped authorization, encryption in transit and at rest, and field-level protection for user tokens. Minimize user data: rotating pseudonymous identifiers are enough for fraud and reach. Retention and deletion operate on identity mapping where legally required; aggregate billing records remain under financial retention policy.

    Rate limits combine producer, publisher, campaign, IP reputation, and device signals. Fraud detection flags click farms, impossible click latency, repeated nonces, and publisher spikes. Models produce versioned decisions with explainable reason codes. Administrative replay and statement actions require dual control and immutable audit logs.

    17. Observability and testing¶

    Measure accepted, rejected, duplicated, quarantined, and attributed events; end-to-end event-time freshness; watermark delay; partition skew; state size; checkpoint duration; sink revision conflicts; and stream-versus-batch monetary variance. Trace a sampled event_id from collector offset through attribution to aggregate and statement evidence. Per-tenant dashboards avoid global averages hiding a large customer outage.

    Tests include property tests for dedupe and window boundaries, deterministic replay golden sets, malformed-token fuzzing, skew and clock simulations, processor kill/restart tests, OLAP outage tests, and reconciliation fixtures with late and duplicated clicks. Canary code writes shadow aggregates and compares them before rollout. A production readiness test sustains 3x peak while one availability zone is unavailable.

    18. Important tradeoffs¶

    • Updating counters in a transactional database is simpler, but cannot absorb write volume, late revisions, or flexible dimensions.
    • Streaming-only billing is faster, but couples money to checkpoint and sink defects. Independent batch recomputation provides a correctness boundary.
    • Global synchronous dedupe gives immediate convergence, but adds latency and an availability dependency. Stable IDs plus asynchronous regional processing are preferable.
    • Full cubes make reads cheap but create unbounded write amplification. Precompute popular dimensions and retain raw columnar facts.
    • A longer lateness window improves completeness but increases state and delays finality. Product-facing provisional data and explicit financial close separate these needs.

    19. Final design¶

    flowchart TB
  subgraph Regions
    E1[Collector A] --> K1[(Regional log A)]
    E2[Collector B] --> K2[(Regional log B)]
  end
  K1 --> P[Validated event stream]
  K2 --> P
  P --> W[Watermarked sharded windows]
  W --> Q[(OLAP projections)]
  P --> R[(Raw immutable archive)]
  R --> C[Canonical billing job]
  C --> X{Reconciliation gate}
  X --> F[(Final statements)]
  X --> H[Investigation queue]
  Q --> A[Reporting API]
  F --> A

    The selected design deliberately has two answer paths: low-latency, revisable projections for product use and deterministic, manifest-backed statements for money. Stable identities and idempotent effects make at-least-once infrastructure safe. Event-time watermarks, sharded aggregation, and regional buffering handle the actual distribution problems rather than assuming ordered traffic.

    20. Interview deep-dive questions¶

    1. How would you change attribution from signed impression ID to a 7-day user-level model?
    2. What happens when a campaign becomes a single hot key at 500,000 events/second?
    3. How do you prove a replay did not double charge an advertiser?
    4. Where is strong consistency necessary, and where is it harmful?
    5. How would privacy deletion interact with immutable billing evidence?
    6. How would you detect a stalled watermark caused by one idle partition?
    7. Which discrepancies should automatically block daily close?

    21. L5 and L6 expectations¶

    An L5 candidate should clarify dashboard versus billing accuracy, estimate throughput and storage, choose a log and stream processor, explain dedupe, event-time windows, sharding, storage, and common failures. They should not claim that a broker magically provides end-to-end exactly once.

    An L6 candidate should define policy versioning and financial finality, isolate tenants, reason about skew and backpressure, design independent reconciliation and safe replay, choose a multi-region ownership model, and connect SLOs to overload and recovery. They should identify organizational controls around fraud models and statement promotion, not only components.

    22. Five-minute summary¶

    Accept signed impression and click events through regional collectors and acknowledge only after a replicated log append. Preserve immutable raw events. Process at least once, deduplicate by stable event ID, resolve clicks against versioned impression and campaign data, and aggregate by event-time windows. Watermarks close initial results; late events publish higher revisions. Two-stage deterministic sharding protects hot campaigns.

    Serve recent reports from tenant/time-partitioned OLAP projections. Treat them as provisional. Each day, independently recompute billable totals from immutable manifests, compare them with streaming output, and finalize an immutable statement through a reconciliation gate. Corrections become adjustments, never statement mutation. Regional buffering favors available ingestion; one fenced control region owns close. Security centers on signed tokens, scoped access, privacy minimization, and audited administration. The essential idea is not exactly-once transport: it is reproducible inputs, versioned rules, idempotent effects, and explicit financial finality.
