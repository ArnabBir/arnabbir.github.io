    Ad Serving Platform¶

    1. Interview roadmap¶

    Design the request-time platform that selects and serves an ad. We will define auction and pacing invariants, budget latency, separate control and data planes, and then explore targeting indexes, auctions, frequency caps, event-time feedback, partitioning, failures, privacy, and billing integration.

    2. Product framing¶

    When a page or app requests an ad, eligible campaigns compete. The winner must satisfy targeting, policy, budget, pacing, frequency, and creative constraints. The response includes a signed impression token used by measurement. Serving must remain fast when reporting is delayed, but it must not knowingly overspend or serve blocked creatives.

    Scope includes campaign configuration, candidate retrieval, auction, pacing, creative selection, and impression/click emission. Creative media storage, advertiser billing aggregation, and sophisticated conversion attribution are external systems. We support CPM and CPC bids with a normalized expected-value auction.

    3. Requirements¶

    • Create/version campaigns, creatives, targeting, bids, schedules, and budgets.
    • Select an eligible ad for request context and return creative metadata.
    • Enforce campaign/account budgets, pacing, and frequency caps.
    • Emit signed impression and click evidence.
    • Apply policy blocks rapidly and provide explainable decisions.
    • Reconcile serving reservations with accepted impressions and billable events.

    Peak is 5 million ad requests/second globally. Selection p99 is under 50 ms at 99.99% availability; control changes propagate in 60 seconds and emergency blocks in 10 seconds. Hard budget enforcement uses escrow with no overspend of the allocated limit. Separately specify the maximum stale spend after a budget reduction, bounded by outstanding grants, or explicitly choose a probabilistic budget with a quantified overspend target.

    4. Correctness invariants¶

    1. A served creative is eligible under one immutable campaign/policy snapshot.
    2. Every response has a globally unique opportunity/impression token with authenticated campaign, price, creative, publisher, timestamp, and nonce.
    3. Budget consumption is idempotent by opportunity ID and bounded under regional concurrency.
    4. Frequency-cap decisions use documented staleness; duplicate impressions do not consume twice.
    5. Blocked campaigns/creatives disappear within the emergency SLO.
    6. Billable totals derive from accepted measurement events, while serving reservations are reconciled and released.

    5. Capacity estimates¶

    At 5 million requests/second and 1 KB request/response, edge traffic is about 10 GB/s bidirectionally. Evaluating all one million active campaigns is impossible. If targeting retrieves 1,000 candidates and local filters reduce to 100 scored bids, fleets perform 500 million simple scoring operations/second.

    Five million impressions/second at 300 compressed bytes produce 130 TB/day; measurement must batch and archive aggressively. A global per-impression budget write would require millions of coordinated writes/second, so budget authority allocates bounded regional/shard tokens. Frequency state for 500 million active users and hundreds of campaigns must be sparse and TTL-bound.

    6. Data model¶

    Campaign(campaign_id, advertiser_id, objective, bid_minor, currency,
         budget_minor, schedule, targeting_version, state, version)
Creative(creative_id, campaign_id, media_ref, policy_status, version)
ServingSnapshot(version, campaigns, targeting_index_ref, policy_hash, created_at)
BudgetGrant(grant_id, campaign_id, serving_cell, amount_minor, spent, expires_at)
AdOpportunity(opportunity_id, request_time, publisher, placement, context_hash)
Decision(opportunity_id, campaign_id, creative_id, score, clearing_price_minor,
         snapshot_version, grant_id, reason_codes)
Measurement(event_id, opportunity_id, type, event_time, received_time, signature)
FrequencyCounter(subject_token, campaign_id, bucket, count, revision)

    Campaign configuration is versioned. Serving snapshots are immutable. Decisions and measurements are append-only. Reporting budget and frequency counters are projections with stable effect IDs; the grant ledger and active reservations are authoritative enforcement state.

    7. Interfaces¶

    POST /v1/ad-requests
{ "opportunityId":"o1", "placement":"banner", "publisherId":"p1",
  "context":{"country":"IN","topics":["sports"]}, "consent":"..." }

200 { "creativeUrl":"...", "impressionToken":"signed...", "ttlMs":30000 }

PUT /v1/campaigns/{id} If-Match: "version-12"
POST /internal/policy/tombstones
POST /v1/measurement/events

    Ad request retries with the same opportunity return the same cached decision when valid, avoiding double reservation. Configuration uses optimistic versions. Measurement acknowledges after durable log append and returns per-item validation status.

    8. High-level architecture¶

    flowchart LR
  Adv[Advertiser UI] --> Control[Campaign control plane]
  Control --> Compile[Validate and compile snapshots]
  Compile --> Dist[Snapshot distribution]
  User[Publisher/app] --> Edge[Regional ad server]
  Dist --> Edge
  Edge --> Index[Local targeting index]
  Edge --> Pace[Local pacing/budget grants]
  Edge --> Auction[Auction + creative choice]
  Auction --> User
  Auction --> Log[(Decision/impression log)]
  Log --> Feedback[Event-time feedback]
  Feedback --> Control

    The control plane validates campaigns, scans creatives, builds targeting indexes, allocates budget grants, and atomically distributes signed snapshots. The data plane is local and read-heavy; it performs no cross-region request-time calls.

    9. Request-time selection¶

    The edge validates publisher and consent, derives coarse context, and queries inverted indexes such as country, placement, device, topic, and schedule. Intersect the most selective posting lists, then filter campaign status, creative policy, grant availability, and frequency. A pre-ranker retains likely winners; the auction computes value and chooses a creative.

    sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
  participant P as Publisher
  participant S as Ad server
  participant I as Targeting index
  participant B as Budget/frequency state
  participant L as Event log
  P->>S: ad request(opportunity_id)
  S->>I: retrieve eligible candidates
  S->>S: auction selects tentative winner and price
  S->>B: reserve priced liability by opportunity ID
  B-->>S: reservation accepted or no budget
  alt reservation accepted
    S->>S: sign token
    S->>L: durable decision/impression intent
    S-->>P: creative + token
  else reservation rejected
    S-->>P: no-fill or bounded retry with another candidate
  end

    The server enforces a 50 ms deadline: 5 ms edge/auth, 10 ms retrieval, 10 ms filters/features, 5 ms auction, 5 ms reservation/signing, and margin. If personalized state is late, serve contextual ads. If policy state is unknown, exclude affected candidates. If no safe candidate remains, return house ad or no-fill.

    10. Deep dive: auction and pricing¶

    Normalize bids to expected value per thousand impressions: CPM bid directly, CPC bid times predicted click probability times 1,000. Quality, policy, and user-experience terms can adjust ranking, but advertiser-visible charging rules must be stable and versioned. A generalized second-price-like auction charges the minimum value needed to beat the runner-up, bounded by bid and floor; first-price is operationally simpler and increasingly common.

    Record candidates' coarse rejection reasons, winning score inputs, clearing price, model version, and snapshot. Do not log sensitive raw user features. Deterministic tie-breaking by hashed opportunity and campaign makes retries stable. Offline replay checks pricing arithmetic and counterfactual policy changes.

    11. Deep dive: budget and pacing¶

    A globally linearizable campaign counter would add WAN latency and become a hot key. A budget authority debits a durable unallocated allowance when issuing monetary grants to regional cells. Maintain settled_spend + outstanding_granted_liability + unallocated <= campaign_budget. A cell atomically reserves a priced liability from its grant using opportunity ID. Grant expiry stops new reservations but does not erase impressions or clicks already authorized: reclaim only a verified unspent remainder after the cell is fenced and its accepted liabilities are reconciled. Unknown usage remains charged against the grant. Correct escrow prevents overspend of the allocated budget; outstanding grants instead bound stale spending after a budget reduction or emergency stop. Reissuing an entire expired grant without that proof can overspend repeatedly, not merely once by a fixed grant bound.

    Pacing compares expected spend with a time-of-day curve. The server probabilistically admits a campaign using a deterministic hash of opportunity ID, avoiding synchronized bursts. Feedback updates observed spend, but grant reservation protects hard budget even when event aggregation lags. A hard CPC budget reserves the maximum charge still possible from the signed opportunity, not merely predicted click probability times price. Expected-value reservations improve utilization but provide only a probabilistic overspend target. Release liability only after its attribution/admissible-lateness window closes or authenticated evidence proves it cannot bill; late bills after close require an explicit adjustment policy.

  Budget[Campaign daily budget] --> Auth[Budget authority]
  Auth --> G1[Cell A grant]
  Auth --> G2[Cell B grant]
  Auth --> G3[Cell C grant]

    12. Deep dive: frequency and event time¶

    Frequency caps may mean at most three impressions/user/campaign/day. A strict global counter is expensive and leaks availability across regions. Route a pseudonymous subject to a home cell where possible and atomically reserve cap capacity by opportunity ID before returning the ad, then settle or release it from measurement and expiry. Counting only delayed impressions permits an unbounded burst of decisions before feedback arrives. During roaming or partitions, allocate small cap grants by region or choose conservative exclusion. Document bounded overexposure.

    Counters use fixed UTC event-time buckets with TTL. Signed tokens prevent invented timestamps. Duplicate measurement events dedupe by event ID/opportunity. Late impressions revise analytics but generally do not retract an already served ad; they affect future cap state if within the live window. Processing-time lag, watermark delay, and reservation age are separate metrics.

    13. Control-plane publication¶

    Campaign updates pass schema checks, budget authorization, targeting validation, creative malware/policy scans, and referential checks. A compiler produces a signed immutable snapshot and delta. Cells download, verify, build indexes off-path, then atomically switch pointers. They report version coverage. Emergency tombstones use a priority channel and are checked in a small local deny set before the main index and before returning a cached decision. A freshness lease shorter than the 10-second removal bound makes a disconnected cell stop affected paid serving; push alone cannot enforce the bound. Snapshot rollback preserves the newest tombstone generation.

    Snapshots permit rollback and request-level version pinning. If a delta is missed, a cell fetches a complete snapshot. Unknown versions never partially merge. This separation keeps advertiser CRUD failures away from ad-request latency.

    14. Partitioning and scaling¶

    Traffic routes to nearby serving cells. Within a cell, requests distribute by opportunity hash; campaign grants shard large budgets among workers. Targeting indexes partition by market/placement and replicate for reads. Popular broad campaigns appear in many index partitions, so compilation compresses posting lists and caches common intersections.

    Decision logs partition by opportunity ID; feedback aggregates by salted campaign ID to avoid hot campaigns. Control data partitions by advertiser but global account budgets use one authority. Backpressure drops optional debug traces first, returns no-fill before violating policy, and preserves accepted measurement in durable local spool.

    15. Consistency and idempotency¶

    Campaign editing and account budget allocation require strong control-plane consistency. Serving is snapshot-consistent per request, though cells may run adjacent versions within propagation SLO. Emergency policy blocks are stronger via push tombstones. Budget reservation is strongly consistent within its grant and globally bounded, not globally instant.

    Opportunity ID deduplicates decision and reservation; event ID deduplicates measurement; aggregate sinks use revisions. At-least-once logs are expected. Billing consumes validated measurement and independently reconciles it with decisions and reservations rather than charging from mutable serving counters.

    16. Reliability and multi-region strategy¶

    • Index corruption fails checksum and leaves previous snapshot active.
    • Control-plane outage does not stop cells until snapshot/grants expire.
    • Budget authority outage spends only existing grants, then no-fills affected campaigns.
    • Measurement-log outage spools durably; if spool is full, stop paid serving rather than lose billable evidence.
    • Model timeout uses bid and contextual quality fallback.
    • Region loss fences new reservations and reconciles outstanding liabilities before reallocating a verified unspent remainder. Expiry alone does not prove money is unspent.

    Campaign control has a single logical leader with replicated storage. Serving is active-active. Grant epochs fence stale cells; rapid disaster reallocation trades availability against overspend bound. Raw decision and measurement logs replicate for billing and audit.

    17. Security and abuse¶

    Authenticate publishers and advertisers, authorize tenant-scoped changes, encrypt data, sign snapshots and impression tokens with rotated keys, and audit configuration and budget changes. Minimize identity through rotating pseudonymous tokens and consent-aware targeting. Never target forbidden sensitive categories.

    Defend against click spam, impression laundering, domain spoofing, creative malware, budget-drain attacks, and scraper traffic. Use ads.txt-like publisher authorization, nonce replay detection, rate limits, supply-quality scoring, and separate fraud adjudication. Emergency policy controls require dual approval except pre-authorized kill switches.

    18. Observability and testing¶

    Measure ad latency stages, fill rate, candidates before/after each filter, snapshot age/coverage, tombstone removal latency, grant remaining/expiry, pacing error, overspend bound, reservation-to-impression mismatch, frequency overage, model fallback, log spool depth, and event-time freshness. Slice by region, publisher, campaign tier, and snapshot.

    Tests cover deterministic auctions and rounding, opportunity retry, concurrent grant exhaustion, snapshot atomicity, stale tombstones, late/duplicate impressions, hot campaigns, authority partition, full spools, and region fencing. Replay sampled auctions against recorded snapshots. Property tests prove spend never exceeds issued grants and one opportunity reserves once. Shadow snapshots compare eligibility and spend before rollout.

    19. Tradeoffs¶

    • Request-time global budget writes add coordination latency; durable escrow grants preserve the allocated budget while bounding stale spend after a reduction or stop. Explicit emergency allowances add separate slack.
    • Precomputed campaign matches are fast but explode across context combinations; inverted indexes plus local filtering are more flexible.
    • Strict global frequency caps reduce overexposure but require identity and coordination; home-cell state with bounded grants balances privacy and latency.
    • Serving from stale snapshots improves availability, but stale policy is unacceptable; separate ordinary expiry from priority tombstones.
    • Charging on reservation is simple but bills unrendered ads; charge validated measurement and reconcile reservations.

    20. Final architecture¶

    flowchart TB
  CP[Versioned campaign control] --> SC[Snapshot compiler]
  SC --> Cells[Active-active serving cells]
  BA[Budget authority + grant epochs] --> Cells
  Kill[Priority policy tombstones] --> Cells
  Cells --> IDX[Local targeting and models]
  IDX --> OUT[Creative + signed token]
  Cells --> EV[(Decision/measurement logs)]
  EV --> AGG[Event-time reporting]
  EV --> REC[Reservation/billing reconciliation]
  REC --> BA

    21. Interview questions¶

    1. How is global overspend bounded without a global request-time counter?
    2. What is the behavior when measurement cannot be durably recorded?
    3. How do campaign blocks outrun normal snapshot publication?
    4. How are CPC and CPM bids compared?
    5. What event time governs frequency caps?
    6. How do retries avoid a second auction and reservation?
    7. How would stronger privacy constraints change targeting?

    22. L5 and L6 expectations¶

    An L5 candidate should budget latency, retrieve candidates through indexes, filter and auction locally, define budget/frequency state, emit measurement, and discuss idempotency and failure. An L6 candidate should quantify grant overshoot, separate control/data planes and emergency policy, define snapshot rollout, event-time reconciliation, privacy boundaries, hot-campaign handling, and regional fencing.

    23. Five-minute summary¶

    Compile validated campaign configuration into signed immutable snapshots and distribute them to active-active serving cells. For each request, use local inverted indexes to retrieve candidates, filter targeting, policy, budget, and frequency, pre-rank, run a versioned auction, reserve spend by opportunity ID, and return a creative with a signed impression token. No WAN dependency sits in the 50 ms path.

    A budget authority durably debits escrow grants before issue; outstanding accepted liabilities remain allocated until reconciled, even after grant expiry. This preserves the allocated budget; outstanding grants bound stale spend after a budget reduction, not an excuse to reissue unknown usage. Event-time measurement deduplicates signed opportunities and reconciles valid impressions against reservations; billing uses those validated events, not serving counters. Frequency uses TTL buckets and documented regional staleness. Atomic snapshot swaps and a separate priority tombstone path balance availability with policy safety. During uncertainty the platform serves contextual or house ads, and stops paid serving before losing billable evidence.
