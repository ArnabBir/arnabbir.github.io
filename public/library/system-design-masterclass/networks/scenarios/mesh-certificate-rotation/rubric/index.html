    Evidence Rubric¶

    Practice home | Exemplars | Interviewer packet

    Score visible evidence, not terminology. Naming mTLS, xDS, GOAWAY, or retries earns no credit without a mechanism, boundary, and failure behavior.

    Scoring model¶

    Each dimension receives 0 to 4 points.

    Score Evidence standard
    0 Missing, contradictory, or unsafe
    1 Names the concern without an enforceable mechanism
    2 Coherent happy path with a material failure or proof gap
    3 Correct mechanism, boundary, evidence, and recovery behavior
    4 Level 3 plus calculation, operational gate, rejected alternative, or evolution judgment

    Safety caps override arithmetic totals.

    Dimensions¶

    A. Framing and invariants¶

    • Defines zero-downtime, peer-authentication, authorization, rollback, and old-CA retirement goals.
    • States that every presented chain must terminate in an active trusted root for the receiving peer.
    • Preserves least privilege and avoids broad fail-open trust or authorization.

    B. Identity, trust, and authorization¶

    • Separates certificate-chain authentication, workload identity mapping, and request authorization.
    • Defines expected identities at API-to-inventory and inventory-to-database boundaries.
    • Tests negative access during and after rotation; a trusted identity is not automatically authorized.

    C. Control-plane and data-plane reasoning¶

    • Separates desired configuration distribution from active proxy state and live traffic effects.
    • Tracks version, acknowledgement or rejection, trust digest, issuer, freshness, and proxy readiness.
    • Handles slow distribution and disconnected sidecars without assuming fleet convergence.

    D. Safe trust and issuer sequence¶

    • Expands trust to old plus new before presenting new-issued certificates.
    • Proves dual trust on fresh connections, changes issuers by cohort, and removes old trust last.
    • Retains a reversible overlap and defines revocation or emergency-compromise exceptions.

    E. HTTP/2 lifecycle and drain¶

    • Distinguishes existing connections from fresh TLS handshakes.
    • Uses graceful GOAWAY, stream completion, bounded drain, reconnect shaping, and forced-close accounting.
    • Tests fresh connections so long-lived old sessions cannot mask an invalid rollout.

    F. Evidence-based diagnosis¶

    • Correlates trust bundle, presented issuer, active config, TLS result, HTTP/2 frames, GOAWAY, and retry records.
    • Distinguishes TLS handshake failure, authorization denial, reset, upstream timeout, and flow-control stall.
    • Ranks hypotheses with supporting and disconfirming evidence before remediation.

    G. Retry, deadline, and overload control¶

    • Calculates cross-layer attempt amplification.
    • Assigns one retry owner per boundary under a propagated end-to-end deadline.
    • Uses retry budgets, exponential backoff with jitter, admission control, and reconnect rate limits.

    H. Observability and validation¶

    • Defines fresh-connection probes, certificate and trust inventory, config convergence, authn/authz outcomes, frame progress, drain health, and retry tax.
    • Splits signals by zone, proxy version, issuer pair, trust digest, response code, and cohort.
    • Gives actionable gates, windows, and owner responses rather than dashboard names alone.

    I. Migration, rollback, and stale fleet¶

    • Uses canary, cohort, zone, and dependency-aware promotion.
    • Quarantines or replaces stale workloads through an explicit policy and exception expiry.
    • Keeps rollback viable, defines the point of no easy return, and proves old-CA removal readiness.

    J. L6 leadership and tradeoffs¶

    • Assigns security, platform, service, database, and incident responsibilities.
    • Quantifies risk and capacity, records decisions, and defines pause authority and audit evidence.
    • Rejects alternatives with reversal conditions and addresses compromise response separately from routine rotation.

    Maximum: 40 points.

    Safety caps¶

    Apply the lowest relevant cap after normalization.

    Defect Maximum outcome
    Presents new-issued server credentials before all eligible clients trust the new CA 17/40; below L5
    Removes old trust before old-issued peers and stale reconnectors are eliminated 17/40; below L5
    Treats possession of any trusted certificate as sufficient authorization 17/40; below L5
    Disables certificate verification or broadens authorization to recover availability 17/40; below L5
    Validates only reused HTTP/2 connections and claims rotation success 23/40; weak L5
    Allows independent layered retries without a shared attempt or deadline budget 23/40; weak L5
    Closes all HTTP/2 connections at once with no graceful drain or reconnect shaping 23/40; weak L5
    Has a safe single-zone sequence but no stale-fleet or rollback treatment in 50/70-minute mode 27/40; borderline L5
    Strong technical plan but no cross-team ownership or completion proof in 70-minute mode 32/40; strong L5

    Level calibration¶

    Outcome Typical normalized total Required pattern
    Below target 0-17 A central trust, authorization, or availability defect remains
    Weak L5 18-23 Basic sequence works, but connection, retry, evidence, or recovery gaps are significant
    Borderline L5 24-27 Safe core with shallow diagnosis, migration, or overload control
    Strong L5 28-32 End-to-end safe rotation with credible evidence, drain, retries, and rollback
    Borderline L6 33-35 Strong L5 plus operational and organizational judgment, with one material leadership gap
    Strong L6 36-40 Shapes risk, quantifies limits, governs migration, and makes reversible cross-team decisions

    Totals do not override safety caps.

    Mode normalization¶

    Score only the listed dimensions. Normalize with:

    normalized = floor(subtotal / available_points * 40 + 0.5)

    Use round-half-up for these nonnegative scores, not a language's unspecified round behavior: 26/32 * 40 = 32.5 becomes 33, including in Python where built-in round(32.5) would give 32. Apply safety caps afterward.

    Mode Scored dimensions Available points Required evidence outside the scored set
    20 minutes A-E 20 One evidence-based failure decision
    35 minutes A-H 32 Rollback boundary stated even though I is not scored
    50 minutes A-J 40 Calculations and stale-fleet handling required
    70 minutes A-J 40 Strong L6 requires ownership, governance, and reversal conditions

    Record subtotal/available -> normalized/40, the mode, and any cap. Normalization compares performance within an assigned mode; it does not fabricate evidence for omitted dimensions.

    Report the numerical band as mode-local, separately from demonstrated level. A 20/35-minute score cannot establish L6 migration/leadership competencies I/J that were not assessed. Keep those unassessed rather than treating normalized points as evidence. These bands are educational calibration, not validated hiring predictions; they are distinct from the track's 100-point rubric.

    Protocol checks behind safety gates: RFC 9846 certificate authentication and RFC 9113 GOAWAY. An eligible peer must be compatible; an aggregate 99.99% probe target cannot waive a known old-only trust island. Quarantine must actually prevent that incompatible path, including reconnects, not merely register an exception.

    Debrief rule¶

    Give one demonstrated strength, the highest-risk gap, the exact evidence needed to close it, and one stretch question. Never average away a trust or authorization safety defect.
