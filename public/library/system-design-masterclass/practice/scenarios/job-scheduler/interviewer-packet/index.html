    Branching Interviewer Packet¶

    Use with the candidate-facing prompt. Do not reveal this file before the exercise.

    Interview goals¶

    Assess whether the candidate can turn ambiguous scheduling language into explicit guarantees, model state and time, design for stale workers and repair, isolate tenants, and connect regional recovery to a real fence.

    Suggested formats¶

    Format Flow
    35-minute L5 Framing 5, architecture 12, leases/retries 8, scale/failure 7, close 3
    50-minute standard Framing 7, baseline 15, two branches 20, close 8
    70-minute L6 Framing 10, baseline 18, three branches 30, migration/close 12
    20-minute drill One branch, one failure injection, one summary

    Opening script¶

    Design a multi-tenant distributed job scheduler for one-time, recurring, and DAG work. It must start 50,000 jobs per second at peak, support one million concurrent executions, and target p99 eligible-to-start latency under five seconds when compatible capacity exists. Explain guarantees, state, dispatch, failures, and regional recovery.

    If the candidate asks what exactly-once means, say: "The platform promises at-least-once attempts. Worker code can call arbitrary external systems. Define the remaining contract."

    Baseline checkpoints¶

    Before branching, look for:

    • separation of schedule, execution, and attempt;
    • durable acceptance and canonical execution state;
    • due-time discovery plus a dispatch path;
    • worker leases/heartbeats and retry bounds;
    • at least one partitioning and tenant-isolation decision.

    If three are absent after 15 minutes, use the recovery branch. If four or five are present, choose a deep branch.

    flowchart TD
  Start[Baseline design] --> Check{Core state and authority clear?}
  Check -->|No| Recovery[Recovery branch]
  Check -->|Yes| Claim{Claims exactly-once effects?}
  Claim -->|Yes| Stale[Stale-worker branch]
  Claim -->|No| Depth{Strongest dimension?}
  Depth -->|Time| Clock[Clock branch]
  Depth -->|Scale| Fair[Fairness and DAG branch]
  Depth -->|Reliability| Region[Regional branch]
  Recovery --> Close[Summary and score]
  Stale --> Close
  Clock --> Close
  Fair --> Close
  Region --> Close

    Branch A: recovery for a stalled candidate¶

    Ask in order:

    1. "What record proves we accepted a job?"
    2. "If every queue message vanishes, how do we find that job?"
    3. "What states can one execution enter?"
    4. "How does a worker prove it still owns an attempt?"
    5. "Which key prevents a client retry from creating a second intent?"

    Positive evidence: candidate makes storage canonical, queue repairable, and claims conditional.

    Stop condition: if answers remain component-only, move to one simple failure and preserve time for a summary.

    Branch B: stale worker and effect semantics¶

    Inject:

    A worker pauses for 90 seconds. Its 60-second lease expires, a retry starts, and then the old worker resumes. Both can reach the payment API.

    Ask:

    • What may overlap?
    • What does a fencing epoch reject?
    • Why can it not retract an HTTP side effect?
    • Does cancellation change the answer?
    • Where does the business idempotency key originate and persist?

    L5 signal: conditional completion and handler idempotency.

    L6 follow-up: distinguish attempt ID, lease epoch, execution ID, and business effect key; discuss scoped credential revocation and infrastructure retry budgets.

    Red flag: "Redis lock means only one worker can run."

    Branch C: clocks and recurring work¶

    Inject:

    A schedule says 01:30 every day in America/New_York. The next DST transition repeats 01:30. The scheduler was also unavailable for three hours.

    Ask:

    • Run once, twice, or skip during overlap?
    • How does fixed interval differ from wall-clock recurrence?
    • Who supplies now to scanners and deadlines?
    • How are duplicate occurrences prevented across scanner failover?
    • How does a schedule edit affect already materialized work?

    L5 signal: persists timezone and defines missed-run behavior.

    L6 follow-up: unique occurrence key, definition version, fenced scanner shard, bounded catch-up, fake-clock cases for gaps, overlaps, leap days, and skew.

    Branch D: fairness, capabilities, and DAG scale¶

    Inject:

    One tenant releases 10 million CPU jobs at midnight. Another has 500 critical jobs. Some work needs GPU model v3. A DAG parent has one million children.

    Ask:

    • How do jobs and workers find a compatible pool?
    • Why not create one queue per capability set?
    • What is selected first: priority, tenant, or job?
    • How do rate and concurrency limits differ?
    • How is fan-out processed without a million-row transaction?
    • Which metric distinguishes shortage from unfairness?

    L5 signal: coarse pools, quotas, and paged fan-out.

    L6 follow-up: weighted deficit round robin, reserves/borrowing, no-fit age, capability deficit, edge-event deduplication, dynamic DAG bounds, and reconciliation.

    Red flag: one strict global priority queue.

    Branch E: queue loss and backlog¶

    Inject:

    The ready queue loses a partition for 20 minutes. The database remains available. On recovery, replay traffic is twice dispatch capacity.

    Ask:

    • Which accepted jobs remain discoverable?
    • How are missing and duplicate notifications repaired?
    • How do you prevent replay from starving fresh critical work?
    • What determines safe drain rate?
    • When is recovery complete?

    L5 signal: outbox or equivalent plus a sweeper and bounded replay.

    L6 follow-up: repair budget, database headroom, hierarchical drain, admission shedding, oldest eligible age, and claim-conflict telemetry.

    Branch F: regional isolation¶

    Inject:

    Region A cannot contact region B but still serves workers and its local database. Region B sees no heartbeat for 30 seconds. Product wants automatic failover.

    Ask:

    • Can B promote on timeout alone?
    • Which authority grants a higher epoch?
    • What must an isolated A be unable to do?
    • How do RPO and replication freshness affect promotion?
    • What happens to in-flight external effects?

    L5 signal: home-region ownership and explicit failover.

    L6 follow-up: quorum-reachable promotion authority, fail-closed claims, epoch propagation, queue rebuild, ambiguous-attempt reconciliation, and game-day proof.

    Red flag: active-active scanning of the same schedule without consensus or disjoint ownership.

    Cancellation probe¶

    Use in any branch:

    Cancellation commits while an offer is in flight, and the worker reports success one millisecond later.

    Expect a conditional state transition policy. Strong candidates distinguish cancellation requested, future-assignment prevention, cooperative worker stop, terminal conflict resolution, and irreversible external effects.

    Evidence capture¶

    Use the rubric. Record exact candidate claims, not impressions.

    Moment Claim or diagram evidence Follow-up Result
    Contract
    Authority
    Race
    Overload
    Region

    Adaptive closing questions¶

    Choose one based on performance:

    • Below bar: "Which single invariant would you add first, and what mechanism enforces it?"
    • L5 borderline: "What breaks when queue notifications are lost after database commit?"
    • Meets L5: "Name the three highest-risk races and the conditional writes that contain them."
    • Strong L5: "How do fairness policy and capability scarcity interact in your metrics?"
    • L6: "Which parts are authoritative, which are rebuildable, who owns repair capacity, and what rollout evidence permits regional automation?"

    Then ask for a summary containing contract, canonical state, hot path, duplicate boundary, overload policy, and regional fence. Use the selected mode's closing allocation: three minutes in the 35-minute screen; up to five in longer loops. Do not add five unbudgeted minutes.

    Debrief guide¶

    • Award L5 for a complete and defensible design, not exhaustive feature coverage.
    • Award L6 only when policy, hidden races, repair, migration, and regional assumptions form one operating model.
    • Do not reward unexplained product names.
    • Apply critical caps from the rubric.
    • Use the exemplars to calibrate response depth, not wording.
    • For an operational extension, use the failure lab.
    • For an evolution extension, use the migration exercise.
