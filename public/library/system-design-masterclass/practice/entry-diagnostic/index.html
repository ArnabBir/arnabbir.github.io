    75-Minute Entry Diagnostic¶

    This diagnostic samples framing, quantitative reasoning, architecture, failure recovery, evolution, and communication. Take it closed-book. Use paper or one local text file. The evaluator may be another person or the learner after a 30-minute break.

    Candidate prompt¶

    Design a multi-tenant notification platform for product teams. A producer submits email, push, or SMS notifications to one user or to an audience. The platform selects a permitted channel, renders a versioned template, calls external providers, and exposes delivery status.

    Assume:

    • 40 million notifications/day; normal peak is 8,000 requests/s and a product launch can produce 40,000 requests/s for ten minutes;
    • payload metadata averages 1 KB; rendered bodies average 4 KB and are retained for 30 days;
    • a tenant may send 5 million notifications in one campaign;
    • API acceptance p99 is under 300 ms with 99.95% monthly availability;
    • an accepted request must not disappear; duplicate provider delivery is undesirable but providers can time out after accepting;
    • user consent and tenant quotas must be enforced;
    • delivery status may lag by one minute;
    • start in one region across three zones; describe a credible path to a second region.

    You may clarify details. State assumptions when no evaluator is present.

    Candidate timeline¶

    Time Task Required artifact
    0-7 min Clarify and frame Prioritized requirements, exclusions, invariants, SLOs
    7-15 min Estimate Peak, campaign skew, storage, concurrency or bandwidth, headroom
    15-25 min Contracts API, identifiers, state machine, core records
    25-42 min Baseline and scale Diagram plus accepted-notification trace
    42-55 min Break and recover Three failure traces, overload policy, recovery evidence
    55-65 min Evolution Second-region and schema/provider migration plan
    65-71 min Evaluator changes Respond to two challenge cards
    71-75 min Close Recommendation, top risks, validation metrics

    Challenge cards¶

    The evaluator chooses two. A solo learner reveals them only at minute 65.

    Reveal challenge cards at minute 65
    1. One provider's p99 rises from 400 ms to 20 seconds while returning intermittent success.
    2. A celebrity campaign sends 80% of the launch traffic to one tenant and one template.
    3. Legal requires a tenant-specific data residency boundary in the new region.
    4. A template schema change must roll out while old producers continue for 60 days.
    5. A zone fails during the 40,000 requests/s launch spike.
    6. A provider callback arrives before the worker records its attempt result.

    Candidate stop line¶

    Do not open the evaluator material until the attempt and 30-minute self-review break are complete.

    Evaluator rubric, reference outline, and worked scoring

    Answer rubric¶

    Score each dimension 0-4 using the competency scale. Multiply by the weight.

    Dimension Weight Full-credit evidence
    Framing and invariants 2 Separates acceptance from delivery; states consent, tenant isolation, durable acceptance, and duplicate-effect expectations.
    Quantitative reasoning 2 Calculates useful values and recognizes the 5x spike plus tenant/template skew.
    API and state 2 Uses request/idempotency IDs, stable status semantics, template version, tenant scope, and pagination.
    Baseline design 3 Keeps acceptance short, durably records work, processes asynchronously, and traces provider interaction.
    Scale and isolation 2 Uses bounded partitions/queues, fair admission, quotas, and hot-tenant isolation without claiming queues create capacity.
    Consistency and duplicates 3 Makes acceptance idempotent, defines attempt identity, handles ambiguous provider outcomes, and reconciles callbacks.
    Failure and recovery 3 Defines deadlines, retry budgets, poison handling, rebuild/replay, zone loss behavior, and recovery evidence.
    Evolution and migration 2 Provides compatibility, staged traffic, data movement, rollback, and decommission gates.
    Operations and security 2 Covers consent authorization, secrets, audit, SLIs, alerts, and capacity/lag signals.
    Communication 1 Structured, requirement-led, responsive, and closes with risks and triggers.

    Maximum weighted points: 4 * 22 = 88.

    Critical checks¶

    Mark the attempt unsafe regardless of total if it:

    • acknowledges before durable storage without stating the loss tradeoff;
    • retries provider calls indefinitely or promises exactly-once delivery;
    • permits cross-tenant reads or bypasses consent;
    • has no overload behavior at 40,000 requests/s;
    • proposes active-active writes without conflict, fencing, or ownership semantics.

    Reference answer outline¶

    This is a scoring key, not a required architecture.

    Contract and useful estimates¶

    • For these estimates, daily volume and send-rate figures mean expanded recipient notifications. If they instead count audience API commands, multiply by measured audience size before sizing workers or storage.
    • Invariants: every accepted command has durable status; tenant A cannot access tenant B; no new dispatch authorized after an opt-out wins the policy/dispatch serialization boundary; repeated command ID returns the original logical result.
    • Delivery is at-least-once processing with idempotent local effects. Provider ambiguity prevents a universal exactly-once claim.
    • Average intake is 40,000,000 / 86,400 = 463/s; normal peak is already 17x average and launch peak is 5x normal peak.
    • One 5-million campaign over ten minutes averages 8,333 notifications/s before retries and channel fan-out.
    • Thirty-day logical body storage is about 40M * 4 KB * 30 = 4.8 TB; 1 KB metadata adds about 1.2 TB if retained for the same 30 days, before indexes and replicas.
    • If mean provider occupancy is 500 ms at 8,000 sends/s, Little's Law gives about 4,000 in-flight calls. If that mean rises to 20 seconds, concurrency would become 160,000, so workers need strict per-provider limits. The challenge supplies p99, not mean; do not substitute that percentile into Little's Law as a measured average.

    Contracts and records¶

    POST /v1/tenants/{tenantId}/notifications
Idempotency-Key: command-id
{recipient|audience, templateId, templateVersion, variables, channelPolicy}

202 {notificationId, status: ACCEPTED}
GET /v1/tenants/{tenantId}/notifications/{notificationId}
GET /v1/tenants/{tenantId}/notifications?cursor=...&status=...

    Core records: notification command, recipient expansion or campaign cursor, immutable template version, consent snapshot/reference, delivery attempt, provider receipt, status event, and audit record. State might progress from ACCEPTED to ELIGIBLE to DISPATCHING to DELIVERED, FAILED, or SUPPRESSED; callback ordering requires monotonic transition rules rather than arrival-order overwrites.

    Baseline¶

    flowchart LR
    P[Producer] --> A[Admission API]
    A --> D[(Canonical store)]
    D --> O[Outbox relay]
    O --> Q[Partitioned work log]
    Q --> W[Channel workers]
    W --> V[External providers]
    V --> C[Callback API]
    C --> D
    D --> S[Status API]

    The API authenticates tenant scope, checks quota and a fast consent policy, validates a pinned template version, and atomically stores the command plus outbox record. Acceptance means this transaction committed across the required zone durability boundary. Expansion is chunked and resumable. Work partitions preserve only the ordering scope actually needed; tenant-aware admission and weighted scheduling prevent one campaign from consuming all capacity.

    An opt-out cannot recall a request already handed to a provider. Serialize the final consent decision with dispatch authorization and state this boundary in the user contract. Workers claim an attempt with a stable attempt ID, recheck current consent when policy requires it, apply per-provider concurrency and retry budgets, and send the attempt ID when the provider supports idempotency. A timeout produces UNKNOWN, not immediate blind retry. Callback and poll-based reconciliation converge state. Status projections may lag and can rebuild from durable events.

    Failure and evolution¶

    • Provider slowdown: open per-provider circuit, stop creating unbounded in-flight work, route only when policy permits, delay low-priority traffic, and expose age by tenant/channel.
    • Zone loss at launch: capacity must sustain core traffic after loss or admission must reject/defer by priority. Queue age and accepted-to-dispatched SLO show recovery.
    • Poison template: bounded retries, quarantine by template version, preserve unrelated partitions, repair, and replay.
    • Region two: begin with disaster-recovery replication and fenced failover. Assign tenant home regions for writes if residency applies. Replicate only allowed data, shadow status reads, canary tenants, prove lag/RPO, then increase traffic. Roll back routing before removing old copies.
    • Schema/template change: readers accept old and new; writers initially emit old, then dual-read or dual-write only with a reconciliation artifact; backfill with checkpoints; switch reads; retain rollback; decommission after old-producer and discrepancy gates pass.

    Worked evaluator notes¶

    Example notes for a plausible candidate answer:

    Observation Score implication
    "Acceptance means the database transaction committed; delivery is later." Baseline and framing at least 2.
    Calculates average QPS and storage but ignores campaign concentration and zone-loss capacity. Estimation 2, not 3.
    Uses idempotency key for API but says a queue gives exactly-once provider delivery. Consistency 1; ask about timeout after provider acceptance.
    On a directional challenge, changes timeout retry to an UNKNOWN attempt plus provider query/callback reconciliation. Consistency is 2 here because key lifetime and safe replay remain unexplained; a directional probe alone permits up to 3, unlike direct repair instruction.
    Adds per-tenant admission and weighted scheduling after seeing skew. Scale 2; level 3 needs thresholds and saturation signals.
    Says "multi-region database" with no write ownership or rollback. Evolution 1 and critical follow-up required.
    Names queue age, provider unknown-attempt age, suppression count, and acceptance errors. Operations 3 if alerts and actions are connected.

    Weighted total Diagnostic interpretation
    0-43 Developing; weighted average is below level 2 or a critical gate remains
    44-65 Coherent baseline with material depth gaps
    66-76 L5-depth evidence, subject to per-dimension and critical-gate checks

Do not invent a successful follow-up. Route to ambiguity drills, failure card F1 with constraint C2, and migration labs M1-M2 before another full mock.

    Study routing¶

    Finding First route Proof before retest
    Framing below 2 Delivery framework, drills R1-R4 and P1 Produce a five-minute contract with three decision-changing questions.
    Estimation below 2 Estimation, drills C1-C10 Complete eight calculations with units and a design consequence.
    API/data below 2 API and data modeling, drills R5-R6 and I2 Trace identifiers and state transitions through one retry.
    Scale below 2 Scaling writes and contention, drills D3-D6 Identify limiting resource, skew, saturation metric, and overload action.
    Consistency below 2 Consistency, failure cards F1-F3 Resolve three ambiguous outcomes without exactly-once claims.
    Reliability below 2 Reliability, all failure decks Complete Design-Break-Recover tables with recovery evidence.
    Evolution below 2 Migration labs Submit compatibility matrix, rollout gates, and rollback trigger twice.
    Communication below 2 Pressure drills P1-P10 and interviewer mode Give a five-minute summary with recommendation, risk, and trigger.

    Retake after at least seven days using the same prompt only if you keep the challenge cards hidden. Otherwise use a new case and the same rubric.
