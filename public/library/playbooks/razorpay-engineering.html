Chapter 01The company is a money-movement platform

A checkout button is the visible entrance to a much larger system. Behind it are merchant onboarding, payment attempts, bank integrations, risk decisions, accounting, settlement, refunds, disputes, reporting, and people who resolve exceptions. Building a Razorpay-like company means building these responsibilities together, not copying the appearance of a checkout page.

The documented product landscape

Razorpay's public portfolio spans payment acceptance, commerce tools, subscriptions, marketplace transfers, orchestration, POS, and banking-related software. Its documentation also covers partners, developer tooling, payroll, and engagement products. Availability is country-, merchant-, bank-, and integration-dependent; a product appearing in the catalog does not establish eligibility for every customer. [S01] [S02] [S03]

Build around four independent promises

Accept an instruction. A merchant must be able to create an order, attempt a payment, or request a payout without accidentally duplicating the economic action when the network retries.

Discover what happened. Banks and networks can respond late, return conflicting observations, or provide final evidence only in reconciliation files. The platform must retain uncertainty rather than invent success or failure.

Account for the obligation. For every accepted financial event, the system must know who owes whom, in which currency and legal entity, under which agreement. This is a ledger responsibility, not a dashboard calculation.

Discharge and prove the obligation. Settlement, payout, refund, and dispute handling change obligations. Independent statements and operational evidence must prove that expected money movement actually occurred.

Separate these promises in the architecture. A durable API response proves only that an instruction was accepted. A payment-success event may justify fulfilment under the relevant product rules, but does not mean the merchant has received its bank settlement. A dashboard balance may include unsettled, reserved, or disputed amounts that are not available to withdraw.

A proposed product-to-service map

Product family Core services to reuse Distinct responsibility
Gateway, links, QR, POS Orders, attempts, connectors, risk Channel-specific customer experience
Subscriptions and mandates Schedules, consent, attempts Authority to initiate future debits
Route and marketplaces Allocation, ledger, settlement Beneficiary eligibility and split obligations
Banking and payouts Beneficiaries, approvals, instructions Bank-account and outbound-rail controls
Reporting and engagement Event projections, consent, analytics Do not become an alternate financial truth

The company boundary matters

A software provider, payment aggregator, acquiring bank, issuing bank, lending partner, and merchant are different parties. Do not give a software service the rights of a bank merely because its dashboard displays an account. Every product needs an explicit legal entity, regulated partner, permitted money flow, customer agreement, and operations owner before engineering enables it.

Build artifact. Maintain a capability register keyed by product, country, legal entity, rail, partner, merchant category, SDK version, and effective date. Each entry contains its onboarding requirements, contract, limits, support model, reconciliation source, and kill switch. This register should drive both checkout eligibility and internal launch approvals.

Validation question. Can an engineer trace one customer's payment from consent to instruction, external reference, journal entries, bank evidence, and merchant settlement without relying on a screenshot or a person's memory? If not, the platform is not yet end-to-end.

The strongest architectural lesson

The data-platform publications describe a distinction between operational sources and downstream analytical systems. In our design, we preserve that separation deliberately: financial decisions use an authoritative transactional store; reports and models use derived data with explicit watermarks. An analytics outage should not prevent a valid payment from being recorded, and an analytics replay should never create a new financial posting.

The deployment talk illustrates why regional expansion is also a configuration-management problem. Copying pipelines allows policies and release behavior to drift. Our design uses a versioned deployment specification that declares data residency, service dependencies, rollout policy, and rollback requirements. The specification is tested before it produces regional deployment plans. This is a design inference, not a claim about undisclosed Razorpay implementation details.

Newer engineering disclosures

Razorpay's May 2026 account describes an internal engineering agent called Slash, integrated with development and collaboration workflows. Its reported productivity and activity are company statements, not an independent assessment of code safety. [S10]

For our platform, assisted coding remains upstream of review, testing, provenance checks, and production approval. An agent may prepare a connector change, but must not bypass network certification, alter settlement configuration without approval, or execute unreviewed financial repairs. The more automated development becomes, the more valuable executable invariants and narrow production privileges become.

Unknowns that must stay unknown

Public sources reviewed here do not establish the complete authoritative ledger schema, consensus protocol, HSM topology, current per-product RTO/RPO, bank-specific retry contracts, or an exhaustive service inventory. The remainder of this book supplies a coherent proposed design for those areas. It is not a reverse-engineered copy of Razorpay's proprietary platform.

Practical exercise. For every architecture claim in a design review, mark it as documented, inferred, proposed, measured in your environment, or contractually guaranteed. A database choice is not justified merely because a company used it in a different subsystem. A performance improvement in a case study is not a capacity forecast.

Chapter 03Parties, rails, and the right to move money

Start with the parties, not the microservices. A payment is an agreement and a sequence of messages among organizations with different responsibilities. The message path, money path, and liability path may be different. A useful architecture draws all three.

The regulatory frame

India's RBI Payment Aggregator Directions of September 2025 consolidate requirements across relevant payment-aggregator categories, including online, physical, and cross-border activities. Licensing, merchant due diligence, permitted fund flows, and safeguarding must be assessed for the actual operating model. A gateway-style technical integration does not automatically authorize a company to collect and settle funds as an aggregator. [S64]

Model the parties explicitly

A customer authorizes a purchase. A merchant sells the goods or service. The platform accepts instructions and orchestrates the contracted payment service. An acquirer or banking partner provides access to acceptance and settlement arrangements. A network or payment system exchanges messages and establishes applicable operating rules. An issuer or remitter bank controls the payer's account or payment instrument. A beneficiary bank credits the destination account.

For cards, authorization, clearing, and settlement are distinct stages. For a bank-transfer rail, a successful debit/credit response and subsequent inter-participant settlement have their own semantics. Do not normalize every rail into a fictional card-style hold followed by capture. The platform may expose a common payment status, but the adapter must retain the underlying rail meaning.

Contracts become executable configuration

Each bank integration needs a signed capability contract represented in software. Record supported modes, currencies, amount bounds, merchant categories, request identifiers, duplicate detection behavior, query endpoints, timeouts, reversal behavior, cutoffs, statement formats, and escalation contacts. Add effective dates and a source reference for every operational limit.

A capability must answer questions such as: Can an accepted transfer be canceled? Does the partner preserve an idempotency key across a regional outage? Can a timeout be queried by our original reference? Does a successful HTTP response mean receipt, validation, or completed debit? These answers determine retry safety and customer communication.

Keep legal entities out of implicit assumptions

Every account, journal, settlement batch, bank instruction, and contract is scoped to a legal entity and currency. Cross-entity movement is an explicit intercompany or partner obligation, never a database update that silently transfers another entity's funds. A group-wide dashboard can aggregate balances for display, but cannot make those balances fungible.

An escrow or safeguarded account is not an unrestricted operating account. Payroll software is not automatically a deposit-taking business. A loan offer displayed in a dashboard does not make the software provider the lender. Regulatory counsel, finance, and partner operations must approve the flow before the engineering team makes it executable.

A useful design-review exercise

Take a proposed feature such as instant settlement. Draw the customer payment, the acquiring receivable, the safeguarded cash, the merchant payable, and any separately funded advance. Identify which entity owns each asset and liability. Then simulate a chargeback after the merchant has been paid. If nobody can identify who bears that loss, the feature is commercially incomplete even if its API works.

Launch gate. No production rail is enabled without an approved money-flow diagram, contractual authority, reconciliation feed, exception process, and named business owner. Legal applicability is reviewed per jurisdiction; this playbook is engineering education, not a substitute for that review.

Chapter 04Availability, correctness, and service commitments

A payment platform needs several service promises, not one impressive uptime number. API availability, payment success, settlement punctuality, support response, and financial correctness measure different things. A system can be technically reachable while customers cannot pay, and can accept requests reliably while accumulating unresolved money movement.

What is and is not established publicly

Razorpay's public terms point to service-specific supplemental agreements. The reviewed sources do not establish one universal contractual uptime, RTO, RPO, or support response commitment covering its whole portfolio. The official status endpoint is a useful operational surface, not a replacement for an executed SLA or an independently calculated history. [S04] [S82]

All numerical targets below are proposed planning objectives for our platform, not Razorpay commitments or measured results.

Indicator Proposed initial objective Measurement boundary

Local order-create latency p99 below 300 ms Server processing, excluding customer and bank time
Internal financial event ingestion 99.9% within 5 seconds From durable verified observation to durable processing
Healthy webhook delivery 99.9% first attempt within 10 seconds Our dispatch latency, not merchant endpoint availability

Settlement timeliness Contract/calendar-specific Due obligations paid or exception communicated by agreed deadline

Define the denominator before the target

Payment success rate might mean successful attempts divided by all attempts, or successful orders divided by unique checkout sessions. Those are different. A customer who retries three times can make attempt-level success look poor while order completion remains acceptable. Conversely, hiding customer abandonment from the denominator can make a broken checkout look healthy.

Track technical availability, bank technical failures, issuer declines, customer abandonment, risk declines, and business-rule rejection separately. Segment by rail, issuer, acquiring partner, merchant cohort, SDK, geography, and release. A valid insufficient-funds decline is not an internal availability failure, but it still matters to the customer journey. Never exclude internal overload by classifying it as a customer problem.

Error budgets are not a license to lose money

For a 30-day month, 99.99% time availability corresponds to 4.32 minutes of unavailability; 99.999% corresponds to 25.92 seconds. These are arithmetic illustrations, not a promise that a monthly average captures flash-sale impact. Use request-weighted and time-window views together.

Financial invariants have no acceptable corruption budget. If journal balancing, ownership, or writer fencing fails, stop affected money movement. The availability loss is preferable to an uncontrolled debit. Track safety incidents separately from latency incidents so that teams do not trade away correctness to improve a dashboard.

Use multi-window burn-rate alerts to identify sustained and rapidly developing budget consumption rather than paging on every isolated error. This follows the SRE approach to error-budget alerting. [S80]

Contract and recovery design

An SLA must specify products, exclusions, measurement source, notice windows, remedies, and dispute process. An RTO is the restoration objective; an RPO is the acceptable loss of acknowledged data under a stated failure. Asynchronous replication cannot by itself justify zero acknowledged financial-data loss. The customer-facing contract must fit the tested architecture, not precede it.

Acceptance test. Inject a bank outage, an internal database outage, a merchant webhook outage, and an issuer-decline spike. Four different dashboards and operational responses should result. If all four become the same red availability graph, the measurement model is too coarse.

Chapter 05Reference architecture: separate decisions from money

The proposed platform has a global discovery and edge layer, region-bound execution cells, an authoritative money core, and asynchronous operational projections. The most important boundary is not between two programming languages. It is between accepting an instruction, observing an external outcome, and changing a financial obligation.

The synchronous path

The edge authenticates the merchant, enforces quotas, resolves the operating entity and cell, and attaches a request identifier. The order service validates amount, currency, merchant eligibility, and the request's idempotency semantics. The payment orchestrator creates a durable attempt, obtains a versioned risk decision, chooses an eligible connector, and persists the intended external operation before sending it.

Keep the synchronous chain short. Checkout rendering should not synchronously call the data warehouse, invoice generator, engagement system, or finance reporting service. Risk dependencies need explicit timeout policies; a low-risk fallback may be allowed for one action while payouts or mandate changes fail closed. A single universal fail-open switch is unacceptable.

The authoritative money core

The money core contains journal accounts, immutable postings, reservations, financial operations, and settlement obligations. It accepts typed commands such as record_capture, reserve_payout, record_refund, and settle_obligation. Each command validates ownership, amount, currency, allowed state, and a unique business-event identifier.

The core does not trust a message merely because it arrived on an internal topic. It verifies the originating service, schema version, evidence reference, and permitted transition. For critical commands, the ledger and its durable event outbox commit in the same database transaction. The ledger never depends on a consumer successfully updating a search index to preserve correctness.

Connectors are isolation boundaries

A connector translates our instruction into a specific partner protocol and translates observations back without discarding native references. Give every partner its own connection pools, queues, rate limits, credentials, circuit breaker, and operational dashboard. One bank's slow sockets must not exhaust all outbound workers.

Connector workers may be stateless, but their operations are not. A durable operation row records request fingerprint, original partner reference, attempts, last observation, and uncertainty deadline. A new worker can resume investigation without inventing a new payment. Provider failover is permitted before an external commitment, or after definitive evidence makes a new attempt safe.

Control plane and asynchronous plane

The control plane manages merchant configuration, pricing, routing policy, feature flags, credentials, and approval rules. Runtime services consume signed or authenticated versioned snapshots. A control-plane outage should not invalidate already approved stable configuration, while emergency revocation has its own bounded propagation path.

The asynchronous plane handles webhooks, emails, reconciliation imports, statements, reporting, fraud features, and support projections. Each consumer has an idempotent inbox, retries, quarantine, and replay tooling. Events describe facts, not permission to repeat money movement.

Why cells rather than one global cluster

A cell contains a bounded merchant population and its critical execution dependencies. This limits overload and deployment blast radius and makes capacity and recovery tests meaningful. The directory mapping a merchant to a cell is small, strongly controlled, and cacheable. Cell migration is a coordinated data and authority transfer, not merely a DNS change.

Design test. Disable analytics, one bank connector, and merchant email delivery simultaneously. Unaffected rails must still accept and account for payments. The affected connector must expose a truthful degraded state, not trigger uncontrolled cross-bank retries.

Chapter 06The domain model and its independent state machines

One status column cannot describe a payment business. An order can be paid while its payment is partially refunded; a settlement can be pending after capture; a dispute can open after the merchant has received funds. Model these dimensions separately and relate them through explicit identifiers and business events.

Documented external semantics

Razorpay documents payment states including created, authorized, captured, refunded, and failed. Its Standard Checkout order lifecycle distinguishes created, attempted, and paid; an order remains paid after a refund. These are public API concepts, not a complete description of rail-level accounting. [S11] [S12]

Proposed entities

An order represents the merchant's commercial request. A payment attempt represents one effort to satisfy it through one method and route. An external operation represents one side-effecting partner instruction. An observation is a response, webhook, query result, or statement record. A financial event is our accepted interpretation of sufficient evidence. A journal records the resulting obligation change.

Keep refunds, disputes, transfers, settlements, and payouts as first-class entities rather than adding dozens of nullable fields to the payment row. A payment can have many refund requests; each refund can have multiple transport attempts but one economic identity. A settlement batch can contain many obligations, while each obligation retains lineage to its source events.

The transition rules

Use versioned transitions with optimistic compare-and-swap or row locking. A worker supplies the expected version and intended event. The database transaction validates the transition and writes the new state plus outbox. A duplicate observation is recorded or recognized but must not create a second journal.

UNKNOWN is an internal investigation state, not a euphemism for failure. It means the platform lacks sufficient evidence about an external commitment. A local timeout cannot make an already-sent debit safe to repeat. A later success may be valid even after the checkout page timed out. The commercial response may then be fulfilment, refund, or a support exception, depending on the order's state.

Do not build a simplistic total ordering such as failed < success and let any higher status win. Evidence sources have different authority and freshness. A provisional timeout, authenticated partner success, settled bank entry, and later reversal have different meanings. Preserve their event times and ingestion times; use a rail-specific reducer with explicit precedence and compensating events.

Amounts, identity, and time

Store money as integer minor units with currency metadata; do not assume every currency has two decimals. Use integer or fixed-precision arithmetic for rates and intermediate calculations with documented rounding. Store UTC instants, business dates, bank value dates, and local settlement calendars separately.

Every entity is scoped to merchant, operating legal entity, environment, and currency where applicable. Test and live identifiers must never address the same resources. A support tool should display the commercial order, attempt, external reference, and financial operation together so that operators cannot confuse transport retries with multiple purchases.

Property test. Generate arbitrary duplicate and reordered observations. Replaying the same evidence set should converge on the same accepted financial history, without a second capture or refund journal. A genuine later reversal should produce an additional compensating event, not erase the earlier success.

Chapter 07Merchant onboarding, underwriting, and activation

Merchant onboarding is the first risk-control system. It establishes who the customer is, what they sell, where they operate, which settlement account they control, and which products they may use. Treat it as an evidence-backed workflow, not a form that eventually flips active=true.

Legal and product scope

The RBI's payment-aggregator framework includes merchant due-diligence and operating obligations. The exact evidence and checks depend on the entity, product, merchant category, and applicable rules. Our implementation must be reviewed against that framework and partner requirements, not use one hard-coded checklist for every business. [S64]

The proposed onboarding pipeline

Create a merchant application with a stable identifier and versioned business profile. Collect business identity, ownership and authorized-person information, business category, website or app details, expected volumes, fulfilment model, refund policy, and proposed bank account. Store sensitive evidence in a restricted document vault; ordinary application tables contain references and verification outcomes.

Verification jobs are asynchronous and independently repeatable. A document-check provider outage should not lose an application. Each check records input fingerprint, provider reference, decision, confidence or reason, reviewer, and expiry. A manual reviewer can request clarification without overwriting the original submission. Changes to legal identity or settlement ownership trigger targeted re-verification.

Activation is a capability vector

A merchant may be permitted to accept domestic cards but not international payments, or to use payment links but not marketplace splits. Represent activation as effective-dated capabilities, limits, reserves, and product agreements. A single account-level status is only a summary.

Separate commercial approval from technical readiness. A merchant integration must also demonstrate server-side verification, webhook handling, refund controls, and reconciliation access. A valid business with an unsafe integration should not receive unrestricted production volume simply because its documents passed review.

Underwriting changes after launch

The initial risk assessment estimates expected transaction size, velocity, fulfilment delay, chargeback exposure, and concentration. Actual behavior may diverge. Compare observed activity with the approved profile; route material changes to monitoring or re-underwriting. Increased reserves or payout restrictions require a documented authority, reason, effective date, and customer communication process.

Avoid silent account-wide shutdowns for every anomaly. Controls should distinguish new acceptance, refunds, settlements, account changes, and access to historical records. A suspended merchant may still require a lawful refund or dispute response. Conversely, allowing account access must not imply permission to redirect funds.

Secure bank-account changes

Treat a settlement-account change as a high-risk financial action. Validate ownership, require strong approval, notify established contacts, enforce a risk-based delay where appropriate, and preserve the previous destination. Approval binds to the exact account fingerprint and merchant entity. Editing the account after approval invalidates the approval.

Launch artifact. Produce an activation dossier containing evidence references, approved capabilities, contract versions, risk limits, settlement destination, initial monitoring plan, and reviewer signatures. The dossier is auditable without exposing raw identity documents to every engineer.

Adversarial test. An attacker gains dashboard access and changes the bank account just before a large settlement. The existing batch must retain its approved destination snapshot or be explicitly re-approved; it must not silently pick up the new account at execution time.

Chapter 08Merchant dashboard, configuration, and partner platforms

The dashboard is a financial control surface. It can reveal sensitive data, change who gets paid, alter routing, and initiate refunds. Its backend needs the same rigor as the payment API, even when the user action looks like a simple button click.

Public product surface

Razorpay documents merchant tools and partner integrations alongside its payment APIs. The partner model creates additional relationships between the platform, an integrating partner, and the underlying merchant. Those relationships need explicit authorization and attribution rather than shared administrator credentials. [S02] [S59]

Separate reads, proposals, and execution

Read-only dashboards query projections with visible freshness markers. A balance view shows the ledger watermark, settled and pending amounts, reservations, disputes, and currency. It does not calculate available funds by subtracting two eventually consistent analytics totals.

A configuration change creates a proposed version. Validation checks product eligibility, syntax, bank capability, commercial agreement, and conflicts. High-risk changes require independent approval. Publishing assigns an effective time and immutable version. Runtime decisions record the version they used so that support can explain why a payment took a particular route.

Permissions follow financial risk

Use distinct permissions for viewing transactions, exporting customer data, issuing refunds, approving payouts, editing beneficiaries, changing API credentials, changing settlement accounts, and publishing routing rules. Scope them by merchant or business unit and impose monetary limits where useful. A user who can create a payout should not automatically be able to approve their own instruction.

For a partner platform, maintain explicit merchant consent, resource scope, product entitlement, and revocation behavior. A partner request records both the acting partner and affected merchant. Never infer merchant ownership from a user-supplied identifier alone; resolve it through the authorized relationship on every operation.

Configuration is production code

A routing rule can create more damage than a code release. Give it schema validation, simulation against historical traffic, staged rollout, a rollback pointer, and an audit trail. Pricing and tax configurations are effective-dated, and changes cannot retroactively rewrite booked fees. A feature flag that changes money semantics must be attached to a documented migration plan.

Emergency controls require careful granularity. An operator may disable a failing connector for new attempts while allowing its status queries, callbacks, refunds, and reconciliation jobs to continue. A blunt service shutdown can prevent the very evidence needed to resolve existing transactions.

Export and support safety

Large exports are asynchronous jobs with tenant-scoped filters, signed short-lived access, encryption, retention limits, and audit events. Redact credentials, full account details, and unnecessary personal data. Support views show masked values and evidence links; they do not expose secrets just because troubleshooting is urgent.

Acceptance test. Revoke a partner's access while an export and a payout proposal are pending. The export must re-check authorization before release; the financial proposal must follow the revocation policy without losing its audit history. An already executed bank payment cannot be undone by deleting the partner relationship.

Operational metric. Measure configuration propagation and stale-version rejection separately from API latency. A platform that serves fast responses using an obsolete settlement restriction is not operating correctly.

Chapter 09Orders, attempts, and idempotent APIs

The order is the merchant's stable commercial intent; the attempt is one effort to pay it. Keeping them separate lets customers retry without turning one purchase into several unrelated records. It also gives the merchant a reliable place to attach inventory, fulfilment, and refund decisions.

Public integration contract

Razorpay's Standard Checkout flow creates an order on the merchant's server and passes its identifier into checkout. The server must verify the returned payment information rather than trust a browser success message. [S12]

Our proposed write contract

POST /orders accepts merchant reference, amount, currency, capture policy, and an idempotency key. The key is scoped to merchant, environment, and operation type. Canonicalize the relevant request fields and store their hash. Repeating the same key and same payload returns the original operation; repeating the key with different economic parameters returns a conflict.

The database transaction inserts the idempotency record, order, and outbox event atomically. A unique constraint resolves concurrent first requests. Do not perform a separate cache lookup followed by an unprotected insert: two workers can both observe a miss. A cache can accelerate replay responses but cannot be the sole record of a financial command.

The general technique of preserving caller intent across retries is described in AWS's idempotent-API guidance. Our retention and conflict rules are deliberately part of the product contract. [S79]

Two identities for two purposes

A request identifier traces an individual transport exchange. An idempotency key identifies one intended business operation. A payment-attempt identifier identifies one customer attempt. A partner reference identifies the external instruction. They are not interchangeable.

Suppose a mobile client times out while creating an order. It retries with the same idempotency key, not a new merchant reference. Suppose the customer deliberately changes from card to UPI after a definitive unsuccessful attempt. That is a new payment attempt under the same order, subject to the rule that an already-paid order cannot accept another successful fulfilment.

Concurrency at completion

Two methods can finish nearly simultaneously. The platform must serialize the commercial decision about satisfying the order, but it cannot pretend that a second externally successful payment did not happen. Record both external outcomes, allocate one to the order, and route the excess success to an explicit refund or exception workflow. Hiding the second payment would leave unaccounted customer money.

Use a uniqueness constraint for the order's accepted fulfilment allocation, not a constraint that prevents recording additional external evidence. The ledger must account for every actual financial event, including unwanted duplicates.

Retention and expiry

An API idempotency cache may expire, but an economic uniqueness record must remain long enough to cover the operation's entire retry and reconciliation horizon. Publish the replay-retention contract. For a payout or refund, preserve a permanent or appropriately retained business-operation reference even when the original HTTP response body is archived.

Failure test. Crash after database commit but before returning the response. The retry must recover the original order. Crash after partner acceptance but before storing the response. The retry must investigate the existing external operation, not create a new payment.

Implementation rule. The platform's own API may support broad idempotency. Do not assume a downstream provider supports the same header or semantics. Each connector must translate and document the guarantee it actually has.

Chapter 10Checkout across web, mobile, and backend systems

Checkout has two simultaneous goals: make a legitimate payment easy and prevent an untrusted client from deciding the commercial outcome. The browser and mobile app render the experience, but the merchant server owns price, currency, order identity, and fulfilment.

The documented trust boundary

For Razorpay Standard Checkout, the payment signature is verified server-side using the server-known order identifier and returned payment identifier. This is different from webhook signature validation. API secrets must not be embedded in a browser or mobile application. [S12]

Proposed checkout session design

The merchant backend creates an order and a short-lived checkout session containing permitted methods, amount, currency, locale, expiry, and a signed reference to server-side state. The client receives only public configuration and a session token. It cannot override the amount by editing JavaScript or replay an old session against a different merchant.

The checkout runtime obtains eligible methods from a versioned capability service. Eligibility combines merchant activation, currency, country, amount, device platform, network capability, and current connector health. Do not show a method that will certainly fail after the customer enters credentials. Conversely, a transient analytics failure should not erase otherwise valid methods.

Browser and mobile behavior

Treat redirects, app switches, WebViews, popup blocking, lost network connectivity, and app backgrounding as normal states. Persist the order reference in the merchant application. When the user returns, query server-side status and show pending when evidence is incomplete. A deep link that says success=true is a hint to refresh, not proof of payment.

Hosted payment fields or a hosted checkout can reduce the merchant's handling of sensitive card data, but actual compliance scope depends on the integration and payment-page controls. SDK supply-chain integrity, origin restrictions, content-security policy, and change monitoring remain important. Avoid custom credential collection unless the organization can support the required security and certification scope.

Fulfilment is a separate idempotent operation

The backend accepts a verified payment event and evaluates whether the order is eligible for fulfilment. It writes fulfilment_requested with a unique order-level key. Inventory, shipping, tickets, or digital entitlement systems consume that request idempotently. A browser callback and a webhook may both arrive; neither should create a second shipment.

If payment succeeds after inventory expires, do not silently overwrite the order to paid-and-shipped. Move to a paid-but-unfulfillable exception and follow the merchant's refund or substitution policy. The customer should see a truthful pending resolution rather than an invented failed payment.

Accessibility and security are product requirements

Support keyboard navigation, clear error messages, readable amount and merchant identity, and safe recovery from interrupted flows. Do not force customers to distinguish internal bank error codes. Display retry guidance only after the backend determines whether another attempt is safe.

Test matrix. Include repeated taps, browser back, two tabs, app termination during bank authentication, changed cart totals, stale checkout tokens, forged signatures, duplicate success callbacks, and a successful payment whose browser response never arrives. The expected result must specify both the customer message and the durable financial state.

Chapter 11Cards: authorization, capture, clearing, and settlement

Card payments are not one operation. Authentication establishes something about the person or device; authorization asks whether a transaction may proceed; capture submits the amount for financial completion under the acquiring arrangement; clearing and settlement establish and discharge obligations. These stages can occur at different times and fail differently.

Design a rail-aware adapter

Our card connector retains network and acquirer references, authorization code where provided, authentication outcome, amount, currency, merchant category, token reference, and lifecycle timestamps. Sensitive fields are minimized and stored only where permitted. Native response codes are retained alongside normalized categories so that operations can distinguish issuer decline, invalid request, network error, and uncertain processing.

Razorpay exposes authorized and captured states in its public payment lifecycle. Its normalized interface is useful to merchants, but an engineer must still understand the actual acquiring contract and rail behavior beneath that interface. [S11]

Authorization is not company cash

A successful authorization can represent a payer-side hold or approval. In our accounting model it does not automatically debit a cash account or recognize settled funds. Track the authorization as operational or memorandum state until the appropriate financial event occurs. Capture may create an acquiring receivable and merchant obligation, subject to the contractual accounting model.

Automatic capture simplifies commerce but may increase refunds when inventory is uncertain. Manual capture gives the merchant time to validate fulfilment but introduces expiry and missed-capture risks. Store capture policy on the original attempt; changing merchant defaults later must not retroactively change an in-flight authorization.

Partial and repeated operations

Maintain authorized amount, captured amount, refunded amount, and disputed amount as separately validated aggregates. Support partial capture only when the partner and product explicitly allow it. Serialize concurrent capture requests and reject amounts exceeding the remaining eligible authorization. A second HTTP response must not be mistaken for permission to capture twice.

Capture requests have their own economic identifiers. If a connector times out, query by the original reference or use a partner-supported duplicate-safe retry. Do not release the customer to another acquiring route merely because capture confirmation is delayed.

Clearing differences and exceptions

A bank file may contain adjusted amounts, fees, reversals, or references that differ from the real-time response. Reconciliation matches the strongest available identifiers and raises exceptions for unsupported differences. It must never silently alter the original authorized amount to make a file balance.

For expired or canceled authorizations, distinguish our cancellation request from confirmed release at the issuer. The customer-facing message should not promise immediate restoration of available funds when the platform does not control that bank action.

Production controls

Separate test credentials, merchant IDs, terminal IDs, and cryptographic material from live processing. Certify each message type and edge case with the acquirer. Store a connector version on each operation so that later investigation can reproduce field mappings and response interpretation.

Acceptance scenario. Authorization succeeds, the capture response is lost, and a later statement shows the captured transaction. The platform must record one capture, create the correct obligation, and avoid a second debit. A delayed failure-like transport response cannot erase stronger financial evidence.

Chapter 12Payment authentication and EMV 3-D Secure

Payment authentication is a rail-specific security step, not a guarantee that funds will move. A customer can authenticate successfully and still receive an issuer decline. A frictionless authentication result is not the same as skipping all risk controls.

Standards and current rules

EMV 3-D Secure supports information exchange between merchant-side and issuer-side components, including frictionless and challenge experiences. The issuer's authentication decision and the subsequent payment authorization remain distinct. [S73] [S74]

RBI's September 2025 authentication directions have a general April 2026 implementation date and a separately dated October 2026 cross-border issuer provision. At this book's 26 September 2026 cutoff, the latter date is still upcoming. Applicability and exemptions must be implemented from the actual rule, not from a blanket assumption that every payment requires an SMS OTP. [S68]

Proposed authentication orchestration

The payment attempt creates an authentication session bound to merchant, amount, currency, instrument reference, device context, and expiry. It records the protocol version and directory/issuer routing context. Browser or SDK messages are correlated to that session; an authentication result for one amount cannot be substituted into another order.

Keep challenge state in a durable short-lived store. A customer who returns from an out-of-band challenge should resume the same session. Repeated callbacks are idempotent. A session timeout becomes an explicit incomplete outcome; it does not automatically imply authorization was never attempted elsewhere in the flow.

Risk data without uncontrolled collection

Collect only signals justified by the payment and security purpose. Separate device attributes from durable customer identity. Protect authentication payloads from logs, analytics exports, and support screenshots. Data minimization should be a schema decision, not a later masking job that tries to find secrets in arbitrary JSON.

The risk system can request stronger authentication where supported, but cannot invent a protocol exemption or liability outcome. The connector records the actual issuer/network result. Any liability-shift determination belongs to the applicable scheme and acquiring rules; it is not a generic boolean set by the checkout team.

Failure and fallback

If the challenge provider is unavailable, eligible fallback must follow the rail and regulatory contract. Do not silently downgrade all customers to a weaker path to improve conversion. A fallback may require selecting another payment method, displaying a clear temporary limitation, or declining the attempt safely.

Authentication retry budgets must distinguish a new customer attempt from repeated delivery of the same protocol message. Limit challenge loops and present meaningful recovery. Replaying a successful authentication cryptogram into an unrelated payment is forbidden even when the instrument and customer appear identical.

Test the whole boundary

Test challenge success with authorization decline, challenge timeout with a late callback, changed amount after challenge, duplicate challenge completion, wrong session binding, unsupported protocol version, and inaccessible mobile return paths. Verify that the resulting operational state and customer message remain consistent.

Design lesson. Keep authentication evidence attached to the attempt and retain enough metadata to explain the decision, but never retain authentication secrets merely for convenience. A secure payment experience is a chain of correctly bound steps, not one successful OTP screen.

Chapter 13Tokenization, saved cards, and CardSync

A saved-card experience should save an authorized reference, not create a new collection of raw card credentials. Token lifecycle, merchant binding, customer consent, and revocation are central parts of the feature. The convenient checkout surface must not obscure those boundaries.

Regulatory and product evidence

RBI's card-on-file framework restricts storage of actual card data by entities other than permitted card issuers and networks; its tokenization explanation describes consent and card/merchant-specific token creation. Do not build a generic persistent PAN vault for Indian merchant saved-card convenience. [S76] [S77]

Razorpay's September 2026 CardSync announcement describes a Google Pay partnership for eligible saved-card checkout experiences. The reviewed CardSync integration page documents a narrower CRED/Visa/browser eligibility set. Treat the later announcement and the narrower integration guidance as different evidence, and verify rollout eligibility before enabling a feature. Announced future biometrics are not universal current availability. [S45] [S46]

Proposed token service

Store token reference, token provider, merchant binding, instrument display metadata, consent evidence, creation time, expiry, status, and permitted usage. Keep the raw network token or cryptographic material in the appropriate restricted domain if handling is authorized; ordinary services use an opaque internal reference.

A token is not automatically portable across merchants, networks, acquiring routes, or use cases. The routing engine must check token compatibility before selecting a connector. An otherwise healthy alternate acquirer may be unusable for the current token. Falling back to raw stored card data is not an acceptable workaround.

Consent and discovery

Saved-card discovery asks whether the customer is entitled to use a particular tokenized instrument in the current context. It must not reveal another person's card metadata merely because a phone number or email matches. Bind the discovery session to an authenticated customer interaction and the relevant consent mechanism.

Keep the consent text version and purpose, but avoid pretending that one checkbox authorizes every future payment. One-time checkout, recurring mandates, and agent-assisted purchases have different authority. A saved token reduces data entry; it does not by itself authorize an unattended debit.

Lifecycle and failure handling

Handle token suspension, deletion, expiry, reissue, and provider notification as versioned events. A token may become unusable after checkout renders. Revalidate at payment initiation and give the customer a safe alternate method rather than retry indefinitely.

If a token provider is unavailable, isolate its dependency. Customers using other methods should continue. Track discovery latency separately from payment authorization latency so that a slow convenience feature does not hold the entire checkout hostage.

Security and tests

Encrypt sensitive token material, limit decrypt privileges, rotate wrapping keys, and exclude it from general telemetry. Token references in logs should be non-reversible and scoped. Deletion requests must reconcile product revocation with lawful financial-record retention; retain necessary transaction evidence without retaining reusable payment credentials unnecessarily.

Adversarial test. A token valid for Merchant A is submitted to Merchant B, or a one-time checkout token is reused for a recurring debit. Both must fail before a bank instruction is sent. A token-status callback received twice must not delete unrelated instruments or mutate historical transactions.

Chapter 14UPI intent, QR, and the Collect migration

UPI payment acceptance is a coordinated interaction between the merchant, payment platform, UPI application, participating banks, and the payment system. The checkout should choose an eligible interaction pattern, bind it to the correct order, and learn the result from trusted server-side evidence.

Current migration status

Razorpay documents a 28 February 2026 migration away from UPI Collect's manual VPA or mobile-number entry for affected integrations. The notice lists exemptions, including specified financial-market categories, iOS contexts, certain mandate operations, eRupi, and PA-CB. Existing recurring mandate execution is distinguished from new registration. Do not label all Collect flows universally unavailable. [S43] [S44]

Intent on supported mobile contexts

Our checkout creates a UPI attempt with amount, currency, merchant identity, order binding, expiry, and a unique transaction reference. It launches an eligible UPI app through the supported intent mechanism. The app-switch result returns the customer to the merchant, but fulfilment waits for trusted backend evidence.

The merchant backend must not accept a client-submitted transaction reference as proof that the intended beneficiary received the intended amount. Match partner reference, merchant, amount, and status. A malicious application can forge a return URL or display a fake success screen.

QR for compatible contexts

A dynamic QR is bound to a particular order or payment request and amount. A static merchant QR is useful for open-ended collection but requires a different matching strategy. The system must distinguish them in its data model rather than assume every QR scan identifies a unique invoice.

Expiry controls the merchant's willingness to accept a new attempt, but cannot erase a payment that arrives after expiry. A late successful transfer becomes a matched late payment or an exception requiring refund or fulfilment policy. Never discard it because the QR record is no longer active.

Status and reversals

Maintain a bounded polling schedule and consume authenticated partner notifications. Polling backs off and stops at product-specific investigation thresholds; it does not mark all unresolved payments failed at a generic timeout. Reconciliation and rail-specific status resolution continue beyond the customer's interactive session.

Record bank reference, payer/beneficiary identifiers as permitted, initiation and observation timestamps, and response categories. Mask personal payment addresses in routine logs. A reversal is a new economic event linked to the original payment, not an edit that makes the original success disappear.

Rollout and safety

Method selection is a versioned eligibility decision. Include device platform, SDK version, merchant category, product, and applicable exemption. Test mobile web, native apps, desktop QR, expired sessions, duplicate notifications, and applications that never return from the bank flow.

Migration artifact. Maintain a matrix of old flow, replacement flow, exempt population, minimum SDK, test cases, and merchant communication. Do not force a universal client update without verifying which integrations actually use the affected API path.

Customer rule. Display pending when the result is unknown, and explain how confirmation will arrive. A confident but false failure message can cause the customer to pay again and create the duplicate that the backend must later unwind.

Chapter 15Turbo UPI, TPAP Pro, and bank-switch integrations

An in-app UPI experience moves more responsibility into the integrating application and platform. It may reduce app switching, but it introduces onboarding, device binding, account discovery, credential-entry components, complaints, and operational dependencies that a simple redirect integration does not own.

Documented scope

Razorpay describes Turbo UPI as an in-app payment experience with onboarding, transaction, and account-related flows. Its TPAP Pro documentation describes a broader UPI application platform, including customer onboarding, account management, payments, mandates, and complaints. These are distinct integration scopes, not interchangeable names for a faster checkout. [S24] [S25]

The TPAP Pro VPA-linking API documents customer and device context alongside payment-source linking. An API example is evidence of an integration surface, not proof that any merchant can become an approved TPAP without the required partnerships and certification. [S26]

Proposed architecture

Create separate services for device registration, customer enrollment, payment-source discovery, VPA management, mandate management, transaction orchestration, and complaint handling. A bank-switch adapter owns the specific partner protocol and operational contract. The mobile SDK handles approved secure credential-entry components; the merchant backend must not collect or log the customer's UPI PIN.

Device registration binds the customer session to the supported device and mobile-number verification process. A device change invalidates or re-evaluates the relevant enrollment state. Store an explicit lifecycle rather than assuming that a long-lived application login proves continuing control of the phone and linked accounts.

Account and VPA consistency

Linking a VPA and a payment source can involve several external steps. Persist a workflow with states such as requested, partner-pending, active, failed, and unlink-pending. A timeout after linking must trigger a query or reconciliation step, not blindly create another address.

Maintain provider-native identifiers separately from internal references. An account's display name or masked number is not a stable uniqueness key. A customer may have multiple accounts, and account availability can change after enrollment. Payment initiation validates the currently selected source and supported capability.

Operational responsibility expands

A full UPI application needs customer-visible history, complaint registration, status inquiry, dispute support, limits, fraud controls, and safe account removal. These are not optional administrative extras. The user experiences the application as a payment product even when a banking partner executes the underlying movement.

Monitor each partner independently: enrollment success, device-verification failures, account discovery, PIN-flow completion, transaction outcomes, and complaint aging. Payment conversion alone will hide an onboarding outage that prevents new users from ever reaching the payment screen.

Certification and rollout

Use bank and network certification environments and approved SDK versions. Maintain backward compatibility across mobile releases because users do not upgrade instantly. A server-side capability flag must be able to disable an unsafe flow for a particular version without blocking all existing customers from viewing history or raising complaints.

Failure test. Enrollment succeeds externally but the app is terminated before local confirmation. On restart, the application must recover the existing enrollment, not ask the user to create conflicting state. The same principle applies to account unlinking and mandate revocation.

Chapter 16UPI mandates, one-time blocks, and Reserve Pay

A mandate is authority with boundaries. It has a payer, beneficiary, amount rule, validity, purpose, and lifecycle. Treating a mandate as a reusable password for arbitrary debits is both unsafe and commercially misleading.

Separate the products

Recurring payment APIs and subscription scheduling are different abstractions: one initiates payments under an eligible recurring authority, while the other manages a billing schedule. Razorpay documents multiple recurring methods, including cards, UPI, and mandate-based bank collection. [S27]

NPCI's September 2026 consolidated circular describes Reserve Pay as a bank-account block supporting multiple successive debits within the mandate. It explicitly says that the block itself is not a payment guarantee; successful debit confirmation matters. This differs from a one-time mandate and from a normal recurring schedule. [S75]

Proposed mandate model

Store mandate ID, provider reference, customer and merchant binding, rail, maximum or fixed amount, frequency constraints, validity, authorized purpose, consent evidence, and state. For blocked-fund products, also track the provider-reported block reference and remaining authorized capacity. Do not treat our local remaining counter as authoritative bank balance.

Every debit request has its own operation ID and references the mandate version. Before sending it, validate current state, expiry, amount, usage limits, required notices, and purpose. Serialize concurrent local debits against the same remaining authority where necessary. The bank still decides whether the debit succeeds.

Revocation races

A revocation can race with an already-sent debit. Record revocation requested and revocation confirmed separately. Prevent new local submissions once revocation policy requires it, but continue resolving in-flight instructions. The user interface should distinguish a requested stop from a confirmed stop and explain any already-processing payment.

Do not delete the mandate to revoke it. Retain consent and transaction lineage under the applicable retention policy. A deleted row makes later disputes harder and can allow an old callback to recreate state incorrectly.

Agent-assisted commerce

An agent may help a user choose a purchase or prepare an instruction, but payment authority must remain explicit, bounded, and auditable. Bind any delegated action to merchant, purpose, maximum amount, expiry, and user consent. The agent cannot expand those boundaries by interpreting a conversation creatively.

Razorpay's 2026 agentic-payments announcement describes pilots rather than universal availability. Our proposed architecture treats such features as gated capabilities with separate certification and risk review. [S47]

Required tests

Test two simultaneous debits near the remaining limit, revocation during processing, expiry while a request is queued, duplicate debit notifications, partial usage followed by release, and disagreement between local counters and bank-reported state. The invariant is not merely that our database counter stays nonnegative; it is that every sent debit was authorized and every observed debit is accounted for.

Design rule. A reserved amount can improve the payment experience without becoming settled merchant money. Deliver goods according to the actual product's successful-debit requirements, not the comforting appearance of a block in the customer's account.

Chapter 17Netbanking, wallets, and ordinary bank transfers

A normalized payments API must preserve the distinctions among redirect-based netbanking, wallet authorization, and an incoming bank transfer. They share some merchant-facing concepts, but their initiation, authentication, expiry, refund, and reconciliation behavior differ.

Product coverage and capability

Razorpay's payment-method and collection documentation includes multiple bank and wallet-based acceptance paths. The existence of a method family does not establish every bank, wallet, currency, or merchant category as available. Our runtime capability register resolves the exact supported combination. [S02] [S22]

Redirect-based payment flow

Create an attempt before sending the customer to a bank or wallet. Bind the redirect session to order, amount, merchant, and expiry. The return endpoint validates state and refreshes status from the backend. It does not accept a query parameter as financial proof.

Preserve a distinction between customer abandonment, bank authentication failure, bank processing, successful payment, and unresolved outcome. If the customer closes the browser, the payment may still complete. A background resolution worker continues independently of the browser session.

Incoming transfers are observation-first

An ordinary incoming transfer may arrive without a recent interactive checkout session. The platform learns of it through a bank notification, statement, or collection feed. Matching uses the available virtual identifier, bank reference, sender details where permitted, amount, and business context.

A transfer that cannot be matched is not revenue and not a disposable log entry. Record it in a controlled suspense workflow with a traceable financial treatment. Operations investigates ownership and either allocates it to a valid obligation or follows the approved return process. Never guess a merchant based only on a common amount.

Wallet integration boundaries

An external wallet balance belongs to the wallet issuer's system. The payment platform should not fabricate a local wallet balance by caching the last successful response. For a closed-loop merchant credit or gift product, use a separate legal and accounting model; do not assume that the same software structure permits a general-purpose stored-value product.

Refunds may require original-transaction references and may have rail-specific timing and limits. A connector declares these capabilities explicitly. A generic refund button should explain unsupported cases rather than silently route money to an unrelated bank account.

Timeout and duplicate handling

Every outbound instruction retains its original partner reference. Every inbound notification has a deduplication key derived from a reliable source identifier, not only amount and timestamp. When a provider lacks a stable event ID, define a documented composite key and a review path for collisions rather than assuming perfect uniqueness.

Bank statements can contain corrections and duplicate file deliveries. Import files immutably, hash them, version parsers, and distinguish file-level deduplication from transaction-level matching. A replacement file may legitimately correct an earlier record; it must not be ignored simply because its date matches.

Acceptance test. The bank return page says failure, but a later bank record proves receipt. The platform records the receipt and invokes the merchant's late-payment policy. It does not conceal the money to preserve the earlier user-interface message.

Chapter 18EMI, BNPL, offers, and financing eligibility

Installments and pay-later options combine payment acceptance with a credit or promotional arrangement. The customer must understand who provides credit, what the total obligation is, and which merchant or issuer funds any discount. Engineering must preserve those distinctions in both the checkout and ledger.

Documented product families

Razorpay's portfolio includes EMI-related payment experiences and offer configuration. Eligibility and commercial terms depend on the payment method, issuer or lending partner, merchant, amount, and campaign. The offer engine cannot promise an installment product merely because the customer has a supported card brand. [S03] [S38]

Proposed eligibility service

The service evaluates instrument metadata, issuer capability, merchant agreement, amount range, tenure, campaign rules, and current partner availability. It returns an expiring offer quote with a version, eligible payment route, customer-visible terms, and required confirmation. The quote is not a credit approval unless the actual lending partner has made that decision.

Bind the selected offer to the order and payment attempt. If the amount changes, reprice and obtain renewed customer confirmation where required. Do not silently extend the tenure or increase the financed amount to make a failed authorization succeed.

Represent the economics explicitly

A merchant-funded discount reduces the merchant's commercial proceeds. An issuer-funded promotion creates a different receivable or reimbursement arrangement. Platform-funded incentives are a platform expense. Customer principal, financing charges, processing fees, and tax treatment belong to the actual contract and applicable rules.

The ledger uses distinct accounts and event types for these obligations. Avoid one discount field that loses who funded it and whether it is refundable. Refund allocation must specify whether promotional benefits reverse, whether installment cancellation requires a partner operation, and how partial refunds affect the remaining customer obligation.

Risk and customer protection

A payment platform should not imply that a loan is approved before the regulated lender's decision. For lending-related journeys, disclosures, consent, data sharing, and money flow need review against the applicable digital-lending framework. The RBI's 2025 directions distinguish regulated entities and their service providers. [S66]

Do not use checkout optimization to hide the cost of credit. Present the relevant amount and tenure clearly and preserve the exact terms accepted by the customer. A support investigation must be able to reconstruct the offer shown at the time, not only the campaign's current settings.

Failure behavior

If eligibility expires or the financing provider fails, offer another method only with the customer's informed selection. Do not automatically charge the full amount to a card when the customer agreed to an installment arrangement. A successful base authorization with failed installment conversion is an explicit exception requiring the contracted resolution process.

Test cases. Cover offer budget exhaustion, concurrent redemption limits, instrument change after quoting, partial refund, full cancellation, duplicate campaign callbacks, and a lender approval arriving after checkout expiry. Verify both the customer obligation and merchant proceeds; a correct gross payment amount can still hide incorrect financing economics.

Chapter 19International acceptance and currency boundaries

International acceptance adds more than a currency selector. It introduces country-specific eligibility, cross-border acquiring, sanctions and risk controls, foreign exchange, settlement currency, customer disclosures, and different evidence requirements. The architecture must know which entity and corridor is providing the service.

Public scope

Razorpay documents international payment acceptance and related methods, subject to activation and eligibility. Its domestic merchant settlement documentation and international product documentation must be read together; a displayed foreign purchase currency does not imply that the merchant receives a foreign-currency settlement account. [S29] [S15]

Proposed currency model

Separate presentment currency, customer debit currency when known, acquiring settlement currency, merchant settlement currency, and platform accounting currency. Every amount has an explicit currency and minor-unit definition. Never compare numeric amounts from different currencies without an approved conversion event.

An FX quote contains base and quote currencies, rate source, spread or fee treatment, rounding rule, expiry, and responsible entity. If the quote expires before execution, either re-quote with the required customer or merchant acceptance or reject safely. Do not silently apply a new rate after the customer approved a different amount.

Routing and eligibility

The routing service checks merchant activation, product category, issuer country, instrument type, corridor, acquiring partner, and applicable controls. A route healthy for domestic cards may not support the current cross-border transaction. Country inference from IP address is a risk signal, not a definitive legal or card-issuance classification.

Store the exact eligibility and risk policy version used. Cross-border rules and bank capabilities change; a later audit must evaluate the historical decision under the rules effective at that time, not today's configuration.

Accounting and disputes

Maintain balanced journals within each currency and legal entity. An FX conversion uses explicit bridge or clearing accounts and records the conversion economics; it is not one journal that balances INR debits against USD credits by comparing raw integers.

Refunds may occur at a different exchange rate or under a contractual original-rate rule. State who bears the FX difference and how fees are treated. A customer-visible refund amount and a merchant's settlement deduction can differ for legitimate reasons, but the system must explain the difference with a traceable calculation.

Data and operational boundaries

International traffic does not authorize unrestricted replication of Indian payment data. Residency and cross-border processing requirements apply to the relevant data and operating model. Use corridor-specific data-flow reviews and region-bound storage policies. [S65]

Support operations need bank references, currency-aware search, value dates, and partner escalation paths. A generic domestic refund estimate must not be applied to an international dispute or bank transfer.

Acceptance test. Process a foreign-currency purchase, partial refund after an FX-rate change, fee adjustment, and merchant settlement. Reconcile each currency independently, then reconcile the conversion bridge. A report that looks correct only after aggregating everything into an approximate INR value is insufficient.

Dynamic versus static identity

Each device has an identity, enrollment state, merchant/store binding, firmware version, connectivity state, and revocation capability. Credentials are unique per device, not one shared fleet secret. Acceptance tests. Duplicate bank notifications produce one financial event and at most one live announcement. A delayed event goes to the historically correct merchant, not the device's new owner. Some collection use cases require more than knowing that money arrived. They require evidence that the payer's account matches an approved customer or investment account. This is a different problem from ordinary payment success and should be modeled as a separate validation result.

Documented product

Razorpay's third-party validation documentation describes a specialized collection capability. Availability and supported flows depend on the regulated use case and integration. The platform must not describe an ordinary successful transfer as validated merely because the payer entered the expected name. [S23]

Proposed validation model

The merchant registers an expected payer relationship with permitted account identifiers and supporting verification references. A payment request binds to that relationship. The connector receives or queries the relevant bank evidence and evaluates whether the actual payer source satisfies the approved rule.

Store payment outcome and validation outcome independently. Possible validation states include pending, matched, mismatched, insufficient evidence, and manually reviewed. A successful payment with insufficient validation is not automatically eligible for the regulated business action. Conversely, a failed payment cannot become successful because the account relationship was valid.

Account matching is not string matching

Names can vary in order, abbreviation, spelling, and transliteration. Account numbers and bank identifiers may be masked or transformed by the rail. Define which source fields are authoritative, what matching algorithm is permitted, and when manual review is required. Never use an unconstrained fuzzy-name score as the sole authority for a sensitive financial allocation.

Retain the decision inputs in a minimized, encrypted form and store the matching-rule version. A later algorithm change should not silently recategorize historical payments without a controlled review process. False matches and false mismatches have different operational consequences and must be measured separately.

Failure and refund policy

When validation fails after funds arrive, record the receipt and restrict downstream use according to the approved policy. Return or refund through the permitted original-source process. Do not allow a mismatched payer to nominate a new beneficiary as a convenient workaround; that can transform a validation control into an unauthorized money-transfer service.

A bank data outage should yield insufficient evidence, not a fabricated match. The merchant should receive a reason category and resolution path without unnecessary disclosure of sensitive account information.

Testing and operations

Test joint accounts, changed bank branches, masked account references, duplicate notifications, stale registered accounts, corrected payer details, and mismatched transfers that arrive after the payment request expires. Verify that a manual reviewer cannot approve their own account change and validation override without the required separation of duties.

Production artifact. Compliance and partner operations sign off on the specification before implementation is considered complete.

Chapter 27Subscriptions, recurring billing, and mandate collection

A subscription is a commercial schedule; a mandate is payment authority; an invoice is a receivable; a payment attempt is an execution. Keeping them separate prevents billing logic from charging without consent or assuming that a scheduled invoice has been paid.

Documented distinction

Razorpay documents Subscriptions as a recurring billing product and recurring-payment APIs as a related but distinct mechanism. Supported methods include card, UPI, and bank-mandate families under their applicable eligibility and operating rules. [S21] [S27]

Proposed billing engine

A subscription stores plan version, customer, merchant, currency, billing anchor, timezone, quantity, trial terms, cancellation policy, and mandate reference. A scheduler creates a billing-cycle object exactly once for each intended period using a unique subscription-and-cycle key.

The cycle calculates charges from the effective plan and usage snapshot, applies proration and taxes under documented rules, and issues an invoice. The collection service checks mandate validity, required notices, amount limits, and customer state before creating a payment attempt. A scheduler retry must recover the same cycle, not create a second invoice.

Time is a domain problem

Monthly billing is not equivalent to adding thirty days. Month-end anchors, leap years, daylight-saving changes in supported regions, holidays, and bank processing windows require explicit calendar rules. Store the intended local billing date and derived UTC execution time separately.

A delayed worker should decide whether to execute the missed cycle, consolidate charges, or require review according to product policy. It must not blindly charge every missed schedule in a burst after an outage. Backlog recovery can otherwise create unexpected customer debits and bank-rate-limit failures.

Dunning and retries

Classify failures into retryable technical uncertainty, definitive payment decline, expired authority, and customer action required. Unknown outcomes are investigated before a new economic attempt. Definitive declines may follow a bounded dunning schedule with notices and customer controls.

Do not retry indefinitely or change the payment method without authority. A customer who cancels a subscription may still owe an already-issued invoice, but the platform must apply the accepted cancellation terms and mandate rules. Subscription cancellation and mandate revocation are separate operations with linked workflow states.

Proration, refunds, and plan changes

Plan changes create effective-dated versions. The invoice records the exact calculation inputs and rounding. A partial-period credit may be applied to a future invoice or refunded, but those are different financial events. A refunded subscription payment does not automatically delete usage history or revoke all future service unless the commercial policy says so.

Acceptance tests

Test scheduler double execution, month-end dates, mandate expiry before collection, revocation during processing, plan change during billing, usage correction after invoice issue, late success after dunning, and retry recovery after a regional outage. The invariant is one intended charge per cycle, not one HTTP request per cycle.

Operational view. Track upcoming renewals, authority failures, collection uncertainty, aged receivables, dunning outcomes, and customer complaints separately. A high invoice-generation success rate says little about whether recurring money collection is healthy.

Chapter 28Route, marketplace splits, and linked accounts

A marketplace payment can create obligations to several sellers, the marketplace operator, tax authorities, and the platform. Splitting a payment is therefore an allocation and settlement problem, not just dividing an amount and sending several transfers.

Documented product

Razorpay Route provides marketplace-oriented transfer and linked-account capabilities. The precise account onboarding, transfer, hold, reversal, and settlement behavior must follow the product's documented and contractual rules. Our implementation below describes a proposed generalized marketplace money core. [S19]

Proposed allocation model

A captured payment creates a source obligation. An allocation plan assigns portions to approved beneficiaries, marketplace commission, fees, taxes, and any required reserve. The plan has a version and a deterministic rounding rule. The sum of allocations cannot exceed the eligible source amount.

Store beneficiary identity and settlement destination snapshots separately. A seller's later bank-account change must not rewrite historical allocations. A linked account may be eligible to receive an obligation before it is eligible for settlement, depending on the approved product policy; the states must be explicit.

One payment, many obligations

The ledger records the source merchant payable and transfers the appropriate portions into seller-specific payable accounts. This is an internal reclassification of obligations until an external settlement actually occurs. Do not label an internal allocation as money received in the seller's bank account.

Each seller obligation retains lineage to the payment and allocation plan. Settlement batches can aggregate many obligations, but reconciliation must still trace a bank payment back to the component items. Aggregation improves efficiency; it must not destroy explainability.

Refunds and reversals

A customer refund may arrive after some sellers have been settled. The refund policy defines which seller, marketplace, or reserve bears the amount and fees. If available seller funds are insufficient, the platform may create a receivable or use an approved reserve arrangement; it must not silently take unrelated merchants' safeguarded money.

A transfer reversal is not necessarily the same as a customer refund. One changes allocation among marketplace parties; the other returns value to the payer. Model both explicitly and prevent duplicate recovery when they occur together.

Concurrency and failure

Serialize allocation changes against the remaining unallocated or reversible amount. If one bank settlement succeeds and another is delayed, preserve the successful leg and investigate the delayed one. There is no atomic multi-bank transaction that can simply be rolled back by a database rollback.

Use a saga with durable per-leg state, not an all-or-nothing fiction. Compensation follows the commercial and rail rules; it may require a new transfer or receivable rather than cancellation of an already-completed bank movement.

Test scenario. A INR 10,000 payment is split across two sellers, one seller is settled, and a INR 2,000 refund is requested. The platform must calculate the contractual burden, reserve available funds, record any shortfall, and produce a statement explaining every party's balance. The same refund request delivered twice must not recover twice from either seller.

Chapter 29Optimizer, routing, and the limits of failover

Payment orchestration can improve resilience by choosing among eligible processors and payment methods. It can also create duplicate charges if it treats every timeout as permission to try another provider. The routing engine must optimize within correctness boundaries, not around them.

Documented product surface

Razorpay Optimizer is documented as a payment-orchestration product. Public product descriptions establish the routing capability, not the exact internal scoring algorithm or a universal guarantee that multiple processors eliminate failures. [S20]

Proposed routing decision

First filter routes by hard eligibility: merchant agreement, country, currency, method, instrument/token compatibility, amount, bank capability, regulatory restriction, and current enablement. Then rank eligible routes using health, latency, observed technical success, cost, capacity, and merchant policy.

Keep risk and commercial constraints ahead of optimization. A cheaper route that does not support the customer's saved token or accepted installment plan is not an eligible alternative. Record the candidate set, selected route, policy version, and reason so that decisions can be reconstructed.

Health signals need disciplined statistics

Separate issuer declines from technical failures. Segment by relevant cohorts and use minimum sample sizes and smoothing. A processor serving a riskier merchant mix can look worse without being technically worse. Exploration traffic should be bounded and exclude flows where experimentation violates the customer's accepted terms.

A circuit breaker opens for a specific route or failure class, not necessarily the entire bank. Preserve status queries, callbacks, refunds, and reconciliation access when disabling new attempts. Those operations are needed to resolve traffic already sent.

Safe and unsafe retry boundaries

A failure before sending an external instruction is usually safe to reroute. A definitive rejection with no financial commitment may permit a new attempt under the relevant contract. A timeout after sending is unknown and must retain the original operation identity.

Do not hedge side-effecting authorization or payout requests across providers as if they were read-only search queries. Two providers can both succeed. Even when a duplicate can later be refunded, the customer may lose available funds and the merchant may incur fees or complaints.

The adapter declares whether it supports idempotent replay, status inquiry by client reference, cancellation, and authoritative final states. A routing policy cannot manufacture these guarantees. When uncertainty cannot be resolved quickly, the correct response may be pending rather than an immediate alternate charge.

Merchant integration and reporting

Normalize common entities for the merchant while preserving provider-native identifiers and evidence. Reconciliation and dispute tooling must work across all processors. An orchestration layer that improves acceptance but leaves finance to manually combine incompatible statements is incomplete.

Acceptance test. Processor A accepts a payment but its response is lost. A health monitor simultaneously marks A degraded. The platform must not send the same economic payment to B. It should query A, await evidence, and communicate uncertainty. Only a policy-supported new customer attempt or definitive resolution can cross that boundary safely.

Loyalty and reward ledger

Use a separate reward ledger for earned, pending, redeemed, expired, and reversed units. Points are not automatically currency. Their redemption value, transferability, and expiry are defined by the program terms. A purchase can earn a pending reward that becomes available only after a return window or fulfilment event.

Refunds and chargebacks must reverse or adjust rewards according to the accepted policy. Prevent a customer from earning points, spending them immediately, and then obtaining a full refund without any recorded recovery obligation. The policy may permit such generosity, but the economic cost must be explicit.

Gift value and stored-value boundaries

A gift card or merchant credit can represent a liability. Model issuance, funding, redemption, partial use, expiry where lawful, and breakage treatment under the approved accounting policy. Do not extend a closed-loop product into a general transferable wallet without the required legal and regulatory analysis.

Separate promotional credits from purchased value. They may have different refund, expiry, and consumer-rights treatment. A single balance number can be displayed for convenience only if the spending and statement rules preserve those distinctions.

Buyer protection and verified-business claims

A buyer-protection program needs eligibility, covered events, claim deadlines, evidence requirements, funding, exclusions, decision authority, and appeal handling. It is not a guarantee that every purchase will be reimbursed. The checkout must show the actual terms and preserve the version accepted at purchase time.

A trusted-business badge should be tied to specific verified attributes and an expiry or review process. It must not imply that the platform guarantees product quality or future solvency unless that is truly the contracted promise. Revoking a badge should not rewrite historical claims about what was displayed.

Data and event integrity

Engagement systems consume payment events through idempotent projections. They cannot modify authoritative payment status to repair a reward discrepancy. Customer segmentation and messaging use purpose-limited data with consent and suppression controls. A payment receipt address is not automatically permission for unlimited promotional messaging.

Acceptance test. A purchase earns a reward, the reward is redeemed, and the original payment is later disputed. The platform must preserve all three events and calculate the contractual recovery or loss without deleting history. A duplicate payment event must not issue a second reward.

Design lesson. Reuse the platform's event, approval, and accounting foundations, but keep each customer promise explicit. A convenient engagement feature becomes a financial product when it creates redeemable value or a reimbursement obligation.

Chapter 31The double-entry ledger: the center of the platform

The ledger answers a precise question: which entity owns each asset or owes each liability, in which currency, because of which accepted business event? It is not merely a table of transaction amounts, and a balanced ledger alone does not prove that the business event was valid.

Proposed account structure

Use accounts scoped to operating legal entity, currency, owner, account type, and purpose. Typical asset accounts include bank cash, acquiring receivables, merchant receivables, and controlled suspense. Liability accounts include merchant payables, seller payables, refund payables, tax payables, and unsettled outbound obligations. Revenue and expense accounts record the platform's own economics.

Debits increase assets and expenses; credits increase liabilities and revenue under this simplified conventional model. Do not describe a debit as universally adding money or a credit as universally subtracting it. The account type determines its normal balance.

A concrete INR example

The following is a teaching example, not Razorpay pricing or a statement of tax applicability. Assume a INR 10,000 captured payment, a INR 200 platform fee, and a hypothetical INR 36 tax component on that fee.

Receive acquiring settlement Bank cash 10,000 Acquiring receivable 10,000
Complete merchant settlement Merchant payable 9,764 Bank cash 9,764

Real implementations may use additional clearing accounts and net settlement from the acquirer. The essential requirement is that partner fees, timing differences, and netting remain explicit rather than disappearing inside a single net amount.

Journal invariants

A journal has a unique business-event key, legal entity, currency, effective time, recorded time, event type, evidence reference, and immutable lines. All lines belong to compatible accounts, and total debits equal total credits. Amounts are positive integer minor units; direction is explicit. A journal cannot mix currencies and claim balance by comparing raw numbers.

Posting occurs through a restricted command service. The database enforces uniqueness, account ownership, valid references, and atomic commit. Cross-line balancing requires a controlled posting procedure or equivalent database enforcement; ordinary row-level checks alone are insufficient. Direct application writes to posted journal lines are prohibited.

Immutability and corrections

Never edit a posted amount to make reconciliation pass. Create a reversing or correcting journal linked to the original, with reason, approval, and evidence. Preserve both accounting effective date and actual recording time so that late adjustments do not pretend to have been known earlier.

A journal can be arithmetically balanced and still be wrong: it may credit the wrong merchant, use the wrong currency, duplicate a capture, or recognize a fabricated bank event. Therefore validate semantic invariants and reconcile against independent sources. The ledger proves internal accounting consistency; bank and partner evidence prove external movement.

Projections and balances

The journal is authoritative. Maintain transactionally updated account balance projections for fast decisions, with sequence numbers and rebuild tooling. Periodically recompute balances from journal lines and compare. A data-warehouse sum or cache entry must not authorize payouts.

Partitioning should preserve atomic posting within the chosen ledger domain. If an operation crosses ledger shards, use explicit inter-shard clearing obligations and a durable protocol; do not assume a message bus provides an atomic cross-shard commit. Begin with fewer, well-defined ledger domains before optimizing for hypothetical global scale.

Proof obligations

Every financial event maps to exactly one accepted posting key, though delivery may repeat. Every posting has sufficient evidence and a permitted event transition. Every external bank movement is matched or held in a controlled exception account. Every merchant statement can be regenerated from immutable events and versioned pricing.

Production gate. Finance signs off on the chart of accounts, posting templates, reconciliation rules, and correction procedures. Engineers test duplicate events, invalid ownership, arithmetic overflow, wrong currency, concurrent posting, rollback, and restore. No uptime target justifies bypassing these controls.

Chapter 32Available balances, reservations, and concurrent spending

A displayed balance is not necessarily spendable money. Funds can be unsettled, reserved for refunds, subject to disputes, held by policy, or already committed to an outbound instruction. The platform must calculate availability under a precise contract and serialize competing claims on it.

Proposed balance categories

Distinguish ledger balance, settled funding, available payable, reserved payable, pending incoming amounts, pending outgoing amounts, and restricted reserves. These categories should be explainable from journals and reservation records, not inferred from a collection of unrelated status flags.

A reservation earmarks an obligation for a particular operation. It does not create new cash and does not prove that the bank will execute the payout. One design reclassifies merchant payable from available to reserved accounts. Another uses a transactionally maintained encumbrance subledger. Choose one coherent model and prevent double subtraction in reports.

Atomic reserve operation

A payout reservation transaction locks or serializes the relevant balance authority, verifies ownership and currency, checks eligible availability, inserts a unique reservation, records the associated operation, and writes an outbox event. Competing requests cannot both spend the same available amount.

With PostgreSQL, row-level locking or serializable transactions can support such invariants, but serialization failures must be retried correctly. The isolation level is not a substitute for defining the invariant and locking the relevant rows. [S78]

A race example

A merchant has INR 1,000 available. Two workers each request INR 800. Both must not read 1,000 and independently approve. Under a locked balance row, the first reserves 800 and the second sees 200. Under a serializable design, one transaction may abort and retry. A Redis lock without a durable database condition is not sufficient protection against process pauses, lock expiry, or failover.

Reservation release is also idempotent. A canceled operation releases only its own active reservation. A duplicate cancellation cannot increase availability twice. Once a bank instruction is in an uncertain processing state, do not release its funds merely because a local lease expired.

Liquidity is different from obligation availability

A merchant may be owed money while the platform has not yet received the corresponding acquiring settlement. Whether early payout is permitted depends on a separately funded, approved liquidity and risk arrangement. Do not count an unsettled receivable as bank cash or fund one merchant's early payout with unrelated safeguarded obligations.

Track bank-account liquidity by legal entity and currency, with operational buffers and pending bank instructions. The sum of merchant-level available obligations can exceed immediately available bank liquidity if the product permits delayed settlement, but the scheduler must respect both constraints before execution.

Negative balances and recovery

A post-settlement refund or chargeback can create a merchant receivable or a permitted negative balance. Model its contractual recovery source, limit, aging, and escalation. A negative number is not an overdraft facility by itself. Do not quietly allow new payouts against a debt unless the approved product explicitly supports it.

Tests and monitoring

Run concurrent reservation, release, capture, refund, and settlement operations. Verify no unauthorized negative availability, no orphan reservation, and no double release. Reconcile reserved totals to live operations and age reservations by reason. An old reservation can indicate a stuck bank instruction; it should trigger investigation rather than automatic deletion.

Invariant. Every unit excluded from availability has a named reason and owner. Every unit released has evidence that its prior commitment no longer needs protection.

Chapter 33Unknown outcomes, late success, and reversals

The most dangerous payment failure is not an explicit decline. It is losing the response after the external system may have committed. The platform cannot solve this by choosing whichever status is more convenient for the user interface.

The uncertainty window

Before sending a side-effecting request, persist the operation and stable partner reference. After sending, record observations independently from the operation's interpreted state. A timeout means the response was not observed within a deadline; it does not establish whether the bank accepted, rejected, or completed the instruction.

The public Razorpay lifecycle includes late-authorization considerations. Our proposed system generalizes the lesson: local interactive deadlines and external financial finality are separate clocks. [S11]

Evidence-driven resolution

A resolution worker queries the partner using the original reference, consumes authenticated notifications, and later matches statement evidence. Each connector defines which source can establish acceptance, completion, reversal, or definitive non-execution. The reducer records its decision and supporting observations.

Do not use last-write-wins on status. A delayed transport error should not overwrite a confirmed payment. A later reversal should not be ignored because success was once observed. A corrected bank file may require a new compensating event rather than a replacement status.

Retry only where safe

A request that failed before leaving our system can be retried or rerouted. A partner-supported duplicate-safe replay may resend the same operation identity. A fresh economic attempt is allowed only when the prior result is definitively non-committing or the customer explicitly initiates an additional purchase under a policy that handles duplicate success risk.

A timeout budget is not an idempotency guarantee. The fact that a bank usually answers in two seconds says nothing about whether it can still complete a request after five seconds. Avoid automatic provider switching for uncertain debits, captures, payouts, refunds, and FX executions.

Late success and commercial state

A customer may abandon checkout or an order may expire while the payment later succeeds. Record the money first, then apply the merchant's commercial policy: fulfil if still valid, refund if unfulfillable, or hold for review. Do not suppress the successful payment because the order is canceled.

A duplicate external success must also be recorded. It can create an excess-payment liability and refund workflow. A uniqueness constraint on the order's fulfilment allocation prevents double shipment, while the ledger still accounts for both receipts.

Reversals and compensation

A reversal is an external event that changes the original financial effect. Link it to the original operation, validate amount and currency, and post a compensating journal. Keep customer refund, bank reversal, chargeback, and internal correction as different event types; they have different evidence and commercial consequences.

RBI's failed-transaction turnaround and compensation framework is scenario-specific. It must not be replaced with one universal refund timer or confused with a merchant voluntarily issuing a refund. [S70]

Operational queue

Track unknown operations by rail, partner, age, amount, and customer impact. Escalation thresholds should reflect contract and risk. Give operators a safe query/reconcile action, not an unrestricted retry button. Every manual resolution records evidence and approval.

Chaos test. Drop the response after partner commitment, crash the worker, replay the queue, fail over the region, then deliver the original success twice. The expected result is one economic operation, one accepted financial posting, and an auditable sequence of observations.

Chapter 34Refunds, partial refunds, and money already settled

A refund is a new financial operation linked to an earlier payment. It is not an edit to the original capture and not simply a negative payment row. The customer, merchant, acquiring partner, and platform can each have distinct timing and funding responsibilities.

Documented product surface

Razorpay documents refund operations and their lifecycle. Actual eligibility, speed, fees, and external credit timing depend on the payment and product. A platform should distinguish request acceptance, processing, and confirmed outcome rather than promise immediate customer-bank credit for every refund. [S13]

Proposed refund command

The command includes payment ID, amount, currency, merchant reference, reason, and idempotency key. A transaction validates ownership, captured amount, previously completed and reserved refunds, dispute overlap, and permitted destination. It reserves the refundable amount and creates a durable refund operation plus outbox.

Concurrent partial refunds serialize against the remaining refundable amount. Two INR 700 requests against a INR 1,000 remaining balance cannot both be approved. Count pending reservations as well as completed refunds; otherwise asynchronous execution can exceed the captured amount.

Funding before and after merchant settlement

Before merchant settlement, a refund can reduce the merchant payable and create a refund obligation. After settlement, the platform may need a merchant receivable, a permitted reserve draw, or another contractually approved recovery mechanism. These are different accounting entries.

For example, a INR 2,000 refund before settlement may debit merchant payable and credit refund payable. When bank execution is confirmed, debit refund payable and credit the appropriate cash or clearing account. If the merchant has already been fully paid, debit an approved merchant receivable instead of pretending that a nonexistent payable still funds the refund.

Fees and taxes are adjusted only according to the applicable contract and tax treatment. Do not automatically reverse every fee proportionally unless that is the agreed rule. Store the pricing version and adjustment calculation.

External execution

Send the original-transaction reference and stable refund operation identity through a compatible connector. A timeout remains uncertain and keeps its reservation. Query or reconcile before resubmitting. The customer refund and the merchant's recovery transaction may complete at different times; expose both operationally.

A refund normally follows the original payment's permitted return path. Sending money to a newly supplied bank account is a different high-risk operation and must not masquerade as a routine refund. It can create fraud and regulatory problems even when the amount matches.

Dispute overlap

A customer may request a refund while a chargeback is open. The system needs a policy to prevent double reimbursement and to provide the acquiring partner with appropriate evidence. It must preserve both workflows rather than deleting one to simplify the status page.

Tests and support

Test duplicate requests, concurrent partial refunds, refund after full settlement, bank timeout, late refund success, dispute overlap, currency mismatch, and a changed merchant bank account. The statement should explain original payment, refund amount, fees retained or adjusted, funding source, and remaining merchant balance.

Customer communication. Say that a refund was requested or processed only when the corresponding evidence exists. Do not equate our successful API response with the time the customer's bank makes the credit visible.

Chapter 35Disputes, chargebacks, and evidence management

A dispute is a time-bound case with financial consequences. It can occur long after the payment and merchant settlement. Winning or losing depends on the applicable network and acquiring process, evidence, deadlines, and customer claim, not on whether the original API returned success.

Documented scope

Razorpay's dispute documentation exposes the dispute-management product surface. The specific deadlines, reason categories, evidence requirements, and financial treatment must be loaded from the relevant partner and scheme rules. Do not encode one universal chargeback lifecycle for all rails. [S14]

Proposed case model

A case stores payment reference, external dispute ID, reason, stage, amount, currency, received time, response deadline, liability status, evidence requirements, and owner. Deadline calculations use the relevant timezone and calendar. Store the source message and rule version so that an operator can explain how the due date was determined.

Separate case state from financial state. A case can be awaiting evidence while funds are reserved or debited. A successful representment may later restore funds. Each financial change produces a journal linked to the case; the case timeline does not overwrite the original payment history.

Evidence service

Collect fulfilment proof, customer communications, authentication metadata, refund records, terms acceptance, and relevant transaction details through controlled connectors. Evidence is versioned, access-limited, and hashed. A submitted package is immutable; later additions create a new version with submission status.

Minimize personal data and redact unrelated information. A support agent should not upload unrestricted database exports because a dispute is urgent. The evidence service should know the required format and size limits and validate the package before the deadline.

Workflow and ownership

Assign cases to merchant self-service, internal operations, or specialist review according to contract and risk. Reminders escalate as deadlines approach. If the merchant does not respond, follow the agreed default action and document it. Missing a deadline is an operational failure even if every API remained available.

Manual decisions require reason codes and appropriate approval. An operator cannot simply mark a dispute won to release a reserve. Financial release requires authoritative external evidence or a separately approved platform-funded commercial concession.

Accounting and double recovery

A chargeback after merchant settlement may create a merchant receivable or consume an approved reserve. If the customer was already refunded, the evidence and financial logic must prevent duplicate recovery. A later dispute reversal restores the appropriate obligation or reduces the receivable; it does not create unexplained revenue.

Track fees separately from disputed principal. Some costs may remain even when the merchant wins, depending on the contract. The statement must preserve that distinction.

Tests and metrics

Test duplicate dispute notices, revised deadlines, evidence rejected by the partner, refund-before-chargeback, partial disputes, multiple stages, and a late reversal after account closure. Monitor response timeliness, evidence completeness, aging, financial exposure, and reconciliation breaks. Win rate alone is a misleading engineering metric because case mix and merchant behavior differ.

Design rule. A dispute system is complete only when case workflow, evidence, ledger effects, partner submission, and merchant communication can be reconciled to one another.

Chapter 36Fees, taxes, pricing contracts, and financial rounding

A payment platform can move the principal correctly and still produce incorrect merchant statements through fee or tax errors. Pricing is executable commercial policy. It needs versioning, effective dates, deterministic calculations, and finance-approved corrections.

Proposed pricing model

A pricing contract is scoped to merchant, operating entity, product, method, currency, corridor, and effective interval. Rules may include percentage fees, fixed fees, minimums, caps, tiers, promotional periods, and partner pass-through costs. Resolve overlapping rules deterministically and reject ambiguous configurations before publication.

A transaction records the selected pricing version and calculation inputs. Recomputing last month's fees using today's contract is prohibited. The system must reproduce the original charge and any later adjustment independently.

Exact arithmetic

Use integer minor units and fixed-precision rational or decimal calculations. Define when rounding occurs: per transaction, fee component, invoice line, or aggregate period. The choice can change totals. Store the unrounded intermediate representation when needed for audit, but post only valid currency amounts.

For a teaching example, a 2% fee on INR 10,000 is INR 200; a hypothetical 18% tax on that fee is INR 36. These numbers illustrate arithmetic only and are not a quote of Razorpay pricing or a universal tax determination. Actual tax classification, rate, place of supply, and invoicing require current professional review.

Revenue is not gross payment volume

Customer principal generally creates obligations to merchants or other beneficiaries. It is not all platform revenue. Separate gross processed value, net settlement, fee revenue, partner costs, taxes payable, incentives, losses, and credit-related income. A dashboard that labels all incoming cash as revenue will mislead both engineering and finance.

Recognize fees at the contractually and accounting-appropriate event. Authorization, capture, settlement, refund, and dispute fees can have different triggers. A failed payment may still incur a partner cost without creating merchant revenue. The ledger needs explicit event templates for each case.

Adjustments and invoicing

A negotiated credit or fee correction creates a credit adjustment with reason, approval, original calculation reference, and tax treatment. It does not edit the captured payment. Monthly invoices reconcile to posted fee and tax events plus authorized adjustments.

Tiered pricing needs a defined aggregation window and treatment of late events. If volume thresholds change the rate retroactively, calculate an explicit true-up journal. If they apply prospectively, record the threshold transition. Do not let batch timing decide which merchant receives a discount.

Configuration safety

Pricing changes receive simulation against representative historical transactions, boundary tests, approval, staged activation, and rollback instructions. A rollback restores future configuration; it does not erase already-booked charges. Financial remediation for an incorrect release is a separate controlled process.

Acceptance tests. Cover one-minor-unit payments, maximum supported amounts, negative adjustments, partial refunds, tier boundaries, cross-currency fees, duplicate events, and a pricing change exactly at midnight in the contract timezone. Verify that line-level totals, merchant statements, invoices, and general-ledger exports agree.

Chapter 37Settlement batches, calendars, and bank instructions

Settlement converts an obligation to the merchant into an actual bank movement. It needs eligibility rules, funding, calendars, approved destinations, batch integrity, and reconciliation. A captured payment is not enough to conclude that settlement is immediately due or executable.

Documented timing is product-specific

Razorpay's payment-settlement documentation describes a domestic default of T+2 working days, with applicable conditions and merchant-specific arrangements. Its POS documentation describes a different domestic default, T+1, again subject to the product and partner. Neither should be generalized to every merchant, international corridor, or banking product. [S15] [S16]

Proposed eligibility engine

For each obligation, calculate the due business date from capture or another contractual trigger, merchant schedule, risk holds, bank calendar, product, and operating entity. Store the calculation version. Eligibility is separate from liquidity and destination validity.

An obligation can be due but held for a documented reason, or eligible but waiting for sufficient bank liquidity. The merchant dashboard should distinguish these states. A generic pending label makes support inefficient and can conceal missed contractual commitments.

Batch construction

Select eligible obligations into a batch under a transaction or equivalent concurrency control that prevents double inclusion. Freeze the membership, total, currency, legal entity, destination snapshot, and manifest hash. Apply maker-checker approval when required. A batch cannot be edited after approval; corrections create a replacement or explicitly approved adjustment.

The bank instruction references the immutable batch and a stable client reference. A batch file is encrypted and signed or authenticated according to the bank integration. File generation success is not bank acceptance, and bank acceptance is not necessarily beneficiary credit.

Execution and uncertainty

Persist submitted, accepted, processing, completed, rejected, and unknown observations as supported by the bank contract. A lost upload acknowledgment is investigated using the original file or batch reference. Re-uploading a renamed file can defeat bank deduplication and create duplicate settlement.

For partial bank execution, record per-beneficiary results. Do not mark the whole batch failed and rerun all members. Successful legs remain complete; uncertain legs remain reserved; definitively rejected legs follow a controlled retry or correction process.

Ledger and reconciliation

One design reclassifies merchant payable into settlement-in-transit at the point the platform makes an irrevocable or appropriately defined outbound commitment. Confirmed bank debit then clears that liability against cash. The exact posting trigger follows the contract and accounting policy; merely constructing a file should not falsely imply execution.

Reconcile batch membership, bank instructions, bank debit records, and beneficiary results. Net bank charges are explicit expense or fee events. The sum of customer-level obligations must tie to the bank-level movement, including any legitimate adjustments.

Calendar and destination tests

Test holidays, cutoffs, month-end, bank outages, destination changes during approval, partial batch rejection, duplicate file delivery, and a restored scheduler selecting already-settled obligations. A settlement holiday calendar is versioned operational data, not a hard-coded weekend check.

Customer promise. Communicate expected settlement dates with the correct product and calendar context. When an exception occurs, expose the reason, amount, owner, and next update rather than silently changing the promised date.

Chapter 38Instant settlements, liquidity, and treasury controls

Instant settlement changes when the merchant receives money, not when the underlying payment magically becomes risk-free. It can require available settled funds, prefunding, or a separately approved advance arrangement. Treasury and risk controls are therefore part of the product architecture.

Proposed funding model

For every expedited settlement, identify the merchant obligation, funding account, operating entity, currency, bank route, applicable fee, and risk limit. The execution service checks both merchant entitlement and actual liquidity. A positive merchant payable does not prove that the relevant bank account has sufficient usable cash.

Maintain liquidity forecasts from expected acquiring receipts, scheduled settlements, refunds, chargebacks, payroll or vendor obligations where applicable, and committed outbound instructions. Forecasts guide treasury; they do not replace confirmed balances for hard execution limits unless an approved credit facility explicitly supports the gap.

Safeguarding and source-of-funds boundaries

Funds held for one regulated purpose cannot be treated as the company's unrestricted working capital. Any advance, credit line, or prefunding arrangement needs a documented legal and commercial basis. The ledger identifies the funding source and resulting receivable or liability so that finance can distinguish normal settlement from financing.

Use separate accounts and permissions for operational cash, safeguarded collections, reserves, and partner-funded facilities as required by the operating model. A global cash dashboard may aggregate them for visibility but must not make them interchangeable in the payout scheduler.

Risk eligibility

An expedited-settlement policy may consider merchant history, fulfilment exposure, dispute rate, transaction type, concentration, and available reserves. Record the policy version and decision. A merchant whose risk changes after capture may have different eligibility for early release than for ordinary contractual settlement.

The policy must not arbitrarily withhold funds without a contractual basis. Risk, legal, finance, and support need a shared explanation and appeal process. Engineering should make restrictions precise and auditable rather than hide them behind an opaque score.

Intraday operations

Track bank-account balances, pending debits, unconfirmed transfers, and minimum buffers. Use bank-source freshness markers. A stale balance response can make the system believe liquidity exists when a large debit is already processing. Reserve liquidity when an instruction is committed and release it only on evidence-backed non-execution or completion accounting.

Avoid automated treasury sweeps that can conflict with settlement execution. Both must use a common account-level commitment model. A treasury transfer between accounts is itself an idempotent financial operation with reconciliation, not a privileged shortcut.

Stress and failure tests

Simulate a large merchant concentration, acquiring settlement delay, refund surge, bank-route outage, and sudden loss of a funding facility. The system should reduce or stop expedited settlement according to policy while preserving ordinary obligations and transparent communication.

Key metric. Measure liquidity headroom and aged unconfirmed debits by entity and currency, not just total cash. A platform can have enough money globally and still be unable or unauthorized to pay from the required account.

Design rule. Do not advertise a faster settlement product until its funding, risk ownership, bank capacity, and outage behavior are as explicit as its API.

Chapter 39Reconciliation, breaks, and financial close

Reconciliation compares independent records of the same economic activity. It detects missing, duplicated, mispriced, misallocated, and incorrectly timed events. It is not a nightly query that forces two totals to match by inserting an unexplained adjustment.

Public product context

Razorpay has publicly described a reconciliation product. That establishes a product capability, not the private matching rules of its own financial core. The architecture here is an original proposed reconciliation system. [S41]

Three layers of comparison

First compare operational transactions with partner transaction records: did the bank see the same payment, refund, or payout? Next compare partner settlement records with bank-account movements: did the expected cash arrive or leave? Finally compare both with the internal ledger and merchant obligations: did the platform account for the correct owner, amount, currency, fees, and timing?

A total-level match can hide offsetting errors. Two merchants can be swapped while the batch sum remains correct. Therefore reconcile identifiers and components before aggregates. Aggregates remain useful as completeness controls, not the sole proof.

Immutable ingestion and normalization

Store each source file or payload with origin, receipt time, period, checksum, parser version, and access controls. Preserve source row numbers and native references. Normalize into a common observation schema while retaining the original representation for investigation.

Files can be late, duplicated, replaced, or partially corrupt. Validate record counts, control totals, required fields, and expected delivery windows. A parser failure creates a visible ingestion incident; it must not quietly produce an empty successful report.

Matching strategy

Use deterministic exact matching on stable references first. Then use approved composite keys and bounded tolerances for known partner behavior. Fuzzy matching should propose candidates for review, not automatically move money between merchants. Every match records rule version, confidence category, and source lineage.

Classify breaks: missing internal event, missing partner event, amount mismatch, currency mismatch, fee mismatch, duplicate, timing difference, unmatched cash, wrong beneficiary, or unsupported correction. Each class has an owner, aging target, evidence requirement, and permitted resolution.

Correcting a break

An operator cannot edit the ledger to make the report green. A resolution either links previously unmatched evidence, corrects a projection, or creates an approved financial adjustment through the normal posting service. The original break and its closure evidence remain available.

For example, a captured payment absent internally but present in authenticated bank evidence may require reconstructing the missing financial event after investigation. A duplicate internal journal requires a correcting journal, not deleting a row. A bank fee not previously modeled becomes an explicit fee or expense event under finance approval.

Close and reopening

A close process checks completeness of expected source feeds, unresolved material breaks, suspense balances, settlement-in-transit aging, fee invoices, and account balance proofs. Closing a period freezes the reporting version; late evidence enters through controlled adjustments or reopening under accounting policy.

Track both event date and recorded date. Backdating a late correction without preserving when it was discovered destroys the audit trail. Reports should distinguish as-booked and restated views where relevant.

Acceptance scenario. A replacement bank file removes one transaction and adds a corrected amount. Re-ingestion must preserve both file versions, avoid duplicate postings, create the correct investigation, and produce an explainable resolution. A green dashboard without that lineage is not reconciliation.

Chapter 40Webhooks, event delivery, and merchant correctness

Webhooks deliver observations asynchronously. They can arrive more than once, out of order, or after the merchant has already queried the same state. The receiver must make processing idempotent, and the sender must make delivery recoverable without allowing replay to repeat money movement.

Documented Razorpay behavior

Razorpay documents at-least-once delivery, duplicate detection using x-razorpay-event-id, no ordering guarantee, a five-second response expectation, and retry behavior over a 24-hour period. Its signature guidance uses HMAC-SHA256 over the raw request body and notes that older secrets may be needed to validate retries after secret rotation. [S17] [S18]

A robust merchant receiver

Read the raw body before parsing or reserializing it. Select the appropriate verification secret, compute the expected signature, and compare in constant time. Validate content type, size limits, merchant endpoint binding, and supported schema. Do not log the full payload indiscriminately.

Persist a verified event into an inbox with a unique provider-and-event identifier, then return success promptly. A worker processes it transactionally. If the inbox already contains the event, acknowledge without repeating fulfilment, refund, or ledger actions. Returning success before durable persistence can lose events when the process crashes.

Events are not an ordered script

A captured event may arrive before an authorized event. A refund event can arrive after the merchant already learned the refund through an API query. Apply valid state transitions or fetch the authoritative resource when needed; do not require every earlier webhook to have arrived first.

Store the original event, received time, signature-verification result, processing status, and business action reference. A webhook event ID deduplicates delivery; the business operation key prevents duplicate economic effects when the same fact is learned through different channels.

Our proposed webhook sender

A transactional outbox records an event only after the underlying state commits. Delivery workers create independent attempts with backoff, jitter, timeout, and per-endpoint concurrency limits. A failing merchant endpoint cannot exhaust the entire dispatch fleet. Large payloads and slow DNS resolution have bounded resource budgets.

Protect outbound delivery from server-side request forgery: validate destinations, restrict private and metadata-network access, revalidate DNS as appropriate, and control redirects. Endpoint ownership verification and secret rotation are first-class operations.

Replay and recovery

Merchants need a safe event-history and replay interface. A replay retains the original event identity and distinguishes delivery metadata from the original event time. It must not look like a new capture merely because it was sent again. The receiving side should be able to rebuild projections without re-shipping products.

If a webhook subscription is disabled after persistent failure, notify the merchant through an independent channel and preserve recovery access. Payment correctness cannot depend exclusively on a webhook endpoint being healthy forever; API queries and reconciliation remain available.

Test. Deliver the same event ten times, reverse the event order, rotate the secret during retries, crash the receiver after persistence, and replay a month-old event. The merchant should converge on the right state and execute each fulfilment or financial action only once.

Chapter 41RazorpayX-style banking software and current accounts

A banking dashboard can provide a unified experience while the actual account and payment execution remain with a banking partner. The engineering must preserve that distinction. Displaying a balance does not make the software platform a bank or the owner of the customer's deposit.

Documented boundary

RazorpayX's public product page explicitly distinguishes its software offering from banking services supplied through partner banks. Its current-account documentation describes the relevant account product, while payout documentation notes that X Lite is not the onboarding path for new merchants. Do not build a current product plan from legacy wallet-like examples without checking eligibility. [S05] [S58] [S52]

Proposed account representation

Store the bank account as an external financial resource with bank, operating entity, account holder, currency, masked display details, verification state, permissions, and partner references. Keep the bank's authoritative balance and our observed balance projection separate, with last-updated time and source.

Our ledger records platform obligations, fees, and operational commitments. A mirror of a customer bank account is a reconciliation or authorization aid, not an unrestricted substitute for the bank's books. Clearly identify which amounts are bank-confirmed and which are pending instructions known only to our system.

Unified experience without hidden coupling

The dashboard can connect accounts, beneficiaries, payouts, vendor invoices, payroll, tax payments, approvals, and statements. These modules reuse the same payment-instruction and audit infrastructure but preserve different business approvals. A payroll approval should not grant permission to send an unrelated vendor payment.

Bank connectors expose capabilities rather than pretending all banks support identical features. Account onboarding, beneficiary activation, payout modes, balance freshness, cutoffs, and statement delivery can differ. The UI renders supported actions from the capability registry and explains unavailable operations.

Account access and change control

Adding an account requires verification of the customer's authority and the partner's onboarding process. Changing account access, approvers, or API permissions is a high-risk administrative action. Record the customer organization, actor, approval, effective time, and affected resources.

Revocation stops new actions but does not erase already-submitted bank instructions. Continue receiving and reconciling their results. A disconnected account may still require statement retrieval or exception resolution under the contract, with carefully limited access.

Failure handling

When a bank balance API is stale or unavailable, display its age and restrict actions according to policy. Do not show a cached balance as live. If an outbound instruction is uncertain, reserve its local commitment while querying the bank. Another dashboard session must not spend the same expected funds simply because the first session timed out.

Acceptance test. A user views a bank balance, another approved process sends a large payment, and the first user submits payroll from the stale screen. Execution must revalidate funding and commitments; the displayed number is not authority to overdraw.

Company requirement. Partner-bank operations, customer support, treasury, and engineering need a shared ownership map. Customers should not be bounced between organizations because the software and bank each claim the other owns an unresolved payment.

Chapter 42Payout orchestration and idempotent bank execution

A payout is a high-consequence write to an external financial system. The platform must prevent duplicate instructions, bind the beneficiary and amount to approval, retain uncertainty, and reconcile the bank outcome. Returning a payout ID is only the beginning of the operation.

Documented API behavior

Razorpay documents payout modes including IMPS, NEFT, RTGS, and UPI under supported account arrangements. Its payout idempotency guidance makes X-Payout-Idempotency mandatory from 15 March 2025 and requires the same body when reusing a key. This is an endpoint-specific contract, not evidence that every Razorpay API has identical idempotency behavior. [S52] [S53] [S54]

Proposed payout state machine

A payout begins as a validated proposal, may require approval, reserves funds, becomes ready, is submitted to a bank, and then reaches a confirmed outcome or an investigation state. A rejection before submission is different from a bank rejection after submission. An internal canceled state cannot undo an already-completed bank transfer.

Persist merchant, debit account, beneficiary snapshot, amount, currency, purpose, approval reference, idempotency key, request hash, bank route, and external reference. These fields are immutable once the operation is approved and submitted. Changes require a new proposal and approval.

Atomic local acceptance

In one database transaction, claim the idempotency key, validate the approval and account authority, reserve eligible funds, create the payout operation, and append the outbox event. If the transaction rolls back, no dispatch event exists. If it commits but the HTTP response is lost, the retry returns the same payout.

The dispatcher claims the operation with a lease and writer epoch, then sends using the stable partner reference. The lease prevents ordinary duplicate workers; the external duplicate-safe contract and resolution protocol address crashes after send. A lease alone cannot guarantee exactly-once bank execution.

Rail selection and bank windows

Select a supported mode using amount, bank capability, customer preference, fees, and actual integration availability. Do not infer product availability from national rail hours alone. A particular bank connector or business account may have operational restrictions even when the underlying rail is available.

Once submitted, do not switch modes merely because the response is slow. An uncertain NEFT or IMPS instruction remains the same economic operation until sufficient evidence resolves it. A new route is not a harmless transport retry.

Completion and reversal

The bank may acknowledge receipt before beneficiary credit. Record those stages according to the partner contract. Confirmed completion consumes the reservation and posts the appropriate financial event. A definitive failure releases or reclassifies the reservation. A later reversal creates a linked compensating event and restores the appropriate balance only after evidence.

Tests

Run duplicate-key requests, mismatched-body retries, simultaneous approval and cancellation, bank timeout after debit, regional failover after submission, and reversal after apparent completion. Verify that the same operation reference survives every recovery path.

Operational rule. An operator may query, escalate, or resolve with evidence. A generic retry button must never generate a fresh idempotency key for an uncertain payout.

Chapter 43Contacts, beneficiaries, verification, and Payout Links

The destination of money deserves its own lifecycle. A contact is a person or business record; a beneficiary account is a financial destination; verification is evidence about that destination; approval is permission to pay it. Combining these into one editable address-book row creates avoidable fraud risk.

Documented product surface

RazorpayX payout documentation distinguishes contacts and fund accounts. Payout Links provide a collection-of-destination workflow in which the recipient supplies eligible details through a controlled experience. The link does not eliminate the need to validate the recipient and payment authority. [S52] [S55]

Proposed beneficiary model

A beneficiary record contains owner relationship, account type, encrypted destination details, normalized bank identifiers, verification state, risk flags, and version. A contact can have multiple beneficiary versions. Historical payouts reference the exact version used, not the contact's current account.

Account verification records the provider, reference, returned evidence, confidence category, and expiry. A successful verification proves only what the provider's process actually checked. It may not prove legal ownership, business legitimacy, or permission to make a particular payment. Those remain separate controls.

Payout Link lifecycle

A payout proposal creates an expiring recipient-claim link bound to intended recipient context, amount, currency, merchant, and purpose. The recipient completes the supported verification and supplies a destination. The platform then freezes the destination snapshot and applies the merchant's approval and risk policy before execution.

Protect links with high entropy, rate limits, short-lived sessions, and careful handling of messaging previews. A leaked link should not allow an attacker to redirect funds simply by being first to click. The proof required depends on the risk and product contract; it cannot rely only on a user-typed name.

Change and takeover controls

A beneficiary change can be more dangerous than a new payout. Notify established contacts, require appropriate independent approval, and apply risk-based delay or additional verification. A vendor email requesting a new bank account is evidence to investigate, not automatic authority.

Bind approvals to the destination fingerprint. If the destination changes after approval, invalidate the approval. An operator cannot edit the account behind an already-approved payout while leaving the approval badge green.

Privacy and retention

Mask account details in normal views and exports. Decrypt only within the narrow execution or verification boundary. Retain necessary payment evidence under the applicable policy, while allowing contact-data correction and removal where appropriate. Deleting a contact should not destroy the audit trail of completed payouts.

Acceptance tests

Test link replay, expired link submission, recipient phone-number changes, duplicate destination registration, same account used by suspiciously many contacts, beneficiary edit during approval, and a verification provider outage. Confirm that a failed verification cannot silently fall back to an unverified payout unless a documented product policy explicitly permits it with appropriate controls.

Design rule. Destination data is not ordinary profile data. Every meaningful change needs provenance, versioning, and a clear relationship to the financial approval that uses it.

Chapter 44Maker-checker, bulk payouts, and scheduled payments

Approval systems are effective only when they bind to the exact action later executed. A green approval badge on an editable spreadsheet is not a financial control. Bulk and scheduled payments make this binding especially important because time and scale separate proposal from execution.

Proposed approval object

An approval request contains the operation type, total amount, currency, debit account, beneficiary snapshots, purpose, schedule, policy version, and a cryptographic hash of the immutable proposal. Approvers authorize that hash. Any material edit creates a new version and invalidates prior approval.

Policies define independent approvers, amount thresholds, business-unit scope, delegation, expiry, and emergency exceptions. Prevent self-approval when separation of duties is required. A user's current role is checked when they approve and again where policy requires before execution; departed employees cannot leave indefinitely reusable approval authority.

Bulk import pipeline

Upload a file into a restricted staging area. Validate format, encoding, row counts, duplicate business references, amounts, currencies, beneficiary identifiers, and control totals. Produce a preview with explicit errors and warnings. The approved manifest records every row and total, not only the filename.

The batch is an orchestration container. Each row becomes a separately idempotent payout operation so that partial execution can be represented safely. A retry of the batch resumes unresolved rows; it does not resend successful rows. A rejected row can be corrected in a new version without rewriting the history of completed rows.

Scheduling

Store the intended business date, timezone, execution window, and funding rule. Approval may occur days before execution, but account authority, beneficiary validity, risk restrictions, and liquidity must be rechecked at the execution boundary. A scheduled instruction is not guaranteed merely because it was approved while funds were available.

Decide whether funds are reserved at approval or shortly before execution. Early reservation gives stronger funding assurance but reduces available liquidity. Late reservation improves flexibility but can fail on the scheduled date. The product must disclose and implement one coherent policy.

Race conditions

Cancellation and dispatch race. Use a state transition that atomically claims an operation for sending; cancellation succeeds only while the operation remains cancelable. Once sent, a cancellation request may become an investigation or partner cancellation attempt, not a guaranteed stop.

Approval-policy changes also need explicit treatment. High-risk changes can require reapproval of pending proposals. Low-risk changes may apply only to new proposals. Store the rule so that execution is not decided by whichever configuration a worker happened to cache.

Tests and operational visibility

Test one bad row in a large file, duplicate uploads, changed beneficiary after approval, insufficient funds at execution, partial bank completion, approver revocation, daylight-saving transitions in supported regions, and worker recovery after a batch is half sent.

Operator view. Show proposed, approved, scheduled, reserved, submitted, completed, failed, and unknown counts with amounts. A single batch percentage obscures the financial difference between unsent work and uncertain bank instructions.

Chapter 45Vendor payments, source-to-pay, and tax remittance

Vendor payment software connects procurement, invoice validation, approval, money movement, and accounting. Its value is not merely sending a bank transfer. It prevents paying the wrong invoice, paying it twice, or losing the relationship between the invoice, deduction, remittance, and accounting entry.

Documented scope

Razorpay documents vendor-payment workflows and a tax-payment application, including TDS-related automation and direct-tax payment experiences. Actual supported categories and filing responsibilities must be checked in the current product and professional tax guidance. Software automation does not remove the business's responsibility for correct classification and filing. [S56] [S83]

Proposed source-to-pay model

Represent supplier, purchase order, goods or service receipt, invoice, credit note, approval, payment allocation, and tax obligation separately. A three-way match compares purchase order, receipt, and invoice where that business process applies. Service businesses may need a different acceptance control; do not force a physical-goods model everywhere.

Invoice ingestion extracts fields into a draft with confidence and provenance. Automated extraction, including AI-assisted extraction, is not authority to pay. Validate supplier identity, invoice number, date, currency, total, tax details, and duplicate indicators. Store the original document and the reviewed structured version.

Duplicate prevention

Use a business uniqueness policy such as supplier plus invoice number plus relevant entity, with a review path for legitimate duplicates or corrections. A file hash alone is insufficient: the same invoice can be scanned twice with different bytes. Conversely, two different invoices can have the same amount and date.

Payment allocations reduce the approved unpaid amount under concurrency control. Partial payments and credit notes are explicit. A failed or uncertain payout does not automatically reopen the invoice for another fresh payment. The payment operation must first be resolved.

Tax obligations and evidence

Withholding calculations use effective-dated rules, supplier classification, thresholds, and reviewed inputs. Store the chosen rule version and calculation evidence. Do not hard-code current statutory rates into application code or assume all vendors have the same treatment.

A tax amount withheld from a vendor payment creates a separate obligation. The platform tracks its remittance, acknowledgment or challan, and reconciliation. Payment of tax and filing of a return are distinct tasks; the product must state which it performs and which remains with the customer or advisor.

ERP integration

Export approved accounting events with stable IDs, account mappings, and period information. The ERP connector is idempotent and maintains acknowledgments. If an ERP import fails, the bank payment remains real; the system retries the accounting projection rather than repeating the payout.

Tests

Test duplicate invoice scans, altered bank details, partial goods receipt, credit note after approval, tax-rule change mid-period, payout timeout, and missing tax acknowledgment. Ensure the support view can trace invoice to approval, payout, bank evidence, tax obligation, and ERP entry.

Design rule. Every automation step has a clear confidence and authority boundary. Extracting an invoice, approving it, paying it, remitting tax, and filing statutory documents are separate responsibilities.

Chapter 46Payroll as a deterministic financial workflow

Payroll combines sensitive personal data, effective-dated employment rules, calculations, approvals, bank execution, and statutory obligations. Employees experience errors immediately, so correctness and explainability matter as much as speed.

Product context

Razorpay's payroll documentation establishes a payroll product surface. This chapter provides a proposed engineering model rather than a statement of its private payroll engine or current statutory rates. Rates, thresholds, filing rules, and effective dates must be maintained from current authoritative guidance and reviewed by payroll professionals. [S57]

Proposed calculation engine

A payroll run references organization, pay period, jurisdiction, employee population, salary structures, attendance or payable-day inputs, variable pay, deductions, benefits, tax declarations, and rule-set version. Freeze these inputs into a calculation snapshot before approval.

Use deterministic fixed-precision arithmetic with explicit rounding at each defined boundary. Produce gross pay, employee deductions, employer contributions, net pay, and statutory liabilities as separate outputs. Re-running the same snapshot must produce the same result. A changed input creates a new run version and a visible difference report.

Effective dates and corrections

Salary changes, joining and leaving dates, unpaid leave, arrears, bonuses, and retroactive corrections must be modeled explicitly. Do not overwrite last month's salary structure and recompute old payslips as if the new terms always applied. Corrections create adjustment lines with references to the affected period.

Statutory rules are a versioned configuration package with legal source, effective interval, test cases, and approver. A government announcement and its effective date are different. The engine should support staged rule activation rather than require an emergency code release for every threshold change.

Approval and disbursement

Approval binds to the employee-level output manifest, bank destinations, totals, and rule version. Sensitive changes after approval invalidate it. The payout system creates one idempotent operation per employee payment, with batch-level control totals and partial-result handling.

A failed employee payment does not require rerunning the entire payroll calculation. Resolve or retry that payment under the original economic identity. A corrected bank account requires an approved destination change, not editing the already-submitted instruction.

Privacy and access

Restrict salary and identity data by role and purpose. A manager may approve attendance without seeing every employee's tax documents. Support exports are masked and audited. Payslips are delivered through authenticated access, not predictable public URLs.

Keep payroll calculation records, statutory evidence, and bank results linked while applying appropriate retention and deletion controls. An employee leaving the company does not mean all payroll evidence can immediately disappear, but it does mean ordinary account access should change.

Tests and close

Test leap-year periods, mid-month joining, unpaid leave, rounding boundaries, retroactive salary changes, duplicate payroll approval, bank partial failure, and rule changes with future effective dates. Reconcile gross-to-net totals, bank disbursements, statutory liabilities, and accounting exports.

Production gate. Parallel-run the engine against independently reviewed payroll for several representative periods before live disbursement. A unit test suite cannot by itself validate every jurisdiction's payroll interpretation.

Chapter 47Corporate cards, Capital, and credit-partner boundaries

Corporate cards and financing products add credit exposure and repayment obligations to the platform. A card dashboard, a loan-distribution service, and a regulated lender are different roles. Product names and old documentation must not obscure who actually extends credit and bears the risk.

Current and historical evidence

Razorpay's current corporate-card page provides the present product surface. The reviewed Capital URL redirected to that page, while older Capital documentation remains accessible through a legacy documentation host. This book does not treat those older pages as proof that every historical lending product is currently available. [S62] [S63]

The RBI's Digital Lending Directions distinguish regulated lending entities and service-provider responsibilities. Any proposed credit journey needs legal and partner review for disclosures, consent, data use, and fund flow. [S66]

Proposed corporate-card services

Maintain cardholder, organization, issuer reference, card status, spending policy, budget, authorization observations, clearing records, statement, repayment obligation, and dispute cases. Sensitive card credentials remain in the appropriate issuer or certified processing domain.

A card spending limit and a corporate budget are different. The issuer may decline an authorization even when a local budget appears available. Conversely, a delayed clearing record can exceed the original authorization or arrive after a card is frozen under permitted scheme behavior. The system must retain and reconcile these realities rather than assume a local toggle controls every downstream event.

Spend controls

Policies can restrict merchant categories, amounts, currencies, time windows, and cardholders where supported by the issuer. Approval binds to the specific policy version. Emergency freeze should propagate promptly, with measured acknowledgment; it is not retroactive cancellation of completed purchases.

Receipts and expense categorization are asynchronous accounting tasks. A missing receipt does not make the bank transaction disappear. Keep policy violation, reimbursement status, accounting category, and financial liability separate.

Lending workflow

For a lending product, represent application, consent, lender offer, underwriting decision, agreement, disbursement, repayment schedule, collections, and closure. The platform may prepare or distribute an offer, but must attribute the actual credit decision and agreement to the responsible lender.

Do not route loan proceeds or repayments through an arbitrary platform balance merely because that is technically convenient. The permitted flow follows the regulated arrangement. Customer-visible cost disclosures and accepted terms are immutable evidence, not a mutable marketing page.

Risk and accounting

Track credit principal, accrued charges where applicable, repayments, refunds, write-offs, and partner fees separately from payment-processing revenue. A merchant's high payment volume is not automatically safe collateral or permission to net any debt from safeguarded funds.

Acceptance test. A card is frozen after authorization but before clearing, an employee leaves, and the transaction later appears on the issuer statement. The platform must retain the obligation, reconcile it, and assign the expense workflow without reactivating the card or deleting the transaction.

Chapter 48Escrow+, forex funding, and purpose-specific money flows

Escrow, export receipts, intercompany invoices, and foreign investment funding may all involve bank transfers, but their legal purpose and release conditions differ. Reusing infrastructure is sensible; reusing the wrong contractual assumptions is dangerous.

Documented product boundaries

Razorpay's Escrow+ page describes a technology role involving bank and trustee arrangements. Its forex-funding page describes assistance with bringing foreign capital and certain other cross-border funds into India. These product pages are not a universal authorization for every escrow or foreign-exchange use case. [S61] [S84]

Proposed escrow workflow

Represent the governing agreement, parties, underlying bank account, trustee or authorized decision-maker, permitted credits, release conditions, disputes, and termination process. A release instruction references the exact agreement clause or approved condition and supporting evidence.

The software records and enforces the approved workflow, but does not impersonate the legal custodian. A business user marking a milestone complete is not sufficient unless the agreement grants that user the relevant authority. Conflicting claims freeze or route the affected release according to the agreement rather than allowing the fastest API caller to win.

Multi-party approvals

Release approval may require several organizations, independent evidence, or a trustee action. Store signatures or authenticated approvals, versions, deadlines, and revocation rules. A changed beneficiary or amount invalidates approval. The final bank instruction uses an immutable release manifest.

Partial releases, fees, interest where applicable, and residual balances require explicit accounting treatment. The platform cannot quietly sweep residual money into its operating account when an escrow agreement ends.

Forex funding and FDI-related workflows

Classify the purpose before selecting the flow. Investment funding, export invoice collection, and intercompany service payments can require different documents, banking review, reporting, and settlement treatment. Maintain a case with purpose, source entity, beneficiary entity, currency, bank references, required documents, and review status.

The engineering system should help professionals complete the correct process, not claim that an automatically generated checklist guarantees compliance. Store the source and effective date of each requirement, and provide a manual review path when facts do not fit a standard template.

Conversion and documentary evidence

FX quotes, conversion execution, bank credit, and documentary acknowledgment are separate events. A successful conversion does not prove that every required filing or certificate is complete. The dashboard shows financial and documentary progress independently.

Preserve the original foreign amount, conversion rate, deductions, INR amount where relevant, value date, and evidence references. Corrections produce new records and approved adjustments. A late document should not cause a second bank transfer.

Tests and launch gates

Test conflicting release claims, expired approvals, changed beneficiary, partial release, bank timeout, missing purpose documents, canceled funding, and residual balance on closure. Require a signed operating agreement, bank/trustee ownership map, approved money-flow diagram, and reconciliation process before enabling the product.

Design lesson. A common payment-instruction engine can serve many products, but the authority to create an instruction always comes from the specific product's legal and commercial workflow.

Chapter 49Fraud detection and real-time risk decisions

Fraud controls decide whether to allow, challenge, review, restrict, or reject an action. They must operate at several boundaries: merchant onboarding, customer payment, beneficiary change, payout, refund, and account recovery. One payment score cannot safely govern all of them.

Proposed risk architecture

An online decision service receives a typed action with merchant, customer or device context, amount, currency, instrument reference, destination, and relevant history. It combines deterministic rules with model scores and returns an action, reason categories, policy version, model version, and expiry.

Feature retrieval has a strict latency budget and explicit freshness. A feature-store outage must not silently turn missing data into a low-risk value. Use a documented degraded policy by action type. For example, an ordinary low-value checkout may have a constrained fallback, while a new high-value beneficiary payout requires stronger evidence or fails closed.

Features and signals

Useful proposed signals include velocity, unusual amount, device change, account age, beneficiary novelty, merchant category, refund patterns, chargeback history, and graph relationships. Each signal needs a defined purpose, data source, retention policy, and quality check. A signal's predictive value does not automatically justify unlimited collection or indefinite storage.

Keep online features reproducible. The decision record should capture the feature snapshot or a recoverable reference and watermark. Without this, investigators cannot explain why two similar payments received different decisions or whether a stale feature caused a failure.

Rules and models have different roles

Hard rules enforce legal, contractual, or safety constraints. Models estimate risk within permitted behavior. A model cannot override an unsupported country or expired mandate merely because it predicts low fraud. Conversely, overly broad rules can harm legitimate customers and require review of false positives.

Deploy models through shadow evaluation, backtesting, calibration checks, and bounded rollout. Delayed labels such as chargebacks mean that immediate conversion improvement is not enough evidence of safety. Monitor drift, loss, customer friction, and operational workload together.

Feedback and case management

A review case contains the triggering action, evidence, reason, decision deadline, and authorized reviewer. Manual decisions feed a labeled dataset only with provenance and quality controls. A support concession is not necessarily proof that the original transaction was fraudulent.

Risk actions must map to precise product controls. Restricting new payouts should not block a merchant from providing dispute evidence. A temporary checkout challenge should not permanently mark a customer as malicious. Store expiry and review conditions for restrictions.

Adversarial testing

Test missing features, stale velocity counters, model timeout, duplicate requests, feature poisoning through merchant-supplied fields, compromised support accounts, and coordinated small transactions that evade simple thresholds. Verify that rate-limit or risk-service overload cannot become an unbounded fail-open path.

Operational metric. Measure prevented loss estimates carefully and separately from observed losses. A rejected payment is not automatically fraud prevented. The engineering objective is a reliable, explainable decision system with bounded failure behavior, not a flattering count of blocked transactions.

Chapter 50Merchant monitoring, AML workflows, and prohibited activity

Risk does not end at onboarding. A merchant can change business model, experience account takeover, process payments for another business, or develop unusual refund and settlement patterns. Monitoring must connect transactional evidence to the approved merchant profile and the applicable legal obligations.

Regulatory anchor

The payment-aggregator framework and applicable financial-crime obligations require product-specific compliance analysis. This chapter proposes the engineering support for monitoring and investigations; it does not prescribe a universal legal filing threshold or replace qualified compliance judgment. [S64]

Proposed monitoring pipeline

Ingest payment, payout, refund, dispute, account-change, and merchant-profile events into a controlled monitoring stream. Rules compare activity with expected volumes, categories, geographic exposure, fulfilment patterns, and beneficial-ownership information. Models may identify unusual relationships, but alerts remain evidence for review rather than automatic legal conclusions.

Maintain watchlist and screening data with source, version, update time, and permitted use. Matching requires careful treatment of names, identifiers, and false positives. A common name alone should not produce an unexplained permanent freeze. Escalation and decision authority belong to the compliance operating model.

Case workflow

A case records the trigger, related entities, transaction set, evidence, reviewer, decision, restrictions, and required follow-up. Restrict access to sensitive investigation details. Customer-facing explanations must be reviewed so they do not disclose information that the applicable law or investigation process requires to remain confidential.

Keep alerts deduplicated and grouped by meaningful behavior. Ten thousand nearly identical alerts can make a monitoring system less effective than a smaller, well-prioritized queue. Track case aging, evidence completeness, review quality, and repeated unresolved patterns.

Product restrictions

Controls can limit transaction amount, new acceptance, settlement speed, destination changes, international methods, or particular products. A restriction has a scope, reason authority, effective time, and review date. The system should not use a generic account-disabled flag for every compliance action.

Money already received still needs accounting and a lawful disposition. A merchant suspension cannot make customer refunds, disputes, or residual obligations disappear. Provide controlled operational paths that preserve evidence and prevent unauthorized release.

Data quality and audit

Monitoring is only as reliable as its entity resolution and event completeness. A merchant using multiple accounts may appear harmless if each is evaluated in isolation. Conversely, incorrectly merging unrelated businesses can create severe false positives. Store relationship evidence and confidence, not just a single opaque graph node.

Audit every manual override, rule change, list update, and restriction release. High-risk overrides require independent approval. The monitoring team should be able to reproduce the rule and data version that generated a case months earlier.

Test scenario. A merchant's normal low-value retail activity suddenly becomes high-value cross-border collection followed by rapid refunds to altered destinations. The system should create a coherent case, restrict the relevant high-risk actions, preserve lawful access to records, and avoid an automatic destructive action that loses the financial trail.

Chapter 51PCI scope, cryptography, secrets, and HSM boundaries

Security architecture begins by deciding which services are allowed to handle sensitive payment data. Encryption is necessary, but a system that decrypts everywhere and logs everything remains dangerous. Minimize the sensitive-data boundary before optimizing its implementation.

Public standards and statements

Razorpay publicly states security and compliance credentials and describes controls in its security documentation. A buyer or builder still needs the current scoped audit reports and responsibility matrix; a logo does not establish that every product and integration is covered. [S40]

PCI DSS v4.0.1 and its applicable requirements are the standards reference for the relevant card-data environment. Requirements that were future-dated to March 2025 are no longer future at this book's cutoff. Scope and validation approach require qualified assessment. [S71] [S72]

Proposed security zones

Separate public edge, general application services, restricted payment-data services, cryptographic operations, administrative access, and analytics. Use network policy and service authorization, but do not treat a private network as sufficient trust. Every sensitive operation authenticates the caller and validates purpose and resource scope.

Keep raw credentials and sensitive authentication material out of general databases, message buses, logs, tracing, crash dumps, and support exports. Token references and masked metadata are usually enough for ordinary business workflows. Logging redaction should be schema-aware and tested with canary secrets.

Key hierarchy

Use envelope encryption with data keys scoped to a meaningful domain and wrapping keys controlled by KMS or an appropriate HSM-backed service. Separate keys by environment, legal or regional boundary where required, and sensitivity. Restrict decrypt privileges to narrow services and record use.

HSMs are appropriate for operations requiring protected key custody or certified payment cryptography. They do not automatically make the surrounding application secure. Define throughput, availability, backup, rotation, quorum administration, and disaster-recovery procedures for the cryptographic service itself.

Rotation and compromise

Every credential has an owner, purpose, scope, creation date, expiry or rotation policy, and revocation path. Support overlapping key versions where protocol semantics require it, such as webhook retries signed with an older secret. Keep rotation from becoming an unplanned outage by testing both old and new versions before retirement.

A compromise runbook identifies affected merchants, operations, data classes, and partner credentials. Revoke or rotate without destroying the evidence needed to reconcile in-flight payments. Emergency credential changes must not cause workers to resubmit uncertain operations under fresh identities.

Software and human access

Use signed build artifacts, dependency scanning, protected release pipelines, short-lived production access, and approval for sensitive actions. Support personnel see masked data by default. Break-glass access expires automatically and generates independent audit evidence.

Acceptance tests. Search logs and traces for planted fake secrets, attempt cross-tenant data access, rotate bank credentials during processing, fail the KMS dependency, and restore encrypted backups with the approved key-recovery process. A backup that cannot be decrypted under controlled recovery is not a usable backup.

Chapter 52Privacy, residency, and a versioned compliance map

Compliance is a set of obligations attached to data, entities, products, and dates. It cannot be implemented as a single compliant=true flag. The engineering goal is to make required controls explicit, testable, and attributable to an owner.

Current primary-source anchors

RBI's payment-data storage clarification addresses storage in India and conditions around foreign processing and cross-border components. A global cloud footprint does not imply permission to replicate all Indian payment data globally. [S65]

India's DPDP Rules 2025 publication includes an enforcement timeline and later corrigendum. Treat applicability as phased and provision-specific rather than assuming every obligation commenced on publication day. The RBI's non-bank PSO cyber-resilience directions likewise have phased applicability by category. [S67] [S69]

Proposed data inventory

Classify each field and dataset by purpose, sensitivity, legal entity, country, source, retention, allowed processors, permitted regions, and deletion behavior. Include logs, backups, analytics tables, feature stores, support attachments, and disaster-recovery copies. Residency is not satisfied if the primary database is local but unrestricted logs flow elsewhere.

A data-flow review follows information from collection through processing, sharing, archival, and deletion. Payment credentials receive the strictest minimization. Do not retain an OTP, PIN, or other sensitive authentication value just because a broad regulatory document discusses transaction-message fields.

Control registry

Each obligation maps to a control, owner, implementation, evidence, test, effective date, and review interval. Examples include regional storage policy, privileged-access review, incident reporting workflow, merchant due diligence, record retention, and secure deletion. Legal counsel determines applicability; engineering implements the approved control.

Use policy-as-code where possible: infrastructure placement restrictions, encryption requirements, export filters, backup locations, and data-access checks. Human controls still need evidence. A quarterly review that nobody can prove occurred is not a dependable control.

Retention and deletion

Different records have different retention needs. Financial journals, dispute evidence, identity documents, raw device signals, and marketing data should not share one indefinite retention period. Apply legal holds explicitly and release them through a controlled process.

Deletion can remove or pseudonymize personal attributes while preserving necessary financial integrity. A ledger entry may retain a non-reversible internal party reference and legally required accounting facts without exposing unnecessary customer details. Design this separation before personal data spreads into immutable free-text fields.

Cross-border engineering

Use region-bound encryption keys, data stores, and processing policies where required. Regional support access is still data access and needs review. Synthetic or properly anonymized test data is preferable to copying live payment data into global development environments.

Validation exercise. Select one payment and trace every copy of its data, including logs, fraud features, support exports, backups, and vendor systems. For each copy, identify purpose, region, access, retention, and deletion behavior. Any unknown copy is an inventory defect that can undermine the whole compliance claim.

Chapter 53Audit trails, support tooling, and safe financial operations

Support teams need enough power to resolve customer problems and little enough power that a compromised account cannot redirect funds. The correct design gives operators controlled actions with evidence and approval, not unrestricted database access.

Proposed audit event

Record actor, actor type, organization, action, resource, request ID, reason, approval reference, before/after fingerprints, policy version, time, and outcome. Sensitive values are masked or referenced rather than copied into the audit log. The audit stream is append-only and exported to a separately controlled store with integrity and retention controls.

Audit events should cover reads of especially sensitive data as well as writes. A support agent exporting thousands of customer records is a security-relevant action even if no payment status changes. Search access, downloads, and break-glass sessions need traceability.

A support timeline that tells the truth

The transaction view combines commercial order, payment attempts, external operations, observations, financial events, journals, refunds, disputes, and settlement references. It distinguishes event time from ingestion time and indicates source authority. This prevents an operator from mistaking a delayed callback for a newly initiated charge.

Show uncertainty explicitly. An unknown payout should offer query, evidence collection, escalation, and approved resolution actions. It should not offer a convenient fresh retry that silently creates a second bank transfer.

Controlled repair commands

A financial repair is a typed command with preconditions, expected effect, dry-run output, evidence, and approval. Examples include linking an unmatched bank record, reversing an erroneous internal allocation, or posting an approved fee adjustment. The command goes through the normal ledger service and emits the same audit and outbox records as other operations.

Avoid production SQL as the standard operating interface. Even when an emergency database action is unavoidable, require a reviewed script, scoped access, backup or rollback analysis, recorded execution, and post-action reconciliation. Do not let urgency become a permanent bypass.

Support impersonation and access

Prefer scoped diagnostic views over full merchant impersonation. If impersonation is necessary, display a clear banner, limit actions, record the session, and require justification. High-risk actions such as changing beneficiaries or approving payouts should remain unavailable through ordinary support impersonation.

Break-glass credentials are short-lived and independently monitored. The person granting emergency access should not be the only person reviewing its use. Customer communications and internal investigation notes have different access requirements.

Tests and metrics

Test cross-merchant search, exported data leakage, approval bypass, altered repair parameters after approval, expired support access, and audit-store outage. Decide which high-risk operations stop when durable audit capture is unavailable; silently dropping the audit trail is not acceptable.

Operational metric. Track unresolved financial cases, repeat contacts, time to evidence-backed resolution, and repair recurrence. Fast ticket closure is not success if the customer still has an unexplained debit or the repair created a new reconciliation break.

A regional launch is not a deployment flag. It changes payment methods, regulated entities, bank partners, currencies, language, tax treatment, customer expectations, support coverage, and data rules. Shared infrastructure should reduce duplication without erasing those differences.

Documented regional surface

Razorpay Curlec's Malaysia site lists local payment methods and products, including FPX, DuitNow, cards, e-wallets, and recurring-payment capabilities. Its subscription page describes regional recurring options. These sources establish a Malaysian product surface, not that Indian APIs and operating rules can be copied unchanged. [S85] [S86]

Proposed regional capability package

A country package defines operating entity, licensing and partner arrangements, currencies, amount precision, payment methods, merchant onboarding, settlement calendars, tax configuration, data residency, support language, and dispute processes. Product services consume the package through a typed capability interface.

Shared domain concepts include order, attempt, operation, observation, journal, refund, and settlement. Rail adapters and legal workflows remain regional. A UPI-specific field must not become mandatory in a generic payment object used for a Malaysian bank-transfer method.

Infrastructure placement

Deploy region-bound execution cells and data stores according to the approved data-flow model. The public Razorpay engineer deployment account is useful evidence of the operational complexity of regional expansion, but does not establish that every service is globally active-active. [S09]

A global control plane can distribute non-sensitive configuration and product metadata where permitted. It should not be a hidden dependency that prevents all regions from accepting payments when one central service fails. Regional snapshots have validity, revocation, and fallback rules.

Commercial and financial separation

Each region's legal entity and currency have separate ledger domains and bank reconciliation. Consolidated corporate reporting translates and aggregates through explicit accounting processes. It does not allow a shortage in one safeguarded account to be covered invisibly by another region's merchant money.

Cross-border products connect the domains through approved corridors, not arbitrary database transfers. Track both sides, FX events, fees, and partner obligations. Regional availability statements must be specific to product and merchant eligibility.

Launch process

Begin with a narrow method and merchant cohort. Complete partner certification, local support training, legal review, reconciliation, incident drills, and finance sign-off. Use real regional calendars and customer journeys in testing. A translated checkout with no local dispute process is not a finished launch.

Tests

Test unsupported method exposure, currency rounding, local holidays, regional data export, country-specific invoice rules, time-zone boundaries, and a global control-plane outage. Verify that a tenant in one region cannot retrieve another region's restricted data through a shared support or analytics endpoint.

Design rule. Reuse the platform's invariants and engineering tools; localize its authority, rails, and customer promises. The capability matrix should make unsupported combinations impossible to select, not merely document them in a wiki.

Developer experience determines whether merchants integrate the platform correctly. A powerful API with ambiguous examples can cause duplicate payments and broken fulfilment. Newer agent interfaces make clear contracts and constrained authority even more important.

Documented developer and agent surface

Razorpay documents a CLI and MCP server. Its March 2026 Agentic Platform announcement uses an early-access framing, while the agentic-payments publication describes pilot activity. Treat these as different maturity states and avoid labeling all agent-related capabilities generally available. [S49] [S50] [S48] [S47]

Proposed API standards

Use stable resource identifiers, explicit environments, integer money amounts, consistent error categories, pagination, request IDs, and documented idempotency behavior. Distinguish a synchronous result from an accepted asynchronous operation. Return a durable operation reference and a status-query path when execution continues later.

Errors should say whether retry is safe, requires the same key, requires customer action, or is prohibited until status resolution. Never encourage clients to retry every 500 response with a new key. Publish sample receivers that verify raw-body signatures and deduplicate events.

SDK and sandbox design

SDKs wrap the API without hiding important state. Defaults include sensible timeouts, bounded retries for eligible operations, request tracing, and secret-safe logging. Mobile and browser SDKs never embed merchant secrets. Version compatibility and deprecation dates are visible.

Test mode simulates product semantics but is not evidence that live banks will behave identically. Provide programmable scenarios for timeout-after-commit, delayed success, reversal, partial batch failure, webhook reordering, and settlement mismatch. A sandbox that only returns happy-path success teaches unsafe integrations.

CLI and infrastructure as code

The CLI supports inspection, test event generation, configuration validation, and controlled deployment of merchant settings. Separate read-only and side-effecting commands. Production operations require explicit environment selection and, for high-risk actions, preview and approval.

Configuration-as-code stores references to secrets, not secrets themselves. Plans show changes to routing, webhook endpoints, payout rules, and settlement settings. The apply operation binds to the reviewed plan version and records audit evidence.

MCP and agent boundaries

Expose narrow tools such as inspect payment, explain error, create a draft refund proposal, or generate an integration sample. Do not give an agent unrestricted SQL, raw API secrets, or permission to approve its own payout. Tool schemas include merchant scope, amount limits, required confirmations, and idempotency semantics.

Treat tool output, customer messages, invoices, and web content as untrusted input. An instruction hidden in a document must not override the operator's purpose or expand payment authority. Side-effecting actions use server-enforced policy regardless of what the model says.

Evaluation

Test agent attempts to change beneficiary, bypass approval, replay a payment, exfiltrate secrets, or interpret a support note as authorization. Evaluate correctness with deterministic state and ledger checks, not only conversational quality.

Design rule. An agent can help prepare and explain a financial action. The platform's authorization, approval, idempotency, and accounting controls still decide whether that action is allowed and how it executes.

Chapter 56Storage, partitioning, and financial consistency

Choose storage by the invariant it must preserve. A ledger needs atomic, durable, constrained writes and reproducible history. Search needs flexible retrieval. Analytics needs large scans. A cache needs low latency. Using one database for all four can create operational coupling; using four databases without an authority model creates inconsistency.

Proposed storage roles

Use a relational transactional store for financial operations, journals, balances, reservations, idempotency records, and outboxes. PostgreSQL is an illustrative choice because its transaction and constraint model supports these requirements; this is not a claim that it is Razorpay's ledger database. Validate the selected engine's actual failure and replication behavior in your environment. [S78]

Use object storage for immutable bank files, evidence, exports, and analytical lake data with appropriate encryption and retention. Search indexes and analytical tables are projections. A key-value cache accelerates configuration and read paths but cannot independently authorize money movement.

Tenant and shard keys

Include merchant or owner, operating entity, environment, and currency in the relevant keys and authorization checks. A globally unique ID does not remove the need to verify tenant ownership. Partition large operational tables by a combination of ledger domain and time where appropriate, preserving the ability to locate records during support and reconciliation.

Do not shard solely by timestamp if every current write then lands on one hot partition. Do not shard solely by merchant if one large merchant overwhelms a shard. A cell can contain several ledger domains and partition hot operational workloads while preserving financial atomicity within a domain.

Atomicity boundaries

A journal, its balance effects, its economic uniqueness key, and its outbox event commit together. A payment-attempt service may have a separate database, but its request to the ledger is idempotent and its state reflects the accepted ledger result. Avoid a design where two services each claim to be the final authority for the same balance.

Cross-domain transfers use explicit clearing accounts and a durable transfer protocol. They may temporarily create matched pending obligations, with reconciliation and timeout handling. Calling two database writes in sequence is not an atomic transfer; a message broker does not make the pair atomic either.

Read consistency

Financial authorization reads the authoritative balance path. Dashboard lists can use replicas or projections with visible freshness. A merchant who requests a refund should receive a read-your-operation view of that request even if the general transaction list lags. Provide operation-specific status endpoints rather than forcing every read to be globally synchronous.

Replica lag is monitored and bounded by use case. Never route a payout availability check to a stale replica because it is geographically closer. Caches include version or watermark information and are invalidated or bypassed on critical changes.

Schema evolution and retention

Use expand-migrate-contract changes. Add compatible fields, deploy readers and writers, backfill with resumable jobs, verify, then remove old paths. Financial history is not rewritten casually during a schema migration. Retention and archival preserve queryable lineage and required uniqueness references.

Tests. Inject replica lag, transaction aborts, deadlocks, disk exhaustion, index corruption in a test environment, and interrupted backfills. Rebuild balance projections from journals and compare. A database benchmark that ignores these failure paths is insufficient for a payment core.

Chapter 57Transactional outboxes, queues, and sagas

Distributed workflows need durable progress without pretending that every service and bank participates in one transaction. The proposed design uses local transactions, outboxes, idempotent consumers, and explicit sagas. The goal is recoverable execution, not a marketing claim of end-to-end exactly-once delivery.

The dual-write problem

If a service commits a payment state and then publishes an event, it can crash between the two. If it publishes first, consumers may act on a state that later rolls back. Put the state change and outbox row in the same database transaction. A relay publishes committed rows and marks progress independently.

The relay can publish twice if it crashes after publication but before recording acknowledgment. Consumers therefore keep an inbox keyed by event identity and apply their business update plus inbox record atomically. Delivery remains at least once; business effects are deduplicated within the defined transaction boundary.

Event design

Events include stable event ID, aggregate ID, aggregate version, operating entity, merchant scope, event type, schema version, occurred time, recorded time, correlation ID, and evidence or operation reference. Avoid placing reusable credentials or unnecessary personal data in broadly distributed events.

Partition ordered streams by the aggregate whose transitions require ordering. A merchant-wide partition can become hot; an order or operation partition may be more appropriate. Ordering on a broker partition does not guarantee global order across API queries, bank files, and callbacks, so consumers still validate state and version.

Saga state

A saga records steps, preconditions, attempts, deadlines, observations, and compensations. For a marketplace payout, steps might reserve obligations, approve the batch, submit bank legs, reconcile results, and finalize statements. Each step is resumable and has a stable economic identity.

Compensation is not time travel. A completed bank transfer may require a new return payment or receivable, not cancellation. A saga must represent partial completion honestly. Do not roll back internal history to make the workflow look atomic when external money already moved.

Retry discipline

Use bounded exponential backoff with jitter for transient errors. Separate transport retry from business retry. Dead-letter or quarantine queues contain reason, payload reference, attempt history, and owner. A poison message should not block all later events indefinitely, but skipping it must create a visible consistency obligation.

Replay tools support dry-run, scoped ranges, schema compatibility checks, and rate limits. Rebuilding an analytics projection can replay facts freely; replaying a command topic that triggers bank writes requires much stronger controls. Prefer immutable facts and typed operation requests with deduplication over arbitrary executable messages.

Backpressure

Protect critical queues from bulk analytics, exports, and notification spikes. Use separate pools and priority classes, while avoiding starvation of reconciliation work. A growing queue of unknown bank operations deserves different treatment from a growing queue of marketing emails.

Acceptance test. Crash at every boundary: before commit, after commit, after publish, after consumer update, after bank send, and after bank confirmation. For each point, document the durable record that enables recovery and prove that replay cannot repeat the economic effect.

Chapter 58Cells, availability zones, and dependency isolation

The proposed infrastructure is organized into bounded execution cells. A cell contains the services and data dependencies needed to serve a defined merchant population. This makes failures, deployments, capacity limits, and recovery exercises smaller and more understandable.

Cell composition

A typical cell has API and checkout services, payment orchestration, risk decision access, connector workers, transactional stores, queues, caches, and local observability. Shared services are limited and have explicit outage behavior. The cell directory maps merchant and product to the correct execution domain and is replicated with controlled updates.

Deploy stateless services across availability zones with anti-affinity and sufficient spare capacity. Kubernetes can help place workloads across zones, but its scheduling primitives do not establish database consensus, bank idempotency, or correct financial failover. Those require separate design and testing. [S81]

Failure domains

Separate connector worker pools by partner so that slow bank calls cannot consume every thread or socket. Separate batch exports, fraud training, and reconciliation parsing from interactive request capacity. Use bounded queues and connection pools rather than relying on autoscaling after overload has already cascaded.

Critical dependencies need budgets. If an API synchronously requires six independent services each available 99.99% of the time, a simplistic independence calculation gives about 99.94% combined availability. Real failures are often correlated, making multiplication only an illustration. Reduce unnecessary synchronous dependencies and design explicit degraded modes.

Capacity after failure

A three-zone deployment that uses nearly all capacity in normal operation may collapse when one zone disappears. Size and test the remaining zones to handle the agreed degraded or full load. Include database connections, partner quotas, HSM capacity, and queue throughput, not only application CPU.

Autoscaling signals should include queue age, concurrency, latency, and saturation. Payment workloads can be bursty enough that pre-warmed capacity is necessary. A bank rate limit remains a hard dependency even when more application pods are available.

Cell isolation versus shared efficiency

Cells duplicate some infrastructure and increase operational overhead. In return, they limit blast radius and make tenant-level availability more predictable. Share build tooling, deployment templates, and observability standards rather than sharing every runtime bottleneck.

A large merchant may need a dedicated or specially sized domain, but avoid promising physical isolation without defining what remains shared: edge, control plane, bank connectors, keys, and support tooling. Isolation is a matrix, not a binary label.

Migration between cells

Moving a merchant requires data transfer, routing coordination, writer fencing, idempotency-history preservation, and treatment of in-flight operations. Freeze or drain the relevant write authority, copy and verify state, switch routing with a new epoch, and keep old callbacks correctly routable. A DNS change alone cannot safely migrate financial authority.

Game day. Remove a zone, slow one bank, and saturate a reporting job at peak load. Unaffected cells and rails should continue within their tested envelope, while affected operations remain durable and correctly classified.

Chapter 59Disaster recovery, fencing, and proof after restore

Disaster recovery is not complete when the health endpoint turns green. A payment platform must prove which operations were acknowledged, which were sent externally, which journals committed, and which bank outcomes remain uncertain. Otherwise recovery can resurrect old instructions or forget completed money movement.

Choose an honest durability model

A baseline design can provide synchronous replication across local availability zones and asynchronous replication to another permitted region. That may support zero acknowledged loss for a covered zone failure, but not automatically for total regional loss. Publish the actual regional RPO and recovery procedure.

A stronger regional-loss objective may require synchronously durable financial intent and journal records across suitably independent permitted failure domains. This adds latency, cost, and availability trade-offs during partitions. The exact quorum topology, failure assumptions, and residency constraints must be designed and tested; two regions with a vague active-active label are not a proof.

Single-writer authority and fencing

Each financial partition has an authoritative writer epoch. A promoted writer receives a strictly newer epoch through a mechanism that prevents the old writer from continuing valid writes. Database and dispatch paths reject stale epochs. Merely expiring a worker lease is insufficient if a paused process can later resume and send an old bank instruction.

Bank-facing dispatch needs particular care. Stop or fence old dispatchers before promoting replacements, retain original external references, and query uncertain operations. A new region must not recreate all pending requests with fresh bank references.

Recovery inventory

Recover the journal, balances, reservations, operation records, idempotency history, outboxes, inboxes, configuration versions, approval manifests, and cryptographic access. Restoring the payment table alone is not enough. A missing idempotency record can make a replay look new; a missing reservation can make committed funds appear spendable.

Treat externally acknowledged operations after the last recoverable checkpoint as a reconciliation problem. Obtain partner and bank evidence and rebuild only through controlled financial commands. Do not guess that absent local rows mean the bank never executed them.

Recovery gates

Before reopening writes, verify database integrity, journal balance, projection equality, writer epoch, key availability, queue checkpoints, and unresolved external-operation inventory. Open read-only status access first where safe, then bounded money movement by product and cell. High-risk operations may remain paused until evidence is complete.

An illustrative warm-recovery objective might be 15 minutes for a specified regional incident, but that cannot coexist with an unqualified five-nines promise if such incidents consume more than the entire error budget. Contractual targets must fit the tested failure model.

Backup and restore testing

Use encrypted immutable backups and point-in-time recovery where supported. Regularly restore into an isolated environment and verify financial invariants, not just database startup. Test key recovery and access controls. A backup containing valid data but missing usable keys or approval history is incomplete.

Critical test. Restore to a point before a payout completed, then ingest the bank's completion evidence. The system must recover the original operation and account for it once, not pay again because the restored local state says pending.

Chapter 60Edge services, rate limits, DDoS protection, and flash sales

The edge protects both the platform and its banking partners. During a flash sale, uncontrolled retries can turn a brief slowdown into a prolonged outage. Good admission control preserves durable progress and gives clients truthful retry guidance.

Proposed request path

Use redundant DNS and edge routing, TLS termination, request-size limits, application-layer protection, authentication, tenant resolution, and quota enforcement. Separate public checkout assets from secret-bearing API endpoints. Cache static assets aggressively while keeping transaction and customer data out of shared caches.

Rate limits are scoped by merchant, credential, endpoint class, and infrastructure domain. A single large merchant must not consume every database connection or bank quota. Reserve capacity for status queries, callbacks, refunds, and incident recovery rather than allocating everything to new payment creation.

Admission before expensive work

Reject or defer excess work before performing costly cryptography, database transactions, or partner calls. A clear overload response should specify safe retry behavior and preserve the client's idempotency key. Do not accept an instruction into an unbounded in-memory queue and then lose it when the process restarts.

For asynchronous work, acceptance means durable enqueue or operation creation. If the durable store is unavailable, return an honest failure rather than pretending the instruction was accepted. The client can retry the same intended operation according to the API contract.

Retry amplification

Suppose a merchant retries three times, the SDK retries three times, and an internal proxy retries three times. One customer action can create many transport attempts. Set an end-to-end retry budget, propagate deadlines, and make one layer responsible for each retry class.

Use jitter and backoff so that clients do not synchronize on exact retry intervals. Side-effecting operations retain their business identity. Read-only status queries can be retried more freely but still need rate limits to avoid overwhelming a recovering partner.

Flash-sale readiness

Preload approved configuration, pre-warm capacity, test checkout asset delivery, and validate bank quotas. Estimate method mix and peak concurrency rather than only average TPS. Simulate customer app switching, slow issuer responses, and merchant webhook delays under load.

A waiting room or controlled admission can be appropriate for some commerce experiences, but it must not hold an already-authorized payment in an ambiguous state. Queue before initiating external money movement where possible, and show the customer a clear state.

Abuse controls

Protect login, API-key validation, order creation, payment-link creation, webhook endpoints, and beneficiary verification from targeted abuse. Separate malicious traffic from legitimate high-volume merchants using authenticated quotas and behavior signals. A WAF rule that blocks legitimate bank callbacks can create financial uncertainty, so changes need staged validation.

Load test. Combine a 10x legitimate burst with a noisy tenant and a slow bank. Verify bounded memory, connection use, queue age, and retry volume. The system should shed work predictably without losing acknowledged operations or starving reconciliation.

Chapter 61Observability, payment success, and error-budget operations

Observability must explain both technical behavior and economic progress. A green CPU chart cannot tell you whether merchants are being paid correctly. A single success-rate number cannot tell you whether the problem is checkout, issuer declines, routing, bank processing, or reconciliation.

Proposed telemetry model

Every request and operation carries correlation identifiers across edge, order, attempt, connector, observation, journal, and settlement. Traces record timings and state transitions without sensitive payloads. Metrics use bounded labels; raw merchant IDs or payment IDs belong in controlled logs or traces, not unbounded metric dimensions.

Emit domain metrics from durable facts where possible. Count accepted operations, unknown outcomes, completed refunds, settlement obligations, and reconciliation breaks. A log line saying success before commit should not increment the authoritative completed-payment metric.

Separate the dashboards

The API dashboard measures valid request handling, latency, saturation, and internal errors. The payment dashboard measures attempts, order completion, method conversion, technical failures, issuer declines, customer abandonment, and risk decisions. The money dashboard measures ledger invariants, liquidity, reserved funds, settlement timeliness, and reconciliation completeness.

The operations dashboard measures aged unknowns, missed source files, dispute deadlines, support cases, and manual repairs. These views are linked but not collapsed into one percentage. A bank outage can leave internal availability healthy while customer success drops sharply.

SLO implementation

Define eligible events and exclusions in code and review them. Use multi-window burn-rate alerts for availability and latency objectives, with separate immediate alerts for safety invariants. The SRE workbook provides the underlying burn-rate approach; the specific objectives and thresholds here are proposed and must be calibrated. [S80]

Avoid paging on every customer decline. Page when internal error budgets burn, a partner's technical failures materially affect customers, a financial invariant fails, or a time-bound obligation is at risk. Route alerts to the owner who can act, with a runbook and relevant evidence.

Synthetic and real-user checks

Synthetic checks exercise order creation, checkout rendering, status queries, and approved test or low-risk production paths. They cannot fully represent every issuer or customer environment. Real-user telemetry identifies browser, SDK, geography, and method-specific failures, with privacy controls.

Reconciliation acts as a slower but stronger correctness monitor. A platform can pass synthetics while misallocating fees or missing bank credits. Treat financial-close controls as part of observability, not merely finance paperwork.

Incident attribution

Record deployment, configuration, model, partner, and infrastructure changes on the same timeline. Correlation suggests an investigation path, not automatic causation. Postmortems separate trigger, contributing conditions, customer impact, financial exposure, and recovery proof.

Acceptance test. Introduce a bug that returns HTTP 200 but fails to enqueue settlement work. Technical status checks may stay green; domain metrics and obligation-aging alerts must detect the failure before the contractual deadline is missed.

Chapter 62CI/CD, configuration releases, and safe migrations

A payment platform changes constantly: code, routing rules, pricing, bank certificates, fraud models, tax rules, and schema. All can affect money. A safe release system treats configuration and data migrations as first-class production changes.

Public engineering evidence

A Razorpay engineer's regional-deployment talk describes standardization efforts involving Spinnaker, Helm, templates, and policy controls. It is evidence of a particular engineering effort, not a requirement to choose those exact tools. Our design borrows the principle of declarative, controlled rollout rather than asserting a complete private deployment topology. [S09]

Proposed release pipeline

Build once from reviewed source, produce a signed artifact with dependency inventory, run unit and integration tests, validate policy, deploy to a realistic staging environment, and promote the same artifact through canary cohorts. Record artifact digest, configuration version, schema compatibility, approver, and rollout timeline.

Separate deployment from feature activation where useful. A dormant code path can be deployed before enabling a bank or merchant cohort. However, feature flags that change accounting semantics require the same rigor as code; they are not an exemption from review.

Canary design

Select cohorts by cell, merchant risk, method, and partner compatibility. Observe technical latency, error rate, payment outcomes, unknown-state growth, ledger posting failures, and reconciliation signals. Some financial defects appear only later, so a short canary cannot prove every settlement or dispute behavior.

Use shadow evaluation for risk or routing decisions without sending duplicate bank instructions. A shadow connector may compare request construction, but must not accidentally execute the same payment against a second partner.

Schema and data changes

Apply expand-migrate-contract: add compatible fields and indexes, deploy mixed-version readers and writers, backfill in bounded batches, verify counts and financial invariants, then retire old fields. Backfills are resumable and idempotent, with checkpoints and audit evidence.

Do not combine an irreversible financial data rewrite with an application rollback plan that assumes the old schema remains valid. For money semantics, prefer new event versions and explicit correction workflows. Historical postings remain interpretable under their original schema and pricing versions.

Bank and key changes

Certificates, IP allowlists, credentials, and protocol mappings have their own expiry and rollout plans. Test overlap where supported and monitor partner acknowledgment. A bank certificate rotation can cause an outage without any application deployment, so it belongs on the release calendar and alerting system.

Rollback limitations

Rolling back code does not undo bank transfers or posted journals. Stop further unsafe execution, restore a compatible version, and reconcile the affected cohort. Financial remediation is a reviewed workflow with explicit correcting events. A deployment system must not replay old queues blindly after rollback.

Release gate. Every change declares blast radius, money effects, compatibility, observability, stop conditions, rollback or forward-fix plan, and financial verification. Emergency releases may shorten timing, but should not remove the minimum evidence needed to recover safely.

A data platform supports merchant reporting, finance analysis, fraud features, experimentation, and company operations. It should make information useful without becoming a second, inconsistent source of financial authority.

Publicly documented snapshots

Razorpay's 2020 account describes a CDC-and-streaming data architecture. A 2022 engineer case study describes Trino and Alluxio analytics. The March 2026 AWS/Razorpay publication describes an EMR/Spark/S3/Delta Lake pipeline with CDC and orchestration components. These are dated analytical-platform disclosures; their data volumes are not payment TPS. [S06] [S07] [S08]

Proposed ingestion layers

Capture committed operational changes through a supported CDC mechanism or transactional event stream. Preserve source transaction identifiers, schema version, ordering metadata, and ingestion time. Land restricted raw data in an encrypted, access-controlled layer, then produce validated and purpose-specific curated datasets.

CDC is not permission to copy every secret into the lake. Apply field allowlists and sensitive-data controls close to the source. Masking after broad replication leaves unnecessary exposure in raw objects, logs, and failed-job outputs.

Reporting correctness

Merchant reports show a data watermark and distinguish operational status from financially reconciled status. A report can be near real-time while settlement figures remain provisional. Finance reports use approved accounting definitions and reconciled sources, not whichever analytical table is fastest.

Maintain metric definitions as versioned code: processed value, captured value, settled value, net revenue, refunds, disputes, and active merchants. A definition change should not silently alter historical board reports without a restatement process.

Late and corrected data

Streams can arrive late and files can be replaced. Use event time and ingestion time, bounded lateness where appropriate, and explicit correction records. A daily aggregate must support restatement rather than permanently dropping late bank evidence after a watermark passes.

Data-quality checks cover completeness, uniqueness, referential integrity, amount/currency consistency, and source control totals. Quarantine bad records with an owner. A pipeline that silently substitutes zero for a malformed amount can create plausible but false reports.

ML feature consistency

Online and offline feature definitions should share logic or validation so that model training does not use information unavailable at decision time. Prevent target leakage from future chargebacks or post-event settlement outcomes. Store feature and model versions with risk decisions.

Training jobs, ad hoc SQL, and large exports have separate resource pools and quotas from interactive payment processing. Analytical cost optimization must not degrade the authoritative database through uncontrolled replica queries or CDC lag.

Tests and lineage

Replay a source interval, introduce a schema change, deliver a late refund, replace a bank file, and rebuild a merchant report. The result should converge with clear lineage and no duplicate financial actions. Replaying analytics events must never call the payout execution service.

Design rule. The data platform explains and analyzes the money core. It does not repair the money core by editing an aggregate until the numbers look right.

Chapter 64Testing, simulators, certification, and chaos engineering

A payment platform needs tests that reason about state and money, not only HTTP responses. The most important failures happen between systems, after one side commits and the other loses the response. These must be reproducible before they occur in production.

A layered test strategy

Unit tests validate pure calculations, state reducers, pricing, matching, and policy rules. Property tests generate sequences of duplicate, delayed, and reordered events and check invariants. Database integration tests exercise real constraints, transaction isolation, deadlocks, and rollback. Connector contract tests verify partner message construction and interpretation.

End-to-end tests cover customer interaction through bank simulation, ledger posting, settlement, and reconciliation. Partner certification uses the actual required test environments and cases. No internal simulator substitutes for scheme, bank, hardware, or regulatory certification where required.

Deterministic bank simulator

Build a simulator that can accept a request and lose the response, return processing before success, duplicate callbacks, reverse a completed operation, delay statements, reject one batch row, and return malformed or contradictory observations. Each scenario is controlled by a test seed and produces a known external financial history.

The simulator's ground truth is independent of our platform's state. Tests compare the two. Otherwise the platform can be wrong in the same way as the simulator and still pass.

Financial properties

Check that journals balance within currency and entity, economic event keys are unique, refunds do not exceed permitted amounts, reservations are not released twice, and settled obligations are not selected again. Verify that wrong-tenant references and unsupported currency combinations are rejected.

Also test semantic errors: a balanced journal crediting the wrong merchant, an incorrect fee version, or an unmatched bank credit. Arithmetic balance alone is a weak oracle. Reconciliation and ownership assertions must catch these cases.

Concurrency and recovery

Use real concurrent workers for order creation, payout reservation, refund requests, and settlement selection. Inject crashes at every commit and send boundary. Run restore tests with incomplete local state and complete external bank evidence. Verify stale-writer fencing and preservation of idempotency history.

Chaos tests begin in controlled environments with explicit safety boundaries. In production, use scoped experiments that do not intentionally create unauthorized financial movements. Testing bank uncertainty does not require risking real customer money when a well-designed simulator can reproduce it.

A release packet includes test results, known gaps, certification status, load envelope, rollback behavior, and financial verification. The small executable model supplied with this book is a teaching aid. Its passing tests demonstrate selected local invariants, not a validated production payment system or a tested distributed bank integration.

Acceptance gate. Before launch, another team should be able to run the failure catalog, reproduce outcomes, and inspect the financial proof without relying on the original implementer's explanation. Repeatability is the difference between a convincing demo and an engineering control.

Chapter 65Capacity planning, costs, and unit economics

Capacity planning starts with an explicit workload model, not a claim that the platform can handle millions of transactions. Payment rate, API rate, journal-write rate, concurrent bank calls, and analytical data volume are different quantities. The numbers below are illustrative assumptions for learning, not Razorpay scale or cloud-price quotations.

An example workload

Assume 100 payment attempts per second on average and 3,000 at a short peak. That is 8.64 million attempts per day. If each attempt drives eight API or internal request operations, the peak request rate is approximately 24,000 per second before retries and background work.

Assume four journals per completed lifecycle on average and two lines per journal. At a sustained equivalent peak, that is 12,000 journals and 24,000 journal lines per second. Balance updates, journal headers, uniqueness records, outboxes, indexes, refunds, and settlement work add substantial writes. Benchmark the actual posting transaction, not a trivial single-row insert.

Storage arithmetic

At an assumed 6 KB of raw operational records per attempt, daily growth is about 51.84 GB in decimal units. Thirty days is about 1.56 TB before indexes and replication. An illustrative 2x storage factor including indexes and 3 copies yields about 9.33 TB for that hot window. Evidence files, logs, backups, and analytics are additional.

These assumptions are deliberately replaceable. Measure real row widths, compression, index amplification, retention, and late-event access. Do not size a financial database solely from the JSON response payload.

Concurrency and partner limits

At 3,000 operations per second and two seconds average external latency, Little's Law suggests roughly 6,000 concurrent external operations in steady state. A latency spike to ten seconds can increase that to 30,000 if admission remains unchanged. Connection pools and worker limits must prevent this from exhausting the platform.

Bank quotas, HSM throughput, database commit latency, and hot-account contention can dominate before CPU. Model each separately. Use the workload's method mix and merchant concentration, not a uniform random benchmark that avoids real hotspots.

Cost model

Separate fixed platform cost, variable compute and storage, partner/network charges, support effort, fraud and dispute losses, compliance, and engineering. Track cost per attempt, successful order, settled payment, payout, and active merchant. Failed attempts still consume resources.

For a purely illustrative per-payment contribution model, assume INR 2.00 net service revenue, INR 1.20 partner cost, INR 0.10 risk loss, INR 0.05 infrastructure allocation, and INR 0.20 variable operations. Contribution is INR 0.45 before fixed overhead and other items. These are invented planning inputs, not actual fees, margins, or industry averages.

Capacity gates

Define normal, peak, degraded-zone, degraded-bank, and recovery-backlog envelopes. Load tests include retries, skewed merchants, settlement bursts, and reconciliation imports. Record the maximum safe throughput before latency or correctness degrades, and maintain headroom below it.

Decision rule. Scale the bottleneck proven by measurement. Adding application replicas will not solve a serialized hot ledger account or a bank's fixed quota. Redesign partitioning, aggregation, or admission only after preserving the financial invariants.

Chapter 66Teams, ownership, on-call, and customer operations

A payment company is an operating organization as much as a software system. Banks have incidents, merchants need explanations, disputes have deadlines, and finance must close the books. Ownership gaps become customer money problems.

Proposed team boundaries

Organize around durable responsibilities: payment acceptance and checkout; bank and network integrations; money core and reconciliation; merchant platform; banking and payouts; risk and compliance engineering; infrastructure and reliability; data platform; security; and customer operations. Smaller companies can combine teams, but should not combine away accountability.

Each critical service has an engineering owner, on-call rotation, operational counterpart, SLO, runbook, dashboard, and escalation path. Each bank has a relationship owner and technical escalation contact. Each financial break class has a finance or operations owner, not only a generic engineering ticket queue.

Cross-functional decision rights

Engineering owns implementation and technical safety. Finance owns accounting policy and close controls. Compliance and legal determine applicable obligations and approved operating flows. Risk owns risk policy within those boundaries. Product owns the customer promise. No single function should unilaterally launch a new money flow without the others' required approval.

Use written decision records for disputes about trade-offs. For example, whether to release merchant funds before acquiring settlement is not merely a latency optimization; it is a funding and risk decision.

On-call and incident command

Separate technical incident command from financial reconciliation ownership. During an outage, one group restores safe service while another tracks potentially affected operations and customer obligations. Both share a live incident record. Recovery is not complete until the affected financial population is identified and reconciled.

Support scripts distinguish pending, failed, refunded, reversed, and settled. Agents should not promise bank-credit timing that the platform cannot control. Merchant communications include the affected product, time window, current state, next update, and any action required.

Do not copy a large-company org chart at inception. Start with a small set of tightly owned domains and experienced specialists in payments, security, finance, and bank operations. Chapter 68A staged roadmap and build-versus-buy decisions

Trying to launch every Razorpay-like product at once creates a large surface of untested money flows. Build the shared correctness foundation early, then expand product scope through explicit evidence gates. The roadmap below is a proposed sequence, not a calendar estimate.

Stage 1: narrow acceptance

Start with one approved market, a small merchant cohort, hosted checkout, orders, payment attempts, one or two partner integrations, server-side verification, refunds, a basic ledger, and daily reconciliation. Use established processors or certified components where building directly would add unnecessary regulatory and security scope.

The gate is not feature completeness. It is the ability to explain every payment and bank movement, handle duplicates and late success, support merchants, and close the books. No unresolved unowned money is accepted as normal operating practice.

Stage 2: reliable money operations

Add robust settlement scheduling, reservations, disputes, fee invoicing, support tools, approval workflows, outbox/inbox patterns, and tested backup recovery. Introduce a second partner only after routing can handle uncertainty safely. More partners without better reconciliation can increase complexity faster than resilience.

The gate includes failure injection, financial invariants, bank certification, source-feed completeness, and a measured operational envelope. Prove that an outage does not require engineers to invent ad hoc SQL repairs.

Stage 3: reusable commerce and banking workflows

Add links, pages, subscriptions, virtual collection identifiers, marketplace allocation, or payouts according to customer demand and operating authority. Reuse the money core and approval system, but give each product its own consent, eligibility, and exception workflow.

Buy commodity capabilities such as messaging delivery, some identity verification, managed cryptographic infrastructure, or certified terminal components where appropriate. Build the differentiating orchestration, accounting semantics, merchant experience, and operational integration that define the company. Vendor use still requires controls and exit plans.

Stage 4: scale and regionalization

Introduce cells, stronger isolation, automated reconciliation, advanced risk models, regional capability packages, and improved DR only when workload and customer commitments justify them. Do not introduce global distributed writes merely to look sophisticated. Complexity must earn its place through measured requirements.

Stage 5: advanced products

Instant settlements, credit-related products, agent-assisted payments, full TPAP-style experiences, and broad POS fleets require additional authority, risk, funding, certification, and operational maturity. Treat them as new businesses sharing infrastructure, not minor checkout options.

Decision framework

For every build-versus-buy choice, compare time to safe launch, control of critical invariants, regulatory scope, integration risk, unit economics, vendor concentration, data access, and exit cost. A managed queue can be a good purchase; outsourcing responsibility for unexplained customer money is not.

Stop condition. If a stage increases unreconciled balances, unknown-operation aging, or manual repair frequency beyond agreed limits, pause expansion. The platform should earn the right to process more complex money flows through evidence, not optimism.

Chapter 69Worked lifecycle: payment, split, refund, and settlement

This example connects the architecture end to end. All amounts and commercial rules are illustrative. Assume a INR 10,000 purchase, a INR 200 platform fee, a hypothetical INR 36 tax component, and marketplace allocations of INR 6,000 to Seller A and INR 3,764 to Seller B after the fee deduction.

1. Create and pay the order

The merchant server creates an order with a stable idempotency key. Checkout creates one payment attempt and the orchestrator persists the external instruction before sending it. A trusted capture observation becomes financial event capture:C1. The ledger debits acquiring receivable INR 10,000 and credits marketplace payable INR 10,000.

A fee event debits marketplace payable INR 236 and credits fee revenue INR 200 plus tax payable INR 36. The order's fulfilment allocation is committed once. Duplicate browser and webhook messages cannot produce a second shipment or journal.

2. Allocate seller obligations

The approved allocation plan debits marketplace payable INR 9,764 and credits Seller A payable INR 6,000 and Seller B payable INR 3,764. No seller bank account has yet been paid. These are internal obligations with lineage to the payment and allocation version.

The acquirer later remits the assumed gross INR 10,000 in this simplified example. Debit bank cash and credit acquiring receivable. If the actual contract nets partner fees, record those separately and adjust the receivable reconciliation; do not force a gross-cash assumption onto a net settlement file.

3. Refund part of Seller A's sale

Before seller settlement, the customer receives an approved INR 2,000 refund attributable entirely to Seller A under the example marketplace contract. The refund transaction reserves the eligible amount, debits Seller A payable INR 2,000, and credits refund payable INR 2,000.

When the original-route refund is confirmed, debit refund payable and credit bank cash INR 2,000. The example assumes the platform fee and illustrative tax are not adjusted; a different contract would require explicit fee and tax adjustment journals.

4. Settle the remaining obligations

Seller A is now owed INR 4,000 and Seller B INR 3,764. The settlement scheduler freezes destination snapshots and batch membership, obtains approval, and submits stable bank references. Confirmed execution debits the seller payables and credits bank cash for INR 7,764 in total, possibly through settlement-in-transit accounts.

After the refund and seller settlements, bank cash remaining from this simplified flow is INR 236. That equals INR 200 fee revenue and INR 36 tax payable, before any other expenses or tax remittance. The customer principal has not been mislabeled as platform revenue.

5. Reconcile four views

The payment record shows INR 10,000 captured and INR 2,000 refunded. The seller allocation view shows INR 4,000 and INR 3,764 settled. The bank view shows INR 10,000 received and INR 9,764 paid out across refund and seller settlements. The ledger balances and preserves every event.

Now introduce a duplicate refund webhook. It adds delivery evidence but no new journal. Introduce a missing Seller B bank acknowledgment. Seller B remains pending or unknown until bank evidence resolves it; the scheduler must not pay again with a fresh reference.

Learning outcome. Payment status, customer refund, marketplace allocation, merchant settlement, platform revenue, and tax obligation are six related but distinct facts. The platform is correct only when all six can be explained together.

Chapter 70Worked failure: payout timeout during regional recovery

Consider a INR 50,000 vendor payout. The merchant approved the exact beneficiary and amount. The local system reserved funds and persisted operation P1 with bank reference B1. The bank executes the transfer, but the response is lost just as the primary region fails.

The unsafe recovery

A replacement worker reads an old snapshot where the payout looks ready. It creates a new bank reference B2 and sends again. The bank sees two distinct instructions and may pay twice. Local idempotency at the API layer does not prevent this if dispatch recovery discarded the original external identity.

Another unsafe path releases the reservation because the worker lease expired. A second payout spends those funds while the first bank debit is real but not yet reflected locally. Both failures come from confusing local process state with external financial state.

The proposed safe sequence

First fence the failed region's writers and dispatchers through the approved authority mechanism. Promote a new writer only with a newer epoch and verified durable state. Recover operation, reservation, idempotency record, approval manifest, and original bank reference together.

Mark P1 as requiring resolution because dispatch may have occurred. Query the bank using B1, ingest authenticated notifications, and retrieve the relevant bank statement or execution report. Do not send B2 merely to meet a customer-interface timeout.

Evidence arrives

The bank confirms that B1 completed. The platform records observation O1, accepts completion event E1, consumes the existing reservation, and posts the completion journal once. The payout API now shows completed with its original operation ID and bank reference.

If the completion webhook later arrives twice, inbox deduplication and the economic event key prevent a second posting. If a stale primary worker wakes up, its writer epoch and dispatch authority are invalid. If the bank later reverses the transfer, that is a separate linked event with its own accounting effect.

What if the local operation is missing?

An asynchronous regional copy may lack an acknowledged operation. That is why the architecture cannot claim zero regional data loss without the necessary durability mechanism. Recovery uses the acknowledged-request evidence, external references, bank records, and controlled reconstruction process. The affected scope stays restricted until the financial history is sufficiently established.

Do not infer non-execution from a missing local row. The bank's independent evidence can prove money moved even when the restored database does not remember the instruction. Reconstruction must preserve original identity and approval evidence or escalate the gap explicitly.

Customer and operational communication

During uncertainty, the merchant sees processing or pending investigation, not a false failure inviting a new payout. Support receives the original reference, affected account, amount, timeline, and next evidence source. Incident command tracks the entire affected operation population, not only the first reported case.

Recovery sign-off. Reopening requires proof that all acknowledged and potentially dispatched operations in the incident window are completed, definitively unexecuted, or owned as controlled exceptions. Service health alone is not sufficient.

Chapter 71Runbooks for the incidents that matter

A runbook should tell an on-call team what to preserve, what to stop, what evidence to collect, and what proves recovery. It should not reduce every incident to restarting a service. The following are proposed operational patterns to adapt and rehearse.

Bank connector degradation

Stop or limit new instructions on the affected route using a scoped control. Keep status queries, callbacks, refunds where safe, and reconciliation running. Identify already-sent operations and preserve their references. Reroute only new eligible attempts or definitively safe retries. Communicate the affected methods and merchant population.

Recovery requires stable partner behavior, backlog reduction, and evidence that uncertainty is not growing. Gradually restore traffic. Do not send the entire queued backlog at once and trigger another bank throttle.

Duplicate-payment suspicion

Freeze automated retry policies for the affected flow. Compare order, attempt, operation, partner reference, and bank evidence to distinguish duplicate delivery from duplicate economic execution. Record every real receipt or debit. Prevent duplicate fulfilment and prepare approved refunds or recovery actions only after identifying the actual financial population.

Do not delete duplicate-looking rows before investigation. They may be the only evidence of a second real bank movement.

Ledger invariant failure

Stop affected posting and money execution immediately. Preserve logs, transaction identifiers, deployed versions, and database evidence. Determine whether the defect is an unbalanced journal, projection mismatch, wrong ownership, duplicate economic key, or incorrect posting template.

Repair through reviewed financial commands and independently verify the affected population. A successful database query is not sufficient sign-off. Finance and engineering jointly approve reopening according to the incident severity.

Settlement file or bank acknowledgment missing

Do not regenerate a new batch identity automatically. Query delivery and acceptance status using the original manifest and bank reference. Compare source obligations, file checksum, bank acknowledgment, and account movements. Handle partial execution per row. Escalate before the contractual deadline and provide merchants with a truthful update.

Credential compromise

Contain access, rotate or revoke the affected credential, and identify the time and resource scope. Preserve evidence and coordinate with the bank or partner. Separate unauthorized instructions from legitimate in-flight work. A credential reset must not trigger fresh retries of uncertain operations.

Reconciliation backlog

Determine whether source feeds are missing, parsers are failing, matching rules changed, or transaction volume exceeded capacity. Prioritize material and aged breaks. Restrict products whose safety depends on unavailable evidence, such as certain expedited settlements. Do not bulk-close exceptions to make the dashboard green.

Common closure checklist

For every incident, establish customer impact, financial exposure, affected operation set, final or controlled-exception state, corrective actions, and verification evidence. Record what changed in code, configuration, partner behavior, or operating process. Follow-up work has owners and deadlines.

Exercise. Rehearse each runbook with a simulator and a cross-functional team. The best time to discover that nobody can retrieve a bank statement during an outage is before a real outage.

A Razorpay-like platform is ready when its customer promises, legal authority, technical behavior, financial records, and operating organization agree. No single load test, audit badge, or architecture diagram proves this on its own.

Product and authority proof

Every enabled product has a capability record, approved merchant population, legal entity, contract, partner, money-flow diagram, limits, consent requirements, and support owner. The platform has measured normal and degraded capacity, bounded retries, tested partner isolation, error-budget alerts, and sustainable on-call coverage. Recovery tests include writer fencing, idempotency history, cryptographic access, queue state, and independent bank evidence.

Sensitive data is minimized, classified, regionally controlled, encrypted, and access-limited. Applicable audits and certifications cover the actual scope. Administrative changes and financial repairs are approved and auditable. Regulatory controls have owners, effective dates, and evidence.

Required uniqueness and locking

The idempotency uniqueness scope includes tenant, environment, action, and key. Refund commands lock or serialize the payment's refundable-amount authority. Reservation commands lock the spendable-balance authority. Settlement selection prevents an obligation from entering competing active batches. Posting uses a unique event key plus payload hash so that a conflicting replay is rejected rather than silently accepted.

Acquire multiple account locks in a deterministic order to reduce deadlocks. Retry transaction aborts with the same economic identity. Do not hold database locks while waiting for a bank response; commit the durable intent and resolve the external step asynchronously.

Archive old operational payloads while preserving required financial references and replay protection. A deleted response cache must not make an old payout key reusable as a new economic instruction. Keep personal data separate from immutable accounting facts where possible. Retention periods and legal holds come from the approved control registry, not this example schema.

APPENDIX B / TECHNICAL REFERENCE

Appendix BCritical-operation pseudocode and proof obligations

The algorithms below describe proposed transaction boundaries. They intentionally separate local atomicity from external execution. Production code needs real database constraints, authorization, key management, partner certification, observability, and recovery testing.

1. Accept an idempotent command

accept_command(scope, key, request):
  canonical = canonicalize_economic_fields(request)
  digest = SHA256(canonical)
  begin transaction
    claim unique (scope, key), or lock the existing claim
    if existing:
      if digest differs: reject CONFLICT
      return existing durable operation reference
    validate tenant, product, amount, currency, authority
    create operation with immutable request and digest
    insert outbox event referring to operation
    bind idempotency claim to operation
  commit
  return durable operation reference

The initial insert and competing-transaction behavior must be implemented correctly for the chosen database. A uniqueness conflict may require a fresh transaction before reading the winning row. Returning an operation reference avoids pretending that asynchronous execution is already complete. A cached HTTP response is optional; the economic operation is not.

2. Reserve funds for a payout

reserve_payout(operation_id, approved_manifest):
  begin transaction
    lock operation and relevant balance authority
    verify manifest hash, approval, destination, entity, currency
    if reservation already exists: return its current state
    assert eligible_available >= amount + applicable charges
    create reservation uniquely bound to operation
    reclassify or encumber the approved available amount
    transition operation to READY
    insert dispatch outbox event
  commit

Do not count unsettled receivables as usable cash without an approved funding arrangement. If several accounts are involved, lock in a deterministic order. A reservation is released only by a valid terminal transition, not by a worker timeout.

3. Dispatch an external instruction

dispatch(operation_id):
  load durable operation and original partner reference
  obtain valid writer epoch and bounded execution lease
  if state is ambiguous or possibly sent:
    query/reconcile original reference; do not create a new one
    return
  durably record SEND_INTENT with request fingerprint
  send through connector using original reference
  persist response as an observation
  reduce observations through rail-specific rules

There is still a crash window between recording send intent and actually sending. Recovery treats this conservatively and uses partner duplicate-safe replay or status inquiry. Without a reliable partner contract, the system cannot infer exactly-once external execution from local code alone.

4. Post an accepted financial event

post(event_key, event_digest, lines, evidence):
  validate event-specific account roles and evidence authority
  assert all lines share permitted entity and currency
  assert positive integer amounts
  assert sum(debits) == sum(credits)
  begin transaction
    claim unique event_key
    if already posted:
      require stored digest == event_digest
      return original journal
    lock affected balance rows in deterministic order
    insert immutable journal and lines
    update balance projections and sequences
    insert journal-posted outbox event
  commit

The digest covers the economic content, not incidental transport timestamps. Restrict direct table mutation so that callers cannot bypass the posting path. A database-level balance enforcement mechanism is preferable where practical, but it still needs semantic account validation.

5. Request a partial refund

request_refund(payment_id, amount, key):
  begin transaction
    claim scoped key and validate identical replay
    lock payment refund authority
    remaining = eligible_capture - completed_refunds
                - active_refund_reservations
    require 0 < amount <= remaining
    check dispute overlap and permitted original return path
    create refund operation and amount reservation
    create funding obligation under approved accounting policy
    append outbox event
  commit

A refund can be financially funded before or after merchant settlement through different accounts. Those posting templates are chosen explicitly. The external result later consumes or releases the reservation through an idempotent transition.

6. Receive a verified webhook

receive(raw_body, headers):
  enforce size and content-type limits
  verify HMAC using the appropriate allowed secret version
  parse and validate schema and endpoint binding
  begin transaction
    insert inbox(source, event_id) if absent
    preserve verified payload reference and metadata
  commit
  acknowledge promptly

worker(event_id):
  begin transaction
    lock inbox item
    if processed: return
    apply allowed business transition idempotently
    mark processed; append any local outbox events
  commit

Provider event identity prevents duplicate delivery effects. Economic keys prevent duplicate effects when the same fact also arrives through an API query or bank file. Signature verification alone does not establish that the event belongs to the correct merchant or expected operation.

7. Approve and execute a settlement batch

build_batch(domain, cutoff):
  atomically claim eligible unsettled obligations
  freeze destinations, membership, total, currency, and entity
  calculate immutable manifest hash
  obtain required approvals over that hash
  create stable bank batch and item references
  dispatch without holding database locks
  resolve each item independently from bank evidence
  settle only confirmed items; preserve uncertain commitments

Do not retry a partially executed batch as a new whole batch. The unit of recovery is the original item operation. Batch totals are completeness checks, not a replacement for per-beneficiary evidence.

8. Promote a recovery writer

promote(domain):
  establish old writer cannot retain valid authority
  obtain strictly newer epoch from the approved authority system
  verify durable log/checkpoint and key access
  restore operations, reservations, idempotency, and outboxes
  inventory potentially sent external instructions
  reconcile gaps before enabling affected money movement
  enable bounded traffic and monitor financial invariants

The exact authority mechanism is infrastructure-specific. A manual checkbox or DNS update is not a fencing proof. The supplied executable model tests selected local invariants only; it does not implement this distributed recovery protocol.

Razorpay Checkout signature verification example

The server-known order ID is used in the signed message, not an untrusted replacement supplied by the browser. Keep the secret on the server. [S12]

import hashlib
import hmac

def verify_checkout_signature(
    server_order_id: str,
    payment_id: str,
    supplied_signature: str,
    api_secret: bytes,
) -> bool:
    message = f"{server_order_id}|{payment_id}".encode("utf-8")
    expected = hmac.new(api_secret, message, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    return hmac.compare_digest(expected, supplied_signature)

After signature verification, validate the payment's merchant, order, amount, currency, and appropriate status through the server-side integration contract. A correct signature does not authorize fulfilment of an unrelated or changed cart.

Razorpay webhook verification example

Verify the exact raw bytes before JSON parsing or reserialization. Secret rotation must account for the documented retry behavior. [S18]

import hashlib
import hmac

def verify_webhook(
    raw_body: bytes,
    supplied_signature: str,
    allowed_secret_versions: tuple[bytes, ...],
) -> bool:
    if not allowed_secret_versions or len(raw_body) > 1_000_000:
        return False
    return any(
        hmac.compare_digest(
            hmac.new(secret, raw_body, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest(),
            supplied_signature,
        )
        for secret in allowed_secret_versions
    )

The body-size limit is an illustrative local policy, not a Razorpay limit. Production code also validates header format, endpoint binding, supported event schema, durable deduplication, and secret-retirement policy. The example performs verification only and does not itself acknowledge or process an event.

An authority checklist for every new side effect

Before implementing an action, answer: Who may request it? What exact parameters did they approve? Which local transaction records it? Which external system may commit it? Who resolves the remaining exception?

The checklist applies to payments, refunds, payouts, FX conversions, mandate changes, beneficiary changes, and treasury transfers. Consistency comes from applying the same discipline to every consequential action, not from forcing every action into the same state machine.

Freshness is a contract

A cached value should carry source time, observed time, and permitted use. A bank balance used for display can tolerate a different age than one used to authorize a payment. A merchant risk restriction can require faster propagation than a cosmetic dashboard setting. Define these bounds per action and test stale-data rejection.

Severity and incident ownership

A suspected unauthorized debit, ledger corruption, or cross-tenant exposure is a safety/security incident even if only one request is affected. A broad checkout outage is an availability incident. A missed settlement deadline is a contractual and operational incident. One event can belong to several categories; use coordinated ownership rather than forcing it into a single team's metric.

Review cadence

Review partner capabilities when contracts, APIs, certificates, or operating rules change. Review product eligibility at launch and after material expansion. Review financial invariants continuously and through close. Review recovery with real restores, not only document updates. Review legal applicability against effective dates, especially for phased rules.

Management rule. Every important control has a named owner, an implementation, and evidence that it worked. A policy document without an executable or observable control is only an intention.

Update protocol

Before using the book as an implementation baseline, recheck product eligibility, bank capability contracts, applicable regulatory effective dates, security standards, partner APIs, and executed SLAs. Record the changed source, affected capability, migration plan, test evidence, and customer communication. Preserve historical versions so that past financial decisions remain explainable.

Limits of public evidence

The reviewed public material does not establish Razorpay's complete private ledger implementation, all current runtime services, every negotiated customer SLA, or a universal per-product recovery guarantee. The architecture in this book fills those design spaces with explicitly proposed mechanisms. It must be validated in the actual operating environment.
