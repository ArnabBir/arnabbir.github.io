Foundations and architecture / Chapter 01

The product is a family of trust models

WhatsApp looks like one application. Engineering it as one undifferentiated service would be a mistake. A private family message, a public Channel update, an automated business reply, and an optional AI request do not have the same recipients, retention rules, or operators.

Public evidence

The multi-device publication describes independent device identities and client-side message fanout. Cloud API documentation separately identifies a business-selected service that decrypts messages on the business's behalf. These are different endpoint arrangements, not contradictory descriptions of one path. [S01] [S51]

Design the product map first

Divide the platform into five product areas. Personal communication contains direct chats, groups, voice notes, media, calls, and Status. Organization contains Communities, announcements, events, polls, and personal chat organization. Public discovery contains Channels and their associated discovery and monetization surfaces. Business communication contains the Business app, Cloud API, templates, Flows, calling, and integrations. Optional intelligence contains on-device features, confidential processing, and explicitly selected AI interactions.

Give every area a written answer to four questions: Who may decrypt the content? Who controls the receiving endpoint? What can the service retain? What does the user authorize when they tap the button? The answers belong in API schemas, UI descriptions, threat models, and tests, not only in marketing material.

A common infrastructure layer can still provide network ingress, account routing, encrypted object storage, abuse controls, billing identities, and operational tooling. Sharing infrastructure must not quietly share plaintext or purposes. In particular, an analytics job authorized to count failed deliveries is not thereby authorized to inspect personal messages.

The running example

Maya uses a phone and laptop. Noah uses a phone. They share a family group with Isha. Maya also contacts a bicycle shop that operates a business integration. In later chapters, Maya removes her laptop, Isha leaves the group, a delivery acknowledgment disappears, and the shop changes its integration partner.

Use these people to ask concrete questions. Should Maya's removed laptop receive the next message? Can Isha read a new group message using a previously obtained group key? Is the shop's customer-service agent an intended recipient? Can an AI summary be generated after the group has disabled the relevant feature? A good architecture makes the answers explicit rather than hiding them behind a generic encrypted flag.

What we will build

The reference design is an original secure-messaging company blueprint, not a reverse-engineered copy of private WhatsApp code. It starts with personal messaging, establishes a defensible cryptographic boundary, then adds media, calls, business workflows, and operations. Optional products must not be dependencies of the basic send-and-receive path.

A regional outage should not require turning off encryption. An AI outage should not stop chatting. A billing deployment should not restart established calls. Those separation goals strongly influence process placement, data ownership, and deployment boundaries throughout this book.

Reading check: A business reply appears in the same conversation UI as a friend's reply. Does that imply the same organization controls both decryption endpoints? No. Identify the recipient and its designated processors, not merely the interface.

Public internals, historical evidence, and unknowns

A credible engineering study distinguishes what a company published from what a designer can reasonably propose. Technology names alone do not establish a current service topology.

A dated evidence timeline

Rick Reed's 2012 talk describes connection scaling using Erlang/BEAM and FreeBSD, including a peak of approximately 2.8 million connections on a particular server configuration. The 2013 talk describes a multimedia architecture from that period. Neither is a benchmark for modern encrypted messaging, current media processing, or today's deployment fleet. [S28] [S29]

The 2021 backup article names ChatD as a connection and authentication front end. Later publications describe key transparency, contact storage, confidential AI processing, and a Rust media-consistency library. These establish particular components and techniques, not a complete map of every backend. [S05] [S02] [S04] [S07]

Five evidence labels

This book uses documented for an explicit statement in a primary source; historical for a dated architecture or measurement; reference design for our own proposed implementation; standard for an interoperable protocol or published algorithm; and experiment for measurements from the included local teaching exercises.

A statement such as "use a consensus-replicated mailbox owner" is a reference design. It is not transformed into a WhatsApp fact by placing a Meta infrastructure citation beside it. Similarly, the existence of a public Signal specification does not establish that WhatsApp deploys every extension currently described there.

Important corrections to common explanations

"WhatsApp uses WebSockets" is insufficiently specific. Browser, native, business HTTP, call signaling, and media transports may differ. This playbook proposes transport adapters around a shared envelope contract and explains the tradeoffs without inventing a universal private wire protocol.

"Messages are never on servers" confuses plaintext with encrypted delivery storage. An offline destination requires some combination of sender retention and server-held ciphertext. "End-to-end encryption means no metadata" is also wrong: a delivery service still needs enough routing and operational information to function. Privacy requires minimizing and separating that information, not pretending it does not exist. [S30]

"A group has one permanent encryption key" misses membership changes, sender state, device changes, and key distribution. "A cloud backup is automatically protected exactly like a chat" misses separate backup settings and recovery paths. The dedicated chapters make these distinctions precise.

How to read changing features

The research cutoff is 28 September 2026. Feature announcements are not evidence of availability for every country, account, operating system, or business integration. Usernames are described using the published reservation and phased-rollout language. Scam Alert is described as a limited Beta. Business documentation has effective-date changes, including changes that are still in the future at this cutoff. [S32] [S10] [S63]

Reconstructing architecture responsibly

Start from a public behavior, derive the constraints it creates, then present several implementation options. For independent devices, for example, every authorized device needs usable key material and a synchronization path. That does not uniquely imply a particular database, queue, or RPC framework.

The practical lesson is to adopt invariants, not brand-name stacks. A well-tested smaller implementation is more valuable than a diagram containing every technology ever mentioned by Meta. Public evidence anchors the study; original reasoning fills design space without pretending to fill confidential knowledge gaps.

The six facts behind a single message

A chat bubble compresses several independent events into a tiny icon. The backend and client must keep the underlying events separate or retries, offline devices, and reports become impossible to reason about.

Start with a fact ledger

The reference design distinguishes intent, prepared ciphertext, durable admission, device delivery, local presentation, and read acknowledgment. Intent says the sender chose a payload and audience. Preparation says an exact encrypted envelope was generated. Admission says the service accepted responsibility under a defined retention policy. Delivery says a particular recipient device acknowledged durable receipt. Presentation is a local UI event. Reading is an optional recipient-generated signal.

None of these facts automatically establishes the next one. An HTTP or transport acknowledgment does not prove that a recipient has opened the app. A notification-provider acknowledgment does not prove the notification appeared. A read marker is an application signal, not evidence of human comprehension.

A compact proposed record

Use an immutable logical message identifier, sender account, sender device generation, conversation identifier, client operation identifier, and creation policy. Store encrypted per-device envelopes separately from the logical message. An envelope identifier includes its intended device incarnation; a replacement phone must not accidentally inherit an old phone's mailbox.

On the client, keep the user-visible message, encryption preparation state, and pending transmission in one transactional local store. If the process dies after encryption but before transmission, restart by resending the stored envelope. Do not generate unrelated ciphertext while pretending it is the same transport attempt.

Two different kinds of duplicates

A repeated envelope is a transport duplicate. A sender intentionally sending "Hello" twice is not. Deduplicate by authenticated operation identity, not message text, timestamp alone, or ciphertext similarity.

The server can deduplicate an opaque request using an operation key and request digest. The receiving client deduplicates logical messages after authenticating the envelope. Both layers matter: server retries can happen even when a sender never retried, and multiple devices can legitimately contribute synchronization copies.

Acknowledgment loss

Maya sends operation M7. The service commits its mailbox entries, but the response disappears. The safe retry returns the original admission result. It does not allocate a new logical message. Noah may receive the envelope twice after a worker crash; his client records one message and can acknowledge the duplicate again.

If an admission response is irrecoverable, the sender shows a pending or uncertain state rather than declaring a failure that encourages manual duplication. User-interface wording is therefore part of correctness, not decoration.

Ordering without a fictional global clock

Preserve order within a sender-device stream using a monotonic sequence, and carry causal references for replies and edits. Different devices and concurrent participants can produce incomparable events. A stable presentation order can combine a server-assigned conversation order, local provisional order, and deterministic tie-breaking, but the choice must be documented.

A global timestamp sort is not a proof of causality. A phone with an incorrect clock must not place a new message permanently before years of history or extend an expiry indefinitely.

Delivery versus business callbacks

The public business platform explicitly provides asynchronous incoming-message and status webhooks. Our proposed personal-delivery state machine is a separate design abstraction, not a claim that consumer clients use that same API. [S52]

Invariant: Every retry must name the fact it is trying to recover. Retrying a network send, a recipient delivery, and a business charge are not interchangeable operations.

Foundations and architecture / Chapter 04

The complete reference architecture

A secure messenger is best understood as several cooperating planes, each with a different latency budget and access boundary.

The client plane

The client owns plaintext composition, message encryption and decryption, local history, search, media preparation, and user-facing privacy decisions. Platform adapters integrate secure key storage, network reachability, background execution, cameras, microphones, notifications, and accessibility. A shared protocol core can reduce cross-platform behavioral differences without forcing every UI to share one rendering framework.

The connection and delivery plane

An edge terminates an authenticated transport and forwards commands to the appropriate account or mailbox owner. It is optimized for many mostly idle connections, bounded buffers, and cheap reconnects. The mailbox service persists opaque delivery envelopes, schedules attempts, handles acknowledgment, and enforces expiry. A presence service supplies short-lived reachability hints, never the sole source of delivery truth.

Separate socket ownership from durable mailbox ownership. Losing a gateway should invalidate connections, not delete accepted messages. Conversely, a durable mailbox must not keep an unlimited in-memory queue because a recipient's TCP connection is slow.

The identity and authority plane

Account registration, device directories, public key material, link authorization, removal, privacy rules, and group membership live here. Use narrow mutation APIs and versioned responses. The delivery path consumes these decisions but does not own them. Critical revocations require a freshness contract: either a current authoritative check or a bounded credential whose residual exposure is understood.

The media and real-time planes

An object service stores encrypted media and transfer artifacts. It handles integrity, quotas, replication, and garbage collection without needing plaintext. Calls have signaling ownership, membership, connectivity negotiation, relay allocation, and packet-forwarding services. Packet forwarding is a distinct fast path; ordinary database request latency must not be paid for every audio packet.

Separate public and business planes

Channels need discovery, content distribution, moderation, subscription entitlements, and public-content storage rules. Business services need tenant access control, API versioning, webhooks, template lifecycle, customer-contact rules, and financial records. Confidential AI processing belongs in an explicitly attested execution environment, not inside ordinary chat workers. Published descriptions of these separate product boundaries motivate this separation. [S41] [S50] [S08]

A send path, end to end

Maya composes a message, resolves a current device list, prepares recipient-specific ciphertext, and commits a local outbox record. The edge authenticates her current device. The mailbox authority validates the routing envelope and applicable account policy, then commits delivery records and an outbox event. Dispatchers use online routes when available, otherwise schedule wakeups and retries. Noah's device authenticates, decrypts, persists, and acknowledges. Maya's device receives a receipt update.

No ordinary server-side component in this personal-message path needs a plaintext search index or a message decryption key. Diagnostic tooling must respect that architecture rather than create a secret alternate path for convenience.

Choose physical boundaries gradually

A startup can implement several logical services in one deployable application with separate modules and tables. Split processes when security isolation, scaling shape, failure isolation, or independent release needs justify it. Hundreds of services are not a prerequisite for correctness. Explicit owners and contracts are.

Data model and invariants before databases

The database choice comes after the meaning of the records. A record that conflates a person, a phone number, and a physical device becomes a security problem when any one of those changes.

Identity with generations

Use a stable internal account identifier. Attach phone-number assignments, optional usernames, and business-scoped identifiers as separate aliases with lifetimes. A device has an identifier plus an incarnation or generation. Reinstalling or re-registering creates a new cryptographic identity even when the human-readable device name is unchanged.

Usernames and business-scoped IDs make this separation especially important: current documentation describes an identifier scoped to the business rather than a universal phone-number join key. [S53]

Core entities

The essential families are Account, AliasAssignment, Device, KeyBundle, Conversation, MembershipRevision, LogicalMessage, DeviceEnvelope, DeliveryReceipt, MediaObject, SyncCheckpoint, and PrivacyPolicyRevision. Add CallSession and CallParticipant for real-time communication; BackupManifest and RecoveryEnrollment for history protection; BusinessTenant, TemplateRevision, ContactGrant, UsageEvent, and BillingAdjustment for enterprise products.

Do not put all of them into one globally serialized transaction. Choose narrow owners. The account owner serializes device changes. The group owner serializes membership revisions. A mailbox partition serializes admission and delivery progress for its assigned recipients. A financial owner serializes usage charges for a tenant and billing period.

Six non-negotiable invariants

A revoked device must not receive newly authorized delivery under an obsolete device list. A repeated operation key with a different request digest must be rejected. An acknowledged recipient envelope must not be resurrected as new content by a replay. A group-key update must exclude removed recipients for the new key epoch. A restored backup must not roll back current account authority. A financial adjustment must reference the original usage fact instead of mutating its historical meaning.

The first invariant needs a precise boundary. It is realistic to prevent new server handoffs after a revocation commit and to stop new client encryption after fresh directory lookup. It is not realistic to erase bytes already delivered or keys already copied to a compromised endpoint.

What may be eventually consistent

A last-seen hint, a noncritical counter, and a search index can lag within their product contracts. Device revocation, one-time challenge consumption, and exclusive call-transfer ownership require stronger coordination at the point of authority. Not every read needs consensus, but every privilege-changing write needs an unambiguous order.

Transaction plus outbox

When accepting a message, write the admission record and the event that schedules its delivery in the same local transaction. Publish that event asynchronously. Consumers use their own inbox table so replay does not repeat side effects. This is an original reference implementation pattern; it is not a claim about a private WhatsApp queue.

Review the impossible cases

If two isolated regions can both authorize the same scarce or exclusive resource, decide how conflicting results are handled before shipping. For account authority, the proposed system chooses safety over accepting writes in both partitions. For temporary presence, it tolerates conflicting hints. This difference is intentional and user-visible.

Reading check: Should a phone number be the primary key of every business customer table? No. Numbers can change or be hidden, while business-scoped identities and account lifetimes have distinct meanings.

Service levels that describe the real experience

A single uptime percentage cannot describe encrypted delivery, offline history, call continuity, and recovery. The service must measure what it controls and expose what it does not.

Contractual versus proposed targets

No universal consumer WhatsApp contractual availability commitment was established in the reviewed sources. The following are illustrative internal targets for the proposed company, not WhatsApp promises: 99.99% eligible message-admission availability; 99.9% online-device delivery within two seconds under a defined network test; 99.95% call setup success after excluding explicit user rejection; and a separate zero-tolerance invariant for acknowledged-message loss within the replicated durability contract.

A promise of zero loss still needs a failure model. Three replicas in one building do not protect against regional destruction. An asynchronous remote copy does not create zero recovery-point loss. Write the durability boundary in plain language and test it.

Four clocks

Measure compose-to-local-save, local-save-to-service-admission, admission-to-device-receipt, and call-request-to-first-audio separately. The first depends on device performance. The second depends on connectivity and service health. The third includes recipient availability. The fourth spans signaling, NAT traversal, media readiness, and user behavior.

An offline recipient is not an admission outage. It is still a pending user outcome. Present it honestly, retain the envelope within policy, and prevent a dashboard from erasing that experience through overly convenient exclusions.

Error-budget arithmetic

For a 30-day month, 99.9% time availability permits 43.2 minutes of unavailability; 99.99% permits 4.32 minutes; 99.999% permits about 25.9 seconds. Request-based budgets differ when traffic is uneven. Calculate both, because a one-minute outage during a major event can affect far more people than one minute overnight.

Recovery contracts by state

For accepted personal-message envelopes, target zero loss after the documented quorum commit within the protected region. For a destroyed region without synchronous remote durability, expose the configured recovery-point bound instead. For in-progress calls, recovering a database record does not recover packet sequence, jitter buffers, or media keys. The product may need a brief rejoin rather than claiming seamless continuity.

For device revocations and backup recovery attempt counters, failover must preserve the most recent authoritative security state. A cache warmed from an old snapshot is not enough. For Channels, serving a slightly older public update may be acceptable; for a removed private recipient, it is not.

Burn-rate operations

Alert on fast and slow budget burn together. A sharp rise in decryption failures can be more urgent than a similar rise in HTTP errors. Track by client version, region, network class, and feature capability without logging message contents. Keep high-risk security alerts outside the normal availability budget: a confidentiality regression is not something to spend the error budget on.

The product decision

Under extreme load, shed typing notifications, redundant read-receipt traffic, and nonessential discovery before durable send admission. Preserve emergency controls such as device removal. A service-level policy becomes useful only when it tells engineers what to protect first.

Accounts, clients, and delivery / Chapter 07

Registration, passkeys, and account recovery

Registration establishes control of an account credential. It does not prove a person's legal identity, guarantee possession forever, or unlock all previous encrypted history.

Current product boundary

An August 2026 announcement describes stronger alphanumeric two-step-verification passwords and support for multiple passkeys. It supersedes explanations that treat the additional account secret as permanently limited to a six-digit PIN. WhatsApp account passkeys remain separately managed even when someone opts into broader Meta Account integration. [S31] [S46]

Proposed registration state machine

Create a short-lived RegistrationAttempt bound to the claimed alias, device-generated public key, risk context, and challenge identifier. Issue an out-of-band challenge only after abuse checks. Store a verifier, not the plaintext code, and impose limits per destination, source network, device, and account-risk cluster. A correct response atomically consumes the challenge and moves the attempt to verified.

A verification success is not a reusable bearer token for unrelated operations. Bind the resulting credential to the new device and explicit next step. Prevent an attacker from replaying a successful response against another alias or changing the device public key after verification.

Why SMS alone is insufficient

A number can be reassigned, a SIM can be replaced, and a user can be persuaded to disclose a code. The reference design layers existing-device approval, passkeys, an additional account secret, risk-based delays, and recovery notifications. Recovery must remain usable when the old device is genuinely lost, so each additional safeguard needs a documented fallback rather than an endless loop.

Do not silently transfer old encryption sessions when a new person acquires a recycled number. Increment the number-assignment generation, establish fresh device identity, and make directory changes visible through the appropriate verification mechanism.

Passkeys are not message decryption keys

A passkey proves authorization through a supported authenticator flow. Its private material and synchronization behavior depend on the platform. The account login credential, device identity key, transport authentication key, and backup recovery material have separate lifecycles. Treating them as one key creates dangerous recovery coupling.

A user who can log in may still lack an old backup key. Conversely, possession of an old backup should not permit reactivating a revoked device. The UI must explain those outcomes without suggesting that encryption has failed.

Recovery as an adversarial workflow

Record which factors were satisfied, which authority approved the reset, and which device generation is being replaced. Send notifications to established channels without exposing sensitive chat data. Delay high-risk changes when the account has recently changed number, recovery information, and device in quick succession.

At commit, revoke old sessions according to policy and publish the new directory version. Long-running workers must not continue using stale registration state. Repeated recovery attempts require a single authoritative attempt budget; independent per-region counters invite brute-force multiplication.

Tests that matter

Race two correct challenge submissions and require one consumption. Retry the winner and return its original result only to the same bound operation. Try a different device key with the same code. Restore an old database snapshot and verify it cannot reactivate a consumed challenge. These tests are more valuable than merely confirming that a happy-path SMS arrives.

Accounts, clients, and delivery / Chapter 08

Linking and removing devices safely

A linked device becomes another endpoint that may receive private content. Adding one is therefore an authority-changing operation, not a convenient QR-code login alone.

Public evidence

The multi-device design assigns an identity to each device and exposes linked-device management. Device Verification separately addresses stolen authentication material. A March 2026 announcement adds warnings for suspicious linking requests, reflecting that cryptographically valid linking can still be socially engineered. [S01] [S03] [S49]

Proposed linking protocol

The companion generates its keys locally and displays a short-lived pairing challenge. The established device scans or otherwise verifies the challenge, shows the companion identity and requested capabilities, and obtains user approval. A pairing transcript binds account, both device identities, random nonces, expiry, and protocol version.

The account authority accepts the new device only with a valid authorization from a currently permitted linking device. Commit the directory revision and link receipt atomically. A screenshot of an expired QR code must not remain an evergreen enrollment credential.

Use a distinct pairing transport protected by an audited protocol. Do not invent a short-code cryptosystem by hashing the visible digits and calling the result a key. Human-entered secrets have limited entropy and require a protocol designed for that constraint.

History is a separate grant

Linking authorizes future participation. Copying past history is another action with its own encrypted transfer, retention, and user expectations. A new device should receive a bounded history package from an authorized source, not raw access to every server-held object associated with the account.

The package includes a checkpoint and identities of its source and destination devices. After it installs the snapshot, the companion catches up from a defined synchronization boundary. Replays from an older package must not undo newer privacy settings or reintroduce expired content.

Removal has two boundaries

At the service boundary, removal increments the directory revision, invalidates the device's authentication, and prevents subsequent fetch or newly authorized handoff. At peer devices, future encryption must use the updated recipient list. Senders need a stale-directory response or another authenticated update path so cached lists cannot remain valid indefinitely.

Already delivered plaintext and copied keys cannot be remotely unlearned. State that limitation clearly. Removing a laptop should stop future access; it cannot guarantee that every prior screenshot disappears from it.

The stale-worker race

A worker reads directory version 12, then Maya removes her laptop and commits version 13. The worker attempts delivery using version 12. The final authority check rejects the old generation before releasing new ciphertext to that device. This check must be tied to the same authority ordering as removal; a read from an arbitrarily stale replica does not satisfy it.

Operational safeguards

Show last activity as a hint, not proof that a device is safe. Alert on unexpected device additions and unusual linking geography without making location a sole authentication factor. Limit device churn, test capability downgrade, and provide a fast account-wide emergency removal path. Usability is part of security: users must be able to recognize and remove a device they no longer control.

Contacts, discovery, and privacy-preserving identity

A phone book is a collection of somebody else's personal information. Uploading its contents merely to discover friends creates a different privacy problem from encrypting messages. Separate discovery from message delivery and separate a human-readable alias from the stable account that receives a message.

What is publicly described

Meta's IPLS publication describes an encrypted storage system for WhatsApp contacts, including hardware-backed protection, key transparency, and restoration. Username reservations announced in June 2026 introduce another discovery path; reservation and phased availability must not be confused with universal enablement. [S04] [S32]

Reference design: three independent records

Maintain an internal account identifier, a versioned alias assignment, and an encrypted personal contact record. The assignment says which account currently controls an alias. The personal record says what Maya calls that account. Maya can label Noah "Plumber" without changing Noah's public identity or revealing that label to other people.

Store an assignment generation alongside cached discovery results. Suppose a telephone number belonged to account A and is later assigned to B. A stale address-book entry must not silently inherit A's cryptographic trust. Show identity changes, invalidate old lookup bindings, and establish a new authenticated session. A username rename is different: the stable account can remain the same while an alias changes.

Encrypt contact objects before durable remote storage. Include a monotonically increasing revision and authenticated account scope. Resolve updates using explicit merge rules: a renamed contact and an independently added note need not overwrite each other. Deletion creates a tombstone that participates in synchronization; absence from an old snapshot is not a deletion instruction.

A memorable name is useful for discovery, not sufficient proof of identity. Exact lookup, optional discovery restrictions, rename cooldowns, and anti-impersonation review reduce harm but do not replace key verification. Never use the display name as a database primary key, signature identity, billing key, or business integration join key.

The client is a small distributed system

The mobile application is not a thin screen on top of a server database. It owns plaintext, cryptographic state, local history, drafts, playback, and work that must survive radio loss. Its storage and concurrency design deserve the same attention as the backend.

Public boundary

WhatsApp's multi-device account explains that linked devices keep their own local message history after an encrypted transfer. That is a useful architectural boundary; it does not reveal the current internal schema of each client. [S01]

Reference client architecture

Divide the application into presentation, command processing, cryptographic session management, a local transaction store, synchronization, and media workers. UI code issues typed commands such as SendMessage or MarkRead. It does not directly mutate ratchet counters or mailbox acknowledgment records.

The local transaction store should record an outgoing logical message, the exact prepared ciphertext envelopes, the updated cryptographic session state, and pending transmission work together. Once those records commit, a process crash can retry the same bytes. Re-encrypting indiscriminately after a crash can consume keys twice or produce an unbounded sequence of attempts.

For incoming work, authentication and bounded parsing happen before any user-visible side effect. Decryption, replay-state advancement, insertion of the local message, and the pending receipt should form a recoverable unit. Acknowledging delivery before durable local insertion creates a message-loss window. Inserting without recording deduplication creates duplicate bubbles after reconnect.

UI state versus committed state

An optimistic bubble should say pending, not delivered. A clock icon can represent durable local intent; a server acknowledgment represents a different milestone. If local persistence fails, do not leave an apparently sent message floating only in memory. Preserve the draft or show a retryable failure.

Use stable local identifiers so a retry updates the same bubble. Reconcile remote event identifiers with local command identifiers instead of comparing body text. Two identical "Yes" messages can be intentional and must not collapse.

Mobile constraints

The operating system can suspend background work at any point. Break downloads into resumable pieces, persist checkpoints, and place strict bounds on memory, thumbnail decoding, and queued writes. Prefer lazy loading of old history rather than materializing an entire conversation at startup. Prioritize call signaling over a large background backup.

Database encryption does not solve an unlocked, compromised endpoint. Protect keys with platform facilities where available, redact app-switcher previews according to policy, and minimize sensitive diagnostics. Accessibility and notification previews need explicit product choices rather than accidental leakage.

What to measure

Measure input-to-bubble latency, local commit latency, reconnect duration, database corruption rate, battery cost, and recovery success. Split these by device class and storage pressure. A fast flagship average can hide an unusable experience on devices with slow flash and little free space.

Connections, transport, and socket ownership

A long-lived connection reduces signaling overhead and allows timely delivery. It does not guarantee durability, identity correctness, or end-to-end confidentiality. Those are separate layers.

Protocol boundaries

Noise is a framework for cryptographic handshakes, while application-layer message encryption protects content across service infrastructure. Treat published frameworks as background; do not infer a complete current WhatsApp packet format from a standard or an old implementation. [S17] [S77]

Reference connection lifecycle

The client authenticates a device incarnation, negotiates capabilities, and receives a connection generation. A gateway registers a short-lived lease mapping that incarnation to a live connection. The mailbox remains durable elsewhere. If the gateway disappears, a new gateway can attach without moving the authoritative message history.

Bind control messages to the authenticated device, not to caller-supplied account fields. A routing hint is not authorization. A device claiming to fetch account B's mailbox must fail even when the requested mailbox shard is reachable.

Use explicit frame lengths and maximum sizes. Apply backpressure before allocating buffers for hostile lengths. Authenticate before expensive work where possible. Compression has information-leak and resource-exhaustion implications; avoid mixing secrets and attacker-controlled data in a way that exposes compression side channels.

Reconnect without a storm

The client uses exponential backoff with jitter, bounded by urgency and battery state. A service-wide disconnection otherwise causes millions of clients to reconnect at the same fixed interval. Separate authentication, directory refresh, mailbox retrieval, and media downloads into priority classes so reconnect does not trigger every subsystem simultaneously.

A heartbeat estimates liveness, not user presence. Mobile NAT mappings can disappear while the app still believes the socket is usable. Do not mark a message failed solely because the connection lease expired; durable mailbox state decides whether another attempt is required.

The duplicate connection race

Maya's laptop briefly has old connection 41 and new connection 42. A late close event for 41 must not erase the routing entry for 42. Perform compare-and-delete against the generation. Apply the same rule to session cleanup, presence expiration, and connection metrics.

Handover and visibility

Switching Wi-Fi to cellular may change the path without changing the application command. Retain the stable command ID and retransmit prepared envelopes only as needed. If a transport acknowledgment is lost, query or retry the application operation safely rather than assuming nothing committed.

Test the boundaries

Inject half-open sockets, reordered disconnects, expired credentials, duplicate frames, oversized lengths, and a gateway crash after admission but before acknowledgment. The desired outcome is reconnection and bounded duplicate transport, not duplicate visible messages or loss of an acknowledged durable command.

Accounts, clients, and delivery / Chapter 12

Durable admission and store-and-forward delivery

An offline recipient is normal, not an exceptional failure. A messaging service must hold encrypted delivery work without pretending that storage equals receipt by a person.

Public behavior versus design choices

WhatsApp's privacy policy describes temporary storage of undelivered encrypted messages and other categories of operational data. Its stated retention is not a promise that every kind of account, backup, business, or diagnostic data has the same lifetime. [S30]

Reference admission transaction

An authenticated sender submits a stable logical message ID, a request digest, recipient-device envelopes, and an expiry. In one transaction, validate current sending authority, reserve any necessary quota, store immutable envelope bytes or durable blob references, and append an outbox event. A repeated ID with the same digest returns the original result. The same ID with different content is a conflict.

A receipt to the sender should mean precisely what the durability policy guarantees: for example, committed to a specified replicated storage group. It must not mean merely buffered in a gateway process. If the product promises cross-region durability, the commit protocol must actually pay the corresponding replication cost.

Mailbox workers

Partition delivery by recipient-device incarnation or a stable account shard. Workers claim bounded batches using leases. Before release to a device, verify that its incarnation is still active. Delivery attempts carry an immutable envelope ID and attempt metadata; attempt IDs do not change the logical message identity.

Recipients durably deduplicate and then acknowledge. The mailbox can delete successfully acknowledged delivery work according to retention policy, while keeping the minimum deduplication evidence needed for the retry contract. Expired work is terminal and should not be resurrected by a replayed queue event.

State machine

Use READY, LEASED, HANDED_OFF, ACKNOWLEDGED, EXPIRED, and CANCELED as operational states. A crash from LEASED returns work to READY after a lease timeout. HANDED_OFF can be uncertain until the receiver acknowledges. Never infer receiver persistence simply because bytes were written to a socket.

The impossible shortcut

A service cannot atomically commit both its own database and a disconnected phone's local database using an ordinary HTTP acknowledgment. The practical solution is at-least-once transport plus stable identities, durable receiver deduplication, and explicit uncertainty. "Exactly once" is a property to define at a particular effect boundary, not a magic queue setting.

Cleanup and recovery

Use retention indexes and tombstones, not full-table scans. Restore mailbox snapshots together with authoritative expiry and revocation state. A restored entry already acknowledged elsewhere must remain harmless if delivered again. Recovery testing must cover duplicates, not just successful database opening.

Encryption and multiple devices / Chapter 13

End-to-end encryption: what it protects and what it does not

End-to-end encryption means the intended endpoints hold the keys needed to read content. Transport encryption to a server is necessary but not sufficient: a server that terminates TLS can normally read the application payload unless another encryption layer protects it.

Public foundation

WhatsApp's security material describes Signal-protocol-based protection for personal messaging. The historical 2016 whitepaper and later engineering disclosures are evidence for concepts and dated implementations, not a complete current cryptographic specification. [S77] [S01]

Threat model before algorithm names

List adversaries: passive networks, malicious relays, a compromised directory, a stolen unlocked device, a compromised legitimate participant, and an attacker replaying old traffic. Different controls address different attackers. Encryption can hide a body from a relay while leaving routing metadata visible. It cannot stop an authorized recipient from photographing a screen.

Separate content confidentiality, authenticity, forward secrecy, recovery after compromise, metadata minimization, availability, and endpoint safety. A design may have one property without another. A system that refuses to deliver any message preserves confidentiality but is not useful; an available system that accepts forged devices fails authenticity.

Reference key hierarchy

Give each device an identity key and independently managed session state. Keep long-term identity separate from ephemeral handshake and message keys. Keys for attachments, backups, group messages, and calls have different lifetimes and distribution paths. Reusing one account-wide master key for every purpose amplifies compromise and complicates deletion.

Use reviewed implementations and cryptographic agility with authenticated negotiation. Unsupported versions should fail explicitly, not silently downgrade. Record the chosen version inside the authenticated transcript. Migration must handle old devices without granting an attacker the ability to force the weakest suite.

Metadata is a separate engineering program

Mailboxes need some destination and timing information. Rate limits need some notion of an account or credential. Debugging needs outcomes. Minimize what each subsystem sees and how long it persists; do not claim zero metadata just because message bodies are encrypted.

An encrypted attachment may still reveal its byte length. A burst of packets may reveal that a call is active. Padding, relays, batching, and anonymous credentials can reduce particular leakage at additional cost, but none justifies an unqualified anonymity claim.

Do not build production cryptography from a chapter

The companion code in this book treats ciphertext as opaque bytes. It does not implement Signal, key verification, secure storage, or a production client. Its role is to test surrounding transaction and lifecycle invariants. Production security requires protocol review, implementation audits, compatibility testing, and a sustained vulnerability-response program.

Starting an encrypted conversation while the recipient is offline

A synchronous handshake would fail whenever the recipient's phone is asleep. Asynchronous messaging instead uses authenticated public key material published in advance, allowing a sender to prepare an initial encrypted message without a live recipient connection.

Standards context

Signal's X3DH specification explains an asynchronous authenticated key-agreement construction involving identity, signed prekeys, and optional one-time prekeys. PQXDH is a separate published post-quantum-oriented construction. Publication by Signal does not establish deployment in every WhatsApp client. [S18] [S21]

Reference prekey service

A device uploads a signed prekey and a supply of one-time public prekeys. The service stores only the public material required for discovery. The device retains the corresponding secrets. It authenticates uploads as belonging to that device incarnation and rejects updates by a revoked incarnation.

Fetching a one-time prekey must have a defined consumption rule. An atomic claim prevents two ordinary fetches from accidentally treating the same key as independently unused. Retries after a lost response need an explicit protocol: either reuse the prior claim under a stable request identifier or follow the protocol's supported recovery behavior. Do not invent a fallback that reuses private material unsafely.

Identity verification

A signature proves that a prekey was signed by a particular identity key. It does not, by itself, prove that the key belongs to the person Maya intended. Account binding, security-code verification, and directory transparency address that separate problem.

If the identity changes, the client must apply a documented trust policy. Automatically accepting every changed identity is convenient but weakens protection against substitution. Blocking indefinitely without recovery can make legitimate device replacement unusable. The product needs understandable warnings and safe ways to re-establish trust.

Inventory and denial of service

Prekeys are a finite resource. A malicious client can exhaust them by repeatedly fetching bundles. Apply budgets and monitor replenishment without exposing sensitive contact relationships in broad dashboards. Signed-prekey rotation needs an overlap window so delayed legitimate messages can still be decrypted, with bounded retention of old private keys.

Initial-message retries

Persist the exact initial ciphertext, selected bundle identity, and session transition before network submission. The receiver may see the initialization twice. Its processing must recognize supported duplicates and avoid generating multiple visible messages or inconsistent sessions.

Review questions

What happens if a bundle response is lost? What if the recipient re-registers before the message arrives? What if a signed prekey rotates while an old message is queued? What if one device is removed while other devices remain? These are ordinary lifecycle cases that must be handled by the complete protocol, not patched with timestamp comparisons.

Encryption and multiple devices / Chapter 15

Ratchets, replay defense, and atomic cryptographic state

A ratchet advances keys as messages are exchanged. The intuition is that knowing one key should not expose every past and future message. That intuition only becomes a security property when the protocol and its state-management implementation are both correct.

Standards context

The Double Ratchet specification combines key evolution with handling for out-of-order messages. Its precise assumptions and current extensions belong to the specification; this chapter explains storage and failure implications rather than prescribing a custom cipher. [S19]

Reference state ownership

Give one serialized worker ownership of each device-to-device session, or use a database transaction with a version check. Two threads must not independently load the same sending-chain state and both derive what they believe is the next unique message key. A mutex in one process is insufficient if several processes share the session database.

Prepare ciphertext and advance the durable session state in one local transaction. Persist a stable outgoing command record with the exact bytes. If the process crashes after commit, retransmit those bytes. If it crashes before commit, no acknowledgment or visible sent state should imply that the command survived.

Out-of-order receiving

Networks can deliver messages 8, 10, and then 9. A supported protocol may temporarily retain skipped keys so 9 remains decryptable. Bound the number of skipped messages, total retained key material, and work required per packet. Otherwise an attacker can request enormous forward jumps and turn cryptographic bookkeeping into memory exhaustion.

Delete consumed message keys according to protocol requirements. Keep enough non-secret replay evidence to prevent a duplicate ciphertext from creating another local event. Decryption success and database commit must be coordinated; advancing replay state without storing the message can lose content after a crash.

Compromise recovery is conditional

Forward secrecy concerns past messages under defined key-erasure assumptions. Recovery after compromise depends on fresh secret material and an attacker losing access. If malware remains on the endpoint and reads each new key, a ratchet does not magically restore confidentiality.

Ordering beyond cryptography

A cryptographic message number is not a universal conversation sequence. Multiple devices and group senders have different sessions. Presentation can preserve per-sender causal relationships and stable reply references without claiming one perfect global order based on wall clocks.

Test plan

Use protocol-library test vectors, then add process-crash tests around every durable boundary. Reorder packets, duplicate initialization, exhaust skipped-key limits, roll back a local backup, and race two sends. Test schema migration of encrypted session state. A library that is mathematically correct can still be integrated incorrectly by a client that commits keys and messages separately.

Key transparency and the directory problem

End-to-end encryption is only useful when the sender encrypts to the intended keys. A compromised directory that supplies an attacker's key can defeat naive encryption without breaking the cipher. Key transparency makes directory behavior auditable.

Public evidence

WhatsApp has published a key-transparency design, and Cloudflare describes its independent auditing role. Meta's AKD repository provides public authenticated-directory implementation material. These are more useful evidence than simply asserting that a server should be trusted. [S02] [S16] [S27]

Mental model

Think of a directory as a versioned map with cryptographic commitments. A lookup includes proof that a key binding appears in a committed state. An auditor checks that successive committed states evolve consistently. The goal is to make targeted substitution, inconsistent histories, or concealed changes detectable under the system's assumptions.

An inclusion proof alone is not enough. Two isolated users could be shown two different valid-looking trees unless the design addresses consistency and independent observation. Freshness matters too: a perfectly valid proof for an obsolete state can still point to a revoked device.

Reference integration

A client records the last accepted directory checkpoint and verifies the new proof before preparing envelopes. It applies an explicit freshness bound and fails safely if required evidence is unavailable. Cached checkpoints can support availability, but their permitted age must match the risk of stale keys.

Treat an account's device-set change as an authenticated event, not a mere cache update. Linking a legitimate laptop and silently inserting an attacker device must be distinguishable through account authorization and verifiable key-state evolution.

Failure and recovery

An audit mismatch should trigger investigation and a contained response. Do not quietly disable verification across the fleet to restore a green availability chart. Provide controlled recovery for stale clients and network outages; a cryptographic check that is bypassed whenever inconvenient is not an enforced boundary.

Rotating auditor keys, changing tree formats, or replacing a root of trust needs a signed migration path. Store migration evidence so older clients can distinguish an authorized upgrade from substitution. Test rollback of both application storage and directory checkpoints.

Limits

Transparency does not establish a person's real-world identity, stop an already compromised endpoint, or prevent a legitimate participant from sharing plaintext. It strengthens a particular infrastructure promise: key bindings and their evolution can be checked rather than accepted blindly.

Learning check

A client receives a valid inclusion proof for a device removed yesterday. Is the lookup safe? Not necessarily. The proof establishes inclusion in a committed state, while current authorization and acceptable freshness determine whether that state is still usable.

Multi-device encryption and client fanout

A person with a phone and a laptop is one account but several cryptographic endpoints. Sending to the account therefore requires a decision about which device identities should receive independent access to the message.

Public evidence

WhatsApp's 2021 multi-device disclosure describes pairwise sessions and client fanout to recipient devices and the sender's other devices. It also distinguishes group Sender Keys from this one-to-one fanout. [S01]

Reference send path

Maya's phone obtains an authenticated, versioned device directory for Noah and for her own account. It prepares an envelope for each eligible endpoint. The content is one logical message; the envelopes are separate delivery objects. Her laptop can receive a synchronized copy even when it did not originate the send.

The device list cannot be treated as a timeless cache. Admission records the directory revision used to prepare envelopes. A stale-list response causes a controlled refresh and preparation of missing envelopes under a supported protocol. It must not generate a second logical message or retry already completed business effects.

Partial delivery is normal

Noah's phone may be online while his tablet is offline. Each mailbox needs its own acknowledgment, expiry, and retry state. The user-facing aggregate status requires a documented rule: delivery to any device, delivery to a designated endpoint, or some other product definition. Do not derive it accidentally from whichever callback arrived last.

Maintain sender-side synchronization for edits, reactions, and reads using stable message identities. A read event on Noah's tablet should not look like a new message authored by his phone. System events and user content may share transport infrastructure while retaining different semantics.

In a simple teaching model, one send prepares R envelopes for recipient devices and S minus one for the sender's other devices. The envelope count is R + S - 1. With three recipient devices and two sender devices, that is four envelopes, not one. This is a sizing model, not a claim about current maximum device counts.

Each additional linked device expands the endpoint attack surface. Device removal stops future authorized distribution; it cannot erase material already delivered. Re-registration can change identity and invalidate trust assumptions independently of a routine companion-device addition.

Avoid placing all device private keys in a shared server-readable account record. That would convert multi-device convenience into server access to personal plaintext. History transfer and key distribution need their own authenticated paths.

Group encryption and Sender Keys

Encrypting a large group message independently for every participant on every send can be expensive. A group sender-key design reduces repeated encryption work by distributing sender-specific key material through authenticated pairwise channels, then using it for a sequence of group messages.

Public evidence

WhatsApp publicly describes using the Signal Protocol's Sender Key scheme for group messaging. The detailed current implementation and every rotation trigger are not fully established by the sources reviewed here. [S01] [S77]

Reference group state

Represent a group with a stable identifier, membership revision, role assignments, policy, and a cryptographic epoch. Represent each participant as an account plus eligible device incarnations. A user leaving, a device being revoked, and a group administrator changing are different events even if they occur close together.

A sender distributes the necessary sender-key material to the authorized endpoints for the applicable epoch. Subsequent group ciphertext references that epoch and sender identity. The relay can distribute ciphertext without knowing the message body. The client still needs authenticity, replay defense, and bounded out-of-order handling.

Removal is not merely a UI update

When Isha removes a participant, the application commits a new membership revision. Future sends must use the authorized membership and appropriate key transition. If a sender remains offline with the old group state, the service should not let stale authority silently define the next audience. A supported refresh/rekey path is required.

A removed participant may possess prior sender-key state. Therefore stopping server delivery alone and changing encryption state are separate controls. Neither retracts old plaintext already learned. The exact security consequences depend on the chosen group protocol and its key-evolution rules.

Scaling membership changes

A busy community can generate substantial key-distribution work when membership changes frequently. Batch compatible updates carefully, impose join/leave abuse limits, and prioritize delivery of control messages needed for decryption. Do not optimize by accepting a known-revoked epoch indefinitely.

The server should maintain a compact membership authority, while clients retain the cryptographic state they need. Avoid broadcasting unnecessary membership metadata to unrelated groups. Large organizations may need subgroups and announcement audiences rather than one unbounded chat.

Failure cases

Test two administrators issuing conflicting changes, a sender preparing a message just before removal, a key-distribution message arriving after the data message, and a removed device reconnecting with an old snapshot. The expected result may be a delayed send or explicit rekey, but not silent delivery under obsolete membership.

Design takeaway

A group is three related structures: a social membership list, an authorization state machine, and a cryptographic audience. Correctness requires them to evolve consistently without pretending that a single timestamp orders every distributed event.

Groups, synchronization, and message semantics / Chapter 20

History synchronization and encrypted application state

Message delivery synchronizes new content. A complete multi-device product must also synchronize archives, starred messages, contact labels, read state, and other preferences. These values have different merge rules and cannot all be treated as append-only chat messages.

Public evidence

WhatsApp describes encrypted recent-history transfer when linking a device and ongoing encrypted synchronization for application state. The server can hold encrypted state without being able to interpret the underlying personal preferences. [S01]

Reference bootstrap

A trusted device creates a snapshot with a sequence boundary, schema version, integrity digest, and explicit scope. It encrypts the bundle for the newly authorized device. The receiving device validates the bundle, imports it transactionally, records the boundary, and then applies later changes.

Without a boundary, updates arriving during snapshot creation can be lost or applied twice. A practical protocol reads a consistent snapshot at revision N, buffers later events, and resumes from N plus one. The snapshot may contain only selected recent history; the UI must not imply that missing older content was deleted everywhere.

Different state, different merge rules

A starred flag can be modeled as a versioned set/unset operation. An archive state may have product-specific precedence against a new incoming message. A read marker can advance within a known ordering domain, but it should not compare unrelated timestamps from different device clocks. A contact rename may need a deterministic last-authoritative-write rule or field-level merge.

Tombstones are essential. Deleting a contact or clearing a setting must survive arrival of older snapshots. Bound tombstone retention only after every supported recovery horizon is accounted for. Compacting too aggressively can make an old device recreate deleted state.

Privacy and integrity

Encryption protects content, while authenticated structure protects against substitution and rollback. Bind each state object to the account, object type, revision, and schema. An attacker should not be able to move a valid ciphertext from one account or field into another meaningful position.

Avoid indexing private application-state types in broad server analytics if the design promises that even their categories are hidden. Operational counters should describe storage and processing success without unnecessarily exposing user behavior.

Recovery choices

If a device is too far behind the retained operation log, request a fresh snapshot rather than guessing a merge from incomplete history. If its credentials were revoked, do not grant a new snapshot simply because it presents an old encryption key. Current access and historical decryptability are separate facts.

Acceptance criteria

A new device should converge after snapshot plus delta replay; duplicates should be harmless; deletion should survive stale replay; and a schema upgrade should reject unsupported structures without corrupting the local database. These properties are more valuable than claiming that all synchronization is "real time."

Receipts, ordering, and the meaning of a check mark

Reference event model

Store separate events for server admission, device delivery, local presentation, and optional read acknowledgment. Each event has a logical message ID, emitting device, event identity, relevant sequence, and server observation time. Avoid one mutable status field updated by whichever callback arrives last.

Delivery and read receipts can be delayed, duplicated, disabled, or aggregated. A newer operational timestamp does not necessarily represent stronger evidence. A late "delivered" event must not regress an already accepted read event. Conversely, a read event for one device should not be misinterpreted as proof that every device has downloaded the message.

Conversation order

There are at least three useful orders: the sender's local creation order, the service's admission order, and a recipient's display order. Offline sends and multiple devices make them diverge. Preserve reply references and per-sender causal relationships with stable identifiers. A wall-clock timestamp is presentation metadata, not a universal sequencer.

For the reference design, assign a monotonically increasing mailbox sequence within each recipient-device stream. This supports efficient reconnect without imposing global ordering across all conversations. Within a chat, deterministic presentation can combine explicit relationships and stable tie-breaking. Do not claim that sorting by server arrival preserves every user's intent.

Read state as private behavior

Receipt settings must be enforced at the event-producing endpoint and through synchronization. A server dashboard should not infer disabled read receipts from unrelated telemetry and expose them through a different feature. Group and channel semantics can differ; document the distinction instead of assuming one rule applies everywhere.

Partial failures

The receiving device commits a message, then crashes before sending its receipt. It will acknowledge again after recovery, using the same message identity. The sender may temporarily see a weaker status although the content already arrived. That is uncertainty, not necessarily data loss.

If the recipient has deleted local history, do not fabricate an unread state that rewrites earlier delivery facts. Deletion and receipt are separate state machines.

Testing the UI contract

Feed the client every permutation of sent, delivered, read, expired, and duplicate receipt events permitted by the protocol. Verify monotonic display rules, settings changes, and cross-device reconciliation. Test that forged receipts from an unrelated account are rejected. An attractive icon cannot compensate for an ambiguous or unauthenticated event model.

Groups, synchronization, and message semantics / Chapter 22

Offline outboxes, reconnects, and lost acknowledgments

Offline support is not a cache toggle. It is a protocol for retaining intent, preserving cryptographic state, discovering what committed, and resuming under current authority.

Reference local outbox

Every command has a stable identifier, a canonical request digest, preparation state, immutable envelopes, creation time, and expiry. The client persists these before considering the operation recoverable. Retries use the same identifier and bytes unless a supported protocol transition explicitly requires new recipient-key preparation.

Distinguish UNSENT, PREPARED, SUBMITTED_UNKNOWN, ACCEPTED, and TERMINAL. SUBMITTED_UNKNOWN is valuable: the client knows it attempted transmission but does not know whether the service committed. Treating this as definitely failed invites duplicate effects; treating it as definitely accepted can lose work.

Reconciliation after reconnect

Authenticate the current device, refresh security-critical account state, reconcile command outcomes, then fetch missing mailbox sequences. Large media transfers and historical synchronization should not block small message recovery. Apply bounded concurrency so reconnect does not saturate a weak phone or a regional backend.

If the account or device was revoked while offline, preserve the user's local draft where permitted but stop transmitting under old authority. Show an actionable explanation. Possession of old keys is not sufficient proof of current permission to send.

Expiry and clock skew

Use authoritative server expiry for accepted delivery work, and explain how locally scheduled messages behave before admission. Device clocks can be wrong by hours. A protocol can carry relative TTL intent and let the server assign a bounded acceptance deadline, while retaining the user-facing scheduled time separately.

A concrete failure

Maya sends message M. The service commits M and its outbox event, but the acknowledgment disappears. Maya restarts and retries M. The service finds the same digest and returns the original admission receipt. Noah's device receives two transport attempts, inserts M once, and acknowledges both safely. There are duplicates in the network but one visible message.

Backlog prioritization

Old expired notifications should not delay a current call invitation. Separate durable user messages from ephemeral presence and typing indicators. Drop stale ephemeral work instead of replaying it after an hour. Fair-share backlog draining across accounts prevents one returning device from monopolizing a shard.

Recovery test

Crash at every boundary: before local commit, after local commit, after server commit, after recipient insert, and before each acknowledgment. Restore from an older client database and replay duplicates. The test should establish which user-visible facts survive each failure, not just that the process eventually reconnects.

Groups, synchronization, and message semantics / Chapter 23

Edits, replies, reactions, and deletion requests

A message is not always immutable at the product level. People correct typos, react, reply, and request deletion. The underlying event history can remain immutable while the displayed projection changes according to authorized operations.

Reference command design

An edit references the original message ID, expected content revision, replacement payload, and actor. A reaction references the target message, reacting account, reaction key, and an operation revision. A reply references an immutable target identity rather than copied text alone. A deletion request references scope and the authority under which it was issued.

Keep the original sender identity stable. An edit must not make a different account appear to have authored the original message. If the product has an edit window, enforce it against authoritative time and current policy; do not rely on the client's local clock.

Race handling

Two devices edit the same message from revision 3. Either serialize them through an explicit last-authorized-write rule or reject one as stale and ask the client to reconcile. Do not let an old retry overwrite revision 5 merely because its network arrival is later.

Reactions are naturally idempotent when represented as desired state for an actor-target pair. "Set my reaction to heart" is easier to retry than "increment heart count." Derive counts from deduplicated state or update the state and count transactionally.

Deletion has limits

A request to delete from participants' supported clients is not retroactive secrecy. Recipients may already have seen, copied, exported, or photographed content. Offline clients may apply the request later. Backups and quoted previews require explicit policy and implementation, not a blanket promise that all traces vanish instantly.

A local delete affects the local projection. A group-admin removal operation has a different authority and audit trail. A security or legal retention decision is another lifecycle entirely. Avoid one ambiguous DELETE endpoint for all of these meanings.

References after removal

A reply to a deleted message can display a tombstone without retaining unnecessary body text. Search indexes, notifications, thumbnail caches, and accessibility descriptions must respect the same current projection. Deleting a bubble while leaving its full content in a push preview cache defeats the intended user experience.

Reconnect and snapshots

Include edits, reactions, and deletion tombstones in synchronization. An old snapshot must not recreate an earlier message body over a newer deletion. Version every projection-changing operation and define the compaction horizon for clients that have been offline for a long time.

Learning check

Why should a reaction request set a value rather than increment a counter? Because retries are routine. A desired-state operation can be deduplicated and reconciled, while an unaudited increment can count the same user repeatedly.

Disappearing messages, view-once media, and local privacy controls

These features reduce persistence or exposure under particular client behaviors. They do not make information physically impossible to copy. Their engineering contracts must be precise about what is controlled and what remains outside the system's authority.

Reference lifecycle

A disappearing message contains an authenticated expiry policy and a defined start event. Expiry from send time, receipt time, and first view produce different behavior. Choose deliberately and test offline cases. The service may stop queued delivery after expiry, while endpoints remove local projections and keys according to the product policy.

View-once media needs a consumption state attached to the intended viewer and device behavior. A server-issued download URL alone cannot guarantee one human view: bytes can be downloaded, copied, or cached. A supported client can enforce a local viewing workflow, but the limitation must remain visible in the threat model.

Transactional consumption

In the reference client, opening a view-once item first records an authorized consumption transition. A crash must not turn the item into an indefinitely reusable attachment. Conversely, consuming it before any display may frustrate users after a device failure. The product needs a clearly bounded recovery rule rather than an accidental race.

Local locks and notification previews

Chat locks protect against casual access to an unlocked application, depending on the platform and authentication mechanism. They are not a replacement for secure device storage or end-to-end encryption. Notification previews, search suggestions, wearable devices, and application-switcher screenshots can expose content outside the locked conversation screen.

Backup and synchronization

A disappearing item should not reappear because an older backup is restored. Persist sufficient deletion or expiry information through the supported recovery horizon. A new linked device should not receive content whose lifetime has ended. Be explicit about retained metadata and any exceptions required for abuse reports or user actions.

User-controlled reporting

A participant can choose to report content that was encrypted in transit. This is a new disclosure by an endpoint, not proof that the service could previously read all chats. The reporting UI should explain what selected content and context will be sent. Keep reporting retention separate from ordinary message-delivery retention.

Test matrix

Test expiry while offline, multiple linked devices opening the same item, clock rollback, app crash during display, notification previews, screenshots on supported platforms, restore from an old backup, and quoted replies. Media, local performance, and notifications / Chapter 25

Encrypted media, uploads, and content delivery

A video can be thousands of times larger than a text message. Sending it through every message queue as an inline payload wastes memory and makes small-message delivery compete with bulk transfer. Use a separate media path while preserving the chat's security model.

Public context

The historical WhatsApp security whitepaper describes encrypted attachment handling. Business Cloud API media has its own authenticated retrieval contract; those business endpoints are not evidence of consumer media internals. [S77] [S66]

Reference media pipeline

The sender prepares the media on the endpoint, generates a fresh media key, encrypts the bytes using a reviewed construction, and uploads the ciphertext. The chat message carries the authenticated media descriptor and key through the end-to-end encrypted channel. The storage service sees an opaque object, size, and operational metadata, not necessarily its image or audio content.

Bind the descriptor to the expected ciphertext digest, media type, size, and supported format version. The recipient verifies integrity before handing decoded data to a media parser. A valid transport download does not establish that the content is safe to decode.

Resumable upload

Create an upload session with maximum size, account scope, expiry, and chunk receipts. Repeating the same chunk with the same digest is harmless; repeating its position with different bytes is a conflict. Finalization verifies the complete object and atomically marks it available. Do not publish a chat descriptor pointing to an object that exists only in a worker's temporary directory.

The service can deduplicate identical ciphertext where safe, but cross-user plaintext deduplication has privacy implications. It can reveal whether a particular file exists and conflicts with independently randomized encryption. Do not assume storage deduplication is free of security cost.

CDN behavior

Cache immutable ciphertext by object generation. Use bounded retrieval capabilities and current authorization where required. URL secrecy alone is weak access control. An expired or revoked descriptor should not gain new authority because a CDN still holds its bytes.

CDN deletion, key deletion, and recipient deletion are separate actions. Removing the origin object does not erase a downloaded copy. Conversely, destroying the only decryption key can make retained ciphertext unusable under appropriate assumptions, but backup copies of that key must be accounted for.

Why server transcoding is not automatic

A server that cannot decrypt personal media cannot simply resize or transcode it. Produce needed variants on an authorized endpoint, use a separately consented processing boundary, or accept the cost of the original representation. An old pre-encryption architecture diagram with server-side FFmpeg does not prove the same authority exists today.

Failure drill

Interrupt upload after the final chunk but before acknowledgment. Retry finalization, then send the message twice. Corrupt one cached chunk. The receiver should show one message, obtain a verified complete object, and reject corruption without leaking decrypted content into logs.

Media, local performance, and notifications / Chapter 26

Low-bandwidth media, voice notes, and local search

A messaging product must work on weak networks, inexpensive devices, and metered data plans. Optimizing only for peak throughput can make the everyday experience slower and more expensive.

Reference representation choices

Prepare a small encrypted preview and one or more explicitly supported media representations on the sender. A low-quality preview can arrive quickly while the full object downloads later. Keep the preview's privacy and expiry aligned with the main object; a deleted photo should not survive indefinitely as a thumbnail.

Choose download concurrency based on foreground activity, network type, battery, and storage pressure. A voice note that the user just tapped should outrank an automatic download of an old large video. Avoid allowing a busy group to exhaust device storage through unattended media fetches.

Voice-note pipeline

Capture audio into bounded chunks, encode locally, persist progress, and let the user review or discard before send. Waveform data is derived content and may reveal speech timing; protect it consistently. Playback speed changes and seeking should not require a new server-side copy of personal audio.

If transcription runs on-device, model files and temporary text still require secure lifecycle management. If it uses a remote processing service, make that separate trust boundary explicit. Do not imply that end-to-end encryption to another person automatically grants an arbitrary transcription service access.

Local search

When message bodies remain on endpoints, the natural reference design indexes decrypted history locally. Maintain a local inverted index keyed by stable message IDs. Updates and deletion tombstones must update the index transactionally or through a recoverable local queue. Search results should not resurrect an expired or deleted message body.

Encryption at rest for the main database is insufficient if a plaintext search index is left beside it. Backups, crash reports, autocomplete caches, and operating-system search integrations must follow the same privacy policy.

Quality versus resources

Compression saves bytes but costs CPU and may harm readability. A document scan needs different treatment from a casual photo. Preserve user control for original-quality files and show size implications. Never repeatedly recompress a forwarded file merely because it passes through another client; that can accumulate quality loss.

Capacity example

At an assumed 24 kilobits per second, a 60-second voice note has about 180 kilobytes of encoded audio before container and encryption overhead. This is planning arithmetic, not a claim about WhatsApp's current voice-note codec or bitrate. Multiply by attachments per active user, not by text-message count alone.

Test matrix

Exercise 2G-like throughput, long pauses, 1% free storage, background suspension, malformed waveforms, huge image dimensions, and local-index corruption. The best optimization is one that improves completion and battery life without silently weakening encryption or dropping the user's content.

Media, local performance, and notifications / Chapter 27

Hostile media parsing and memory safety

Encrypted content can still be malicious. Encryption authenticates who could produce a payload under a key; it does not guarantee that a video container, image header, or document is safe for a decoder.

Public engineering evidence

Meta's January 2026 publication describes WhatsApp's migration of its media-processing library, wamedia, from C++ to Rust across supported clients. This is evidence of a specific security-oriented component migration, not a claim that all WhatsApp software is written in Rust. [S07]

Reference defensive pipeline

Place size checks, format recognition, structural validation, and decompression limits before expensive decoding. A ten-kilobyte file can declare an image with enormous dimensions. A container can contain deeply nested boxes or inconsistent lengths. Reject impossible structures early and cap total work.

Run risky decoders in a sandbox with no unnecessary network, filesystem, or key access. Pass only the bytes needed for the operation. Separate thumbnail generation from the main account process so a parser failure does not automatically expose the device's entire message database.

Memory safety is not semantic safety

A memory-safe language reduces classes of memory corruption, but it does not prevent logic errors, algorithmic denial of service, integer mistakes in business rules, or incorrect cryptographic integration. Native codec dependencies and foreign-function interfaces remain part of the attack surface.

At each language boundary, define ownership, buffer length, mutability, and lifetime. Fuzz the boundary rather than only the high-level parser. Validate that unsafe blocks are narrowly scoped and reviewed.

Migration without format regression

Build a corpus of valid and invalid files across years of client behavior. Differentially compare old and new parsers where the old behavior is correct, while deliberately changing unsafe acceptance. A compatibility test should not preserve a vulnerability merely because old clients accepted it.

Deploy by codec and operation, observe crash and rejection rates, and retain a safe fallback only if it does not reintroduce the vulnerability. A kill switch should disable risky processing, not silently route everything back to the unsafe implementation.

Secure diagnostics

A parser crash report should include format and failure category without uploading private media by default. User-submitted samples need explicit consent and tightly controlled access. Synthetic fuzzing inputs can cover broad structure without collecting real conversations.

Readiness gate

Require sustained fuzzing, sanitizer coverage where applicable, resource-limit tests, sandbox escape review, and recovery from partial local writes. Verify that malformed media cannot cause endless redelivery loops or block unrelated conversations. Security and availability meet at this boundary: a single attachment must not disable the entire client.

Media, local performance, and notifications / Chapter 28

Push notifications, presence, and ephemeral signals

Push services wake devices; they should not become the authoritative message store. Presence and typing indicators improve the experience; they should not become an expensive or privacy-invasive history of every user action.

Reference push architecture

After durable mailbox admission, a delivery worker sends a minimal wake-up notification through the platform's push provider. The client authenticates and fetches its encrypted work. The push payload should contain no more information than necessary for the chosen privacy contract.

A push acknowledgment means the provider accepted the notification, not that the phone received the message. Providers can delay or coalesce notifications. The client must reconcile its mailbox on foreground activation even when no push arrived.

Preview policy

Some products support notification previews generated on the endpoint. The extension or background process that decrypts and formats a preview is a security-sensitive component. It needs bounded execution, protected key access, and clear behavior when the device is locked. A generic fallback notification is preferable to leaking content after a lock-policy change.

Presence as a lease

Represent online presence as an expiring hint tied to a connection generation. Do not infer it from permanent account activity. A stale disconnect must not clear a newer connection's lease. Respect who is allowed to see the hint and how user settings affect it.

Typing indicators are ephemeral events with short TTLs. Never drain a backlog of yesterday's typing indicators after reconnect. Coalesce them and cap broadcast frequency. In large groups, user experience often improves when the system deliberately suppresses excessive detail.

Fanout control

Notifications can multiply dramatically across devices and groups. Use per-account and per-conversation coalescing, deduplication, and quiet-hour policies. The underlying message remains durable even when several wake-ups are merged. Keep business marketing notification controls separate from personal chat delivery.

Failure and abuse

Attackers can exploit presence to monitor routines or use repeated notifications to harass a recipient. Rate limiting and block enforcement must apply before notification release. A blocked sender's message should not reveal itself through a push preview even if the chat screen later hides it.

Acceptance tests

Test a push arriving before its mailbox read replica catches up, a notification delayed beyond expiry, a device token reassigned after reinstall, a blocked sender racing admission, and a client waking without any push. The invariant is that push accelerates discovery of authorized work; it never creates authority or substitutes for durable delivery state.

Calls and real-time media / Chapter 29

Call signaling and the first-answer race

A call is a coordinated real-time session, not a message with a large attachment. Signaling establishes who is invited, which device answers, what media capabilities are compatible, and when the session ends.

Public boundary

WhatsApp has described encrypted call-key distribution across devices and participant-key changes for group calls. The reference state machine below is an original design, not a disclosed internal call-service schema. [S01]

Reference call lifecycle

Create a call ID, caller device, recipient account, invitation expiry, and call generation. Send invitations to eligible recipient devices. Each device can ring, decline locally, or attempt to answer. The first valid answer commits the selected device under a compare-and-set or equivalent transaction.

Cancel ringing on the other devices after the answer commits. Those cancellation events can be delayed, so a second answering device must receive an authoritative already-answered result. UI timing alone cannot prevent two endpoints from believing they own the call.

Separate three states

Signaling established means the parties agreed on a session. Connectivity established means a viable network path exists. Media healthy means packets are arriving and decoding at acceptable quality. A call can succeed at the first two and still have one-way audio.

Maintain explicit transitions such as INVITING, ANSWERED, CONNECTING, ACTIVE, RECONNECTING, and ENDED. A terminal end generation must reject late answers and stale media-control commands. Do not restart a terminated call because an old invite arrives after reconnect.

Capability negotiation

Negotiate codecs, video layers, transport support, and encryption parameters through authenticated signaling. Bind the result to the call generation. A malicious intermediary must not be able to downgrade security by modifying an unauthenticated capability field.

Timeouts and billing boundaries

Consumer call setup and business call usage may have different accounting contracts. A ring event is not necessarily a connected minute. For a proposed business system, meter from precisely defined connected intervals and deduplicate reconnect events. Do not let retries create multiple charges for one interval.

Test the race

Have Noah answer simultaneously on phone and laptop. Crash the selected device after answer commit but before media starts. Decide whether the product permits a controlled transfer to another device or ends the attempt; either choice needs an explicit generation transition. The system must never silently create two active instances of the same participant identity.

Calls and real-time media / Chapter 30

NAT traversal, ICE, STUN, and TURN

Two phones can both reach the internet yet be unable to receive unsolicited packets from each other. NATs, firewalls, carrier networks, and changing interfaces make media connectivity a search problem.

Standards context

ICE specifies candidate gathering and connectivity checks. TURN provides relay behavior when direct paths are unavailable or undesirable. These standards explain the design space; they are not a claim that every WhatsApp transport path implements every option exactly as described here. [S22] [S23]

Reference connectivity flow

Gather local and server-reflexive candidates, obtain authenticated relay candidates where needed, and exchange candidates through the call's secure signaling channel. Probe candidate pairs and select a working path according to priority and policy. Keep credentials scoped and short-lived to prevent a relay from becoming an open bandwidth proxy.

Direct media can reduce latency and relay cost, but it can expose endpoint IP addresses to the peer. A privacy mode can deliberately prefer relays. That is a product tradeoff, not simply a network optimization failure.

Network changes

Moving from Wi-Fi to cellular can invalidate the selected path. An ICE restart or analogous supported procedure uses a new connectivity generation. Late responses for the old generation must not overwrite the new path. Continue or pause media according to the available buffer rather than restarting the entire logical call unnecessarily.

Relay placement

Place relay capacity near users and good network interconnects, while considering residency, abuse controls, and failover. A geographically near server can still have a poor route to the carrier. Measure actual round-trip and loss characteristics instead of using distance as the only selector.

A relay that forwards encrypted media need not hold its content keys. Keep signaling authorization and relay allocation separate from decryption authority. Restrict relay destinations and allocation lifetime so stolen credentials cannot be used for arbitrary traffic amplification.

Capacity arithmetic

For N two-person calls, per-direction payload bitrate b, and relay fraction r, a simple relay model carries approximately 2Nrb incoming payload bits per second and the same outgoing amount. Add transport overhead, retransmission, and video separately. Do not count only one side of the relay when planning network interfaces.

Failure tests

Block UDP, force symmetric NAT behavior, expire relay credentials, change IP mid-call, blackhole only one direction, and exhaust a relay pool. Distinguish a connectivity failure from a cryptographic failure in diagnostics without exposing private session secrets. A resilient client tries supported alternatives within a budget rather than looping indefinitely.

Calls and real-time media / Chapter 31

Audio, video, jitter, and congestion control

Real-time media has a deadline. A packet that arrives after its playout time may be useless even though it was eventually delivered correctly. This makes call engineering fundamentally different from reliable file transfer.

Public evidence and standards

Meta's MLow publication explains a low-bitrate audio codec designed around quality, compute, and bandwidth constraints. RTP provides standard concepts for sequence numbers, timestamps, and media transport. Neither source establishes a universal WhatsApp bitrate or one fixed codec on every device. [S12] [S24]

Reference audio path

Capture samples, suppress echo and noise where supported, encode frames, encrypt, packetize, transmit, reorder in a bounded jitter buffer, conceal loss, decode, and play. Each stage adds delay and consumes CPU. Improving one stage can worsen another: a larger jitter buffer hides network variation but makes conversation less responsive.

Use media timestamps for playout scheduling and sequence numbers for loss/reordering analysis. Do not confuse them with wall-clock time or message-order counters. Clock drift between devices needs compensation over long calls.

Congestion response

Estimate available bandwidth from feedback, loss, delay, and delivery behavior. Adapt video resolution, frame rate, and encoding rate; preserve intelligible audio where possible. Aggressive retransmission can worsen congestion. Recover only packets that can arrive before their usefulness deadline, and use redundancy or concealment where appropriate.

Video keyframes are expensive bursts. Several participants requesting keyframes simultaneously can overload a weak uplink or relay. Rate-limit requests and coordinate layer changes. Screen sharing may need readable text more than high frame rate, so it deserves a different quality policy from a moving camera.

Illustrative latency budget

A teaching budget might allocate 20 ms capture, 15 ms processing, 45 ms network, 40 ms jitter buffering, and 10 ms decode/output: 130 ms total one-way. These are chosen example values, not measured WhatsApp performance. A slow device or long-distance path can exceed the budget even when servers are healthy.

Quality metrics

Track call setup success, one-way audio, packet loss, freeze duration, audio underruns, reconnection time, and user-reported quality. Average bitrate is not a quality metric by itself. Analyze by device, codec, network, and call topology, using minimized and aggregated telemetry.

Test reality

Use recorded network traces, random loss, burst loss, reordering, thermal throttling, Bluetooth changes, and simultaneous camera use. Verify that the controller stabilizes instead of oscillating between high and low quality. A codec benchmark on a clean network does not validate the complete calling system.

Reference architecture

Use authenticated membership to decide who receives key material. A waiting participant should not gain access to media keys simply because a network connection to the router exists. Removing a participant requires both forwarding restrictions and the protocol's required key transition.

Scaling example

If each of ten participants receives nine full-rate streams, downlink demand can overwhelm mobile devices. Layout-aware forwarding might prioritize the active speaker and a few thumbnails. Key epochs and routing generations

Tag media/control state with an appropriate epoch. Late packets from an old epoch may be discarded or handled according to a bounded transition policy. Server failure

A router failure can require a new path even if the logical call continues. The coordinator selects replacement capacity and clients establish new transports. Existing encrypted frames may be lost; reconnecting the database does not recover missing live audio. Set honest continuity objectives and measure audible disruption.

Security limits

Current product context

The July 2026 announcement introduces WhatsApp Web calling, waiting rooms, screen sharing, and transfer of an active group call between devices, with gradual rollout. Its specific group-call transfer scope should not be expanded into an unsupported claim about every call type. [S37]

Reference transfer protocol

The current participant device requests a transfer to a linked, active target device. The coordinator creates transfer generation g+1 with a short expiry. The target proves its device identity, establishes media connectivity, and receives only the keys authorized for the current call membership.

After readiness, an atomic transition selects the target as the active participant endpoint. The old endpoint receives a stop command. Because that command can be lost, routers and control services reject old-generation activity after the cutover. UI messages alone are not sufficient fencing.

Avoiding both gaps and duplicates

Make-before-break reduces audible interruption but briefly uses extra resources. Break-before-make is simpler but creates a gap. Either approach must define whether both devices can transmit during overlap. A safe reference policy can allow the target to receive while muted, then atomically transfer transmit authority.

If the acknowledgment of cutover disappears, both devices query the authoritative transfer result. They do not create a new call or independently decide they won. A canceled transfer leaves the original call active if it is still healthy.

Bind camera, microphone, and screen-share permissions to the user's explicit action. A screen picker is not permission to share every future window. On navigation or logout, stop capture and clear sensitive transient data. Shared-browser sessions need particularly visible linked-device controls.

Call privacy, hostile signaling, and recovery

A phone can be attacked before its owner answers a call. Signaling parsers, preview data, and media negotiation are therefore security-sensitive even for calls the user intends to ignore.

Public engineering evidence

WhatsApp's call-security publication describes protections associated with silencing unknown callers and an option to relay calls to protect IP addresses. Reference admission policy

Before delivering expensive call setup data, evaluate block lists, caller permissions, account state, and abuse budgets. Limit ringing frequency and simultaneous invitations. Keep rejection reasons coarse enough to avoid exposing private account relationships to an attacker.

Do not parse complex untrusted media descriptions in a privileged process merely to decide that a call should be silenced. Perform minimal bounded validation first, then pass permitted data into isolated components. Authentication reduces spoofing but does not make an abusive authenticated caller safe.

Privacy tradeoffs

Relaying traffic can conceal a participant's network address from the peer while revealing some traffic metadata to relay infrastructure. Document that tradeoff. Do not describe a relay as making the user anonymous to every party. Minimize logs and avoid storing full session identifiers in widely accessible network telemetry.

Recovery contract

For a failed media path, attempt bounded path re-establishment under the same call identity and current generation. For a lost call coordinator, recover durable membership and ownership before accepting new control operations. A recovered state store cannot reconstruct audio that nobody received during the outage.

End-to-end encrypted backups

A backup solves device loss by retaining recoverable history. It also creates a new concentration of sensitive data. Encrypting messages during delivery does not automatically encrypt every backup under a key unavailable to the storage provider.

Public evidence

WhatsApp's backup design uses encrypted backup data and a separate recovery mechanism. A later rollout adds passkey-based protection to simplify the user experience. Backup protection remains a distinct feature and trust boundary from ordinary message delivery. [S05] [S43]

Reference backup manifest

Create a versioned manifest containing account scope, backup generation, schema, encrypted chunk references, integrity roots, and a snapshot boundary. Encrypt each chunk with a reviewed construction and protect the required recovery material separately. Do not leave plaintext thumbnails, indexes, or attachment keys outside the protected set by accident.

Incremental backups can reuse unchanged encrypted chunks while preserving manifest integrity. A partial upload should not replace the last complete backup. Publish a new root only after all referenced chunks are durable and verified. Retain a bounded prior generation for recovery from corruption, consistent with deletion policy.

Recovery identity versus decryption

The ability to receive an SMS on a recycled number should not automatically reveal a previous owner's backup. Account verification, backup ownership, and recovery-secret possession are separate checks. A passkey can simplify access without making its recovery assumptions disappear.

Explain failure honestly: if the necessary recovery secret is unavailable and the service cannot decrypt it, support may not be able to restore history. A hidden universal override would contradict the privacy guarantee.

Deletion and expiry

A backup can preserve content that the live client later deletes. Design an explicit lifecycle for backup generations and privacy tombstones. Restoring an older backup must apply current account and device authority before reconnecting. Old group membership or login sessions cannot be treated as current merely because they are present in a snapshot.

Resource management

Backups compete with battery, storage, and bandwidth. Schedule them opportunistically, show last successful completion, and distinguish "backup started" from "recoverable backup committed." A green toggle is not proof that a recent usable backup exists.

Validation

Regularly restore synthetic accounts into clean devices, verify integrity, and measure missing media, schema compatibility, and time to first usable chat. Test low storage and interruption at every upload boundary. Backup engineering is complete only when restoration is demonstrated, not when uploads succeed.

Public surfaces, backups, and recovery / Chapter 39

HSM-backed recovery and bounded guessing

A human-memorable recovery secret can be weaker than a randomly generated encryption key. A hardware-backed recovery service can limit online guessing while avoiding ordinary application-server access to the protected secret material.

Public evidence

Meta describes a geographically distributed HSM-based Backup Key Vault with majority-consensus replication. The May 2026 update explicitly distinguishes WhatsApp's hardcoded fleet public keys from Messenger's over-the-air fleet-key distribution mechanism. Do not transfer the Messenger-specific mechanism to WhatsApp without evidence. [S06]

Reference security contract

The client authenticates the expected vault fleet before sending recovery requests. The vault enforces a bounded attempt policy and releases recovery material only after a successful supported proof. Application servers should not be able to reset the attempt counter or substitute a fleet that ignores the policy.

Replicate attempt state with the same authority as the recovery decision. If each region independently permits ten guesses, an attacker can multiply the budget by switching regions. A failed attempt that consumes budget must remain consumed after process or regional recovery.

Consistency versus availability

When a quorum is unavailable, preserving the guessing limit may require refusing recovery temporarily. That is a deliberate security/availability tradeoff. Quietly falling back to an unbounded software verifier defeats the system's purpose.

The encrypted backup can remain safely stored while the vault is unavailable. Distinguish backup storage availability from recovery-key availability in service objectives and incident communication.

Operational controls

Fleet provisioning, firmware changes, key rotation, and destruction procedures need dual control and independently reviewable evidence. Hardware tamper resistance is not a substitute for a secure deployment process. Debug interfaces, emergency maintenance, and migration tooling can undermine an otherwise strong design.

Rotation and migration

A new fleet needs an authenticated trust transition. Clients must know which keys and versions are acceptable. Migration must preserve attempt budgets and protected state without exporting secrets through an untrusted operator-controlled path. Keep old and new fleet authority from simultaneously granting inconsistent recovery decisions.

Teaching-model limitation

The companion includes a small transactional attempt-budget exercise. It shows why failed attempts must commit and why replay IDs matter. It does not emulate an HSM, verify hardware attestation, implement password-hardening cryptography, or prove resistance to a malicious operator.

Failure drill

Lose the recovery response after the vault accepts a proof. Retry with the same operation identity. Then fail over to another replica and replay a failed attempt. The outcome must respect both idempotency and the total guessing budget; a timeout is not permission to reset either.

Store a template identifier, locale, category, components, policy status, and immutable revision. A send command pins the approved revision and validates supplied parameters against its schema. Represent the window with authoritative qualifying interaction evidence and an expiry. It is not the same as a marketing consent grant, a call permission, or a free-entry pricing window. A customer can be eligible for one operation but not another.

Business communication and integrations / Chapter 50

Throughput, messaging limits, and fair scheduling

A throughput limit controls how quickly work is admitted or sent. A messaging limit can control how many distinct users a business reaches over a period. A recipient pair limit controls pressure on one destination. These are different dimensions.

Public scope

WhatsApp's platform and limits documentation distinguishes per-number throughput from business-portfolio messaging limits and capability changes. Exact limits depend on account eligibility and current contracts; hardcoding one number as universal is unsafe. [S50] [S62]

Reference scheduler

Use hierarchical budgets: platform, region, business portfolio, phone number, campaign, and recipient. Reserve only the resources actually required, and release unused reservations after an explicit terminal outcome. A shared phone number used by multiple partners needs coordinated limits rather than independent full allowances.

A weighted fair queue can prevent a large campaign from starving a small business's support replies. Prioritize time-sensitive service work according to policy, but cap priority abuse. Expire obsolete messages before spending scarce delivery capacity.

Backpressure

Return explicit retry guidance when admission is limited. Clients should use bounded exponential backoff and jitter. A queue that grows without bound is not a reliability strategy; it converts overload into delayed, often unwanted messages.

Track queue age as well as depth. Ten thousand messages can be healthy for a high-throughput campaign and disastrous for a low-rate sender whose messages expire in minutes. Estimate drain time from effective service rate, not nominal peak quota.

Distinct-recipient accounting

A rolling unique-recipient limit needs a deduplicated scope and time model. Multiple messages to one user are not necessarily multiple new recipients. Approximate structures may be suitable for planning, but enforcement near a contractual boundary needs an error policy and authoritative correction path.

Illustrative queue arithmetic

If arrival is 1,200 messages per second and service is 1,000, backlog grows by 200 each second. A five-minute overload adds 60,000 messages. Restoring service to 1,200 merely stops further growth if arrival stays 1,200; recovery requires service above arrival or reduced admission.

Test plan

Simulate one noisy tenant, sudden capability reduction, partner sharing, expiration, retries, and a regional capacity loss. Verify that the scheduler preserves fairness and does not duplicate external sends while moving work between workers.

Storage, partitioning, and authoritative ownership

Choose storage from access patterns and invariants, not from a list of fashionable databases. Account authority, encrypted mailboxes, media objects, and aggregate analytics have different consistency and retention needs.

Evidence boundary

Meta-wide infrastructure publications describe broad storage and compute evolution, but they do not provide a complete current WhatsApp database inventory. The choices in this chapter are a proposed architecture. [S73]

Reference data placement

Use a transactional store for device membership, command idempotency, current account authority, and outbox creation. Partition durable delivery work by recipient-device or account shard. Put large encrypted media in object storage. Keep short-lived connection routing in an expiring cache. Store aggregate analytics in a separate system with explicit privacy controls.

A directory lookup is small but security-critical. A media download is large and immutable. A presence update is frequent and disposable. Placing them in one generic key-value table can obscure their very different failure contracts.

Shard keys

A stable account hash gives predictable ownership but can create hot accounts or large groups. Group fanout may need its own partitioning by recipient ranges. Avoid a shard key that requires moving all data whenever a phone number or username changes.

Use logical shard IDs mapped to physical storage groups. This allows controlled rebalancing without changing user-visible identifiers. A migration needs a cutover generation, replay boundary, and a method to reject stale writes at the old owner.

Index design

Mailbox reads commonly need recipient incarnation, state, sequence, and expiry. Idempotency lookups need account scope plus command ID. Deletion jobs need object lineage and retention category. Design indexes for these operations rather than repeatedly scanning content tables.

Secondary indexes are derived state. If an index is stale, it may omit work or return obsolete candidates. The final authority check must consult the appropriate current record before sensitive release.

Blob lifecycle

An uploaded object can be incomplete, finalized, referenced, expired, or awaiting garbage collection. Garbage collection must not delete an object whose publication committed but whose reference index is lagging. Use a grace period and authoritative reachability checks appropriate to the failure model.

Backup and restore

Back up each store according to its role. Restoring encrypted mailboxes without current revocations is unsafe; restoring a presence cache is usually unnecessary. Recovery plans should explain which data is authoritative, which can be rebuilt, and which represents observations that cannot be recreated.

Readiness gate

For every table and object class, name its owner, shard key, write serialization rule, retention policy, recovery objective, and deletion path. If nobody can answer those questions, the architecture is not yet operationally complete.

Actors, Erlang, and the lessons of historical scale

WhatsApp's early engineering is famous for serving many connections with a small team. The useful lesson is disciplined measurement and a runtime suited to concurrency, not that copying a historical language choice automatically reproduces the system.

Historical evidence

Rick Reed's 2012 and 2013 presentations discuss Erlang, FreeBSD, connection scale, operational tuning, and media infrastructure of that era. Their dates matter: they predate many current product and encryption boundaries. [S28] [S29]

Reference actor model

An actor-like process can own connection state, serialize local events, and isolate failures. Supervision restarts failed components. Lightweight concurrency is attractive for many mostly idle connections. However, an actor mailbox is not automatically a durable queue, and a process restart is not transaction recovery.

Persist acknowledged work outside ephemeral process memory. If an actor sends an external effect and crashes before recording the result, supervision can restart it but cannot determine whether the effect happened. Stable operation IDs and reconciliation remain necessary.

Backpressure inside the runtime

Monitor mailbox length, scheduler utilization, garbage-collection pauses, binary memory, and slow recipients. A process that cannot keep up can accumulate unbounded work even when the machine's average CPU appears moderate. Drop expired ephemeral events and limit per-actor queued bytes.

Large binaries should not be copied unnecessarily between processes. Ownership and lifetime rules matter for memory pressure. Avoid using a single global coordinator that becomes the bottleneck behind millions of otherwise independent actors.

Language boundaries

Cryptography, codecs, storage clients, and operating-system integration may involve native code. A crash or blocking call in the wrong boundary can undermine runtime isolation. Use appropriate scheduling and isolation for CPU-heavy or unsafe components, and test failure containment.

Modern build choice

A new company could choose Erlang/Elixir for connection-heavy services, or a carefully designed Go, Rust, Java, or other stack. The decision should reflect team expertise, libraries, observability, deployment tooling, and measured workloads. The reference design depends on invariants, not one mandatory programming language.

Performance methodology

Measure a representative workload: idle connections, reconnect bursts, encrypted envelope sizes, slow clients, packet loss, and background maintenance. Report both throughput and tail latency at a defined error rate. A benchmark with empty messages and no durable writes is not a messaging-service capacity result.

Learning exercise

Separate three claims: the runtime can host many processes; the server can hold many sockets; and the service can durably deliver many authorized messages. Each requires a different experiment, and success in one does not prove the others.

Infrastructure and production operations / Chapter 59

Regions, ownership epochs, and disaster recovery

Multiple regions improve resilience only when the system knows which component may write each authoritative fact. A regional outage can create two apparent leaders, delayed acknowledgments, and stale workers that resume after recovery.

Public versus proposed

Meta's broader infrastructure material provides context for large distributed fleets. It does not establish the exact current regional topology or failover protocol of every WhatsApp subsystem. The following is a reference design. [S73] [S74]

Ownership model

Assign each logical shard an owner epoch. Every authoritative mutation carries that epoch, and the storage boundary rejects stale values. A lease helps decide when to take over, but the fencing check is what prevents an old owner from committing after takeover.

A cache of the epoch is not sufficient if it can be stale at the final write. The epoch must be checked with the same serialization mechanism that protects the mutation. External effects need additional fencing or idempotency because a database check cannot recall a packet already sent to another organization.

Data-specific recovery

Account and device authority need strong ordering. Durable mailbox data needs a clear replication and acknowledged-loss contract. Presence can be rebuilt. Media objects may be replicated asynchronously with a publication rule that prevents references to unavailable data. Live calls need path recovery; lost audio is not restored from a database backup.

Quorum and availability

Synchronous replication across distant regions can increase latency. Asynchronous replication can leave a recovery point gap. Choose and state the tradeoff. Do not promise zero acknowledged data loss under arbitrary regional failure while acknowledging writes before the required replicas have them.

Failover sequence

Detect the failure, stop or fence the old owner, establish the new epoch, recover committed state, reattach gateways, drain queues at a controlled rate, and verify invariants. Keep revocations and deletion barriers current before reopening sensitive release paths.

Failback

Returning to the original region is another migration, not a simple DNS change. Reconcile divergent observations, preserve the newest authority, and reject stale delayed work. Treat failback as carefully as failover; many systems are tested only in one direction.

Exercise

Partition region A from the control plane while it remains connected to some clients. Region B takes ownership. A later resumes. Its old mailbox release, device-link, and call-control operations must be rejected or safely reconciled. A green health check on A does not restore its authority.

Infrastructure and production operations / Chapter 60

Asynchronous work, outboxes, and overload control

Messaging infrastructure is full of background work: delivery, media cleanup, webhook retries, backup jobs, key replenishment, and metrics. Reliable queues help, but correctness depends on how work is created and consumed.

Reference transactional outbox

Commit the business state change and an outbox row in the same transaction. A publisher later copies that row into the event system. If publication succeeds but its acknowledgment is lost, it may publish again. Consumers must therefore deduplicate the intended effect.

At a consumer, record event receipt and the local state mutation atomically. If the effect is external, create a new durable command with a stable identity rather than pretending the local transaction also committed the remote service.

Partitioning and ordering

Order events where the domain needs it: one account's device changes, one group's membership, or one business command. Global ordering across every message is expensive and usually unnecessary. Preserve event versions so consumers can reject stale updates even when different partitions or retries reorder delivery.

Poison events

A malformed or unsupported event should not block an entire shard forever. Quarantine it with minimal sensitive data, alert an owner, and define whether later independent work can continue. A dead-letter queue is not a resolution process; someone must investigate and safely replay or discard its contents.

Expiry and cancellation

A canceled job may already be leased. Workers must check current cancellation and authority before the consequential boundary. Deleting a queue entry alone cannot guarantee cancellation. Use generation numbers to prevent an old worker from completing a replacement job.

Backpressure

Set bounded queues, per-tenant budgets, and admission control. Reserve capacity for security-critical state changes and existing conversations during a bulk campaign surge. Optional analytics or large downloads should not starve device revocation or message acknowledgment.

Reconnect jitter

When a region recovers, spread client reconnects and queue drains. A deterministic one-second retry from every client can create periodic overload even when average capacity is adequate.

Test plan

Inject duplicate publication, consumer crashes, stale versions, poison payloads, queue retention expiry, and external side-effect uncertainty. Verify that an event-system claim such as at-least-once delivery is translated into a precise application effect, not an unsupported assertion that everything happens exactly once.

Security engineering and the software supply chain

A secure messaging protocol can be undermined by a compromised build, a malicious dependency, an overprivileged support tool, or a vulnerable client parser. Security must cover the entire path from source code to endpoint execution.

Reference build controls

Pin dependencies, record provenance, sign release artifacts, and separate build authorization from deployment authorization. Use reproducible or independently verifiable builds where practical. Protect signing keys with strong operational controls and require multiple reviewers for sensitive release changes.

Client update risk

The client is an encryption endpoint. A malicious update could read plaintext before encryption. Therefore update transparency, review, release auditing, and rapid rollback are central to the privacy model, not peripheral DevOps tasks. Web clients additionally need protection against compromised delivery and third-party scripts. [S15]

Secrets and service identity

Use scoped service credentials, short-lived tokens where supported, and key rotation with explicit overlap. A media worker should not manage account recovery. A webhook receiver should not modify unrelated business billing. Enforce these boundaries in infrastructure policy and application authorization.

Parser and dependency isolation

Memory-safe components reduce risk, but sandboxing and resource limits remain necessary. Review native-code boundaries, archive extraction, image decoding, URL fetching, and document previews. Prevent server-side request forgery in business media-fetch paths by restricting destinations and redirects.

Account takeover response

Detect suspicious linking, credential misuse, and unusual device changes. Provide clear user controls to inspect and remove linked devices. Recovery procedures must resist social engineering and recycled-number attacks; support convenience cannot become a universal bypass.

Vulnerability response

Maintain a bug bounty or responsible disclosure channel, triage severity, reproduce safely, patch, and communicate appropriate scope. Audit what data or authority was exposed rather than relying only on whether a crash was observed. A protocol-level bug can matter even without visible service downtime.

Production gates

Require threat models, external review for cryptographic changes, dependency scanning, fuzzing, least-privilege tests, secure logging tests, and incident drills. No single audit permanently certifies the platform. Security is an operating capability that must survive organizational growth and frequent releases.

Version protocol capabilities, local schemas, server schemas, key formats, media formats, and feature policy independently but track compatible combinations. A client advertising a capability must actually enforce its security semantics. Unknown mandatory fields should fail safely rather than be ignored as optional decoration.

Expand, migrate, contract

For a schema change, first deploy readers that understand both forms, then write the new form, migrate old data, and finally remove compatibility only after the supported horizon. Encryption-state migrations need especially careful rollback rules: old code may not safely interpret state advanced by new code.

Canary strategy

Use internal and synthetic accounts, then small cohorts across representative devices and networks. Observe correctness failures, not only CPU and crash rates. A release that sends duplicate messages or weakens audience checks may have excellent latency.

Fault injection

Crash at each transaction boundary, partition regions, lose acknowledgments, reorder events, skew clocks, fill disks, expire keys, and corrupt snapshots. For clients, suspend background work and simulate OS process termination. For calls, inject burst loss and asymmetric connectivity.

Model-based tests

Write a small state machine for admission, device removal, group epochs, and recovery. Generate sequences of operations and assert invariants. A model should be simpler than production but explicit about what it omits. Passing a local model does not prove a distributed implementation matches it.

Property examples

A duplicate command never changes its committed payload. A revoked device does not receive newly authorized handoff after the defined boundary. A stale group epoch cannot authorize a new message. An expired job is not revived by queue replay. A failed recovery attempt is not refunded by a process crash.

Test evidence

The companion's actual executed tests are listed separately from the broader integration catalog. This distinction matters: a written scenario is not a passing test, and a passing SQLite test is not a multi-region consensus result. Production launch requires both executable local checks and representative system-level evidence.

Infrastructure and production operations / Chapter 64

Capacity planning and cost models

Capacity planning starts with workload composition. Text messages, attachment bytes, idle sockets, call minutes, backup traffic, and business webhooks stress different resources. One headline "messages per second" number is not enough.

Illustrative workload

Assume 10 million daily active users, 40 logical sends per user per day, and a peak-to-average factor of six. That gives 400 million daily sends, about 4,630 average sends per second, and about 27,778 peak sends per second. These are invented planning inputs, not WhatsApp statistics.

With an assumed average of 2.5 delivery envelopes per logical send, peak envelope admission is about 69,444 per second. At 1.2 kilobytes of stored envelope metadata and ciphertext on average, raw daily envelope volume is about 1.2 terabytes before replication, indexes, and retention effects.

Media dominates bytes

Assume 8% of logical sends contain a 1.5-megabyte encrypted attachment. That produces roughly 48 terabytes of new media per day before reuse, replication, and download fanout. A small change in average attachment size matters more than optimizing a few bytes from a text envelope.

Connections and calls

If two million devices are concurrently connected, estimate memory per connection, heartbeat traffic, reconnect bursts, and load-balancer state. Do not apply a historical socket benchmark without matching its operating system, memory, durability, and traffic assumptions.

For 100,000 simultaneous two-person calls, 40% relayed, and 32 kilobits per second of assumed payload per direction, relay incoming payload is about 2.56 gigabits per second and outgoing payload is similar. Video, overhead, retransmission, and headroom add materially.

Cost equation

Monthly cost combines compute, durable writes, storage, replication, CDN/egress, media relay, push-related infrastructure, observability, support, security, compliance, and staff. Cloud list prices are not production procurement facts. Use a parameterized worksheet or script and insert current vendor quotes rather than inventing a universal cost per message.

Headroom

Reserve capacity for a regional loss, reconnects, compaction, key rotation, and backups. Running every subsystem at its measured maximum leaves no recovery capacity. A system can survive the initial failure and then collapse while replaying its backlog.

Planning discipline

Measure each assumption in a pilot, keep units explicit, and publish sensitivity analysis. Report which cost scales with users, messages, device fanout, media bytes, and call topology. Recalculate before adding a feature that changes the trust model or multiplies fanout.

Building the company and sequencing the platform

A WhatsApp-like company needs more than a messaging backend. It needs trustworthy clients, cryptographic expertise, reliable operations, abuse response, business support, and a sustainable way to maintain compatibility across years of devices.

Phase one: a narrow private messenger

Start with one-to-one messaging, a small supported device set, reviewed cryptographic libraries, durable local outboxes, a transactional mailbox service, encrypted attachments, and clear account recovery. Use a modular architecture with explicit ownership rather than dozens of microservices managed by a tiny team.

Do not launch with custom cryptography that has not been reviewed. Do not compensate for weak endpoint security with a polished privacy claim. Build operational visibility without collecting plaintext by default.

Phase two: multi-device and groups

Add device-directory authority, safe linking and removal, history transfer, group membership, key distribution, and offline reconciliation. These features change security assumptions; they are not simply extra screens. Invest in model-based tests and adversarial recovery drills before broad rollout.

Phase three: real-time and ecosystem

Add calls, regional media relays, group-call routing, business APIs, and partner integrations only with dedicated owners. A business platform needs consent, webhook durability, billing, and abuse controls. A public channel product needs moderation and discovery infrastructure distinct from personal chats.

Phase four: optional AI and advanced privacy

AI assistance, confidential processing, and privacy-preserving analytics require specialized security and evaluation capabilities. Treat them as separate consented services with measured trust boundaries. Do not use them as a reason to centralize private chat history unnecessarily.

Team ownership

Assign owners for client state, cryptographic protocol integration, account authority, delivery, media, calls, backups, business platform, trust/safety, security, and reliability. One team may own several domains initially, but every domain needs a named incident and migration owner.

Commercial reality

Consumer reliability costs money even without a direct per-message fee. Business usage, paid features, and discovery products can fund the platform, but monetization must not silently change privacy promises. Document data-use boundaries and keep revenue experiments from weakening security enforcement.

Launch evidence

Require restore tests, abuse-response procedures, support escalation, capacity measurements, external security review, and a staged rollout plan. A feature-complete demo is not an operational company. The capability to recover honestly from failure is part of the product users are choosing.

Operating objectives and incident runbooks

A runbook should guide a responder from a user-visible symptom to safe containment. It should not be a collection of restart commands with no explanation of which facts might already have committed.

Messaging backlog

First determine whether admission, mailbox delivery, or device acknowledgment is delayed. Inspect queue age, shard skew, replication health, and recent releases. Reduce optional work and prevent retry amplification. Do not purge encrypted messages to make a queue graph look healthy without a documented data-loss decision.

Device-directory inconsistency

Contain new sensitive operations if current authority cannot be established. Preserve evidence of conflicting revisions or transparency failures. Do not disable key checks fleet-wide as an availability shortcut. Communicate that some actions are temporarily unavailable rather than falsely assuring users that every send remains safe.

Call-quality incident

Separate signaling failures from connectivity and media degradation. Check relay saturation, network paths, codec rollout, and client build distribution. A regional reroute can worsen latency, so validate the effect before moving all calls. Existing calls and new-call admission may need different handling.

Business webhook failure

Confirm receiver availability, signature configuration, certificate validity, queue durability, and tenant mapping. Recover within the provider's retry horizon where possible. If events have aged beyond available replay, reconcile through supported reports or business records and disclose gaps; do not fabricate missing status history.

Backup recovery incident

Distinguish storage unavailability from vault or client compatibility problems. Preserve attempt budgets. Never reset recovery counters casually during an outage. A temporary inability to restore is preferable to silently bypassing the controls protecting every backup.

Post-incident review

Record the timeline, authoritative facts, uncertainty, user impact, contributing conditions, and corrective actions. Include privacy impact as well as availability. Did diagnostics collect more information than necessary? Did a support workaround broaden access? Did recovery resurrect revoked authority?

Error-budget policy

Use sustained user-visible failures to slow risky releases and fund reliability work. Define exceptions for urgent security patches rather than applying a simplistic deployment freeze. An error budget is a decision tool, not permission to deliberately lose a certain number of private messages.

Ownership

Every runbook needs an incident commander, technical owner, communication owner, and escalation path. Practice with synthetic accounts and recorded failures so responders understand the system before a real outage forces them to improvise.

Worked journey: a lost acknowledgment and a removed tablet

This scenario connects local persistence, encrypted fanout, durable admission, device authority, and user-visible status. All service names and records here belong to the reference design.

Initial state

Maya has phone M1 and laptop M2. Noah has phone N1 and tablet N2. Noah's device directory is revision 12. Maya writes message C7 and her phone prepares encrypted envelopes for M2, N1, and N2 under the supported sessions.

The local transaction commits C7, the exact envelope bytes, updated session state, and pending send work. The UI shows pending. Nothing has yet established server durability.

The uncertain commit

The admission service validates Maya's active device and current relevant directory state, then commits C7 and its delivery events. Its response is lost. Maya's client records SUBMITTED_UNKNOWN and later retries C7 with the same request digest.

The service returns the original receipt. It does not create another logical message or derive identity from the message text. The UI can now show server acceptance according to the product contract.

A recipient device is removed

Before N2 fetches its mailbox, Noah removes it. The device directory advances to revision 13 and N2's incarnation becomes inactive. A delivery worker already holding an N2 task attempts release. Its final check against current authority rejects the handoff.

N1 remains authorized and receives the message. It commits the local message and deduplication record, then its receipt is lost. A repeated transport delivery is recognized and acknowledged without adding another bubble.

What can be claimed

The service can claim one logical admission, authorized delivery to N1, and no newly authorized release to N2 after the defined revocation boundary. It cannot claim that N2 forgot any older messages it already possessed. It also cannot claim Noah read C7 without the relevant endpoint evidence.

Recovery complication

A mailbox replica restores from a snapshot taken before revision 13. Before serving, it must obtain current authority or consult an authoritative release gate. The old snapshot must not reactivate N2. Restoring data is not restoring permission.

Test mapping

The companion exercises stable command digests, duplicate admission, rollback, per-device handoff checks, stale owner epochs, and recipient deduplication. A production implementation must additionally test distributed races, cryptographic session behavior, actual device persistence, and network partitions.

Lesson

The essential design is not "put messages in a queue." It is a chain of explicit commitments, each with a different owner and an honest account of what remains uncertain.

Worked journey: group removal, rekey, and history sharing

A group membership change affects social state, authorization, cryptographic key distribution, and queued delivery. This scenario shows why a single "member removed" notification is not enough.

Initial state

Isha administers group G at membership epoch 21. Maya, Noah, and Rowan participate. Maya is temporarily offline with epoch 21 cached. Rowan has already received and decrypted earlier group messages.

Isha removes Rowan. The authoritative group record commits epoch 22. Future group operations must use the new membership and the protocol's required key transition. A server event informs active clients, but correctness must not depend on every device receiving that event immediately.

Stale sender

Maya reconnects and submits a newly prepared group send under epoch 21. The reference admission service rejects stale membership authority. Maya refreshes the group state and follows the supported rekey path before preparing a valid new send.

This may delay delivery. That is preferable to silently distributing future content under an obsolete audience. The exact cryptographic protocol determines how sender-key state changes; the teaching model only enforces the surrounding epoch invariant.

Queued old content

A message accepted before removal may already have been released to Rowan. The product must define whether undelivered pre-removal work is canceled, allowed, or otherwise handled. This reference design conservatively blocks new release to a currently removed endpoint where the release contract requires current membership. It does not pretend to retract earlier disclosure.

New member and history

Isha adds a new volunteer at epoch 23 and explicitly authorizes a bounded history share. The history grant records the selected range, recipient, approving actor, and current group revision. An encrypted bundle is prepared by an authorized endpoint.

If the new member is removed before release, the grant is re-evaluated. A stale history worker cannot rely solely on the earlier approval. Existing participants are notified according to the product's disclosure policy.

Recovery

Replay membership changes out of order, restore an old group cache, and deliver a delayed key-distribution message. Current authority and cryptographic compatibility must remain aligned. An old event cannot reopen membership or recreate a consumed history grant.

Lesson

The group is not one list copied to every machine. It is an evolving authorization and key-distribution protocol. Test the boundary between old knowledge and future access explicitly; no amount of encryption can make a former participant unlearn content already received.

Worked journey: a group call transfer during network failure

Moving a call from a phone to a browser is easy to describe and difficult to coordinate under failure. The system must preserve participant identity, media keys, and a single authoritative transfer outcome.

Initial state

Maya is in an active group call on M1. She requests transfer to linked laptop M2. The call coordinator verifies M2 is active and eligible, then creates transfer T8 from generation 8 to 9 with a short expiry.

M2 joins in a non-transmitting preparation state, obtains current membership information through the supported secure path, and establishes media connectivity. Its microphone permission is granted. M1 remains active during preparation.

Cutover uncertainty

The coordinator commits M2 as the active participant endpoint at generation 9. Its acknowledgment to M1 is lost as M1 changes networks. M1 continues sending old-generation control traffic. Media routers and authoritative control services reject activity inconsistent with the committed generation.

M2 begins transmitting under generation 9. The UI may briefly lag on M1, but UI lag must not imply continued authority. When M1 reconnects, it queries the result of T8 rather than creating a second transfer.

Coordinator failure

The coordinator's region fails immediately after commit. A replacement recovers the committed transfer and advances the shard owner epoch. If the commit was not durably replicated under the promised contract, the system must acknowledge that uncertainty rather than invent a guaranteed result.

The old coordinator later returns with an obsolete owner epoch. Its attempt to restore M1 as active is fenced. Current call generation and current shard ownership are separate checks; both are needed.

Media interruption

Some packets are lost during the path change. Jitter buffers can conceal only bounded loss. The service reports a reconnecting state and attempts supported recovery. It cannot reconstruct missing speech from the database. A seamless user experience is an objective to measure, not a theorem implied by durable signaling.

Additional failures

If M2's permission is denied before cutover, cancel T8 and retain M1 when healthy. If the user ends the call during transfer, the terminal generation wins and both endpoints stop. If M2 is revoked before cutover, reject the transfer.

Lesson

A transfer is a small distributed transaction around a real-time system. Stable operation identity, readiness, generation fencing, and explicit uncertainty matter as much as the media protocol.

Worked journey: business retries, opt-out, and a pricing change

A business notification crosses several systems: an internal workflow, the messaging platform, a customer's device, webhook delivery, and accounting. Their events do not arrive in a perfectly ordered sequence.

Initial state

The bicycle shop prepares an appointment reminder for Noah. Its internal command B9 pins the recipient's business-scoped identity, consent purpose, approved template revision, and expiry. The shop stores B9 before calling the provider.

The provider accepts the send, but the response disappears. The shop records UNKNOWN rather than inventing a failure. It does not generate a fresh independent reminder every time a worker restarts. It uses the provider's supported evidence and reconciliation options to resolve the original attempt.

Opt-out race

Noah opts out before a second queued reminder is dispatched. The shop increments the consent revision. The worker holding the old queued task checks current permission and refuses release. A marketing grant, service window, and call permission remain separate; one cannot be substituted for another.

The already handed-off first message cannot necessarily be recalled. The operator sees the distinction between canceled-before-send and cancellation-too-late. Honest status is better than a misleading universal "canceled" label.

Duplicate webhook

A delivery update arrives twice in different HTTP batches. The receiver validates each raw-body signature, stores the logical event once, and applies the CRM update transactionally. Its HTTP acknowledgment follows durable capture. A later read event strengthens the delivery projection without duplicating the underlying business action.

Pricing transition

A late event is processed after a rate change. The billing pipeline uses the applicable event-time and contract rules, retaining the pricing version and source evidence. The October 1, 2026 changes announced in the reviewed documentation are not backdated to September merely because processing occurred later. [S63]

Credit correction

An eligible adjustment references the original charge. Two workers attempt the same credit. A unique credit identity and bounded remaining-credit check permit one effect. A different goodwill payment, if authorized, is recorded separately rather than disguising it as another reversal of the same charge.

Lesson

A messaging platform integration is not a synchronous function returning "done." It is an evidence-driven workflow with permission, external uncertainty, asynchronous status, and financial reconciliation. Each boundary needs its own durable identity and owner.

Production gates and the final architecture checklist

A platform is ready when its promises are backed by evidence across security, correctness, reliability, usability, and operations. A long feature list does not establish readiness.

Gate one: identity and keys

Device linking is authenticated and visible. Revocation blocks future authorized operations at a defined boundary. Key lookup is verifiable under the chosen design. Cryptographic libraries and migrations have been reviewed. Recovery cannot silently bypass the privacy model.

Gate two: durable messaging

Local intent survives process failure. Admission has a documented durability contract. Retries preserve stable identities. Recipients deduplicate before visible effects. Expiry, cancellation, and current authority are checked before consequential release. Queue replay does not resurrect deleted or revoked state.

Gate three: groups and calls

Membership and cryptographic audiences evolve consistently. History sharing is explicit. Calls separate signaling, connectivity, and media health. First-answer and transfer races have authoritative outcomes. Real-time recovery objectives account for audio that cannot be replayed.

Gate four: business and public products

Business plaintext endpoints are documented. Consent, templates, limits, subscriptions, and partner scopes are enforced. Webhooks are authenticated and durably captured. Channels and AI features disclose their distinct trust models. Billing uses effective-dated evidence and bounded corrections.

Gate five: privacy lifecycle

Backups are restorable under the stated recovery assumptions. Deletion and revocation survive old snapshots. Derived artifacts and downstream copies have explicit lifecycle rules. Telemetry minimizes content and linkability. Optional processing cannot silently fall back to a weaker confidentiality boundary.

Gate six: operations

Runbooks identify authority and uncertainty, not just restart commands. Regional failover and failback are tested. Capacity includes recovery headroom. Releases cover old clients and weak devices. Support access is scoped and audited. Security incidents have a practiced response path.

What remains private

Public sources reveal valuable architecture, protocols, contracts, and engineering decisions. They do not reveal every current internal service, database layout, key rotation rule, routing policy, or production limit. This playbook uses those sources to explain principles and build an explicit reference design without pretending the unknown details are known.

Final mental model

A private communications platform is a chain of carefully bounded disclosures and commitments. Encryption protects content between intended endpoints. Durable state protects acknowledged work. Current authority controls future actions. Recovery must preserve all three rather than restoring one by weakening another.

Ten critical algorithms

C1. Prepare an outgoing message on the endpoint

begin local transaction
  require current local session version
  result = reviewed_crypto.prepare(session, plaintext, recipients)
  persist result.updated_session
  persist command(id, digest, exact_ciphertext_envelopes, PREPARED)
commit

The pseudocode identifies a transaction boundary, not a cryptographic construction. A production library's state model may require a different integration pattern. Do not regenerate a new send identity after every process restart.

C2. Admit a command with its outbox

begin authority transaction
  require authenticated active sender and current owner epoch
  if command(account, id) exists:
    require same request digest; return original receipt
  require current audience and valid expiry
  insert immutable logical message and device envelopes
  insert outbox delivery intents
  insert command digest and durable receipt
commit
return receipt

The outbox makes eventual event publication recoverable. It does not prevent duplicate publication; consumers still need their own effect identity.

C3. Release an envelope under current authority

begin release-authority operation
  require current device incarnation and account state
  require envelope belongs to this device
  require unexpired work and applicable current membership
  require current shard owner epoch
  record authorized handoff attempt
commit
release ciphertext through authenticated channel

The exact linearization point and transport race must be specified. A database transaction cannot recall bytes already released. The guarantee is bounded to the defined authorized handoff, not retroactive erasure at a recipient.

C4. Persist before acknowledging delivery

authenticate and decrypt using reviewed protocol integration
begin local transaction
  if message identity already applied: return prior local result
  persist message projection and replay state consistently
  persist pending delivery receipt
commit
send receipt; retry receipt if acknowledgment is uncertain

Authentication and replay handling are protocol-specific. The local teaching model assumes valid input and tests only the transaction/deduplication portion.

C5. Change a group's future audience

begin membership transaction
  require authorized actor and expected membership epoch
  apply role/member change
  increment membership epoch
  append membership-change event
commit
clients refresh and perform required cryptographic key transition
new sends must satisfy current epoch and audience checks

Key redistribution is not made magically atomic by the server transaction. The complete protocol needs supported transitional behavior, with confidentiality preferred over silently accepting obsolete authority.

C6. Select one answering device

begin call transaction
  require invited account and active device
  require call not ended
  if winner exists: return authoritative winner
  set winner = answering device
  advance call state and append notifications
commit
other ringing endpoints reconcile against the winner

Media connectivity can still fail after this commit. A controlled transfer or termination policy handles that case; another endpoint must not simply overwrite the winner.

C7. Consume a recovery attempt durably

begin protected recovery operation
  if attempt ID exists: require same proof identity; return result
  require authenticated expected vault and remaining budget
  verify proof under protected policy
  if wrong: decrement remaining budget
  persist attempt result
commit even when proof is wrong
return accepted / rejected

An exception that rolls back the failed-attempt counter would defeat the budget. The production proof and hardware enforcement are outside the SQLite demonstration.

C8. Capture and consume a webhook

check body size and signature over exact received bytes
resolve authorized tenant and subscribed asset
persist authenticated event envelope durably
return HTTP 200
for each logical update:
  begin transaction
    insert inbox identity if absent
    apply local business change and outbox intent
  commit

An event can repeat in another HTTP batch. Deduplicate the intended update, not only the HTTP request hash. A valid signature does not authorize arbitrary tenant claims inside the body.

C9. Dispatch under current business permission

load queued command
require not expired, not canceled, and current tenant access
require purpose-specific permission and current template/window policy
record stable external attempt identity
perform supported provider call
if response lost: retain UNKNOWN and reconcile
never invent provider idempotency that its API does not promise

For external systems, the unavoidable gap between local record and remote effect must be handled explicitly. An outbox alone cannot guarantee exactly one external message.

C10. Apply a bounded financial credit

begin financial transaction
  if credit ID exists: require same original charge and amount
  require same tenant and currency scope
  remaining = original amount - committed eligible credits
  require 0 < requested credit <= remaining
  append credit and accounting event
commit

Use exact arithmetic and immutable correction records. A separate goodwill payment should not be disguised as an over-credit of the original charge.

Implementation companion / Appendix G

Reproducible exercises and interpretation

What was executed

The local companion's 101 tests passed. They run real local SQLite transactions, including concurrent threads and injected rollback failures. They do not call WhatsApp, a bank, a mobile push provider, an HSM, or a remote confidential-computing service. Authentication and cryptographic protocol correctness are outside the model.

The database schema contains 24 teaching tables. The broader entity blueprint is conceptual and should not be confused with this smaller implementation. The code intentionally keeps all local writes serialized through SQLite to make the protected invariants visible; that serialization is not a model of multi-region consensus.

Reproduction

python -m pip install pytest matplotlib
cd code
python -m pytest -q
python experiments.py

The book-generation environment additionally uses mistune, WeasyPrint, PyMuPDF, CairoSVG, and Pillow. The source bundle includes scripts and original SVGs, not font files. A rebuild on another machine can have different pagination if its fonts or rendering-library versions differ.

Measured synthetic outcomes

In the seeded acknowledgment exercise, 10,000 logical commands produced 1,168 simulated lost responses. A deliberately naive fresh-identity retry policy produced 11,168 effects. Keeping stable identities produced 10,000. This is a model of effect counting, not a measured network reliability rate.

For 10,000 simulated reconnecting clients, a synchronized one-second retry produced a peak of 10,000 clients in one bucket. Uniformly spreading the same clients across sixty one-second buckets produced a peak of 187. Real retry distributions, backoff stages, and correlated networks require broader simulation.

The queue exercise peaked at 12,000 messages after a sixty-second service deficit. Increasing service above arrival drained it by elapsed second 90. Merely matching arrival would have preserved the backlog.

Planning calculations

The fanout chart assumes two sender devices and one to six recipient devices. The media-latency chart uses chosen component budgets totaling 130 ms. The relay chart assumes 40% relay use and 32 kbit/s per direction. Capacity charts assume forty sends per daily active user, peak factor six, and 2.5 envelopes per logical send. None of these is a WhatsApp production statistic.

What to add next in an implementation

Test actual protocol libraries, encrypted local storage, process suspension, real packet loss, replica partitions, key rotation, HSM recovery, API certification, accessibility, and privacy-policy enforcement across all clients. Add a distributed test harness that can demonstrate the chosen linearization points under concurrent revocation and handoff. Treat the local model as an executable explanation, not a security certification.
