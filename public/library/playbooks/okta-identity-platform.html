01. The identity platform: a mental model

What are we actually building?

An identity platform is a collection of security decisions, durable records and delivery mechanisms. It answers four different questions: who is asking, how confidently we know that, what they may do, and whether that permission should still exist. A login page is only the visible tip. The engineering challenge is keeping those answers consistent across employees, customers, partner organizations, machines, agents and thousands of independent applications.

Our proposed company has two product experiences on a shared foundation: workforce identity for administrators managing an organization, and customer identity for developers embedding identity into their applications. They may share cryptographic libraries, policy primitives and operations without sharing every database, deployment or product lifecycle. Do not mistake a common logo for one uniform runtime.

Vocabulary that prevents design errors

An identity is a durable platform record. A principal is an entity allowed to act: a human, service, device or agent. An authenticator proves control of a credential. A session remembers an authentication event. A token conveys a scoped assertion to a particular receiver. An entitlement is a permission or role in an application. An organization may mean our paying tenant or a business customer inside that tenant; those are different boundaries.

Authentication is not authorization. A valid signed token says what its issuer asserted; it does not automatically authorize every resource operation. Identity proofing, authentication strength and federation assurance are also separate dimensions in NIST's model. A successfully verified email address is not proof of a person's legal identity. [P20]

Design from invariants

We begin with properties that must survive retries, races and outages. A tenant can never read another tenant's identity records. An authorization code cannot create two independent successful grants. A removed administrator cannot regain authority from a stale policy cache. No support engineer can obtain a customer credential from diagnostics. Every privileged decision is attributable to a principal and an evaluated policy version.

Represent these as executable tests and database constraints, not aspirational statements. For example, a connector worker receives a tenant-scoped capability. Its database transaction must reject a resource belonging to any other tenant even when its request contains a valid-looking user ID. A replay test runs two token exchanges at the same instant and proves at most one grant is committed.

Security does not always mean denying everything during failure. A cached low-risk app assignment may remain usable under a documented lease, while a privileged recovery request must stop when its approval ledger is unavailable. Define failure policy per operation before writing fallbacks.

The four planes

The serving plane handles login, token exchange, session checks and authorization. The control plane manages tenants, applications, policies, keys and releases. The asynchronous plane provisions users, executes workflows, sends events and builds reports. The security plane verifies devices, evaluates risk, records evidence and propagates revocation. Some security services are synchronous; the label does not mean they can fail independently without consequences.

The central design goal is to avoid putting the global control plane, billing, analytics, arbitrary customer code or a slow SaaS connector on every login's critical path. Serving cells receive validated, versioned configuration and continue operating within explicit freshness bounds.

The security contract

For every API write down the caller, tenant, target object, required authority, replay protection, audit event and failure behavior. For every browser redirect write down who controls the destination and which request it is bound to. For every stored secret write down why it is needed, who can decrypt it and how it is revoked.

Learning exercise. Trace one employee signing in to payroll. Identify at least three sessions: browser-to-identity-provider, browser-to-payroll and possibly payroll-to-its-backend. Explain why ending the first does not necessarily end the other two. That distinction returns in Chapters 8, 13 and 42.

03. What public sources reveal about Okta infrastructure

Evidence, not invented internals

Okta's November 2024 infrastructure paper describes repeatable, isolated cells, spanning edge components through data storage. It names Redis, Elasticsearch, Kinesis and ProxySQL, and describes container use across ECS and Kubernetes, with differing adoption by product. It identifies AWS infrastructure and a first GCP cell. These are dated disclosures, not a complete September 2026 deployment manifest. [O01]

The same paper discusses Conductor built around Amazon Simple Workflow Service, release automation involving Lambda and Terraform, and other deployment tooling. Its active-active-active description concerns availability zones within an AWS region. That does not establish globally synchronous, multi-region database writes. We should copy the failure-containment idea, not presume identical present-day tools. [O01]

The 2022 high-availability paper provides older evidence of separate data roles, replication, failover and protected backups. It is useful architectural history; current disaster-recovery documentation should govern descriptions of current recovery behavior. [O02] [O03]

Auth0 separately published a 2022 private-cloud architecture centered on immutable container deployments, Kubernetes, GitOps and infrastructure/platform/runtime layers. It discusses AWS and Azure and managed data services. Its current private-cloud documentation describes dedicated deployment choices. This is evidence about Auth0's architecture, not proof that the workforce product uses the same database or orchestration system. [A01] [A02]

What we can infer, carefully

Isolation into repeated units reduces the number of customers exposed to one capacity accident, database failure or rollout. That is an architectural inference, not a guarantee that a cell has no shared external dependencies. DNS, certificate issuance, deployment credentials, a shared policy artifact or a global traffic manager can still create correlated failure.

Different product runtimes explain why recovery coverage can differ by feature. They also suggest a sensible way to build our company: share well-defined identity contracts, but give high-risk or operationally different workloads their own execution boundaries. We need not migrate every service to the same scheduler to obtain consistent security policy.

Unknowns we must not fill with guesses

The public materials do not give a current authoritative list of all database engines, table schemas, replication modes, instance counts, autoscaling thresholds, cache TTLs, cryptographic signing arrangements, tenancy placement algorithms or internal cost per user. This book's versions of those mechanisms are explicitly proposed designs.

We also do not know every internal incident response exception or customer-negotiated SLA. A marketing availability number is not a source for unpublished RPO promises. A whitepaper diagram is not evidence that a particular customer is on that exact topology today.

The design we adopt

Our reference platform uses regional serving cells with their own authoritative transactional data, security-state service, local caches, signing service and asynchronous workers. A global management layer publishes signed configuration and routes a tenant to its home cell. We isolate customer-provided code and connector egress from token issuance.

At initial scale, several logical services can live in one carefully modular application. Cell isolation is a deployment and data-ownership discipline, not a demand to create hundreds of microservices. We split a service when it needs a distinct scaling profile, security boundary, storage invariant or on-call owner.

Architecture review question. When a dependency is described as global, ask what happens if it returns yesterday's correct answer, an invalid answer or no answer. Those three failures require different defenses. A cached tenant route may be safe; a stale permission revocation may not be.

04. SLAs, SLOs, SLIs and recovery promises

Four numbers that must not be conflated

An SLA is a contractual commitment and remedy. An SLO is an operational target. An SLI is the measured indicator. RTO is the recovery-time objective, while RPO bounds tolerable data loss. A support response target measures when someone responds, not when service is restored. These definitions drive different engineering decisions.

Okta publicly promoted 99.99% availability, but the customer's executed agreement, order and service-specific terms determine the binding scope. The Trust site reports an aggregate metric whose incident inclusion threshold refers to impact on at least 10% of customers; this is not equivalent to each tenant's observed success rate. [O05] [O04] [O09]

A concrete historical contract example

The publicly available 2022 Auth0 SLA covers its defined core user and machine-to-machine authentication services at 99.99% monthly availability. It uses successful-minute accounting, specified exclusions and a production-use scope. Its credits step through 5%, 10%, 20% and 50% as availability falls through the stated bands, capped at 50%; a written claim deadline also applies. This is a historical Auth0 agreement example, not the universal current Workforce SLA. [O06]

The current support definitions distinguish Basic, Silver and Gold. The published P1 initial-response targets are respectively two hours, one hour and thirty minutes. They are support-plan targets, not a promise that an outage will be fixed within those intervals. [O07] [O08]

Recovery coverage is product-specific

Current Okta recovery documentation describes standard failover as usually occurring about one hour after infrastructure failure detection. Enhanced recovery discusses a five-minute RTO and initiation within five minutes, with explicit product exclusions, including several governance, workflow, privileged-access, posture, threat-protection and Auth0 offerings. During documented failover, administration is read-only and users cannot reset passwords. Do not rewrite these statements as a universal five-minute full-service restoration guarantee. [O03]

The arithmetic of availability

For a 30-day month, 99.9% permits 43.2 minutes of unavailability; 99.99% permits 4.32 minutes; 99.999% permits 25.92 seconds. These are arithmetic budgets, not interpretations of any vendor contract. A single five-minute complete outage already exceeds a 30-day four-nines time budget. Consequently, a recovery mechanism can meet its RTO while the service misses its monthly availability objective.

Our initial proposed external commitment is made only after measured evidence. Internally, target a higher serving-plane SLO than the contract to preserve room for correlated incidents. Do not promise five nines merely because components run in three availability zones. An independence formula such as multiplying component availabilities is misleading when those components share a release, region or credential.

05. The reference architecture: cells and trust boundaries

Start with an independently operable cell

A cell is a bounded deployment that can serve a set of tenants without synchronous reliance on another serving cell. It includes ingress, authentication orchestration, protocol adapters, policy evaluation, session and credential state, directory access, signing, audit capture and queues. It has a capacity envelope and its own release cohort. Cell independence does not mean duplicating every analytics system.

Requests arrive through a protected edge. The edge resolves a verified hostname to a tenant and a cell placement version. A cell router validates that route, attaches authenticated routing metadata and dispatches to a protocol endpoint. The endpoint constructs a security context; it does not trust a tenant ID supplied in an arbitrary request body.

The authentication orchestrator evaluates an immutable policy snapshot against a directory record, device evidence and transaction state. It requests additional proof if needed. Once satisfied, the token service commits the grant and produces an assertion through the signing boundary. A transactional audit outbox records what happened before asynchronous consumers update search, risk features and downstream systems.

A practical initial stack

For the proposed first production generation, use a managed relational database for strongly consistent identity and grant state, a bounded Redis-compatible cache for non-authoritative acceleration, a durable event log for fan-out and object storage for encrypted archives. Use a mature scheduler your team can operate. Kubernetes is appropriate only when its deployment and security costs are justified; managed containers can be simpler initially.

This is a recommendation for our design, not a statement that Okta uses this exact stack. PostgreSQL-like examples in the appendices express relational invariants without mandating a vendor. The important requirements are transactional uniqueness, predictable failover, point-in-time recovery, workload isolation and observable consistency.

Keep dangerous work off the hot path

Custom code, SCIM calls, document checks, bulk imports and report generation receive separate worker pools and quotas. Inline hooks are the exception: customers explicitly choose a synchronous dependency and its timeout policy. A long-running report cannot exhaust the database connections reserved for a token exchange. A connector cannot reach the cloud metadata service or a different tenant's private network.

The global control plane provisions cells, assigns tenants and signs configuration bundles. A cell serves from a validated local copy. Configuration has an expiry or renewal lease appropriate to its risk. Emergency deny events use a more urgent channel and an independently measured propagation objective; they do not wait for a nightly configuration export.

Security boundaries and service identity

Every service authenticates its peers and authorizes the requested operation. Possession of a network route is not authorization. Give the token signer a narrow interface accepting a validated issuance envelope rather than arbitrary bytes from any workload. Give a connector worker permission to retrieve only the secret for its tenant and connector instance, for a short time.

Separate production cloud accounts from corporate IT and from customer support. Separate deployment authorization from the ability to read customer data. Separate key administration from normal signing. When shared infrastructure is unavoidable, document the common-cause failure and test it.

A cell lifecycle

A new cell is provisioned from reviewed infrastructure code, bootstrapped with workload identities, populated with synthetic tenants, load-tested, exercised through failover and added to placement gradually. Tenant migration uses a versioned ownership record and a write fence; it is not just changing DNS while both cells accept writes. Draining preserves issuer URLs and signing continuity.

Acceptance tests. Disconnect the global console while a cell serves normal logins. Saturate workflow workers while measuring token p99. Send a valid tenant-A session to tenant-B's hostname. Attempt an old placement version after migration. Each test should produce a predictable outcome and an explanatory audit record.

06. Tenants, organizations, custom domains and residency

The first table is the tenant boundary

A tenant record contains immutable ID, lifecycle state, home region, cell placement, allowed residency envelope, configuration generation, billing reference and security policy defaults. A tenant's display name and domain are mutable aliases, never its primary key. Customer identities inside a B2B application belong to an organization within that tenant; a user can have different roles in multiple organizations.

Create a tenant through a durable workflow: reserve its identifier, allocate storage, install default policies, create a verified administrator bootstrap invitation, provision its default hostname and only then mark it active. Each step is idempotent. An abandoned signup remains non-serving until garbage collection verifies it has no customer data or outstanding ownership claim.

Domain ownership is a security operation

To add login.customer.example, issue a random ownership challenge and verify the required DNS record. Maintain a uniqueness constraint on canonical domain names across active tenants. Normalize internationalized names carefully, distinguish exact hostnames from suffix rules and reject ambiguous wildcard ownership. After verification, request a certificate and activate routing only after the certificate and tenant binding agree.

Domain deletion should remove routing, revoke the platform association and stop renewal, with a safe retention window to prevent stale references being reattached unexpectedly. Monitor certificate expiry, DNS drift and abandoned CNAMEs. Do not allow arbitrary forwarded-host headers to choose a tenant or issuer.

A custom domain can affect cookies, OIDC issuer checks, redirect allowlists and WebAuthn RP IDs. Treat domain changes as migrations with compatibility testing, not branding-only changes. A passkey registered for one relying-party scope is not automatically portable to a different domain. [P07]

Enforce isolation in every layer

Use composite tenant/object keys, tenant-qualified uniqueness and tenant-qualified foreign keys. Add database row-level enforcement where appropriate, but do not use it as a substitute for application authorization. Cache keys include tenant ID, object type, object ID and relevant version. Search queries require a server-injected tenant filter. Queue envelopes and object-store paths also carry the tenant boundary.

Encrypting each tenant's records with a different key can reduce exposure and support lifecycle controls, but encryption alone does not prevent an authorized service from returning another tenant's plaintext. Authorization and data routing remain necessary. Cross-tenant support tooling should require scoped, expiring access with customer-visible evidence.

Residency is a data-flow graph

A residency promise must name which data is constrained: directory attributes, secrets, session state, audit logs, backups, analytics, support attachments and operational telemetry. A regional database with worldwide logs is not an all-data regional deployment. Establish region-aware egress rules and document subprocessors and recovery locations.

For our design, each tenant has an allowed-region set. Placement, backup policies and connector execution check this set. Disaster recovery may only promote a location inside it unless an explicit customer agreement permits otherwise. Global routing metadata contains the minimum needed to find the tenant, not its entire profile directory.

Deletion and suspension

Suspension should distinguish commercial restrictions from security containment. A billing synchronization failure must not accidentally lock out administrators or prevent emergency offboarding. Security suspension increments a tenant security epoch and invalidates active capabilities at participating services.

Deletion is a staged operation: stop new processing, revoke credentials, terminate sessions, export permitted evidence, delete active records, expire derived indexes and apply retention rules to backups. Keep a narrowly scoped deletion journal so a restore cannot resurrect deleted accounts. A legal hold may preserve specific evidence while still preventing operational access.

Acceptance test. Restore yesterday's backup after a tenant was deleted today. The recovery process must replay the deletion journal before making the cell routable. Otherwise backup recovery becomes an account resurrection vulnerability.

07. Universal Directory, schemas and identity sourcing

One person, many source records

A universal directory is not just a users table. It reconciles HR identities, directory accounts, external federation subjects, application profiles, group memberships, devices and workload identities. Our model separates a stable principal from its source accounts and from its application-specific representation. Never use email as the universal immutable identity key.

A principal contains status, type, display attributes, lifecycle timestamps and security epoch. A source account contains connector ID, immutable source ID, observed version and provenance. Attribute values carry the source, transformation version, observed time and precedence rule. This makes a wrong department or manager explainable rather than mysterious.

Attribute mastering

Suppose HR is authoritative for employment status and manager, AD for login name, and the user for a preferred display name. Define authority per attribute, not a single global last-writer-wins rule. A lower-priority connector must not reactivate a terminated employee because it observed an older active=true value.

An update is evaluated against source ordering and lifecycle rules. Store both the raw observation and the resulting canonical change. Reject or quarantine invalid enum values, oversized arrays and forbidden changes to immutable identifiers. Transformations run with a constrained expression language, not arbitrary network-enabled code on a directory transaction.

For conflicting identities, use a review queue and an explicit merge plan. A merge preserves provenance, aliases, audit history and app-account correlations. It must not silently combine authentication credentials from two different people because they once shared a recycled email address.

Schema evolution

Schemas are tenant-scoped, versioned and typed. Core security fields are reserved; extensions live in namespaces. Distinguish missing, explicit null and empty values. New optional fields can be added without rewriting every row. Breaking type changes require a migration with validation statistics, rollback rules and downstream connector impact analysis.

Index common lookup keys and selected searchable attributes. Do not let arbitrary customer attributes create unlimited indexes or unbounded regular-expression queries. Offer bounded pagination and explicit filter capabilities. Use a search replica for discovery, but perform security-sensitive reads against authoritative state or a proven freshness boundary.

Groups and membership

Support static groups, source-managed groups and rule-derived groups. Derived membership needs dependency tracking: a change to department can invalidate affected rules, not trigger a full-directory scan for every user. Large groups require paged membership storage and incremental evaluation. Nested groups need cycle detection and bounded traversal.

Membership versions matter. A policy decision can record the group revision it used. Revoking a privileged group should propagate with a stronger freshness requirement than updating a directory display label. Storing a huge flattened group list in a long-lived token creates stale access and token-size problems; resource-specific authorization or short-lived claims can be safer.

APIs and operational behavior

Proposed endpoints include POST /v1/principals, PATCH /v1/principals/{id}, POST /v1/source-observations and GET /v1/principals/{id}/provenance. Administrative writes accept an expected version to avoid silently overwriting a concurrent change. Bulk imports create job records with checkpoints, rejected-row reports and rate limits.

The critical metrics are source lag, rejected updates, unresolved identity collisions, membership recomputation lag and deactivation propagation. A directory can have excellent HTTP availability while being operationally wrong because its HR feed stopped yesterday.

Worked example. HR marks an employee terminated at source version 900. AD later sends version 420 with an active flag. The canonical lifecycle rule rejects reactivation, records the observation and leaves the employee disabled. The connector's old data remains useful for diagnosis without overriding the higher-authority termination.

08. Single sign-on and the browser session model

SSO is reuse of trust, not reuse of a password

Single sign-on lets an application rely on an authentication performed by an identity provider. The application still creates and owns its local session. Our platform therefore manages an IdP session and federation transactions, while each relying application manages its own session, cookie lifetime and resource authorization.

Imagine Maya opens payroll, signs in with a passkey and then opens the expense application. Payroll redirects to the IdP. After successful authentication, payroll validates an assertion and creates its cookie. Expenses later redirects to the same IdP, which may reuse its existing session if policy permits. Expenses receives a fresh assertion intended for expenses, not payroll's cookie.

Three checks before reusing an IdP session

Our serving logic checks the session's validity, the requested application's policy and the freshness of the authentication evidence. A session authenticated yesterday may be sufficient for an internal wiki but insufficient for changing a bank account. Store authentication time, methods, assurance properties, device binding and last sensitive reauthentication; do not reduce all of them to one is_authenticated boolean.

The session record uses a random opaque identifier with only a hash retained where practical. The cookie is Secure and HttpOnly, has an intentional SameSite setting and is scoped as narrowly as the supported flows allow. CSRF defenses protect state-changing account and administrative operations. Rotate session identifiers after authentication and privilege elevation to prevent fixation.

Different protocol response modes have different cross-site navigation behavior. Test redirect-based OIDC, SAML form POSTs and logout across supported browsers, rather than assuming third-party cookies will always work. For our customer SDK, prefer top-level redirect and a backend-for-frontend session where suitable; never place durable refresh tokens in URLs or ordinary application logs.

Application launch and home discovery

The end-user dashboard shows only currently assigned applications and renders deep links from validated configuration. Launching an app does not itself grant access. An application can initiate login independently, and its request still passes policy. A malicious returnTo parameter must not turn the dashboard into an open redirect.

Home-realm discovery selects an upstream provider using explicit organization context, verified domain rules or a user choice. Avoid exposing a directory of valid employee addresses through different error messages. Email-domain discovery is a routing hint, not proof that the visitor belongs to that company.

Step-up and session refresh

An app can require recent, stronger evidence. The IdP evaluates the request against the tenant's policy and produces a new assertion only after the needed proof. Our session may retain multiple evidence records with separate timestamps, so adding a passkey does not falsely make an old device posture report fresh.

Sliding expiry improves convenience but needs an absolute maximum lifetime. Device loss, administrator disablement and compromised credentials can revoke a session before that maximum. Resource applications that validate tokens offline will not automatically learn about the change; Chapter 13 makes that limitation explicit.

Logout is a distributed workflow

RP-initiated logout defines an application-to-provider logout interaction and registered post-logout redirection behavior. It is not proof that every other relying application has terminated its own session. [P27]

Our UI distinguishes 'sign out of this app', 'sign out of the identity provider' and 'end supported sessions everywhere'. The last action creates a revocation job with delivery status per app. A failed remote logout is visible rather than hidden behind a reassuring green check.

Acceptance tests. Confirm that an app cannot replay another app's assertion, a stale privileged session triggers step-up, an unassigned app cannot obtain a grant through a direct URL, and logout status honestly reflects downstream failures. Record SSO success separately from credential-entry success: a working login page does not guarantee a working federation integration.

09. OAuth: build the authorization server

Responsibilities and security baseline

OAuth separates a client requesting access, a resource owner, an authorization server issuing credentials and a resource server enforcing access. Our first supported interactive flow is Authorization Code with PKCE. The original framework, PKCE extension and current OAuth security BCP are the normative anchors; do not ship a server by copying old implicit-flow examples. [P01] [P04] [P02]

Our server implements discovery, authorization, token, revocation, introspection and key-publication endpoints through separate validated request types. A client registry stores client ID, public or confidential type, registered redirects, allowed grants, permitted resources, credentials, owner, tenant and policy. A public client is not secured by embedding a secret in downloadable software.

The authorization transaction

At /authorize, parse and normalize only according to the protocol's exact rules. Load the client within the verified tenant. Validate the requested response mode, redirect, scopes and resource before using the redirect for an error response. An invalid unregistered redirect produces a local error page, not a redirect to attacker-controlled content.

Create an authorization transaction containing a random handle, tenant, client, registered redirect reference, requested scopes, target audience, PKCE challenge and method, policy revision, creation time and expiry. Protect browser correlation and consent state against CSRF. Store only the minimum needed to resume the transaction; never trust hidden form fields as the authoritative source of grant parameters.

Authentication and consent occur against this immutable request context. A malicious tab cannot swap a low-risk resource for a high-risk resource after the user approves. The consent screen uses registered, reviewed application metadata and clearly states the requested access. Organization-wide administrator approval and individual consent are different grant sources in our model.

Code issuance and redemption

After policy succeeds, create a short-lived opaque code with high entropy and store its hash, not the raw code. The code record points to the grant context and is marked unused. Our design gives codes a deliberately small validity window, configurable within supported interoperability constraints.

At /token, validate client authentication when required, redirect binding, PKCE proof and code expiry. Then atomically reserve the code for one issuance operation. Two racing workers must not both produce committed grants. Invalid proofs should not consume a legitimate code in a way that creates a trivial denial of service, although repeated failures are rate-limited and audited.

Token generation crosses a transaction boundary when a remote signer is involved. One approach is an issuance record with states RESERVED, SIGNED, COMMITTED and FAILED, keyed by a unique issuance ID. A worker can recover after signing without creating a second grant. Tokens become externally usable only under the committed issuance policy. Avoid holding a hot database row lock across an unbounded HSM call.

A simpler initial design performs bounded signing before a short atomic commit and discards any token from a failed commit. The server must never return the discarded token. Choose one model and prove its crash behavior; do not casually mix both.

Access tokens and resource boundaries

For our API, the token describes issuer, subject, audience, permissions, expiry and grant identity. A resource server verifies the intended issuer and audience, not just the signature. A request for the payroll API cannot use a token minted for the photo API. Scopes express delegated permission but usually do not replace object-level checks such as ownership of a particular invoice.

Use opaque access tokens with introspection where immediate centralized status checks matter, or short-lived JWTs where local verification and availability matter more. Neither option is universally superior. Opaque tokens shift load and availability dependency to introspection; JWTs introduce a revocation delay unless resources participate in an additional mechanism.

Non-interactive clients and operational controls

Client credentials represent the application's own authority, not a human user's consent. Give every service a distinct client identity, owner and resource policy. Prefer asymmetric client authentication when the environment supports secure key custody; rotate keys with overlap and audit every use. A shared client secret across many services makes attribution and revocation difficult.

Token requests get per-client and per-tenant rate limits, bounded input sizes and uniform error semantics. Observe denied grants, invalid proofs, signer latency, code replay and refresh-family reuse separately. Redact secrets, codes and tokens before telemetry leaves the request process.

Production gate. Run standards conformance, parallel redemption, redirect fuzzing, issuer-confusion, malformed JOSE, clock-skew and crash-recovery tests. Correctness is a property of the full transaction, not just the happy-path HTTP exchange.

10. OpenID Connect: authentication on top of OAuth

What OIDC adds

OIDC adds an authentication layer, including an ID token and standardized identity claims, on top of OAuth. An ID token is intended for the client; an access token is intended for a resource server. Their validators and uses must remain distinct. OIDC Core defines issuer, audience, nonce and authentication-related claim semantics. [P03]

In our platform, an OIDC provider is a tenant-scoped issuer with a discovery document, signing keys, supported capabilities and policy. A customer application configures its exact issuer and client ID. It must not dynamically trust any issuer or key URL supplied by an untrusted token.

Building the relying-party experience

Provide supported SDKs that create a transaction-bound state value and nonce, perform PKCE, exchange the returned code on the correct channel and validate the ID token before creating an application session. Keep the application session opaque and server-controlled where practical. The SDK should reject suspicious callbacks rather than exposing raw parsing errors or tokens to browser analytics.

A backend-for-frontend reduces browser token exposure but does not remove CSRF, session theft or backend security requirements. A pure browser app needs a deliberately reviewed token storage and refresh strategy. Native applications should use the external user-agent patterns described for native OAuth rather than collecting enterprise passwords inside arbitrary embedded views. [P24]

Subject identifiers and account linking

Our internal principal ID is not automatically the public OIDC subject. A stable public subject can be scoped to an issuer, and pairwise subject identifiers can reduce correlation across clients. Store the mapping as durable identity data. Do not regenerate a subject when the user's email or display name changes.

At federation boundaries, the account key is the trusted issuer plus its subject identifier. Email can be an attribute and a discovery hint, but it is not sufficient to merge accounts. Require an explicit linking ceremony or a narrowly defined enterprise policy with adequate provider assurance. An attacker controlling a different provider should not gain access by asserting the victim's email string.

Claims as a data contract

Expose only claims required by the application and allowed by policy. Claims have schemas, maximum sizes and clear sources. Custom claims use controlled namespaces and cannot overwrite reserved protocol fields. An application expecting a string role list must not receive an unbounded nested object because a tenant edited a profile mapping.

Keep high-churn authorization state out of long-lived ID tokens. A user's group membership at authentication time is not guaranteed to remain current for the lifetime of an application session. For privileged authorization, query a fresh permission decision or use a bounded session re-evaluation mechanism.

The UserInfo endpoint is an authenticated resource with its own scope and subject consistency checks. It is not a public directory lookup. Cache profile responses carefully so one user's details are not reused for another session.

Signing keys and configuration changes

Publish a JWKS containing public keys only. Clients cache it but need a safe refresh path during rotation. Unknown key IDs should trigger a bounded, coalesced refresh, not one unbounded network request per attacker-supplied token. Keep retired verification keys available long enough for valid outstanding credentials, unless emergency compromise requires a more disruptive response.

Issuer URL changes are migrations, not cosmetic edits. Update application configuration, discovery, redirects and federation dependencies deliberately. A transparent hostname alias can still break exact issuer matching. Test custom-domain behavior and both old and new clients before cutover.

Okta-specific distinction

Okta distinguishes the organization authorization server from custom authorization servers: the former issues tokens for Okta's own resource context, while custom servers support customer-defined API authorization policies. Do not treat an org-server access token as a stable contract for an unrelated custom API. [O18]

Acceptance tests. Supply a valid token for another client, another issuer and another resource; each must fail in the relevant validator. Change email without changing subject. Rotate keys while clients have cached JWKS. Verify that a successful OAuth grant without the expected OIDC authentication context cannot silently create an application login.

11. SAML 2.0 and legacy federation

Why an old protocol remains a first-class product

An enterprise identity platform needs to connect applications with very different federation capabilities. SAML expresses authentication assertions and associated protocol messages in XML. The SAML Core and Profiles documents define the underlying structures and browser SSO behaviors. Our implementation should rely on mature, reviewed XML-signature and SAML libraries rather than custom cryptographic parsing. [P05] [P06]

Model the integration, not just a certificate

A SAML connection stores tenant, IdP or service-provider role, entity IDs, allowed assertion consumer endpoints, signing and encryption preferences, trusted keys, NameID mapping, attribute mappings and allowed initiation modes. Metadata imports are parsed in an isolated, size-bounded environment. Remote metadata refreshes follow SSRF protections and never replace trusted keys without a controlled trust policy.

For SP-initiated SSO, the application creates an authentication request and tracks its request ID. The IdP validates the connection, authenticates the user under policy and returns an assertion to the configured consumer. The SP validates the assertion before creating its own session. RelayState is application state to correlate safely, not a permission to redirect anywhere.

Signature verification must match the data consumed

The parser disables external entities and dangerous XML features. The validator applies a strict structural profile, validates trusted signatures and consumes the exact signed assertion or response element it validated. Merely finding any valid signature somewhere in an XML document is insufficient. Duplicate IDs, ambiguous structures and unsupported algorithms are rejected.

Our adapter produces a typed internal assertion object only after validation. Downstream application logic never reparses the original XML independently, because that can make the application consume a different element from the one the security library validated. This separation is a key defense against signature-wrapping classes of mistakes.

Recipient, destination, audience, timing and request correlation are validated against the stored integration and transaction. Use a bounded clock-skew allowance. Store assertion IDs in a replay ledger with an expiry that covers their acceptance window. A second worker receiving the same assertion must not create another authentication event.

IdP-initiated SSO and compatibility

Some applications need IdP-initiated launch. It lacks the same prior request correlation as SP-initiated SSO, so our default is to enable it only per reviewed integration. Restrict landing destinations, require app assignment, shorten assertion validity and document the weaker transaction-binding assumptions. Do not quietly enable every compatibility switch to make a troublesome integration pass.

Implement per-application attribute mapping with explicit schemas and preview tools. An employee number might be the target application's username; an email address might not be. Capture mapping versions in diagnostic events without exposing assertion contents broadly. Support certificate overlap and planned rotation with an integration test before old-key retirement.

WS-Federation and secure web authentication

A legacy WS-Fed adapter should translate only validated protocol results into the common internal authentication context. Its token types, request parsing and validation remain protocol-specific; do not send XML tokens through an OIDC JWT validator. Limit the adapter's attack surface and maintain a clear support matrix.

Secure Web Authentication, where a browser extension fills an application's password form, is a different mechanism from federation. It retains a password dependency and needs an encrypted credential vault, trusted extension distribution, strict origin matching and robust handling of page changes. It does not inherit the phishing resistance of a passkey just because the user launched it from an SSO dashboard.

Diagnostic experience

Build a safe 'test integration' flow showing validation stages, expected audiences, certificate fingerprints and sanitized claim names. Never ask customers to paste live SAML assertions into unrestricted support tickets. Provide short-lived, tenant-scoped debug access and automatic secret redaction.

Acceptance tests. Rotate certificates, replay a response, alter an unsigned field, add duplicate IDs, send an assertion to the wrong consumer and submit a response after its allowed time. Compatibility testing should include multiple SP implementations and both redirect and POST transport behavior where supported.

One authorization system, multiple client environments

API access management adds resource registration, scopes, claim policies, grants, consent, client authentication and enforcement guidance. In our design, an API resource has a stable audience ID, owner, permission schema, token format and revocation requirements. A scope definition includes a description, grant eligibility and whether additional consent or authentication is needed.

The authorization server must reject privilege expansion at every exchange. A requested scope is an upper bound on what the client asks for, not proof it should receive it. Effective permission is the intersection of client eligibility, user authority, resource policy and the approved delegation.

Design a token-exchange policy explicitly

Our exchange request identifies the presented subject credential, the requesting client and the intended resource. The server validates the subject credential in its own trust domain, authenticates the requesting client and evaluates whether that client may act in the claimed relationship. A new token records the relevant actor chain without allowing it to grow without bound.

Do not implement exchange as 'any valid token can become any other token'. Maintain a graph of permitted trust edges and a maximum delegation depth. Sender-constrained token handling

Our DPoP implementation maintains a bounded replay cache and verifies a proof in the context of the exact protected request. Load balancers need a trusted canonical external URL and method representation; otherwise proxy rewriting can make correct binding ambiguous. A successful proof verifies key possession, not the safety of the software holding the key.

For mutual TLS, the edge-to-service contract must carry the verified client certificate identity through an authenticated channel. Arbitrary forwarded certificate headers are not trusted. Certificate renewal requires an overlap strategy and clear rules for tokens bound to the old key. Sender constraint also does not rescue a resource server that ignores audience or permission checks.

Device and asynchronous flows

Rate-limit device-code creation and polling separately. Bind the human approval screen to a recognizable device or action, and avoid teaching users to approve arbitrary codes delivered by strangers. Our device approval record is single-use, expires promptly and stores the requesting client and resource.

CIBA-style flows move interaction to another channel. The approval must clearly identify the request and be associated with the intended user; it cannot be a generic push that authorizes an unspecified future action. High-risk business actions should also use a transaction-specific application approval object, rather than assuming authentication consent alone approves a financial operation.

Product boundaries

High-assurance financial profiles may impose a specific combination of OAuth mechanisms, algorithms and certification requirements. Label those profiles separately from ordinary OIDC support. Our company should not market 'FAPI compliant' merely because PAR and signed JWTs exist. Build a version-pinned conformance project and obtain the relevant evidence.

Acceptance tests. Attempt exchange across an unauthorized trust edge, replay a sender proof, rotate the client certificate mid-session, poll too quickly and approve an expired backchannel request. Each grant family gets independent policy tests and operational dashboards.

13. Tokens, sessions, revocation and key rotation

Model credentials by purpose

Our data model separates IdP sessions, OAuth grants, access tokens, refresh-token families, SAML assertions, recovery tokens and enrollment challenges. They have different audiences and replay rules. A generic tokens table with an ambiguous type field invites one credential to be accepted in the wrong context.

For JWTs, use explicit validation profiles with trusted algorithms, keys, issuers, audiences and token types. JWT best-current-practice guidance addresses algorithm and cross-context confusion; a successful signature alone is not sufficient validation. [P12]

Refresh rotation as a state machine

Store a refresh family with client, principal, resource, initial authentication, absolute expiry and revocation state. Each refresh token is a random secret whose hash maps to one generation. A successful refresh atomically marks generation N consumed and creates generation N+1. Concurrent workers cannot both advance the same generation.

A reused old generation may indicate theft, but it can also arise when the first response was lost and a legitimate client retries. Choose a documented policy. A strict policy revokes the family on reuse and accepts the resulting reauthentication cost. A retry-tolerant policy needs bounded recovery of the same issuance result, bound to the same authenticated client or sender key, rather than minting a new branch.

For our high-risk profile, use strict rotation. For selected managed clients, allow a short issuance-recovery window with a request ID and proof binding. Encrypt any briefly retained response material and delete it at window expiry. Do not call an unrestricted grace period 'replay detection'.

Revocation is not one operation

Revocation can target a credential, grant, session, device, principal or entire tenant. A principal security epoch invalidates capabilities that reference an older epoch at enforcement points that check it. A derived-grant graph lets disabling an agent or user invalidate its refresh families and token-vault access.

The OAuth revocation endpoint standardizes a revocation request; introspection provides a way to ask about token state. Neither makes an already-issued JWT disappear from every independent resource server. [P17] [P16]

Our contract must state the enforcement boundary. Locally verified JWT access tokens may remain usable until expiry unless resources check a revocation feed, epoch cache or introspection service. Applications also maintain their own cookies. Therefore 'disable user' has several timestamps: directory commit, new-login denial, local refresh denial, event delivery and each resource's actual enforcement.

Measure those timestamps and expose incomplete downstream actions. A successful queue insertion is not proof that access has ended. For a critical system, require short-lived access, fresh authorization checks and an application-specific session termination mechanism.

Planned key rotation

A signing key has states generated, published, active, retiring and retired. Publish a new verification key before issuing tokens with it. Switch issuance after propagation, retain the previous key through the longest supported validation window and then retire it. Tenant-specific key rings reduce compromise scope but increase operational and customer-integration complexity.

Emergency compromise is different. Stop using the affected key, identify its issuance window, revoke associated grants where needed and notify customers that offline validation may continue accepting old signatures until their trust configuration changes. A signing-key rotation alone is not universal session revocation.

Storage and logs

Never store raw bearer tokens in searchable audit logs. Use stable internal grant IDs and privacy-preserving token fingerprints for correlation. Restrict any necessary encrypted secret storage to the service that needs to redeem the secret, and separate key administration from normal reads.

Acceptance tests. Lose a refresh response, retry it on two nodes, restore a database snapshot with old consumed tokens, rotate keys while JWKS caches are stale and revoke a user whose app validates JWTs offline. The test report must describe residual access honestly, not just mark the IdP operation successful.

14. Identity Engine: adaptive authentication as a state machine

From a fixed login form to a policy-driven interaction

An identity engine decides which steps are needed for a particular transaction. A user might need identification, primary authentication, authenticator enrollment, device verification, consent or stronger proof. Okta's Interaction Code model exposes an interaction and remediation process before a one-time code exchange; its implementation guide requires PKCE and defines client authentication requirements. [O16] [O17]

Our proposed analogue keeps a server-owned transaction with a monotonically increasing version. The client receives allowed next actions and display information, not a license to invent its own state transition. A malicious client cannot skip enrollment simply by posting authenticated=true.

A concrete transaction record

Store transaction ID hash, tenant, client, principal when known, policy version, required assurance, completed evidence, allowed actions, attempt counters, expiry and last transition. Keep sensitive challenge material separately. The browser receives an opaque handle and a version-bound action token. A transition uses compare-and-swap so simultaneous tabs cannot fork one authentication transaction.

An evidence record says what was proved, by which authenticator, when, for what challenge and with which verified device context. Policy consumes evidence properties, not marketing names. For example, 'phishing-resistant and user-verified within five minutes' is more precise than 'MFA completed'.

Policy composition

Separate global session policy, application sign-in policy, authenticator enrollment policy, account-management policy and resource authorization. Define precedence and conflict semantics. In our design, mandatory security constraints cannot be relaxed by a lower-priority app rule. Business rules can choose among permitted methods, but cannot authorize a banned authenticator.

Compile policies into a typed, bounded decision representation. Reject invalid references, contradictory conditions and unbounded expressions before publication. Produce an explanation trace naming the matched rules and unmet requirements without leaking hidden risk-detection logic to an attacker.

A policy update gets a new immutable version. Ordinary in-flight transactions can finish under a bounded snapshot when safe; emergency deny changes are rechecked before credential issuance. Otherwise a user disabled halfway through authentication might still receive a new grant from an old snapshot.

Risk and remediation

Risk inputs include network context, device posture, session history and verified external signals. They have timestamps and provenance. Missing evidence is not silently equivalent to safe evidence. Policy defines whether an unavailable risk service triggers step-up, denies a privileged action or permits a low-risk cached path.

Avoid a remediation loop where the system demands a factor the user is not allowed to enroll. The policy compiler can detect some loops; runtime instrumentation catches the rest. Provide a safe recovery path that does not weaken the original assurance requirement merely because the user is stuck.

UX and accessibility

Hosted login and embedded SDKs should use the same server-side policy decisions. Accessible forms, keyboard navigation, localization, explicit cancellation and meaningful retry messages are part of the security design: confusing users increases mistaken approvals and support bypasses. Rate-limiting responses should avoid revealing whether an account exists.

Acceptance tests. Replay an action from an earlier transaction version, complete an old challenge after policy changes, disable the principal just before issuance and open two factor-selection tabs simultaneously. Verify both state correctness and clear user-facing recovery behavior. The interaction engine's availability SLI includes terminal successful outcomes, not merely rendering its first screen.

15. Passwords, delegated authentication and migration

Password verification is an isolated security workload

Passwords remain necessary for many migrations and compatibility scenarios. Our platform stores a salted, versioned password verifier, never a recoverable password, unless a separate legacy password-vault feature explicitly requires encryption for replay. Those two cases must use different storage and access paths.

Argon2id is a candidate for our general-purpose password verifier. Benchmark its memory, CPU and latency costs on production hardware and under attack load. RFC 9106 supplies the algorithm and parameter guidance; regulated deployments may require a different approved cryptographic configuration. Do not invent a fast hash or assume one parameter set is right for every fleet. [P19]

Admission control before expensive work

A memory-hard verifier can be attacked as a resource-exhaustion target. Apply network, account and tenant-aware admission limits before scheduling verification. Keep enough capacity reserved for legitimate customers and administrator recovery. A global queue with no tenant fairness lets one attacked customer degrade all others.

Store algorithm ID, parameters, salt, verifier and creation version with the credential. A successful login can upgrade an old verifier to a stronger approved format through a compare-and-swap write. Failed logins must not trigger expensive rehashing. A pepper, when used, is a separately controlled secret with an explicit loss and rotation plan; it is not a replacement for salts or strong hashing.

Password policy and user experience

Use length, compromised-password screening, throttling and secure recovery rather than arbitrary complexity theater. Current NIST guidance distinguishes single-factor and MFA password contexts, discourages arbitrary periodic changes absent compromise, and emphasizes appropriate verification controls. Apply the exact requirements of the assurance profile being claimed. [P20]

Our UI allows password managers and paste. Error timing and wording minimize account enumeration without obscuring operational errors from authenticated administrators. Avoid permanent account lockouts that any outsider can repeatedly trigger. Prefer progressive delays, risk-based challenges and recovery controls with documented tradeoffs.

Delegated authentication

Some enterprises keep AD or LDAP authoritative for passwords. Our cloud sends a bounded authentication job to a customer-installed outbound agent; the agent verifies against the directory and returns an authenticated result tied to the job. The password is not logged or retained, and transport is protected end to end through the trusted agent channel.

This creates a real availability dependency on the customer's agent fleet and directory. A cloud IdP can be healthy while delegated login fails. Expose agent health, queue delay and directory response separately. Do not silently fall back to an outdated cloud password unless the customer has explicitly chosen and secured that behavior.

Migration without an unsafe big bang

A migration plan maps immutable users, supported hash formats, recovery state and app identifiers before cutover. A just-in-time password migration can verify against the old system once, then create the new verifier after successful proof. Prevent a compromised old endpoint from linking an attacker to a different new principal by binding the migration result to the expected immutable account.

Bulk hash imports need signed manifests, access controls, encrypted transfer, algorithm compatibility validation and deletion evidence for staging files. Some hashes cannot be securely or compatibly migrated; require reset or continued delegated authentication rather than pretending every hash is portable.

Acceptance tests. Saturate the password pool, verify fair admission, simulate a dead directory agent, upgrade a verifier concurrently with a password change and restore an old credential snapshot after a reset. The security epoch and current credential version must prevent resurrection of the old password.

16. Multi-factor authentication: factors, orchestration and abuse

Count independent proof, not screens

An MFA system manages enrollment, challenge issuance, verification, revocation and recovery across authenticator types. Two prompts are not automatically two independent factors. The assurance decision depends on what is actually proved and how the methods can be attacked. Our policy model records method properties and verified context, rather than granting equal strength to SMS, push and hardware-backed public-key authentication.

TOTP uses a time-based moving factor and a shared secret. A server must protect that secret, accept only its configured time window and prevent reuse of an accepted code within the relevant window. RFC 6238 is the protocol anchor, not a complete enrollment or recovery design. [P18]

Factor records and lifecycle

A factor record contains principal, type, enrollment status, key or encrypted secret reference, verified ownership, device association, creation time, last use and revocation state. Enrollment progresses from pending to proved to active; pending factors cannot satisfy sign-in requirements. Enrolling a new factor is itself a sensitive action requiring adequate existing proof or an approved recovery process.

For TOTP, show a provisioning secret only in a protected, short-lived enrollment flow and require a successful test code. Store the secret encrypted with restricted decrypt permissions. Clock drift handling must be bounded and observable. Hashing the secret is not sufficient because the verifier needs it to compute expected codes.

SMS, email and voice

These are delivery channels with external dependencies and different assurance limits. Our implementation uses provider abstraction, delivery receipts, spend caps, geography controls and abuse detection. Switching providers improves resilience but does not prove that the recipient actually owns the intended identity or that the channel is phishing-resistant.

A code record is bound to the authentication transaction, recipient, purpose and expiry. Codes for password recovery cannot be reused for sign-in or enrollment. Store a keyed verifier rather than the raw code where possible; short numeric codes need online attempt limits because their entropy is small.

Do not declare delivery success when an SMS provider merely accepted the API request. Observe provider acceptance, delivery feedback and user verification independently. Avoid leaking the full phone number or email address on unauthenticated screens.

Push approval

A push notification wakes a registered authenticator; it is not itself a trusted approval. Our app retrieves a challenge over an authenticated channel, displays the verified origin and action, and returns a signed response bound to the transaction. Add number matching or equivalent interaction for supported push scenarios, limit repeated prompts and surface 'this was not me' as a security signal.

An attacker can still socially engineer users into approving a real challenge. For privileged paths, require a phishing-resistant method rather than assuming a more polished push screen solves the problem. Notification-provider outages should not block an independently usable public-key authenticator.

Product release boundaries

Okta's public authenticator documentation distinguishes phishing-resistant methods, while its September release notes label the new NFC authenticator path as self-service EA in Preview. Our implementation plan keeps hardware transport support separate from the assurance properties of the underlying credential. [O22] [O10]

Acceptance tests. Attempt code replay, enroll a factor during a stolen low-assurance session, flood push requests, spoof a delivery callback and lose the notification provider. Measure challenge latency, verification success, delivery failure, enrollment abandonment and fraudulent-approval reports by factor type. A single aggregate MFA success metric hides both security and usability problems.

17. WebAuthn, FIDO2 and passkeys

The server verifies public-key proof

WebAuthn lets a browser mediate creation and use of scoped public-key credentials. The server stores a public key and credential metadata, not the user's fingerprint or face template. WebAuthn Level 3 is a W3C Recommendation at this book's cutoff. The complete normative verification algorithms remain the implementation authority; use a maintained library and conformance tests. [P07] [P08]

Our platform acts as a relying party for its hosted login domain. A credential belongs to that relying-party scope. Passkeys may be synced through a platform ecosystem or bound to an individual authenticator; policy must distinguish acceptable properties without claiming that every passkey is hardware-bound.

Registration design

Begin only after the user has sufficient authority to enroll a credential. Create a cryptographically random, expiring challenge bound to tenant, principal, session, RP configuration and intended operation. Store it in a single-use challenge ledger. Choose opaque user handles and avoid exposing unnecessary personal information in authenticator metadata.

The browser requests credential creation and returns client and authenticator data. Validate the expected challenge, operation type, origin, RP binding, user-presence and required user-verification properties, supported algorithms and applicable attestation policy. Store only a successfully validated credential. Authenticator attestation is a separate trust and privacy decision, not universally mandatory. [P07]

Our credential record contains tenant, principal, credential ID, public key, RP ID, supported transports, backup-related properties, enrollment evidence and status. The credential ID uniqueness rule is scoped to the serving RP model and checked against existing mappings. Store attestation evidence only when the policy actually needs it and retention is justified.

Authentication design

A login challenge is bound to the current interaction and required assurance. For a discoverable credential flow, map the returned credential and user handle to a principal only after the proof is validated. For an identified-user flow, constrain eligible credentials to the expected account. Avoid revealing whether a particular user has registered a passkey through a public API.

The verifier returns a typed evidence object, not just true or false: principal, credential, verified user presence, verified user verification, relevant authenticator properties and verification time. The interaction engine decides whether that evidence meets the application's policy. A successful credential signature does not itself grant application access.

Concurrency and replay

Challenge consumption and authentication completion need an atomic boundary. Two workers can validate the same cryptographic proof, but only one may complete the associated transaction. If the browser retries after a lost response, return a safe transaction status or restart; do not create independent sessions from the same one-time challenge.

Authenticator signature counters can be useful signals, but non-increasing or zero counters are not universally definitive evidence of cloning, especially with synced credentials. Apply the specification's semantics and credential context rather than a simplistic rule that rejects every counter that did not increase. [P07]

RP IDs, custom domains and migrations

Choose RP scope before launching broadly. A customer moving from a vendor hostname to a custom hostname may need a new credential enrollment strategy. Related-origin functionality and browser support require explicit validation; they are not a universal workaround for domain ownership. Maintain a tested browser, platform and authenticator compatibility matrix.

Our policy can prefer device-bound credentials for privileged administrators and allow synced passkeys for lower-friction customer login. This is a risk choice, not a claim that synced passkeys are inherently unsafe. Plan recovery that does not reduce a strong passkey deployment to an easily phished email reset.

Operational tests

Test cancellation, duplicate registration, expired challenges, wrong origins, wrong RP IDs, authenticator loss, user-verification failure, unsupported algorithms and multi-device behavior. Use physical authenticators and actual browser combinations in addition to mocks. Track registration completion, authentication completion, unsupported-device rates and recovery demand.

Critical invariant. No biometric template is required in our server design. The server needs verified cryptographic evidence from an authenticator, not a database of users' physical traits. Keep diagnostics and support language aligned with that architecture.

18. FastPass, Okta Verify and a device-bound analogue

Public capability and our implementation boundary

Okta describes FastPass as passwordless, phishing-resistant authentication using Okta Verify and device context. Its current overview lists support across Android, iOS, Linux, macOS and Windows, with capability details depending on platform. The proprietary internal implementation is not fully public. The following is a proposed device-bound architecture, not a reverse-engineered description of Okta Verify. [O20]

Establish device trust at enrollment

A desktop or mobile client generates a key in the strongest available protected key facility under the selected policy. Enrollment binds the public key to a principal and device record after an adequately authenticated ceremony. Device identity is not just a serial number supplied by an untrusted app.

Where platform attestation is available and appropriate, validate its chain, freshness and stated properties. Where it is unavailable, label the assurance honestly. A software-protected key may be acceptable for some users but should not be presented as equivalent to hardware-backed protection.

The device record stores enrollment version, key reference, owner, management state, allowed authenticator functions, last trustworthy posture observation and revocation status. Reinstalling the app or replacing hardware should create a reviewed lifecycle transition, not silently inherit trust from a copied device label.

Challenge routing

The browser starts an authentication transaction. A platform-specific broker or registered application channel wakes the authenticator. The authenticator retrieves the challenge directly from the trusted service and verifies the intended origin, application and transaction. It obtains any required local user verification and signs the complete challenge context.

The server validates the signature, device status, challenge expiry and evidence requirements, then atomically completes the interaction. Local IPC, loopback listeners and custom URI handlers are sensitive surfaces: authenticate callers where possible, use random transaction secrets, enforce allowed origins and reject unsolicited messages. A native application installed on the same device must not be able to ask the authenticator to sign an unrelated privileged login.

Posture is evidence with a shelf life

Disk encryption, screen lock, OS version, endpoint protection and management enrollment are observations, not permanent truths. Give each signal a source, timestamp, validity duration and confidence. A self-reported 'healthy' flag from an untrusted process should not carry the same weight as a verified managed-device assertion.

Separate posture collection from the latency-critical proof where practical. Cache signed observations with short policy-appropriate validity. When a required posture provider is unavailable, policy chooses a stronger challenge or denial rather than silently treating missing data as healthy.

Client engineering is a product discipline

Desktop and mobile clients need signed builds, secure auto-update, staged rollout, rollback, crash reporting with redaction, accessibility and platform-specific compatibility testing. They also need tamper and downgrade defenses appropriate to the threat model. A server-only team underestimates the work required for a reliable endpoint agent.

Support teams need a diagnostic bundle that reports versions, connectivity and sanitized state without private keys or live session tokens. Remote support should never require exporting the device's signing key. Device revocation must invalidate new authentication immediately at the platform's verification boundary, while offline use remains separately bounded.

Acceptance tests. Attempt a challenge from a malicious local application, replay a proof from another transaction, downgrade the client, clone a software device record and revoke the device during authentication. Verify that policy distinguishes unavailable posture from failed posture and that both outcomes remain explainable to administrators.

19. Device Access, desktop MFA and offline security

Desktop login is not browser login

Okta Device Access includes desktop-related capabilities such as Desktop MFA and platform-specific password synchronization or platform SSO integrations. These have different deployment and recovery constraints from web authentication. [O21]

Our proposed endpoint product integrates with supported operating-system login extension points and a managed local agent. Its responsibilities include secure enrollment, local policy enforcement, online proof, bounded offline behavior, updates and recovery. It must survive boot-time networking gaps and cannot assume a web browser session is already available.

Design offline behavior first

A disconnected laptop cannot receive an instantaneous cloud revocation. This is a physical limitation, not a bug solved by adding another queue. Define an offline lease: how long a previously trusted device may authenticate locally, which factors are accepted and which actions are prohibited. Privileged devices may require a shorter lease or no offline access.

Store offline verification material in protected local storage with anti-rollback measures appropriate to the platform. Avoid caching reusable cloud passwords. Bind local state to the device and principal, and consider what a disk-image restore or clock rollback would do. A wall-clock expiry alone is weak when an attacker controls the local clock.

Reconnection reconciles revocation, policy versions and audit events before extending the lease. A revoked device should not obtain a new lease just because it has a valid-looking old local session. Remote wipe, device management and identity revocation are separate controls with different reliability and permissions.

Password synchronization and platform SSO

Password synchronization creates a consistency problem between local and remote credential systems. A partial failure can leave the user with one password accepted locally and another remotely. Model synchronization as a versioned operation with explicit success and repair states, not a best-effort background write with no visibility.

Prefer operating-system-supported mechanisms over intercepting arbitrary credentials. Clearly separate passwordless login, local account unlock and cloud SSO: success in one context does not automatically establish all three. Document supported account types, shared-device scenarios and emergency recovery paths.

Safe recovery

A recovery code or helpdesk override should be scoped to one device, principal, purpose and validity window. High-risk overrides require dual approval and a reason. The endpoint records use locally and uploads evidence on reconnect. A universal static bypass password across the fleet would turn one support compromise into a fleet-wide incident.

Provide a break-glass plan for a bad client release or broken policy without silently bypassing all security. Examples include a signed emergency policy with narrow device targeting, a tested recovery environment and out-of-band administrator verification. The recovery mechanism itself needs the same review as the normal authenticator.

Fleet operations

Ship through canary rings covering OS releases, hardware families and management configurations. Monitor boot/login duration, extension crashes, offline lease usage and recovery events. A desktop release that adds ten seconds to every login can be operationally unacceptable even when it is cryptographically correct.

Acceptance tests. Disconnect a device for longer than its lease, roll back its clock, restore an old disk image, revoke it while offline, lose power during a credential update and deploy a faulty agent to a canary group. The outcome must preserve a documented balance between access and containment rather than an accidental platform-specific bypass.

20. Adaptive risk, behavior detection and network policy

Risk is a decision input, not an oracle

Our risk service combines observed behavior with explicit policy. Inputs can include IP reputation, network zone, device trust, login velocity, failed attempts, new geography, unusual application access and verified external threat signals. Some signals are deterministic rules; others are statistical models. Keep the distinction visible to operators.

A valid user traveling, changing networks or using privacy-preserving infrastructure may look unusual. A familiar device may still be compromised. Therefore a high risk score generally selects additional controls; it does not establish guilt, and a low score does not justify skipping mandatory security requirements.

Online and offline components

The online evaluator reads a compact feature snapshot and produces a decision within a strict latency budget. Streaming processors update histories, counters and derived features asynchronously. Offline jobs train and validate models. Keep model training and large graph queries off the login request path.

Each feature contains event time, ingestion time, source quality and expiry. Out-of-order events are expected. A login from an old timestamp should not overwrite the most recent device location. A missing reputation response gets an explicit unknown state, not a zero-risk numeric default.

Network zones and abuse defense

Administrators can define trusted or restricted network zones, but policy should not mistake an IP address for a person. Corporate NATs aggregate many users; mobile networks move users between addresses. Our zone compiler validates CIDRs, precedence and overlap and provides a simulation tool before publication.

Credential stuffing defenses operate before expensive password verification. Use layered limits across account, source, tenant and global capacity. Preserve privacy and avoid allowing attackers to lock out an entire company by exhausting a shared tenant counter. Suspicious traffic may face challenges or reduced admission without consuming the reserved high-assurance administrator path.

Model governance

Version features, models and thresholds together. Record which version influenced a decision so an incident can be reproduced. Evaluate false positives and false negatives across relevant customer populations; avoid training on data that violates residency or contractual limits. A model deployed for one customer population may not generalize to another.

Shadow mode compares proposed decisions with the current policy without enforcing them. Canary tenants and rollback controls reduce blast radius. A global threshold change is a production release and should receive the same scrutiny as code.

Failure behavior and explanations

For our standard employee apps, a temporarily unavailable risk model may trigger a phishing-resistant step-up using cached baseline policy. For highly privileged changes, deny until required signals are available or use a separately approved emergency process. Define this by operation and tenant contract.

Expose useful explanations such as 'new unmanaged device and recent failed attempts' while avoiding detailed disclosure of fraud-detection internals to unauthenticated users. Administrators need a way to report false positives and inspect signal freshness. Do not let a support override permanently mark a compromised identity as safe.

Acceptance tests. Delay the event stream, replay old location events, fail the reputation provider, introduce a deliberately biased threshold and saturate a corporate NAT. Measure challenge burden, confirmed compromise detection, decision latency and the proportion of decisions made with stale or unknown evidence. Availability without evidence quality is not a meaningful adaptive-security result.

21. Enrollment, account recovery and identity verification

Recovery is another authentication system

A strong passkey deployment can be undermined by a weak 'lost my device' process. Recovery changes who can authenticate, so it must have its own policy, evidence, approval, delay and audit model. Our platform treats enrollment of a replacement authenticator as a privileged identity change, not a customer-service convenience.

Separate common recovery cases

A user with one remaining trusted factor can enroll a replacement after step-up. A user with no factors but a managed device may have a different path. A privileged administrator with no trusted evidence requires a higher-assurance organizational process. Do not collapse all three into an email link.

Recovery tokens are opaque, purpose-bound, one-time and short-lived. Store their hashes and bind them to the intended account, pending action and initiating context. A token for beginning recovery cannot be reused to change email, disable MFA or grant an administrator role. Sensitive changes recheck current account status immediately before commit.

Notify previously verified channels about factor additions, resets and recovery attempts without revealing secrets. For high-risk changes, a cooling-off period can allow the real user or security team to react. The product should clearly show pending changes and provide an authenticated cancellation mechanism.

Helpdesk and administrator recovery

A support operator needs a scoped role, strong authentication, a valid case and an auditable reason. High-impact overrides require another independent approver. The system should make unauthorized shortcuts impossible, not merely against policy. Support staff should not see raw credentials or arbitrarily impersonate users.

Use customer-specific recovery contacts and verified organizational procedures. A caller knowing an employee's name, manager and phone number is not strong proof; those details may be public or exposed elsewhere. Avoid knowledge-based questions as the sole recovery factor.

Identity verification providers

Okta documents integrations with providers including CLEAR1, Incode and Persona, and labels an account-management verification capability as EA. Provider verification is a separate function from ordinary IdP routing. [O23] [O24]

Our IDV integration uses a bounded verification session, explicit consent, a signed callback and a normalized result containing assurance and evidence references. The identity platform should retain the minimum necessary result rather than copying identity documents into every service. A provider's positive result must be matched to the intended recovery subject and transaction.

Document fraud checks, liveness and legal-identity proofing have different capabilities and failure modes. Do not market a provider response as certainty. Define manual review, appeal, privacy retention and regional restrictions. Provider outage should not silently downgrade a privileged recovery to a weaker channel.

Recovery after compromise

After a confirmed account takeover, rotate relevant authenticators, revoke sessions and refresh families, examine account-linking changes, revoke agent delegations and review privileged assignments. Resetting only the password may leave attacker-added passkeys or OAuth grants active.

Acceptance tests. Attempt recovery with a stolen low-assurance session, replay a provider callback, approve a request after its subject was disabled and have one helpdesk operator try to bypass dual control. Measure time to legitimate recovery, fraudulent recovery rate, unexplained overrides and incomplete downstream revocation. Security and usability must both be visible.

22. Inbound federation, social identity and account linking

Become a broker without losing the trust boundary

A broker accepts authentication from external identity providers and issues credentials to its own applications. This can support enterprise federation, social login and organization-to-organization connections. Each upstream connection is a separately configured trust relationship with its own issuer, keys, subject mapping, assurance policy and allowed tenants.

Provider routing and validation

Our routing policy chooses a connection using explicit application or organization context and verified discovery rules. The chosen connection becomes part of the immutable login transaction. A callback cannot switch to a different provider merely by naming another connection ID.

The upstream protocol adapter validates its response using that connection's trust configuration. It produces a normalized authentication context with issuer, subject, time and verified attributes. Only then does the account-mapping service find or create the local principal. A normalized object should preserve provenance so downstream policy knows whether an attribute came from HR, a social provider or user input.

Just-in-time creation

JIT creation is convenient but must be permissioned. A valid external identity is not automatically entitled to create an account in any customer organization. Check the connection's enrollment policy, organization invitation or other approved membership rule. Bind newly created users to the trusted upstream subject rather than only its email.

When an upstream provider changes an email address, update the attribute without creating an unrelated new person. When an email address is recycled, do not merge the new owner with the previous account. Store aliases and linkage history with a controlled unlink process.

Explicit linking

A safe linking ceremony proves control of both accounts or uses an administrator-approved enterprise mapping with appropriate assurance. Display which provider and account will be linked. Require recent authentication before adding a new login path to an existing privileged account.

Unlinking must leave at least one usable, policy-compliant authenticator or recovery path unless the account is being intentionally disabled. Revoke affected sessions and grants when a link is removed for security reasons. Account merges need a reversible plan for app assignments, entitlements and audit history; not every merge is safely automatic.

Federation assurance and MFA claims

An upstream 'MFA' assertion is not a universal guarantee of a specific factor strength. Our trust configuration maps only understood, contractually supported authentication context into local evidence. For sensitive applications, perform local step-up when upstream evidence is insufficient or stale.

Social-login consent and enterprise SSO enrollment solve different problems. A social provider can establish control of its account but not membership in an employer's managed workforce. Organization-to-organization federation should define which side owns lifecycle, termination and emergency revocation.

Resilience and migration

Expose upstream availability separately from broker availability. Circuit breakers prevent a slow provider from exhausting all serving threads. A fallback connection is allowed only when it proves the same identity at the required assurance; 'try any available provider' is not a safe outage strategy.

For provider migration, maintain stable local subjects, test a population mapping, support a controlled overlap and retire the old connection only after reviewing remaining users. Exportable mappings and audit history reduce customer lock-in without weakening account linking.

Acceptance tests. Present the same email from two unrelated issuers, change upstream subject unexpectedly, replay a callback on another organization, unlink the last authenticator and fail the upstream provider. Verify that no fallback silently grants a different principal access.

23. Secure Partner Access and delegated external administration

Partners are not ordinary employees

A supplier, reseller or contractor organization often needs access to selected applications and the ability to administer its own members. Giving its administrator full access to the workforce admin console is excessive. Okta's Secure Partner Access announcement describes a dedicated partner administration experience with isolated populations and delegated controls; the consulted article labels it Early Access. [O25]

Our proposed external-access product creates a partner realm under a sponsoring tenant. A realm has an owner, approved applications, allowed enrollment methods, lifecycle policy and delegated administrators. It is not a separate copy of every platform service, but it is an explicit authorization boundary.

Authority cannot expand by delegation

A sponsor grants a partner administrator permission to invite users, assign selected app roles or manage a limited group. The delegated permission is bounded by the sponsor's current policy. A partner admin cannot create another admin with broader authority than their own, edit workforce recovery policy or assign internal-only applications.

Every administrative API evaluates actor, sponsor tenant, partner realm, target resource and requested action. Hiding console menu items is not sufficient. Automated tests should call the APIs directly with cross-realm object IDs and prove denial.

Invitations and partner federation

An invitation is bound to a realm, intended role, application scope and expiry. Redemption requires adequate identity proof and cannot silently change the invited role. Email forwarding should not become an unintended transferable administrator credential. For higher-risk partner roles, require sponsor approval after redemption.

Partners can federate their own IdP, but the sponsor chooses acceptable assurance and may require local step-up. The partner IdP's membership claims do not automatically authorize access to all sponsor resources. A partner connection's compromise should be containable by disabling that realm and its grants.

Lifecycle and periodic review

External relationships need explicit end dates, periodic recertification and ownership. An engagement ending should revoke access even if the partner's directory still lists the user as active. Inactive realms and orphaned partner administrators are reported to the sponsor.

Keep sponsor-visible audit records of invitations, role changes, federation configuration and recovery. Provide a partner-facing subset without exposing internal workforce data. Cross-company incident handling should identify who can freeze access and how the partner is notified.

Commercial and UX considerations

Partner administration should be understandable to someone who does not operate an identity platform daily. Provide a narrow dashboard, clear application names and actionable errors. Self-service reduces support burden only when authority boundaries and recovery paths are clear.

Meter partner populations separately from employee populations if the commercial model requires it, but do not let metering delays alter authorization. Bulk partner imports need dry runs and an explanation of which users will lose access. A sponsor must be able to revoke a whole realm without first repairing every individual partner account.

Acceptance tests. Have a partner admin target another realm, invite a more privileged role, change its own sponsor policy and retain access after the relationship expires. Test both UI and direct API paths. Track stale partnerships, unowned realms and time from sponsor revocation to downstream enforcement.

Workforce identity often begins with an administrator-approved population. Our CIAM service therefore shares identity primitives but gets separate abuse controls, capacity pools and developer-facing configuration.

Hosted login as the default secure integration

Public signup and account lifecycle

25. Actions, Forms and safe customer extensibility

Extensibility creates a new trust boundary

Customers want to add claims, call business systems, block suspicious users and collect custom fields. Auth0 Actions provides a managed extensibility model; its September 2026 changelog marks reusable Actions Modules GA. Our implementation must treat every customer extension as untrusted code, even when the customer administrator intentionally installed it. [A06] [A04]

Versioned execution packages

An extension version contains source digest, dependency lockfile, runtime version, declared permissions, secret references, timeout and output schema. Publishing produces an immutable artifact after static checks and tests. A flow binds to explicit versions, not mutable 'latest' code. Shared modules are independently versioned and reviewed so one change cannot silently alter hundreds of authentication flows.

Our runtime uses strong process or micro-VM isolation appropriate to the threat model, resource quotas and no ambient cloud credentials. Each invocation receives only the tenant-scoped data necessary for the trigger. Secrets are fetched just in time and cannot be listed globally. Customer logs are separate from platform security logs and are automatically redacted where possible.

Network access and SSRF

Outbound calls pass through a controlled egress service. Block cloud metadata endpoints, loopback, private addresses unless explicitly configured for a private integration, and unsafe redirects. Re-resolve and validate DNS at connection time to reduce rebinding attacks. Rate-limit by tenant and destination.

Allowing a customer to configure a URL is not permission to reach every network address. A 'webhook test' button needs the same SSRF defenses as production. Private connectivity should be a separately authenticated, auditable capability rather than an implicit bypass.

Synchronous versus asynchronous triggers

A pre-token extension can delay or deny login, so it belongs to the serving dependency budget. Give it a short deadline and a documented fail-open or fail-closed policy. Security decisions generally cannot be skipped silently; optional enrichment should be moved to an asynchronous path or omitted safely.

A post-login CRM update can run asynchronously with durable retries. External side effects need idempotency keys because retries are inevitable. Extension code must not assume that an invocation happens exactly once, nor should the platform claim exactly-once execution across arbitrary customer APIs.

Result validation

The extension returns a typed result: permitted custom claims, an approved denial reason or a defined next interaction step. It cannot overwrite issuer, subject, audience or other protected fields. Enforce size limits and validate values before adding them to tokens. A malicious extension should not be able to mint an administrator role beyond the installing actor's authority.

Forms are versioned UI schemas bound to an interaction. Validate fields server-side, protect sensitive data and make consent text versioned. Avoid arbitrary HTML or scripts where a constrained component model works. Accessibility and localization tests belong in the publishing gate.

Operations and rollback

Provide a replay harness using sanitized recorded inputs and mocked external responses. Shadow a new version before enforcing it, then canary by tenant or traffic share. Emergency disable must preserve the intended security behavior: disabling a deny hook cannot silently allow everyone.

Acceptance tests. Run an infinite loop, allocate excessive memory, exfiltrate a secret, call metadata endpoints, return oversized claims and time out during a login. Verify isolation, bounded latency and a customer-visible diagnostic that does not expose another tenant's data.

26. Fine-grained authorization and relationship-based access

Login does not answer object-level permission questions

A project application may ask whether Maya can edit document 17 because she belongs to a team that owns its folder. Encoding every document permission in an ID token is impractical and stale. A fine-grained authorization service evaluates relationships and policies at request time.

OpenFGA documents authorization modeling with relationship tuples. Google's Zanzibar paper is an important research reference for large-scale relationship authorization and consistency. Our proposed service borrows the modeling idea without claiming Zanzibar's exact implementation or performance guarantees. [A09] [R01]

Data and model

Store tuples such as (document:17, parent, folder:9) and (team:finance, member, user:maya). A versioned model defines relations, computed permissions and allowed subject types. A check request specifies tenant, subject, action, object, model version and any approved contextual relationships.

The model compiler rejects undefined types, unsupported recursion and dangerous complexity. Traversal has depth, node and time budgets. Cycles are handled deliberately. A graph service should not allow an attacker to create a relationship pattern that makes every authorization check consume seconds of CPU.

Consistency is part of authorization

Suppose an administrator removes Maya from finance and immediately requests a permission check. A stale replica must not respond 'allowed' under a promise of read-after-write revocation. Our API returns a revision token for writes and supports checks that require at least that revision. Stronger modes may wait or route to an authoritative replica; faster cached modes disclose their freshness contract.

Cache keys include tenant, model version, tuple revision or invalidation generation, subject, action and object. A global TTL alone is not adequate for high-risk revocations. Avoid using an eventually consistent search index as the sole authority for access decisions.

Policy boundaries

Relationship authorization and authentication policy solve different problems. The graph may say Maya is an editor, while the application's policy still requires a managed device for downloading confidential material. Combine them through a clear decision interface that records both relationship revision and contextual policy version.

Contextual tuples supplied by a client are especially sensitive. Only trusted application services may add narrowly scoped context after verifying it. A browser cannot claim 'I am a member of finance' and ask the graph to treat it as fact.

Administration and explainability

Provide model tests, sample queries and a 'why allowed' explanation for authorized administrators. Acceptance tests. Remove a relationship then check with the returned revision, create a cycle, exceed traversal budgets, submit an unauthorized contextual tuple and change a model to broaden access. 27. Non-human identities and workload credentials

Machines need identities, owners and expiry

A service account is not merely a human account with a shared password. Our platform gives each workload a principal, owner team, purpose, environment, allowed resources, credential policy and lifecycle. This includes application backends, automation, CI jobs, connector agents and scheduled tasks.

Bootstrap trust without permanent fleet secrets

A workload needs an initial way to prove where it runs. Depending on the environment, this can be a validated cloud workload assertion, a cluster-issued service identity or an enrolled certificate. The identity broker validates issuer, audience, runtime attributes and permitted trust relationships before issuing a short-lived credential.

Do not accept any token from a familiar cloud provider as proof of the intended workload. Bind the trust policy to the expected account, project, cluster, namespace, service and environment as applicable. A development workload must not inherit production permissions because its service name matches.

Credential choices

Prefer short-lived credentials with automated renewal where the resource supports them. For unavoidable API keys or passwords, use a secret vault with scoped retrieval, rotation and exposure detection. A static credential still needs an owner and last-use telemetry. 'No human logs in with it' does not make it low risk.

Our credential inventory tracks issuance, rotation, usage, expiry and revocation. A service may have overlapping keys during a safe rotation window, but an indefinite collection of old keys is not a rotation strategy. Notifications escalate to an owning team, not one employee who may leave.

Authorization and attribution

A service credential expresses the service's authority. Acting on behalf of a user requires an explicit delegation relationship and an audit chain. Do not place a user ID in an unsigned request header and call it user delegation. Token exchange policy and resource-level checks preserve both the acting workload and the represented subject.

New workloads pass an approval or policy-based registration process. Production access can require an owner, repository, deployment identity and expiry review. Machines also need emergency containment. Disabling the principal stops new issuance; participating resources must enforce revocation or short expiry for existing credentials. A discovered leaked key may require resource-side rotation even after the IdP principal is disabled.

Measure credential age, owner coverage, unused privileges, issuance anomalies and time to effective containment.

31. SCIM 2.0: provisioning as a distributed synchronization system

Authentication does not provision an account

SSO proves identity at login. Provisioning creates and updates the account and its memberships in the destination system. SCIM standardizes resources and operations for this synchronization; its schema and protocol specifications are distinct. Okta documents SCIM as a provisioning integration mechanism. [P09] [P10] [O19]

Our platform supports both an inbound SCIM server and outbound SCIM clients. Inbound traffic lets an external authority manage a defined population. Outbound workers reconcile our desired state into customer applications. Never share one unrestricted credential across all tenants' connectors.

Resource and correlation model

A connector account maps (tenant, connector instance, source immutable ID) to a destination resource ID. Store external identifiers as attributes with defined authority; do not assume every externalId is globally unique across unrelated systems. Keep the destination's version or ETag, last observed attributes and last successful reconciliation.

Support the advertised Users and Groups schemas, including extension namespaces. Correctly distinguish missing fields, explicit nulls, empty arrays and primary multi-valued attributes. Parse filters using a bounded grammar rather than string concatenation into SQL. Enforce tenant scope before applying any client-supplied filter.

Inbound server behavior

The SCIM endpoint authenticates the provisioning client and restricts it to its configured population and operations. A bearer credential for one enterprise connection cannot enumerate all users in the paying tenant. Administrative profile sourcing policy still decides which fields the connector may change.

Implement the supported create, read, update, patch and delete behavior faithfully. PUT and PATCH have different semantics; do not treat both as arbitrary partial JSON merge. Advertise optional capabilities such as bulk operations only when implemented. Return protocol-appropriate errors and pagination metadata. [P09] [P10]

Use optimistic concurrency when available. An update based on an old destination version should be retried through a read-and-merge policy, not blindly overwrite a newer change. Bound page sizes and filter complexity. A full import can use checkpointing and reconciliation because offset pagination over a changing dataset may skip or repeat records.

Outbound execution and the lost-response problem

Suppose we send POST /Users, the app creates the account, but the response is lost. Retrying the POST may create a duplicate because not every SCIM server supports a generic idempotency header. Our connector first tries to correlate through a stable external identifier or a supported lookup. It verifies a unique match before recording the destination ID.

If the result is ambiguous, quarantine the operation for reconciliation rather than guessing by display name. Connector-specific behavior belongs in a versioned capability profile. A universal retry loop cannot safely cover every vendor's implementation quirks.

Desired state and observed state

Maintain a desired membership revision and a last-observed destination revision. A worker computes the minimal safe change, sends it and records the outcome. If a newer desired revision arrives while the old command is in flight, finish or supersede the old operation according to its semantics, then converge to the latest state.

Deactivation is not necessarily deletion. Many integrations use active=false to disable access while preserving records; deletion can have different business consequences. Our connector configuration explicitly states what 'offboard' means for that application. It can also include license removal, group removal and session termination as separate actions.

Retries, quotas and failure visibility

Honor rate-limit responses and retry guidance, use exponential backoff with jitter and cap concurrency per destination. Separate authentication failures, permanent validation errors, transient server failures and ambiguous outcomes. A dead-letter queue is a work queue for repair, not a place where failed offboarding quietly disappears.

Expose three states to administrators: desired, last sent and last verified. 'Provisioned' should mean the agreed verification condition has been met. For critical deactivation, raise an incident when the destination remains active beyond the configured objective.

Acceptance tests. Lose a successful create response, reorder updates, reject a PATCH path, paginate while the source changes, remove a group during membership sync and return 429 for an hour. Verify eventual convergence without duplicate accounts or reactivation of terminated users. Test against actual target implementations, not only a permissive local mock.

32. Lifecycle management: joiner, mover, leaver and reconciliation

Lifecycle is a long-running business transaction

A new employee may need a directory identity, an email account, five applications, a laptop and an approval for finance access. These operations span independent systems and cannot share one database transaction. Our lifecycle engine represents the process as a durable plan with steps, dependencies, retries, compensations and evidence.

Okta Identity Governance documentation includes lifecycle management and Workflows in its broader governance offering. Our design treats lifecycle as a foundational service regardless of how commercial bundles are packaged. [O26]

Joiner

A trusted HR observation creates a pending principal with a start date and immutable source correlation. Policy derives baseline groups and app assignments. Some resources can be prepared before the start date without enabling login. Activation is a distinct scheduled transition, evaluated against the latest employment status.

A cancelled hire should revoke invitations and stop pending provisioning. If accounts were already created, compensating actions disable or remove them according to each application's policy. A compensation is not always a perfect rollback: an email may already have been sent or a license consumed. Record these irreversible effects.

Mover

A department or role change recomputes desired access. The safe order depends on business policy. For mutually exclusive roles, remove the old entitlement before adding the new one. For continuity-sensitive access, a short approved overlap may be acceptable. Encode that choice explicitly rather than relying on connector execution order.

Separate birthright access derived from policy from exceptions granted by approval. When the employee changes departments, an old exception may need reevaluation rather than automatic preservation. Store grant provenance so the system can explain why each permission exists.

Leaver

Termination starts with an authoritative lifecycle change and a security epoch increment. Stop new authentication and refresh, revoke managed sessions, disable agents owned solely by the user or reassign them, remove privileges and dispatch downstream deactivation. Preserve records required for audit without keeping the account operational.

Do not wait for all SCIM endpoints to respond before blocking the user locally. Conversely, do not claim offboarding is complete when only the local account is disabled. The lifecycle case shows each application's status, last attempt, residual risk and escalation owner.

For scheduled termination, evaluate time zones carefully and allow urgent override. A connector carrying yesterday's active=true must not reverse a newer termination. Source authority and lifecycle monotonicity rules are more important than simple arrival order.

Durable workflow implementation

A plan stores an immutable input snapshot, policy version, per-step status and idempotency keys. Workers acquire short leases and checkpoint after each durable result. Side effects are retried through connector-specific reconciliation. Use an outbox so a committed employment change cannot lose its lifecycle event.

Manual repair actions are versioned commands with actor and reason, not edits to worker database rows. A replay tool can resume only failed or unverified steps without repeating completed destructive actions. Rate controls protect downstream systems during a bulk layoff, acquisition or HR import.

Reconciliation closes the loop

Events can be missed, external admins can change access and integrations can drift. Periodic reconciliation compares authoritative desired state with observed destination state. The remediation policy may automatically remove unauthorized access, request approval or open a case. A full scan should not starve urgent deactivation jobs.

Acceptance tests. Cancel a hire after partial provisioning, move an employee between conflicting roles, terminate during a connector outage and replay the HR feed out of order. Track time to local deny, time to downstream disable, unverified steps and access that lacks a valid grant source.

35. Workflows: a durable low-code execution engine

A workflow is a recoverable program

A low-code canvas hides implementation complexity, but it does not remove distributed-systems problems. A workflow may wait for approval, call three APIs, branch, loop and resume days later. Our engine stores durable execution state and runs short leased tasks; it does not keep a process sleeping for the entire workflow lifetime.

Okta publishes Workflows resource and execution limits, including throttling behavior. Even an offering described in terms of unlimited active flows does not imply unlimited memory, concurrency or external API capacity. [O30] [O31]

Definition and execution model

A flow definition is an immutable graph with typed inputs, steps, branches, loops and output schemas. Validate it for missing references, unbounded expansion and incompatible types before publication. An execution binds to the definition version that started it, so editing the canvas does not change a half-completed approval process unexpectedly.

Store execution ID, tenant, trigger ID, definition version, current state and event history. A scheduler creates runnable tasks. Workers claim tasks, perform bounded work and append results. Timers are durable records indexed by due time; a scheduler restart must not lose sleeping executions.

Side effects and idempotence

Each side-effecting step gets an operation identity derived from execution and step instance. A connector uses that identity where the destination supports idempotence, otherwise it reconciles. The engine can guarantee durable task bookkeeping, but it cannot guarantee exactly-once effects in every third-party API.

A retry policy distinguishes transient errors from invalid requests and ambiguous outcomes. Branching on 'request timed out' should not assume the remote operation failed. A payment-like or destructive action may require manual verification before replay.

Compensation and cancellation

Cancellation stops future steps but cannot erase emails already sent or accounts already created. A compensation graph can undo reversible actions under policy. Record which compensations succeeded and which require human repair. Do not call compensation an atomic rollback across the internet.

Long-lived executions need credential reauthorization. A workflow started by an administrator must not retain unlimited power after that administrator loses their role. Recheck current authority for sensitive steps or bind the flow to a separately approved service principal with a clear owner.

Tenant fairness and limits

Use per-tenant concurrency, memory, execution-duration and step-count budgets. Reserve capacity for urgent security workflows such as offboarding. A tenant's accidental infinite loop cannot exhaust the global scheduler. Queue age and throttling status are visible to the customer.

Secrets are referenced, not embedded in flow exports. Logs redact sensitive fields and support field-level retention. Connector calls pass through the same controlled egress used elsewhere. A low-code HTTP card is still an SSRF surface.

Authoring experience

Acceptance tests. Crash a worker after a remote side effect, restart the timer service, revoke the initiating user's privilege during a wait and create a flow with explosive fan-out. Verify durable progress, bounded resource use and honest reporting of ambiguous external outcomes.

36. Event hooks, inline hooks and notification delivery

Two extension mechanisms with different reliability contracts

Event hooks notify consumers after something happened. Inline hooks participate in a live decision. Okta documents these separately; our platform preserves that distinction in APIs, quotas and failure policies. [O32] [O33]

Event hooks

A committed security or lifecycle change writes an outbox record. A publisher emits an event with tenant, event ID, type, aggregate ID, aggregate version, event time, schema version and sanitized payload. Delivery workers send it to registered endpoints with authenticated signatures and replay-resistant timestamps.

Delivery is at least once in our contract. Consumers deduplicate by event ID and handle out-of-order aggregate versions. We expose delivery attempts, response classification and the next retry time. A successful consumer acknowledgment means the endpoint accepted the event, not necessarily that its entire business process completed.

Endpoint registration includes ownership verification, HTTPS validation and egress safety checks. Secret rotation supports an overlap window and explicit key IDs. Never let webhook response bodies inject configuration or secrets into platform logs.

Inline hooks

A synchronous hook receives a minimal context and returns a strictly typed response within a deadline. Its decision authority is limited by the installing administrator's scope and the trigger's contract. It cannot change protected identity or protocol fields arbitrarily.

Timeout behavior is configured by purpose. A mandatory security decision can fail closed; optional profile enrichment may omit enrichment safely. The UI must warn when a hook introduces a third-party availability dependency into login. Circuit breakers and dedicated connection pools prevent one endpoint from exhausting all serving threads.

Use immutable hook versions and capture which version ran in the policy trace. A hook should not call back into an API that recursively triggers itself without a recursion guard. Request IDs and causal metadata help detect loops.

Email, SMS and push notifications

Notification services use templates, localization, recipient policy and provider routing. Separate security notifications from marketing messages. Apply spend and abuse controls, but reserve capacity for critical security alerts. Provider acceptance, delivery feedback and user action are distinct events.

Templates use safe substitution and approved links. Recovery URLs are generated by the identity service, not customer-provided arbitrary strings. A notification should reveal only the necessary account information. Expired messages should not accidentally revive old recovery tokens.

Delivery operations

Use exponential backoff with jitter, bounded retries and dead-letter handling. Retrying every failed destination at the same instant can produce a recovery storm. Per-destination concurrency and tenant fairness protect both the platform and customers.

A customer can replay selected events through an authorized repair operation. Replay preserves the original event identity or explicitly marks a new delivery identity, so consumers can distinguish duplicate delivery from a new business event. Retention and replay windows are part of the product contract.

Entitlement catalog

A catalog entry has resource, entitlement key, description, owner, sensitivity, eligibility policy, approval policy, expiry rules and provisioning mapping. Group related permissions into bundles for usability, but retain the underlying permissions for conflict checking and review. A friendly bundle name must not hide a newly added administrator permission.

Import entitlement metadata from connected applications and normalize it without losing source identifiers. Changes to a destination's role definition can broaden access even when our assignment record is unchanged. Track schema and permission-definition versions and flag material drift.

Grant ledger

Each grant records principal, resource, entitlement, source, authorizing policy or request, valid-from, valid-until and status. Sources include birthright policy, approved request, direct administrator grant and imported external access. Imported access without known approval is not automatically legitimate; it becomes a reviewable observation.

Use a state machine such as requested, approved, provisioning, active, revoking and revoked. 'Active' should have an agreed meaning about downstream verification. Grant expiry schedules revocation and remains visible until verified. A failed removal does not disappear just because the logical expiry time passed.

Separation of duties

A conflict rule states which combinations are forbidden or require an exception. For example, creating a supplier and approving its payment may be incompatible. Evaluate conflicts against effective access, pending approved grants and relevant cross-application roles, not just one local group list.

Prevent concurrent approvals from bypassing the rule. Two requests that are individually safe can become unsafe together. Serialize on the relevant principal/resource conflict domain or use a transactionally maintained conflict reservation. Recheck immediately before activation.

Exceptions require an owner, justification, compensating control and expiry. A permanent checkbox labeled 'ignore conflict' undermines the governance model. Review exceptions periodically and expose them in certifications.

Policy-derived access and overrides

When a policy changes, compute a proposed grant diff and show its impact before enforcement. Distinguish removing policy-derived access from removing an explicitly approved exception. Our design stores provenance so one source cannot accidentally erase another without a defined precedence rule.

Direct administrator overrides are powerful and should be rare, scoped and audited. A policy engine must know whether an override temporarily suppresses policy, coexists with it or expires back to policy control. Ambiguous precedence leads to access unexpectedly returning after revocation.

Metrics and evidence

Track unowned entitlements, grants without valid provenance, expired-but-active access, conflict exceptions and downstream drift. A governance dashboard showing only approval volume misses the central question: does current access have a valid, current reason?

Acceptance tests. Approve conflicting requests concurrently, change a bundle to include a stronger permission, expire access during a connector outage and remove a policy while an exception remains. Verify the ledger preserves a complete, explainable chain from business reason to actual destination access.

38. Access requests, approvals and time-bound elevation

Requests turn business intent into constrained grants

An access-request system lets users ask for access without turning every administrator into a ticket router. Our request record contains requester, beneficiary, resource, exact entitlement version, business reason, requested duration, risk context and approval-policy version. Requester and beneficiary can differ only under explicit delegated authority.

Approval policy

Choose approvers from resource ownership, management hierarchy, sensitivity and conflict rules. Resolve the approver set when the request is evaluated, and re-evaluate if key ownership changes before completion. Avoid self-approval, approval cycles and routing to a terminated manager.

High-risk requests may need multiple independent approvals. An approver authenticates recently and sees the exact permission, duration and beneficiary. Email or chat can notify the approver, but the final action should be bound to an authenticated, one-time approval capability with clear context.

An approval is not a blank check. Hash or version the material request fields. If the requester changes the entitlement or extends duration, obtain new approval. Cosmetic edits can be allowed without restarting the process, but define them narrowly.

Decision and provisioning

After approval, recheck eligibility, conflicts, principal status and current policy before activating the grant. A request approved yesterday should not provision access to a user terminated this morning. The grant ledger and provisioning outbox commit together.

The UI separates approval from fulfillment. 'Approved, awaiting destination' is different from 'active'. Failed provisioning creates a repair path and does not cause the approver to unknowingly approve duplicates. Cancellation after approval may require compensating revocation if provisioning already occurred.

Time-bound access

Use a durable expiry timer and a verified revocation workflow. A short-lived access request is only as temporary as its downstream enforcement. For resources supporting ephemeral credentials, prefer credentials that expire locally. For static group assignments, monitor removal completion and escalate failures.

Renewal is a new decision with current context, not silently extending the old approval indefinitely. Maximum cumulative duration can prevent a series of small renewals from becoming permanent privileged access.

Chat integrations and usability

Slack-like or email-based request surfaces should show a concise summary and link to the canonical record. Treat external message IDs as notification references, not authorization. Verify callbacks, audience and replay, and do not trust a display name as the approver's identity.

Provide delegation for vacations and clear escalation after an approval deadline. Escalation must preserve separation of duties; 'no one answered, so auto-approve admin access' is not an acceptable default. Low-risk birthright requests can be automatically approved by a documented policy instead.

Audit and metrics

Store each decision, actor, authentication context, policy version and downstream action. Track request completion time, approval delay, abandoned requests, self-approval attempts and expired access still active. Business efficiency and security correctness should both be measurable.

Acceptance tests. Change a request after approval, terminate the beneficiary mid-flow, remove the approver, submit two conflicting requests and fail the expiry worker. Verify that no stale approval grants broader authority than the approver actually reviewed.

39. Access certifications and audit evidence

A certification asks whether existing access is still justified

Access requests govern new grants. Certifications review what already exists, including old, imported and exceptional access. Our campaign defines scope, snapshot time, reviewers, decision options, deadlines, escalation and remediation policy. Reviewers need the business context to decide, not just a list of cryptic group names.

Snapshot and live reality

A campaign operates on an immutable snapshot of grants and relevant context. Store resource definitions, ownership and grant provenance at snapshot time so the review can be reconstructed later. Live access can change during the campaign; identify differences rather than silently rewriting what the reviewer saw.

For a revoked decision, apply remediation to the current matching grant carefully. If access was removed and later regranted under a new approved request, the old decision may need reevaluation rather than deleting an unrelated new grant. Versioned grant IDs and provenance make this distinction possible.

Reviewer assignment

Reviewers may be managers, resource owners, application administrators or independent control owners. Prevent self-review where prohibited and handle departed or unavailable reviewers. A delegate receives a bounded review scope and cannot alter the campaign policy.

Show last use, sensitivity, conflicting access, source and justification when available. Mark missing or stale usage data explicitly. 'No observed use' is not proof that access is unnecessary when telemetry coverage is incomplete.

Decisions and remediation

A reviewer can retain, revoke or request further information according to policy. High-risk retain decisions may require a reason. Bulk review is useful, but guard against approving thousands of entitlements without meaningful context. Provide summaries that highlight anomalies rather than encouraging blind approval.

Revocation decisions create durable grant-removal commands. The campaign is not fully remediated until the destination's agreed verification condition is met or an exception is approved. Track open remediation beyond the review deadline. A completed questionnaire without access removal is not an effective control.

Evidence storage

Preserve campaign definition, snapshot digest, reviewer identity, decision time, evidence displayed, policy version and remediation outcome. Store immutable exports with integrity metadata and controlled retention. An auditor should be able to reproduce what was reviewed without needing access to mutable production tables.

Retention is not uniform across product data. Okta's published governance guidance distinguishes longer-lived summary reports from shorter-lived detailed records, and its System Log has a separate retention period. Our own contracts should state retention per record type and offer controlled export. [O28]

Campaign operations

Large campaigns create read and notification load. Build them asynchronously from consistent snapshots, paginate reviewer tasks and isolate report generation from authentication databases. Deduplicate reminders and respect localization and time zones. Campaign cancellation must preserve already-recorded decisions and evidence.

Acceptance tests. Change a grant during review, remove a reviewer, retry a decision callback, fail downstream revocation and export the campaign after live data changes. Verify that the evidence remains internally consistent and that the UI does not call unresolved remediation complete.

40. Privileged access: SSH, RDP, vaults and just-in-time credentials

Privileged access is a separate enforcement product

Okta Privileged Access documentation describes server access, credentials, approvals and components such as clients, agents and gateways. The September 2026 announcement also discusses broader resource types with future availability language. Do not assume every announced database, Kubernetes or network-device capability is already GA. [O29] [O13]

Our proposed PAM platform covers privileged identity, approval, credential issuance, connection enforcement, recording and cleanup. It must integrate with the identity provider while retaining an emergency path that does not create a circular outage dependency.

Resource and policy model

A resource record identifies host or service, environment, owner, sensitivity, supported access methods and credential strategy. Access policy specifies eligible principals, required assurance, approval, duration, allowed accounts and recording requirements. Discovery does not automatically authorize a discovered host.

Prefer short-lived certificates or dynamically created credentials where the target supports them. For SSH, a reviewed certificate-based design can limit user, principals and validity. For systems requiring passwords, use a vault with checkout, rotation and tested recovery. RDP may need a gateway and credential injection strategy that avoids exposing reusable administrator passwords to the operator.

Connection establishment

The user authenticates with strong recent proof and requests a resource. The policy engine checks eligibility and any approval. A grant authorizes a narrow connection window and resource account. The client or gateway validates the target identity to avoid sending privileged credentials to an impostor host.

The resource agent or gateway checks the grant, establishes the session and reports lifecycle events. Do not assume a certificate's future expiry will terminate an already-established SSH or RDP session; active-session termination is a separate enforcement function. Our policy specifies both new-connection expiry and maximum session duration.

Recording and command controls

Record sessions where supported and legally appropriate, encrypt recordings and tightly restrict playback. Recording failure has an explicit policy: block access when mandatory, or permit under an approved exception with an alert. A recording should be tamper-evident and linked to the exact access grant.

Command filtering can reduce risk for constrained workflows but is not a universal sandbox. Shell escapes, interpreters and indirect execution make broad administrator sessions difficult to constrain safely. For high-risk tasks, a purpose-built administrative API or approved automation can be safer than an unrestricted shell.

Vault and rotation

A vaulted secret has an owner, target, allowed readers, rotation method and last verified rotation. Rotation is a transaction across vault and resource: if one side changes and the other does not, access can break. Use versioned pending and active states, verify the new credential and retire the old one only after success.

Emergency accounts are stored and accessed under dual control, with use alerts and post-use rotation. They must be tested periodically. An emergency credential that nobody can retrieve during an IdP outage is not a recovery plan; a widely known emergency password is not a secure plan.

Operations

Gateways and agents need regional capacity, patching, failover and outbound connectivity controls. Privileged session traffic and recordings have different bandwidth and retention economics from web login. Separate their failure domains and support commitments.

Acceptance tests. Revoke a user during an active session, fail recording storage, rotate a secret with a lost response, impersonate a target host and lose the primary IdP. Verify both containment and the approved break-glass process, including the evidence generated by emergency use.

41. Access Gateway and legacy application protection

Modern identity in front of an old application

A legacy application may understand only headers, a local session or Windows Kerberos. An access gateway authenticates the user through the identity provider, applies policy and translates the result into the application's expected mechanism. Okta publishes Access Gateway reference architectures, including Kerberos-based patterns. [O43]

Protect the backend boundary

The gateway is useful only if attackers cannot bypass it. Restrict the application network so it accepts traffic from the gateway or an equivalent trusted path. Remove or overwrite all client-supplied identity headers. Authenticate gateway-to-backend traffic and ensure the application interprets only the agreed header contract.

A request containing X-User: administrator must never become authenticated merely because it reached the reverse proxy. Header names, encodings, multi-value behavior and case handling should be tested with the actual backend stack. Request smuggling and conflicting proxy parsing are part of the gateway threat model.

Session translation

The gateway maintains its own session tied to the validated IdP authentication and application policy. It can require step-up for sensitive paths and enforce idle or absolute timeouts. If the backend creates another session, that session's expiry and logout behavior must also be understood.

A gateway logout can remove its own cookie while leaving a backend session active. Use backend-specific termination where available and document residual access. Avoid caching personalized responses across users; cache keys and HTTP headers must reflect authentication context.

Kerberos integration

A Kerberos translation path needs a reviewed service identity, constrained delegation or other supported trust setup, keytab protection and correct service principal naming. The gateway should obtain only the target service authority needed for the request. Broad delegation across an entire domain creates unnecessary risk.

Directory and ticketing infrastructure become dependencies. Monitor their health separately and deploy redundant components where required. A gateway being reachable does not mean a Kerberos-backed application is available. Time synchronization, DNS and certificate configuration can all affect the path.

Policy and configuration

Application definitions contain external hostname, backend targets, health checks, allowed paths, identity mapping and required assurance. Publish configuration atomically with a version and rollback. Test a new rule against recorded sanitized requests before broad rollout.

An administrator configuring a backend URL receives a constrained capability, not arbitrary network access from a privileged gateway. Enforce destination restrictions and prevent the gateway from becoming a route to unrelated internal administration services.

Availability and operations

Run multiple gateway instances across failure domains, with session-state strategy and load-balancer behavior explicitly tested. Keep administration on a protected path separate from public traffic. Logs include request, identity and policy correlation without recording credentials or sensitive bodies unnecessarily.

Acceptance tests. Access the backend directly, spoof identity headers, replay a gateway session, fail the Kerberos dependency, change routing during live traffic and submit ambiguous HTTP framing. A legacy integration needs both identity correctness and reverse-proxy security, not just a successful SSO redirect.

42. Continuous identity security, ITP and Universal Logout

Authentication is not the last security decision

A session can become risky after login: a device is lost, an endpoint is compromised, an administrator removes a role or a token is stolen. Continuous identity security consumes those changes and updates access. Okta documents Universal Logout as part of its threat-protection capabilities, with integration-specific support rather than automatic control over every application's sessions. [O34] [O35]

Signals and subject correlation

A signal identifies its source, subject, event type, event time, expiry and evidence. The receiver authenticates the source and maps the subject to a local principal, session or device. Incorrect subject mapping can terminate the wrong user's access or leave the compromised session active.

OpenID's Shared Signals Framework defines a framework for security-event exchange between cooperating parties, including event-stream and subject concepts. It is a transport and interoperability foundation, not a guarantee that every receiver enforces every event identically. [P21]

Our receiver validates signatures, audience, issuer, replay identifiers and stream authorization. It handles duplicate and out-of-order events and records whether each signal was accepted, ignored or rejected. A threat signal from an untrusted sender cannot disable arbitrary accounts.

Decision and action

A response policy can require step-up, shorten session lifetime, revoke refresh grants, suspend a device, freeze an agent or request downstream logout. Policies are versioned and tested. A single noisy detector should not automatically suspend an entire enterprise without an explicitly approved escalation rule.

The response creates a durable incident/action record. Each target enforcement point acknowledges receipt and, where possible, reports completion. Distinguish delivery from enforcement. For an app with only a logout API, a timeout may leave uncertainty about whether the session ended; reconciliation or a retry strategy is needed.

Universal Logout boundaries

Our integration catalog records whether an app supports session termination, refresh revocation, token revocation or only future-login denial. The administrator sees these capabilities before relying on 'logout everywhere'. Unsupported applications remain a documented residual risk.

A JWT already accepted by an offline resource can remain valid until expiry. A browser app may keep its own session after the IdP session ends. Short credential lifetimes, introspection, revocation feeds and application-specific APIs provide different tradeoffs. The platform should state the resulting containment window, not imply that one button changes every system instantly.

Prevent loops and security storms

Two connected security products can repeatedly trigger each other. Include causal IDs, deduplication and action-state checks. Rate-limit bulk containment while preserving urgent per-principal actions. A malicious or buggy signal source must not exhaust the control plane.

Maintain a separate emergency channel for high-confidence revocations if ordinary event processing is delayed. Its operation should be simple, authenticated and heavily audited. It also needs capacity tests: a company-wide compromise can produce far more revocations than normal traffic.

Customer identity sessions

Auth0's Continuous Session Protection documentation describes risk-aware session controls. Our CIAM design uses the same separation between local session management and downstream resource enforcement, with explicit refresh-family and application-cookie behavior. [A08]

Acceptance tests. Replay a security event, send it for the wrong issuer-subject pair, revoke during token issuance, make a destination acknowledge without enforcing and create a feedback loop between signal providers. Measure detection-to-local-deny and detection-to-verified-downstream-containment separately.

43. Identity Security Posture Management and attack graphs

Find risky access before it is used

Posture management inventories identities, permissions and configurations across connected systems, then identifies exposures such as excessive privilege, missing MFA, orphaned accounts or dangerous relationships. Okta's ISPM overview describes agentless collection and contextual analysis across human and non-human identity data. [O36]

Collection and provenance

Each collector has a tenant-scoped credential, declared API permissions, rate budget and checkpoint. Store source timestamps and collection coverage. Missing data must be visible: a disconnected cloud account should not appear perfectly secure because no findings were produced.

Normalize identities and resources into a graph without merging by name alone. A user in a cloud account, a SaaS application and a directory may be related, but correlation needs stable identifiers and evidence. Preserve uncertain matches as hypotheses rather than silently creating a false identity chain.

Findings as reproducible queries

A finding is the result of a versioned rule against a defined graph snapshot. It contains affected entities, evidence, severity rationale, first seen, last seen and remediation guidance. Examples include a privileged service account with no owner or a path from a weakly protected identity to a sensitive resource.

Risk ranking considers exposure, privilege, resource sensitivity and evidence quality. A long graph path is not automatically exploitable; edges have preconditions and confidence. Distinguish a confirmed configuration defect from a possible attack path requiring validation.

Remediation and safety

Remediation can open a ticket, request approval, revoke a grant or change a configuration through a connector. High-impact changes require preconditions, expected versions and rollback or repair plans. A posture tool must not remove a production service account merely because it appears unused in incomplete telemetry.

Close findings only after re-observation or another agreed verification. A successful remediation API call is not enough when the destination applies changes asynchronously. Suppressions need an owner, reason and expiry so accepted risk does not disappear permanently from view.

Data isolation and scale

Graph construction and large joins run in an analytical security plane, not in the login database. Partition by tenant and apply per-tenant compute budgets. Cross-tenant statistical learning requires explicit privacy and contractual controls; raw identity graphs are highly sensitive.

Freshness objectives differ by signal. An emergency compromise indicator may require seconds, while a full entitlement inventory might take hours. Show both age and coverage in the UI. A posture score without freshness metadata can be dangerously reassuring.

Current-product caution

Recent Okta announcements discuss expanded identity threat and posture capabilities, including integrations associated with acquired technology. Announced integration does not establish that every capability is shipped, available in all regions or governed by the same SLA. The release registry from Chapter 2 must remain the source of availability truth. [O13]

Acceptance tests. Disconnect a collector, merge two same-name users incorrectly, run a rule against stale data, remediate with a stale version and suppress a finding indefinitely. Verify that coverage gaps and uncertainty remain visible and that remediation cannot cross tenant boundaries.

44. Administrative control, custom roles, Aerial and support access

The administration plane is the highest-value target

An administrator can change authentication policy, enroll factors, add federation, grant privileges and export data. Our admin console and API therefore require stronger assurance than ordinary end-user access. Critical actions use recent phishing-resistant proof, narrow roles and complete audit trails.

Okta Aerial provides a multi-org administration experience through a dedicated management context. Our analogous cross-organization console should be treated as a separate high-impact control plane, not a convenience page with unrestricted background credentials. [O37]

Roles and resource sets

A role defines actions; a resource set defines where those actions apply. An assignment joins actor, role, resource set, conditions and validity. Custom roles cannot grant permissions the assigning administrator lacks. Evaluate authorization on every server-side API call, including bulk exports and hidden console endpoints.

Support delegated administration of groups, apps, partner realms and governance campaigns. Avoid one global 'admin' boolean. Separate key administration, security policy, billing, reporting and user recovery because they have different risks and owners.

High-risk changes

Adding a federation provider, changing a signing key, weakening MFA or assigning a super administrator can affect many users. Require a semantic change preview, strong recent authentication and, for selected policies, dual approval. Record the old and new configuration digest and the decision context.

Protect the last viable administrator recovery path without making it an unreviewed backdoor. A tenant should be able to configure multiple independently secured emergency accounts, test them and receive alerts on use. Our own corporate emergency access must not depend entirely on the customer-facing service it is meant to repair.

Customer support access

A support engineer begins with no standing access to customer data. A customer or authorized incident process grants a scoped, expiring capability tied to a case. The session is recorded or otherwise fully audited, and actions are limited to the approved resource set. Viewing configuration is different from changing it.

Avoid broad impersonation. Where a diagnostic simulation is necessary, use synthetic or explicitly delegated contexts and label them. Never let support retrieve a user's password, passkey private key or live refresh token. Redact uploaded artifacts before they enter general support storage.

Multi-org management

A global console can inventory organizations, aggregate status and request access, while actual writes still require an authorized context in the target organization. Cache only minimal metadata centrally. A compromise of the inventory service should not automatically authorize writes to every customer org.

Cross-org bulk changes need dry runs, per-org approvals where required, staged execution and partial-failure reporting. Do not retry a partially successful operation as an unbounded global command. Record which organization accepted which configuration version.

UX as a safety mechanism

Make dangerous actions visually distinct and explain their blast radius. Show active organization and tenant clearly to prevent administrators editing the wrong environment. Search results and copied URLs must preserve tenant context. A secure API can still be operationally dangerous if the console makes production and staging indistinguishable.

Acceptance tests. Have a delegated admin call a hidden full-admin API, change a resource set during an operation, use an expired support grant and compromise the multi-org inventory credential. Verify that authority remains scoped and that customer-visible evidence identifies every privileged action.

45. System Log, audit evidence, reporting and SIEM

Security evidence starts at the transaction boundary

An identity platform must explain who changed what, which policy allowed a login and whether access was actually removed. Our audit system begins with a durable event written with the authoritative state change. Search indexing is asynchronous, but losing a search cluster must not lose the underlying security event.

Okta's public guidance describes a 90-day System Log retention window and export paths such as API or log streaming. Governance record retention differs. Our own retention promises must be explicit by data class and product, not one blanket number. [O38] [O28]

Event schema

An audit event contains event ID, tenant, timestamp, actor, actor type, target, action, outcome, request ID, policy version, authentication context and relevant object versions. For delegated actions, record both human subject and acting service or agent. Keep outcome and reason separate so analysts can distinguish a legitimate denial from a system failure.

Do not log passwords, bearer tokens, full assertions, private keys or unnecessary personal data. Use internal grant IDs and carefully designed fingerprints for correlation. Treat IP addresses, device details and user behavior as sensitive data with access and retention controls.

Integrity and retention

Archive event batches in encrypted object storage with retention protection and integrity manifests. Hash chaining or signed manifests can reveal tampering, but only if roots and verification processes are independently protected. 'Append-only' at the application layer does not stop an administrator with unrestricted storage permissions.

Retention policies separate operational logs, customer audit, security investigations and legally required evidence. Deletion workflows must account for each. Avoid promising immediate deletion from immutable backups while also promising long fixed retention; define how access is restricted and records expire.

Search and reporting

Build search projections keyed by tenant and time, with indexed action, actor, target and outcome. Enforce tenant filtering in the service, not only the client query. Bound expensive searches and exports so a large investigation cannot exhaust authentication resources.

Show ingestion lag and coverage. A search returning no events may mean no activity, delayed indexing or a broken pipeline. Dashboards should make those states distinguishable. Reports are versioned queries against defined data snapshots so results can be reproduced.

Customer streaming

Log streams and SIEM exports use scoped credentials, destination validation, durable offsets and replay windows. At-least-once delivery requires event IDs for deduplication. A destination outage creates visible lag and escalation rather than silently dropping events.

API pagination uses stable cursors or documented ordering semantics. A customer exporting while new events arrive should not miss records because an offset shifted. Provide checkpoint examples and warnings about filtering out events needed for incident reconstruction.

Detection engineering

Security rules should consume normalized audit events and preserve links to raw evidence. Test detections with synthetic events and controlled exercises. Version rule logic and suppressions. An alert without enough context to identify the principal, tenant and action creates expensive investigation work.

Acceptance tests. Crash after a state commit, delay search indexing, duplicate event delivery, change retention policy and attempt cross-tenant export. Verify durable capture, honest lag reporting and consistent redaction. An audit trail is a security product, not a side effect of application logging.

46. Cryptography, KMS, HSMs and key management

Design a key hierarchy, not a collection of secrets

Our cryptographic architecture separates token-signing keys, data-encryption keys, key-encryption keys, client credentials, connector secrets and audit-integrity keys. Each has a purpose, owner, access policy, rotation process and compromise response. Never reuse one secret across unrelated protocols merely because both need a signature.

Envelope encryption

Encrypt sensitive records with data keys, and protect those data keys through a managed key service or HSM-backed hierarchy. Include tenant and purpose as authenticated encryption context. Decryption requests verify the caller's workload identity and authorization for that context.

Per-tenant keys can reduce blast radius and support customer-specific controls, but they increase key count, rotation complexity and recovery dependencies. A shared key hierarchy with tenant-specific data keys may be an appropriate initial design. The choice depends on threat model, contract and operational capacity, not a generic claim that one pattern is always secure.

Token signing service

The signing interface accepts a typed issuance request from authorized token services. It enforces key purpose, issuer and algorithm policy and records the issuance ID. Avoid an unrestricted 'sign these bytes' endpoint accessible to every workload.

A remote HSM call for every token can become a latency and availability bottleneck. Options include sufficiently provisioned HSM signing, carefully controlled local signing keys or hierarchical arrangements with short-lived delegated keys where supported. Evaluate compromise scope, throughput, compliance requirements and rotation before choosing. Do not claim HSM use automatically makes the whole service compliant.

Secret access and rotation

Connector credentials are decrypted only by the appropriate execution worker and retained in memory for the minimum practical duration. Secret rotation has pending, verified and active states when the destination must change too. Keep rollback or repair instructions for partial rotation failures.

Customer-managed key features introduce another failure mode: the customer may disable a key or break permissions. Define what stops working, how cached keys are handled, whether backups remain recoverable and how emergency support operates. Key revocation is powerful, but it does not necessarily erase plaintext already present in caches or authorized external systems.

Availability and disaster recovery

Keys must be available in permitted recovery regions under a controlled replication or rewrapping process. A perfect database backup is useless if its decryption keys are lost. Conversely, copying every key to every region undermines residency and isolation goals.

Test key-service outage, throttling, permission errors and accidental deletion. Serving caches can reduce dependency latency but extend exposure after revocation. Document cache lifetimes and emergency flush behavior. Security-sensitive key-state changes need stronger propagation than routine configuration updates.

Cryptographic agility

Store algorithm identifiers and key versions, but do not allow untrusted inputs to choose arbitrary algorithms. Maintain an approved suite, migration tests and inventory of all cryptographic uses. New algorithm adoption requires interoperability and performance evidence. Avoid homemade cryptography; use reviewed libraries and applicable validated implementations where required.

Acceptance tests. Disable a key, rotate during peak traffic, restore encrypted backups into a recovery cell, ask the signer to use the wrong key purpose and attempt cross-tenant decrypt. Verify both denial and recoverability, and prove that key administrators cannot silently bypass normal data-access policy.

47. Secure engineering, supply chain and support-system lessons

The platform includes the company's own systems

An identity provider can be compromised through source control, CI, employee endpoints, support tools or deployment credentials, not only through its login API. Our security program treats corporate IT and customer-facing infrastructure as connected risk domains with carefully limited trust.

Okta's November 2023 root-cause disclosure described support-system access involving files containing session tokens. Its later November update expanded the known exposure to contact information for support-system users, with stated exceptions. These are distinct findings; repeating only the original narrow scope would be misleading. [O41] [O42]

Secure development lifecycle

Every feature begins with a threat model covering assets, actors, trust boundaries, abuse cases and security invariants. Protocol and cryptographic changes receive specialist review. Automated checks include dependency scanning, secret detection, static analysis, fuzzing and tests for tenant isolation and authorization.

Security review is not a final meeting before launch. Requirements such as revocation, audit, recovery and rate limits must be designed before the data model hardens. A feature without a safe rollback or operational owner is not production-ready.

Build and deployment chain

CI workers use short-lived workload identities, isolated execution and minimal repository permissions. Build artifacts are immutable, signed or otherwise provenance-verified and tied to reviewed source. Production deployment verifies provenance and approval rather than trusting a mutable image tag.

Separate the ability to change source, approve deployment and access production data where feasible. Emergency deployment paths are explicit, limited and audited. A compromised developer laptop should not possess standing credentials that can rewrite every production cell.

Support artifacts

HAR files, screenshots, logs and database exports can contain credentials and sensitive customer data. Provide a sanitization tool before upload and run server-side inspection as defense in depth. Quarantine suspicious artifacts, restrict access by case and expire them according to policy.

Do not rely solely on customers to remove every secret correctly. Support tooling should display token fingerprints or sanitized metadata rather than raw credentials. Any discovered live credential triggers a containment workflow and notification according to the incident process.

Corporate identity and emergency access

Use phishing-resistant authentication, device controls and short-lived privileged access for employees. Keep personal browser profiles and unmanaged credential synchronization away from privileged work. Customer production access requires purpose-bound elevation, not ordinary corporate SSO alone.

Maintain independently secured emergency access for repairing the identity platform itself. Test that the emergency path works during a customer-facing outage without becoming a permanently available bypass. Monitor and review every use.

Incident response

Prepare playbooks for signing-key compromise, cross-tenant exposure, leaked support tokens, malicious connector code, dependency compromise and regional failure. Preserve evidence, contain safely, identify affected tenants and communicate what is known, unknown and changing. Do not infer that absence of one log proves absence of access when telemetry coverage is incomplete.

Customer communication and incident scope can evolve as evidence improves. Keep a timeline of findings and corrections. Security commitments should include investigation quality and transparent updates, not just a promise to publish an impressive postmortem.

Acceptance tests. Simulate a malicious dependency, compromised support account, leaked deployment credential and missing audit source. Verify isolation, emergency revocation, evidence preservation and a practiced customer-notification workflow.

48. Storage, partitioning, caches and consistency

Classify data by the consequence of staleness

Identity data is not one homogeneous workload. A stale display name is inconvenient; a stale revoked administrator can be a security incident. Our storage design assigns each data class an authority, consistency requirement, retention policy and recovery objective before choosing a database.

Partitioning

Begin with a tenant-aware relational schema and partition the fleet by cell. Within a cell, shard when measured size or throughput requires it. A tenant placement record maps tenant to cell and, if necessary, shard. Keep that mapping versioned and cacheable; it should not require a global database lookup for every token request.

Large tenants may need dedicated shards or cells. Avoid scattering one ordinary tenant's security transaction across many shards unnecessarily. Co-locate principal, grant and replay state when atomicity requires it. A globally distributed primary key does not solve cross-shard transaction design.

Tenant-qualified foreign keys prevent an app assignment for tenant A from referencing a principal in tenant B. Every uniqueness constraint expresses its real scope: login name within a directory or tenant, external subject within a connection, and idempotency key within an operation domain. Use immutable internal IDs so renaming a user does not rewrite the identity graph.

Hot and cold paths

Token issuance uses small indexed reads and short writes. Large profile search, certification campaigns and reporting use replicas or derived stores. Reserve database connection pools for security-critical traffic and bound transaction duration. A bulk import should not hold locks that block refresh-token rotation.

Store large audit bodies and recordings outside the transactional database, with metadata and integrity references inside it. Use retention-based partitioning for high-volume event data. Measure index amplification and write costs before allowing arbitrary tenant-defined indexes.

Cache design

A cache entry includes tenant, object identity, version and expiry. Cache-aside can work for ordinary profile reads, but security-sensitive invalidation needs more than best effort. A revocation event updates a local security epoch or deny structure; high-risk operations verify current authority if freshness cannot be established.

Do not use an evictable cache as the only record that a code or assertion was consumed. Eviction would turn replay protection into a probabilistic feature. If a cache accelerates a durable replay ledger, a miss must consult the authority, not assume unused.

Negative caching needs care. Caching 'user not found' can delay legitimate onboarding, while caching 'not revoked' can extend compromised access. Define separate TTLs and invalidation rules. Prevent stampedes with request coalescing and bounded refresh work.

Consistency during failover

A replica that has not received a password reset or refresh-token consumption cannot safely serve all security operations. Replication lag is not just a performance metric; it describes potential resurrection of old authority. Our failover gate checks a security-state watermark and revocation journal before enabling sensitive writes.

A relational primary with asynchronous regional replication is a reasonable cost and latency choice, but it cannot honestly promise zero cross-region data loss. A synchronous multi-region security ledger offers stronger durability at higher latency and partition-related availability cost. State the tradeoff instead of using 'active-active' as a substitute for a consistency model.

Schema evolution

Use expand-migrate-contract changes. Add compatible fields, deploy readers and writers that understand both forms, backfill in bounded batches and remove old fields only after all versions are retired. Security semantics must remain compatible: a missing new field should default safely, not accidentally grant authority.

Acceptance tests. Evict replay caches, fail a replica before a revocation arrives, migrate a tenant while writes continue and deploy mixed schema versions. Verify tenant isolation, monotonic security state and predictable behavior under stale reads. Storage correctness is the foundation for every feature above it.

49. Transactions, outbox, events and idempotence

The database commit and the event must agree

A common failure is committing a user disablement and then crashing before publishing its event. Another is publishing an event and rolling back the state change. Our transactional outbox records the business change and its event in one database transaction. A separate publisher reads committed outbox rows and delivers them at least once.

Event envelope and ordering

Each event carries tenant, event ID, aggregate ID, aggregate version, event type, schema version, occurred time and causal request ID. Partitioning by aggregate preserves useful local order without pretending the entire platform has a single global sequence. Consumers compare versions and handle gaps or out-of-order delivery.

A consumer writes its deduplication record and local effect in one transaction when possible. A crash before commit causes a safe retry; a crash after commit sees the deduplication record. For an external API effect, this local transaction cannot make the remote world atomic. Use destination idempotence or reconciliation.

Idempotency keys

Administrative create APIs store a request digest and result under a tenant-scoped idempotency key. Repeating the same key and body returns the same logical result. Repeating the key with a different body returns a conflict. Keys have documented retention and cannot be reused forever without a lifecycle policy.

The key must include the authorization context or be checked against it. An attacker cannot guess another caller's key and retrieve their result. Sensitive response material is not retained casually; some operations should return a resource reference rather than replay a secret.

Leases and fencing

Workers claim tasks with an expiry and a fencing generation. A stale worker may continue running after its lease expires, so a lease alone does not prevent duplicate effects. The destination or authoritative state must reject operations from an old fencing generation when that mechanism is available.

For external systems that cannot enforce fencing, design operations to converge and detect ambiguity. A provisioning worker may safely set a desired value repeatedly but cannot assume a repeated 'create' is safe. Store correlation and verify actual destination state.

Sagas and compensations

A lifecycle plan coordinates multiple local transactions. Each completed step records its effect and any available compensation. Compensation runs under current security policy and may require approval for destructive actions. It is not guaranteed to recreate the exact pre-operation world.

For example, offboarding should not roll back local disablement merely because one SaaS app failed to deactivate. The safe action is to keep local access blocked and escalate the residual downstream access. A generic saga engine needs domain-specific compensation rules.

Replay and repair

Keep event schemas backward-compatible or provide explicit upcasters. A replay against a new consumer version should not resend all customer emails or reprovision deleted accounts accidentally. Separate rebuilding a projection from executing external side effects.

Repair tools operate through authorized commands with dry-run reports and bounded scope. Never ask an operator to delete random queue messages or edit state rows manually without preserving evidence. A replay request itself is an auditable administrative action.

Backpressure

Queues absorb bursts but do not create infinite capacity. Monitor oldest-message age, per-tenant backlog, retry rate and dead-letter count. Prioritize security revocations over low-value analytics. When a destination is down, circuit-break it rather than repeatedly consuming global worker capacity.

Acceptance tests. Crash at every boundary around commit, publication, external call and acknowledgment. Run two workers with overlapping leases. Replay old schemas and rebuild a search projection without redoing side effects. The deliverable is a demonstrated invariant, not a claim of 'exactly once' based only on a message broker feature.

50. Edge networking, DNS, DDoS defense and fair admission

The edge is a security and reliability boundary

The edge terminates public traffic, applies coarse abuse controls, resolves tenant routing and forwards authenticated metadata to serving cells. It should reject malformed or oversized requests before they reach expensive parsers or password verifiers. Its own global reach also creates a potential common-cause failure.

Okta's published rate-limit documentation describes limits by endpoint and other dimensions rather than one universal platform QPS number. Our product similarly defines quotas by operation, tenant, client and workload, with clear response headers and supportable capacity plans. [O39]

Routing and DNS

Custom domains map to a verified tenant and a versioned placement. The edge does not trust arbitrary host-forwarding headers. A cell independently verifies the route so a compromised or stale edge cannot trivially cross tenant boundaries. Preserve exact issuer behavior across regional routing.

DNS failover has caching and propagation limits. A changed record does not instantly move every client. Connection reuse, resolvers and client behavior must be tested. Health checks should test meaningful authentication paths, not merely whether a load balancer returns HTTP 200.

Use redundant authoritative DNS and carefully managed certificate issuance where justified. But multiple providers do not automatically create resilience if they share credentials, configuration automation or the same erroneous zone update. Test provider failure and bad-change recovery separately.

Admission control

Apply cheap checks first: method, path, size, syntax and coarse source limits. Then apply tenant and client budgets before expensive authentication or signing. Token buckets or equivalent mechanisms can support bursts while enforcing sustained limits. Avoid hard global locks in the rate-limiter itself.

Different operations consume different resources. Password verification is memory and CPU intensive; token signing may depend on HSM capacity; log export uses bandwidth and storage reads. A single requests-per-second limit cannot protect all three effectively.

Fairness under attack

Reserve capacity for each tenant's normal critical journeys and isolate extraordinary bursts. A large customer should not consume every worker simply because it pays for a high quota, nor should a low-tier customer's attack block everyone else. Queueing disciplines and separate pools enforce the policy.

Protect account-level limits from malicious lockout. An attacker should not be able to disable a user's login indefinitely by making bad guesses. Combine throttling with risk, stronger proof and safe recovery. Expose useful retry guidance without revealing valid accounts.

DDoS and dependency failures

Volumetric defense, WAF rules and bot controls belong at layers capable of absorbing the traffic. Origin services still need capacity limits because application-layer attacks may look syntactically valid. A challenge provider is another dependency; define what happens when it fails.

Origin endpoints should not be directly reachable through an unprotected alternate hostname. Administrative and support interfaces need separate access controls. Webhook and connector egress restrictions prevent the platform from being used to attack internal or external targets.

Observability

Track accepted, rejected, queued and dropped requests by reason, tenant tier and endpoint without creating unbounded metric cardinality. Provide per-tenant detail through logs or controlled analytics. Distinguish legitimate quota exhaustion from platform overload.

Acceptance tests. Flood password verification, issue oversized SAML documents, exhaust one tenant's quota, fail DNS health checks and route with an old placement version. Verify that unaffected tenants retain their SLO and that abuse defenses cannot be bypassed through a secondary domain.

51. High availability, disaster recovery and safe restoration

Availability and recoverability are different properties

Within a region, our cell runs stateless services across three failure domains, with a transactional store that supports safe leader failover and enough spare capacity to survive one domain loss. Across regions, choose an explicit recovery design based on data-loss tolerance, latency, residency and cost. Never describe two writable replicas as safe merely because they are both running.

Public Okta recovery documentation distinguishes standard and enhanced behavior and identifies unsupported products. It also says infrastructure recovery is not a remedy for every DDoS, third-party or configuration problem. Our platform needs separate plans for infrastructure loss, malicious deletion and bad configuration. [O03]

Two defensible consistency choices

Regional primary with asynchronous standby. This offers low local latency and simpler operations. Cross-region RPO is bounded by measured replication and backup progress, not zero by assumption. Promotion risks losing recent security changes unless a separate durable journal or conservative recovery policy addresses them.

Synchronous cross-region security state. A quorum-replicated ledger can preserve acknowledged code consumption, credential changes and revocations across a region loss, provided the quorum survives. It adds latency and can refuse writes during partitions. Geographic placement determines whether a single-region loss leaves a quorum. This design is more expensive and must be benchmarked under realistic network conditions.

Our initial reference design uses regional transactional authority plus a separately protected security-change journal and explicitly bounded recovery promises. A premium zero-loss security-state profile is a later architecture gate, not a checkbox applied to the same asynchronous design.

Fencing before promotion

Failure detection does not prove the old primary is dead. Before enabling writes in a recovery region, establish exclusive authority through a consensus-backed ownership epoch, storage fencing or another demonstrably safe mechanism. Old writers must be unable to commit or issue accepted credentials under the new epoch.

The recovery controller checks replica position, security journal watermark, key availability, policy version, tenant residency and downstream dependencies. It starts in a restricted mode if freshness cannot be proven. Global routing changes only after the cell passes synthetic journeys and fencing checks.

Read-only is not enough for authentication

Even when administrators cannot edit profiles, authentication writes security state: code consumption, challenge replay records, refresh lineage, lockout counters and audit events. A failover design that restores only a read replica may be unable to authenticate safely. Either provide a writable, authoritative security-state path or explicitly disable operations that require it.

A conservative recovery mode can permit selected previously established low-risk sessions while blocking recovery, new enrollment and privileged changes. This is a product policy with clear customer communication, not an improvised workaround during an incident.

Backups are not replicas

Replication copies accidental deletion and malicious changes. Maintain encrypted, immutable or retention-protected backups in a separately controlled account, with tested point-in-time recovery. Protect decryption keys and restore tooling independently. Regularly restore into an isolated environment and verify data and security invariants.

A restore can resurrect revoked users, consumed refresh tokens and old credentials. Replay the security and deletion journals before serving. Where freshness cannot be recovered, invalidate affected credential families or epochs and require reauthentication. Preserve issuer and key behavior deliberately; restoring old signing material can create new trust problems.

Failback and drills

Failback is another migration, not a reverse DNS flip. Establish the authoritative write history, reconcile changes, fence the outgoing region and move traffic gradually. Test failback as often as failover; many designs prove only the first half.

Measure detection, decision, fencing, promotion, validation, routing and customer recovery separately. RTO should name its start and end conditions. RPO should name the data class. A five-minute routing change is not necessarily five-minute restoration of every authentication and administration feature.

Acceptance tests. Partition regions without killing either, lose a region during token redemption, restore before a password reset, disable the recovery key and simulate a bad global policy. Record residual access and data loss honestly. A recovery promise is credible only when the exact failure mode has been exercised.

52. Release engineering, canaries and zero-downtime changes

A release is a security event with a blast radius

Code, configuration, policy, connector packages, device clients and database schemas all change behavior. Our release system treats them as versioned artifacts with ownership, approval, test evidence and rollback conditions. A configuration-only change can lock out more customers than a code change.

Immutable promotion

Build an artifact once from reviewed source, record provenance and promote the same digest across environments. Environment-specific secrets and configuration are injected through controlled references. Do not rebuild production from a branch that may have moved since staging tests.

Deployment identities are short-lived and scoped to a cell or environment. A release orchestrator requests the necessary capability; it does not hold a permanent all-production administrator key. Every stage records who approved it and what evidence passed.

Canary strategy

Start with synthetic tenants and internal test populations, then low-risk consenting cohorts, then a small cell subset. Observe real user journeys, protocol errors, policy deny changes, token issuance and downstream effects. Aggregate latency alone can hide a severe regression for one authenticator or tenant configuration.

Canaries should cover diversity: custom domains, SAML variants, large directories, passkeys, delegated AD authentication and customer extensions. A canary population using only the simplest login path provides false confidence.

Automatic stop conditions

Define rollback or pause thresholds for error-budget burn, unexpected denial spikes, false allows, tenant-isolation alerts and data corruption. Security regressions can demand an immediate stop even when availability improves. A deployment should not continue because an unrelated aggregate dashboard remains green.

Use feature flags for controlled activation, but treat flags as production configuration. Flag evaluation should not require a global network call on every login. Store signed, versioned local state and retain a safe fallback. Remove obsolete flags to reduce the number of untested combinations.

Database and policy compatibility

Expand-migrate-contract schema changes allow old and new binaries to coexist. Backfills run with rate limits and pause on serving impact. Rollback is safe only while old code can interpret new data. Mark irreversible migrations and require a forward-repair plan.

Policy changes use semantic diffs, simulations and impact estimates. Reverting a policy may not undo grants already provisioned downstream; those need explicit remediation. Similarly, rolling back a connector binary does not erase an account it created.

Device and connector releases

Endpoint agents need rings and platform-specific rollback. Connector releases need destination compatibility tests and canary integrations. Keep a support matrix and end-of-life process. Security updates may need accelerated rollout, but accelerated should still mean controlled and observable.

Zero planned downtime as an engineering goal

Rolling updates, connection draining, backward-compatible APIs and replicated capacity can avoid many maintenance outages. They do not eliminate dependency failure or every schema migration risk. Promise customer-visible availability based on measured journeys, not on whether the deployment command exited successfully.

Acceptance tests. Deploy mixed versions, roll back after a backfill, change a security flag globally, break one SAML integration and update a desktop client on a canary ring. Verify that the release system stops at the intended boundary and preserves enough evidence to explain the failure.

53. Observability, SRE, performance and chaos engineering

Observe customer journeys and security invariants

A healthy CPU graph does not prove users can sign in. Our observability starts with synthetic and real-user journeys: identify, authenticate, step up, issue a token, launch an app, refresh and revoke. Measure availability per tenant cohort, region, protocol and authenticator, with privacy-aware aggregation.

SLI implementation

A journey has a defined start, terminal outcome and timeout. Distinguish user cancellation, legitimate policy denial, invalid credentials, dependency failure and platform error. Do not count a software-induced denial as success merely because the response was HTTP 200. Security false allows are tracked separately and receive immediate attention.

Latency is decomposed into edge, policy, directory, authenticator, external provider, signing and persistence time. Report server processing separately from human interaction, but retain end-to-end user experience metrics. A fast server with a broken push delivery path is not a good authentication experience.

Logs, metrics and traces

Propagate request and causal IDs across synchronous and asynchronous work. Traces carry sanitized identifiers, not bearer tokens or full profiles. Metrics avoid unbounded user-level labels; detailed per-tenant diagnosis uses controlled logs and analytical queries.

Record queue age, replica lag, key-service latency, policy-cache freshness, agent health and downstream revocation lag. For each dependency, monitor both availability and correctness indicators. A risk provider returning empty data quickly can be worse than a visible timeout.

Error budgets and alerting

Use multi-window burn alerts to detect both fast outages and slow degradation. Page on customer impact or imminent loss of a critical invariant, not every transient pod restart. A sustained budget burn pauses risky releases and creates corrective work with an owner.

Low-volume tenants need synthetic checks and carefully designed time-based indicators because request-based ratios can hide outages when no one is actively logging in. Global aggregates should not mask a cell or a small regulated customer. Customer-facing status must accurately describe scope.

Performance engineering

Load tests cover login bursts, refresh traffic, bulk imports, certification scans, key rotation and emergency revocation. Use realistic tenant size distributions and expensive policy cases. Benchmark steady state and failure state; losing one availability zone changes queueing and database load.

Track p50, p95 and p99, but also maximum queue age and timeout rates. Capacity plans include CPU, memory, database IOPS, connection counts, signer throughput, network egress and external quotas. Autoscaling is not instantaneous, so maintain headroom for the time needed to add capacity.

Chaos exercises

Inject failures in a controlled environment and then carefully scoped production drills: dead database leader, stale replica, delayed event stream, DNS failure, unavailable HSM, broken customer hook and clock skew. State the expected outcome before the experiment. A chaos test without an invariant is merely disruption.

Exercise security failures too: expired keys, replayed codes, wrong-tenant routing and stale policy after revocation. Verify that emergency controls work while ordinary administration is degraded. Record detection and repair time, not only whether the service eventually recovered.

Incident operations

A primary responder, incident commander, communications owner and relevant specialists have clear roles. Use a timeline, status updates and a decision log. After recovery, analyze contributing conditions and assign durable corrective actions. Repeated 'human error' explanations indicate a missing system control.

Acceptance tests. Demonstrate that a single-tenant outage pages the correct team, a token leak never appears in traces, a false-allow test triggers a security incident and a region drill produces an auditable SLO report. Observability should prove the platform's claims, not merely decorate dashboards.

54. Privacy, compliance, sovereignty and data lifecycle

Compliance is scoped evidence, not a logo

An identity company handles sensitive personal and security data across products, regions and subprocessors. Public trust materials describe product-specific controls and documentation, but certifications and contractual commitments must be checked for the exact service and deployment. This book does not assert that a proposed architecture is certified by design. [O40] [O09]

Data inventory and minimization

Catalog profile attributes, credentials, device signals, risk history, logs, recordings, support files and model-training data. For each, record purpose, legal or contractual basis, owner, residency, retention and deletion mechanism. Avoid collecting identity documents or biometric data when a provider result or public-key proof is sufficient.

Default logs should contain the minimum necessary for security and operations. A debugging feature that captures entire authentication payloads can create a second credential database. Restrict and expire exceptional diagnostics.

Residency and subprocessors

Trace data through storage, backups, telemetry, support and external verification providers. A tenant's regional setting must influence execution and egress, not just database placement. Document cross-region recovery and support access explicitly. Customer-managed keys do not by themselves guarantee data residency.

Maintain a subprocessor inventory and change process. A new SMS provider, analytics service or support system can alter the data flow even when the core application code is unchanged. Security and privacy review should be part of procurement and connector onboarding.

Retention, deletion and legal holds

Define retention by data class and customer agreement. Operational deletion disables access and removes active records; backup expiry and immutable audit retention follow their documented schedules. A narrowly controlled deletion journal prevents restored backups from recreating deleted operational identities.

Legal holds preserve specific evidence under authorized process. They should not keep the account active or permit ordinary product access. Explain conflicts between deletion requests and required retention, and involve qualified legal and privacy professionals for jurisdiction-specific decisions.

Evidence generation

Controls need evidence: access reviews, key rotation, restore tests, change approvals, vulnerability remediation, incident exercises and supplier reviews. Generate evidence automatically from the systems that enforce the control. A manually maintained spreadsheet saying backups were tested is weaker than signed restore-test results linked to the actual backup.

Separate control design, operating effectiveness and external assessment. A policy document is not proof that the policy was followed. A certification for one product or environment does not automatically cover a newly acquired service or an EA feature.

Customer contracts and shared responsibility

Contracts should state what the platform protects and what customers must configure: application token validation, downstream session behavior, administrator security, connector permissions and recovery contacts. Avoid vague shared-responsibility language that hides critical requirements in fine print.

Support data exports, access requests and deletion workflows with authenticated, auditable APIs. Verify the requester's authority, especially for organizations containing many end users. An unauthorized 'privacy export' can become a bulk data breach.

Acceptance tests. Trace a tenant's data through a recovery drill, delete a user then restore a backup, export audit records under a scoped role and enable a new external provider. Verify that residency, retention and access policies are actually enforced. Obtain deployment-specific legal and compliance review before making regulated-service promises.

56. Building the company: teams, support, billing and trust

The company is part of the system design

A reliable identity service needs product engineering, SRE, security, integration engineering, client engineering, support, customer success, legal, privacy, finance and sales operations. A small founding team can build a focused product, but cannot responsibly promise every protocol, endpoint agent, global region and 24x7 specialist response at once.

Team boundaries

Organize around durable responsibilities: core identity and protocols; directory and lifecycle; device and authentication clients; governance and privileged access; customer identity and developer experience; platform reliability; and security engineering. Teams own APIs, data, runbooks, SLOs and incident response, not just feature delivery.

At an early stage, several responsibilities can share one team. Split when cognitive load, deployment risk or on-call burden requires it. A modular application with clear ownership is often healthier than many services with no reliable owner.

On-call and support

Build a sustainable rotation with primary and secondary coverage, escalation specialists and incident command. A rotation with too few people becomes fragile during illness, leave and long incidents. Follow-the-sun support can improve coverage, but handoffs need shared timelines and clear ownership.

Support tiers define coverage hours, response targets, escalation and customer responsibilities. Do not sell a thirty-minute response without staffing and paging infrastructure to meet it. Distinguish response, mitigation, restoration and root-cause timelines in both contracts and dashboards.

Support engineers need safe diagnostic tools and training in identity attacks. They should not improvise recovery bypasses to close tickets faster. Customer satisfaction metrics must be balanced with security review and override monitoring.

Billing and entitlements

Meter agreed business units such as active users, managed identities, premium features, machine usage or workflow executions. Define deduplication, time windows and late-event handling. Billing data is derived from durable usage events and is reconciled; it is not the sole synchronous authority for a login decision.

Entitlements are versioned contractual capabilities cached in serving cells. A billing-system outage must not disable established authentication. Commercial suspension uses a deliberate process with notices and protected access to recovery and security administration. Fraud or security containment follows a separate path.

Sales and solution engineering

Solution engineers validate customer architecture, migration constraints, protocol compatibility, data residency and downstream revocation expectations before a commitment is signed. Maintain a capability and limitation register. A sales promise that bypasses engineering review can create an impossible SLA or unsafe integration.

Offer reference integrations, migration tooling and test environments. Complex enterprise adoption often depends on deployment and change management as much as protocol correctness. Track time to successful production use, not just contract signature.

Trust operations

Maintain security questionnaires, evidence packages, incident communications and a public status process. Publish product-scoped commitments and explain how customers can export their data and migrate away. Trust is strengthened by accurate limitations and operational evidence, not by unsupported claims of perfect security.

A vulnerability disclosure process has intake, triage, remediation and researcher communication. External assessments and relevant certification work are planned expenses. Customer contracts should align with the actual deployment and support model.

Unit economics and prioritization

Measure cost and support burden by feature and customer segment. A connector with little adoption but constant repair can consume disproportionate engineering time. A premium feature that introduces global synchronous dependencies may cost more in reliability than in compute.

Acceptance gate. Before expanding a product family, identify its on-call owner, specialist support path, security reviewer, migration story, documentation budget and measurable cost model. A company like Okta is an ecosystem of operated products, not one large authentication codebase.

Build the differentiating system, not every primitive

Use reviewed cryptographic and protocol libraries, managed infrastructure where appropriate and established operating-system facilities. Choose one wedge: for example, workforce SSO for a defined application set, or developer CIAM for a specific B2B segment. Exit only after conformance tests, tenant-isolation tests, restore drills and external security review. Do not call the product enterprise-ready because ten happy-path demos succeeded. Capacity and error budgets should be measured on the exact supported journeys.

Stage 2: supportable production

A roughly nine-to-eighteen-month planning phase can add resilient cells, SCIM, lifecycle reconciliation, delegated administration, durable hooks, customer exports and stronger operational tooling. Scale support and on-call before widening the SLA. Launch new regions only after residency, key recovery and incident procedures are tested there.

Machine identity belongs early enough that internal services and connectors do not accumulate shared permanent credentials. Advanced customer code execution and endpoint agents can wait until isolation and release processes are mature.

Stage 3: portfolio expansion

Governance, Workflows, device clients, privileged access and advanced CIAM are separate substantial programs. Over an illustrative eighteen-to-thirty-six-month horizon, prioritize based on customer need and team expertise. Each gets its own threat model, product owner and operational gate.

Agent identity can reuse workload ownership, token exchange, governance and credential brokering. Start with constrained tools and explicit delegation rather than promising a universal autonomous-agent security layer. Version rapidly evolving integrations and maintain tested compatibility profiles.

Stage 4: mature global operation

Broader portfolio parity, dedicated deployments, specialized compliance environments, deep connector coverage and premium recovery profiles are multi-year investments. A three-to-five-year planning horizon may still be optimistic depending on scope and staffing; treat it as a scenario to refine through evidence, not a promise.

The hardest work often appears after initial launch: migrations, customer-specific edge cases, incident learning, external vendor changes and safe deprecation. Reserve capacity for maintenance rather than assigning every engineer to new features.

Build-versus-buy review

Evaluate each dependency on security control, portability, latency, availability, compliance scope, operational burden and exit cost. Managed databases reduce some operational work but do not design your consistency model. A third-party SMS provider removes telecom integration work but adds availability, fraud and spend dependencies.

Record decisions as ADRs with alternatives and reversal triggers. Revisit when scale, regulation or customer requirements change. Avoid choosing technology solely because a historical Okta whitepaper named it.

Release gate. Every stage must demonstrate a safe failure story, not just a larger feature list. Expansion should stop when unresolved security or reliability debt threatens existing customers.

58. Worked end-to-end example: hire, login, delegate and offboard

The scenario

Northstar is a fictional customer using our platform. Maya joins its finance team, signs in to payroll with a passkey, requests temporary invoice-approval access and uses an approved analysis agent. Later, a device compromise is detected and Maya eventually leaves the company. This trace shows how the chapters fit together.

1. HR creates the person

The HR connector observes employee HR-1042 at source version 18. It submits a tenant-scoped observation. The directory creates principal P-77, records HR provenance and keeps the account pending until its start date. A transaction writes both the principal change and the lifecycle outbox event.

The lifecycle plan derives baseline groups and desired application accounts. SCIM workers provision payroll and expenses with stable source correlation. Payroll creates the account but its response is lost. The connector queries by its supported correlation key, verifies a single match and records the destination ID without creating a duplicate.

2. Activation and passkey enrollment

On the start date, policy rechecks HR status and activates the principal. Maya redeems a purpose-bound invitation and completes the approved initial proof. The WebAuthn service creates an enrollment challenge tied to P-77, the tenant and the login RP. After verification, it stores credential W-9 and activates it.

The enrollment event notifies an existing verified channel and updates the audit stream. No fingerprint data is stored by our server. A second tab replaying the enrollment response cannot create a separate active credential transaction.

3. Payroll SSO

Payroll redirects Maya to the authorization endpoint with registered client, redirect, state and PKCE context. The platform resolves Northstar's tenant from the verified hostname, creates transaction T-201 and evaluates policy version 44. A passkey challenge succeeds with the required user verification.

Before issuing the code, the engine checks that P-77 remains active and that no urgent deny applies. The token endpoint atomically consumes code C-301, commits grant G-401 and returns audience-bound credentials. Payroll validates its ID token and creates its own application session. That session is distinct from the platform session.

4. Temporary privilege

Maya requests invoice approval for four hours. Request R-501 identifies the exact entitlement version and duration. The manager and resource owner approve. The conflict checker verifies that Maya does not also hold a prohibited supplier-creation role, including pending approved grants.

The grant ledger creates E-601, the connector provisions it and the system verifies destination state. The expiry timer is durable. If the destination later refuses removal, the request remains 'expired, remediation pending' rather than falsely reporting full completion.

5. Agent delegation

Maya starts an approved finance-analysis agent. The platform authenticates agent principal A-88, validates its runtime proof and checks the connection policy. A derived grant permits read-only access to selected invoice data under Maya's authority, with a specific audience and short lifetime.

A document retrieved by the agent instructs it to export all records to an external URL. The execution gateway rejects the destination and action because tool output cannot expand policy. The model never receives the vault's refresh credential.

6. Compromise response

A trusted endpoint signal reports compromise of Maya's device. The receiver validates and correlates it to device D-12, creates incident I-701 and applies response policy. New authentication from the device is blocked, relevant sessions and refresh families are revoked, and the agent's delegated access is stopped.

Universal logout requests are sent to supported applications. Payroll confirms termination; an older reporting app does not support remote logout. The incident view shows that residual risk and the maximum relevant token/session window. Security staff can apply an application-specific containment step rather than assuming everything ended.

7. Offboarding and recovery safety

HR later reports termination at version 29. The directory increments the principal security epoch and rejects future activation from older source observations. The lifecycle engine disables downstream accounts, removes privileged grants and reassigns or suspends owned agents. Each destination has a verified outcome or an escalation.

A regional disaster then requires restoring an older backup. The recovery process replays the security and deletion journals before routing traffic. P-77 remains terminated, consumed refresh generations remain invalid and agent delegations are not resurrected.

The trace's invariants

Stable IDs connect HR observation, principal, authentication, grant, approval, agent and incident. No external API call is assumed atomic with our database. Every privilege has provenance. Every revocation has an enforcement boundary. Every restore rechecks security history. Those properties, more than any particular technology choice, make the platform coherent.

59. Conformance, adversarial tests and a practical learning lab

Test contracts, not just endpoints

A production identity platform needs protocol conformance, tenant-isolation tests, property tests, fuzzing, load tests, recovery drills and human workflow exercises. Unit tests for a successful login cover only a small part of the risk. Build an executable specification of the invariants introduced in Chapter 1.

Protocol and parser tests

Use version-pinned standards and relevant conformance suites. Test OIDC issuer and audience validation, PKCE, redirect handling, SAML signature structure, WebAuthn challenges, SCIM patch semantics and supported advanced grants. Negative tests matter as much as happy paths.

Fuzz untrusted parsers with size and time limits: JSON, XML, JOSE, filter grammars, metadata and callback parameters. Run parsers in contexts that cannot access unexpected networks or files. A parser crash should not bring down an entire cell.

Property and concurrency tests

Express properties such as 'one code produces at most one committed grant', 'a tenant-A credential never reads tenant-B data' and 'a terminated principal cannot be reactivated by an older source observation'. Generate random sequences of operations and failures, not only handpicked examples.

Use deterministic scheduling or repeated concurrency tests for refresh rotation, approval conflicts and worker leases. Database constraints should enforce the invariant even when application code races. Test serialization failures and retries explicitly.

Integration matrix

Maintain tested combinations of browsers, native platforms, authenticators, upstream IdPs and destination applications. Connector compatibility includes vendor-specific pagination, deactivation and duplicate behavior. An integration test should verify the actual business outcome, such as the destination refusing login after disablement.

Customer-provided extensions run through sandbox escape, network access and resource-exhaustion tests. Desktop agents need boot, offline, update and recovery tests on real supported operating systems. Mocked cryptographic proof is useful for unit tests but insufficient for platform compatibility.

A staged learning lab

Start with a local, isolated tenant and two sample applications. Implement an opaque session and OIDC code flow with PKCE using mature libraries. Add a relational code-consumption transaction and prove concurrent redemption safety. Next add passkey enrollment and a second application to observe independent app sessions.

Then build a mock SCIM destination that deliberately loses responses and returns rate limits. Implement desired-state reconciliation and offboarding. Add a durable outbox, a simple approval workflow and a relationship-authorization check. Finally simulate a stale restore and prove that the revocation journal prevents account resurrection.

All exercises should use owned test systems and synthetic users. Do not test real customer tenants or third-party services without authorization. The source bundle includes an educational concurrency model and schema examples; these are learning aids, not a deployable identity provider.

Operational exercises

Run a signing-key rotation, expired-certificate incident, dead directory-agent incident, region partition and support-token exposure drill. Include support and communications staff. A technically correct recovery can still fail customers if nobody knows what to communicate or which dependency is affected.

Exit criteria

Before GA, require independent security review, supported-profile conformance, measured SLOs, restore evidence, on-call readiness, customer documentation and a signed capability matrix. Before a new feature reaches GA, repeat the relevant subset rather than assuming the original platform assessment covers it.

Acceptance gate. A release candidate fails if it creates a false allow, crosses a tenant boundary, loses a committed revocation or cannot recover from its documented failure modes. Performance improvements do not compensate for broken identity invariants.

The final review is evidence-based

A production-readiness review asks whether the team can operate the service safely at the promised scale and failure conditions. Each answer points to a test result, dashboard, runbook, approved design or contract. 'We use a managed service' is not evidence that our own integration is recoverable.

Readiness checklist

Area Evidence required before launch
Identity correctness Tenant isolation, replay resistance, policy and account-linking tests
Protocols Supported-profile conformance and interoperability matrix
Data Ownership, constraints, migrations, retention and restore tests
Reliability Journey SLIs, capacity under failure and alert routing
Security operations Key compromise, account takeover and support-access runbooks
Recovery Fencing, security-state watermark, backup and failback evidence
Customer operations Documentation, migration, export, support and status process
Commercial scope Capability registry, exclusions, SLA measurement and cost model

Runbook: token issuance outage

Confirm customer impact by tenant and protocol. Check edge admission, database authority, policy freshness and signer health. Stop risky releases. Do not disable signature validation or bypass grant checks to restore a green dashboard. Route to a healthy serving path only when ownership and security state are valid.

If the signer is unavailable, use the approved redundant signing path or fail safely. If a key is compromised, follow the compromise runbook rather than ordinary availability failover. Record what credentials may have been issued and what customers must invalidate.

Runbook: offboarding backlog

Identify principals with overdue downstream deactivation. Confirm local authentication and refresh are already blocked. Prioritize privileged destinations, inspect connector authentication and rate limits, and reconcile ambiguous outcomes. Escalate unsupported or unavailable destinations to their owners with explicit residual access.

Do not re-enable the principal to make provisioning retries easier. Repair through the connector's scoped service identity. Close the incident only after the agreed enforcement condition or an approved compensating control is verified.

Runbook: suspected cross-tenant exposure

Contain the affected path, preserve evidence and revoke implicated service capabilities. Determine whether the issue is read exposure, write corruption or credential issuance. Use tenant-scoped forensic queries and avoid creating a second exposure through broad exports. Security and legal teams coordinate notification based on verified scope and applicable commitments.

A lack of observed access is not proof of no exposure when logs are incomplete. Communicate uncertainty accurately and update the scope as evidence develops. Fix the root authorization or routing boundary, not only the triggering request shape.

Core architectural decisions

Keep ADRs for cell boundaries, tenant partitioning, code-consumption authority, refresh retry policy, JWT revocation, cross-region consistency, key hierarchy, extension isolation and support access. Each ADR contains context, alternatives, chosen tradeoff, consequences, tests and reconsideration triggers.

For example, choosing short-lived JWTs reduces resource-server dependence on introspection but accepts a bounded offline revocation window. The ADR must name that window and which applications require stronger enforcement. A decision without consequences is incomplete documentation.

Risk register

Track risks such as shared global configuration, weak customer recovery, incomplete connector coverage, stale security-state replication, endpoint-agent rollback, external provider concentration and unsupported downstream logout. Assign an owner, severity, current mitigation, next action and review date.

Do not bury known limitations in an appendix that sales and support never read. The capability registry, contract process and admin UI should expose the operationally important ones.

What 'done' means

The platform is ready when supported journeys work, unsupported journeys fail clearly, security changes propagate within stated bounds, failures are contained, data is recoverable without resurrecting authority, and the company can support what it sells. Reaching that state is an ongoing engineering discipline, not a one-time architecture diagram.

The most useful reading loop is to implement a small slice, test its failure modes, inspect the evidence and then expand. The remainder of this book provides schemas, algorithms and exercises to make that loop concrete.

Read the schema as an invariant map

Example: consuming a one-time object

BEGIN;
UPDATE authorization_code
   SET state = 'reserved', issuance_id = :issuance_id
 WHERE tenant_id = :tenant_id
   AND code_hash = :code_hash
   AND state = 'unused'
   AND expires_at > :trusted_now;
-- Require exactly one updated row.
-- Commit an issuance intent or complete the chosen issuance protocol.
COMMIT;

This fragment is only the atomic reservation. The caller must first validate the client, PKCE proof, redirect binding and transaction, then safely complete signing and grant commit as discussed in Chapter 9. A reservation with no recovery state can strand legitimate requests after a crash. A reservation that automatically expires back to unused can create replay risk if a token already escaped.

Index and access strategy

Index exact lookups used on the serving path: tenant and subject, credential ID, token hash, grant ID and active session. Index durable jobs by due time and state, and outbox rows by unpublished status. Keep bulk analytical scans off the primary serving workload. Measure query plans on realistic tenant-size distributions, including the largest supported tenant.

Every repository method takes an explicit authenticated tenant context. Avoid utility functions such as get_user(id) that silently omit tenant scope. A safer interface is get_principal(context, principal_id, required_freshness), where the context cannot be constructed from an arbitrary unverified request field.

Row-level security and service roles

Database row-level policies can provide defense in depth, but connection pooling and session-variable handling need careful tests. A reused connection must not retain the previous tenant's context. Separate migration, support, analytics and serving database roles. No ordinary serving role should disable row-level protection or inspect all secrets.

Data migration protocol

An identity migration records source snapshot, target mapping, validation counts, rejected records and security-state watermark. Perform a dry run, compare stable identifiers, then migrate in bounded batches. Verify credentials and app mappings separately from profile attributes. A migration that preserves names but changes subjects can break account ownership even when row counts match.

B1. Authorization-code redemption

redeem_code(request, verified_tenant_context):
    client = authenticate_or_identify_client(request)
    assert client belongs to verified_tenant_context
    code = load_code_by_hash(tenant, hash(request.code))
    validate_expiry_and_transaction(code)
    validate_client_redirect_and_pkce(code, request, client)
    recheck_principal_status_and_urgent_denies(code.subject)

    intent = transaction:
        reserve_unused_code_with_compare_and_swap(code)
        create_issuance_intent(bound_request_digest)
        append_security_outbox_event("issuance.reserved")

    signed_result = bounded_sign(intent.approved_claims)

    result = transaction:
        lock_issuance_intent(intent.id)
        recheck_required_security_epochs()
        commit_grant_and_refresh_family_if_requested()
        mark_code_consumed_and_intent_committed()
        append_security_outbox_event("grant.created")

    return signed_result_only_after_successful_commit

The recovery worker must know whether the result was signed, committed or returned. It must not reopen a consumed code. Any briefly retained response-recovery material is encrypted, access-controlled and bound to the original client and request. A strict implementation may instead require restarting authentication after an ambiguous response; document the usability tradeoff.

For offline JWTs, avoid returning a signed token before commit, because a resource could accept it without consulting your database. The design must also prevent a compromised internal path from obtaining arbitrary signed but uncommitted credentials.

B2. Strict refresh rotation

refresh(request):
    authenticate_client_and_validate_sender_proof_if_required()
    locate_token_generation_by_tenant_and_hash()

    transaction:
        lock_family()
        deny_if_family_or_grant_revoked_or_expired()
        recheck_principal_and_tenant_security_epochs()
        if presented_generation_is_not_current_active:
            revoke_family()
            append_event("refresh.reuse_detected")
            commit_then_return_denial()
        consume_current_generation()
        create_next_generation_with_random_secret_hash()
        create_bound_issuance_record()
        append_event("refresh.rotated")

    complete_issuance_under_the_selected_signing_protocol()

Proposed freshness classes

Class A: authority-changing state. Credential reset, termination, role revocation, code consumption and refresh lineage require the strongest available consistency for the affected decision. Fail closed or use a documented restricted mode when freshness cannot be established.

Class B: bounded decision context. Device posture, risk features and some group snapshots may be cached for a policy-defined lease. The decision records the evidence age. Expired evidence becomes unknown, not automatically safe.

Class C: descriptive and analytical data. Display names, dashboards and historical reporting can tolerate eventual consistency when freshness is visible and they do not become hidden authorization inputs.

RPO by data class

A platform can tolerate losing a recent analytics projection if it can rebuild from the durable log. Losing an acknowledged credential revocation is fundamentally different. Our contracts should not use one vague 'RPO' for both. Specify whether the recovery guarantee covers primary records, security changes, audit capture, search and external effects.

For an asynchronous cross-region design, measure the last durably replicated security watermark. If the watermark cannot cover acknowledged changes, either disclose the potential loss, recover from an independent journal or invalidate the affected authority conservatively. Never label the design zero-RPO solely because backups exist.

Error-budget calculations

monthly_time_budget_seconds = days_in_month * 86400 * (1 - SLO)
request_error_budget = valid_requests * (1 - SLO)
burn_rate = observed_bad_fraction / allowed_bad_fraction

Time-based and request-based measures answer different questions. A quiet tenant can experience a long outage with few failed requests. Use both appropriate synthetic coverage and customer-request indicators. Define how no-traffic intervals, planned changes and external dependencies are handled before a dispute arises.

Dependency scorecard

For each third party, record its role, regions, quotas, timeout, circuit breaker, fallback, data shared and incident contact. A fallback must preserve the required security properties. A second SMS provider may improve delivery resilience, but falling back from a passkey to SMS changes assurance and cannot be treated as a transparent availability improvement.

Minimum incident evidence

An incident timeline should include detection, confirmed scope, affected journeys, containment decisions, ownership changes, customer updates and recovery verification. Preserve the exact deployed versions and relevant watermarks. Distinguish platform availability, correct authorization and downstream enforcement throughout the report.

Security-critical changes require explicit reviewer sign-off on the relevant invariants. Keep test fixtures versioned so an SDK, parser or policy change can reproduce earlier failures.

The educational state model in the source bundle runs four tests: concurrent single-use code redemption, tenant-bound consumption, atomic disablement plus outbox, and strict refresh-generation reuse. Passing those tests proves only those simplified transitions. It does not prove OAuth compliance, cryptographic safety or production readiness.
