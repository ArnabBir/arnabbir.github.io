This book follows local commerce from discovery and an accepted basket through merchant execution, Dasher assignments, physical custody, customer outcomes and reconciled obligations. It then opens the infrastructure, data, ML and operating systems behind that journey.

The central distinction

An observation is not a promise. A dispatch proposal is not a committed assignment. A committed order is not proof of delivery. CHAPTER 01

What company are we actually building?

The question. How do we build a local-commerce platform rather than a restaurant-ordering demo?

Published evidence

DoorDash reported 970 million consolidated orders for Q2 2026. Its 2026 platform updates describe shared infrastructure being rolled out across DoorDash, Wolt, and Deliveroo, not a completed single global application. Deliveroo's acquisition completed on October 2, 2025; SevenRooms' completed on June 13, 2025. Ownership and technical integration are different facts. [S01] [S02] [S03] [S79]

The reference design

Think of the company as three interacting systems. A commerce system establishes what can be bought, from whom, at what price, and under which terms. A logistics system finds a feasible way to perform work in the physical world. An obligation system records what every participant is owed and whether those obligations have been discharged. Customer applications, merchant tools, and Dasher applications are different views of these systems, not three independent businesses glued together by notifications.

A restaurant order requires menu modifiers, kitchen acceptance, preparation time, pickup, and a short freshness window. A grocery order requires uncertain shelf availability, substitutions, weighted quantities, and a final basket that may differ from the initial authorization. Merchant-owned ordering needs delivery without marketplace discovery. A stocked fulfillment center needs receiving, bins, picking, shrinkage, and inventory accounting. Reservations and customer relationship software need table availability and consented guest history rather than courier dispatch. Reuse identity, money, experimentation, observability, and integration primitives where their contracts align; do not pretend these domains share one lifecycle.

A sensible initial product boundary is one geography, one merchant type, one fulfillment model, and a narrow set of supported payments. Build a correct restaurant marketplace first, including merchant rejection and Dasher cancellation. Expand into retail only after the order model supports amendments and item-level outcomes. Add merchant-owned channels through the same logistics commitment API, but with separate commercial terms and integration credentials. Global expansion is a compatibility and operating-model problem, not merely translating strings.

How to read this book

Every chapter separates published evidence from an original reference design. Named systems and publication dates identify what DoorDash has disclosed. Proposed schemas, algorithms, targets, diagrams, and operating procedures explain how a company could implement the capability; they are not leaked internals. Source references lead to a dated registry. Product eligibility and API access must be rechecked before integration.

The illustrations describe responsibilities and trust boundaries, not the company's complete private deployment. Numbers marked illustrative are deliberately constructed examples. The executable companion proves selected local invariants only. Building a production company additionally requires operational teams, merchant and payment partners, regional review, safety processes, and repeated recovery exercises.

CHAPTER 02

Architecture evolution without a fictional universal stack

The question. Which public architecture claims can safely be combined?

Published evidence

DoorDash's 2020 migration account describes moving beyond a monolith. A 2021 checkout article describes Kotlin, Cassandra, Kafka, Cadence, and gRPC for that service. Later publications discuss a fulfillment migration to CockroachDB, a broad service-mesh migration, and a 2026 clusterless feature store. These are domain-specific snapshots with different dates and motivations. [S04] [S05] [S20] [S42] [S55]

The reference design

Create an architecture evidence ledger before drawing a technology map. Each entry has a claim, publication date, domain, environment, reported maturity, and known limits. “Used by checkout in 2021” is a much narrower statement than “the order database is Cassandra today.” A paper about a prototype, evaluation, or planned rollout does not establish company-wide production adoption. A performance improvement measured for selected endpoints is not a universal multiplier.

The most useful architectural lesson is the progression of constraints. An early monolith offers cheap local transactions, simple deployment, and fast product iteration. Growth exposes shared database contention, inconsistent ownership, expensive deployments, and fault coupling. Service extraction improves isolation only when data and operations move with the service. Otherwise, a network boundary has merely been added around a shared schema. A platform layer becomes worthwhile when several domains repeatedly solve discovery, metrics, secrets, streaming, or feature serving in incompatible ways.

For a new company, begin with a modular commerce application and separately isolate the location stream, dispatch worker, payment connector, and notification worker. Use a relational transactional core while its operational envelope fits. Extract a domain when its scaling pattern, security boundary, release cadence, or ownership makes the additional distributed-system complexity worthwhile. A speculative microservice per database table creates coordination work before it creates capacity.

Keep an architecture decision record for each extraction. Document the source of truth, event contract, migration order, rollback boundary, and deletion of the former write path. During a migration, compare results without executing customer-visible effects twice. Traffic shadowing is safe for pure reads; shadowing a refund or merchant order submission is not. A database cutover needs a final watermark, source fencing, verified target completeness, and an escape plan that does not resurrect stale writers.

Reading performance claims

Classify every number by denominator. Consolidated orders per quarter, internal service requests per second, model evaluations, feature reads, and database operations are different measurements. A single customer journey can generate many of the latter. Geography, acquired brands, peak windows, request fan-out, and asynchronous enrichment alter the relationship. Never size the platform by multiplying an unrelated published number by an arbitrary safety factor.

CHAPTER 03

The domain model and its non-negotiable invariants

The question. What must remain true when everything else is delayed, duplicated, or wrong?

Published evidence

The published checkout and dispatch accounts solve different responsibilities: progressing an order through durable work and selecting useful delivery assignments. Public Drive events also distinguish courier confirmation, pickup, drop-off, and cancellation. These distinctions motivate, but do not reveal, the complete private entity model. [S05] [S06] [S32]

The reference design

Use separate identifiers for a consumer purchase, a merchant order, a fulfillment task, a courier route plan, a payment attempt, and an accounting journal. A purchase may contain orders from two stores. One courier route may carry several orders. An order can have multiple payment attempts but only an explicitly bounded total captured amount. A replacement delivery is a new task linked to the original incident, not an overwrite that makes the first attempt disappear.

Represent the commercial lifecycle as DRAFT -> SUBMITTED -> ACCEPTED -> FULFILLED, with explicit rejection, cancellation, and partial outcomes. Represent the operational lifecycle independently: UNPLANNED -> OFFERED -> ASSIGNED -> PICKUP_STARTED -> PICKED_UP -> DELIVERED, with unassignment, return, and exception states. Payment has its own authorization, capture, refund, and unknown-outcome states. A customer-facing status is a projection of these facts and should never become the source of truth for any of them.

The central logistics invariant is one compatible active plan authority per Dasher, not one order per Dasher. A valid batch intentionally contains multiple orders. Each active order assignment belongs to one committed plan version, and every accepted revision preserves capacity, pickup-before-drop-off precedence, required certifications, and already completed stops. An old optimizer result cannot overwrite a newer accepted plan.

Commercial invariants include immutable accepted terms, explicit consent for material amendments, unique operation identities, currency-preserving money arithmetic, and refunds bounded by captured funds allocated to the relevant tender. Accounting journals balance independently per currency. An event may be delivered repeatedly, but its business effect is applied once according to a durable operation key. Safety and restricted-item checks cannot be bypassed because a cache or classifier is unavailable.

Model time carefully. Store event occurrence time, server observation time, commit time, and an aggregate sequence. Device clocks are untrusted. An offline pickup report can arrive after a cancellation request; ordering by ingestion timestamp alone produces a false history. Record both and resolve according to the transition contract, with a human exception path where physical evidence conflicts.

CHAPTER 04

The high-level design: separate decisions from commitments

The question. How should requests, durable state, and physical execution fit together?

Published evidence

DoorDash has described service cells, multi-cluster deployment, and a separate shared-services path. Its newer mesh account describes a broad migration to common traffic infrastructure. The exact private placement of every service and database is not public. [S43] [S42]

The reference design

Put consumer, merchant, courier, and partner traffic behind distinct authenticated gateways and backend-for-frontend adapters. The edge terminates transport, applies quotas, identifies the market and tenant, and preserves a correlation identifier. It does not decide whether an order was accepted or a refund is valid. Those decisions belong to domain services with authoritative data.

The synchronous discovery path reads store availability, search indexes, menu snapshots, predicted delivery ranges, and benefit estimates. Those responses can tolerate bounded staleness and graceful degradation. The checkout path must revalidate the accepted commercial contract and create a durable purchase intent. The logistics decision path reads order constraints, courier observations, preparation forecasts, and traffic estimates; it outputs proposed plan changes. A separate commitment service checks current revisions and records accepted assignments transactionally.

Partition the platform into market-serving cells. A cell contains enough commerce, logistics, event processing, and operational state to continue bounded service when another cell fails. Keep global configuration, identity directories, feature artifacts, and analytical workloads outside the critical local path where possible. Replicate signed configuration snapshots into cells rather than synchronously calling a global configuration database for every delivery. Shared capabilities need explicit capacity and failure budgets because they can defeat cell isolation.

A cell is an ownership and blast-radius boundary, not necessarily one Kubernetes cluster or one availability zone. A deployment may span several zones. The storage quorum determines which failures can be survived without conflicting writers. Market movement and brand migration require an authoritative routing epoch; changing DNS alone is not ownership transfer. Cross-cell access should use declared APIs or asynchronous replication, never invisible direct reads into another service's database.

Use events to maintain read models, train models, reconcile operations, and fan out notifications. Commit the source state and its outgoing event atomically within the owning store or use a verified changefeed with equivalent coverage. Subscribers track application-level identities. Keep analytical failures from blocking already committed fulfillment, while retaining enough audit evidence to reconstruct every high-impact decision.

Plane Typical work Failure behavior
Serving Browse, quote, order status Degrade optional enrichments
Decision Forecast, rank, optimize Use bounded fallback proposals
Commitment Accept order, assign plan, post money Preserve invariants; fail safely
Control Rollouts, policy, routing ownership Continue signed last-known-good configuration within limits
Review exercise. Unplug the model-serving platform. The system should still be able to retrieve existing orders, complete assigned deliveries, process valid evidence, and calculate already-defined obligations. It may quote more conservatively or stop accepting new complex work.

CHAPTER 05

Reliability contracts: availability is not delivery quality

The question. What does reliable service mean for a business that moves real things?

Published evidence

Public engineering posts discuss overload, observability, and fault injection. Drive documentation also contains customer delivery expectations. None of the sources reviewed establishes one universal public contractual uptime SLA for every DoorDash product. A delivery-time field or product-specific promise is not an infrastructure availability contract. [S44] [S45] [S46] [S29]

The reference design

Define three layers. A service-level indicator is a precisely measured quantity: the fraction of valid checkout submissions durably acknowledged within a deadline. A service-level objective is the internal target for that indicator. A service-level agreement is an external commitment with scope, exclusions, remedies, and a measurement method. Never market a proposed engineering objective as a verified DoorDash guarantee.

Track both correctness and usefulness. A successful HTTP response that creates the wrong basket is not good availability. A fast dispatch proposal that cannot be committed is not successful assignment. An order that arrives late because the restaurant is overloaded is a product-quality failure even when every server is healthy. Conversely, a customer who intentionally cancels should not be counted as an infrastructure outage. Preserve numerator and denominator definitions in version-controlled metric specifications.

An illustrative starting contract might target 99.95% valid checkout acknowledgment within two seconds, 99.99% retrieval of active-order status within one second, and 99.9% eligible assignment decisions within their configured decision deadline. These are proposed targets, not promises, and require representative load and dependency testing. Measure downstream merchant acceptance and final delivery distributions separately. Set a freshness objective for location and inventory, a backlog-age objective for event processing, and a zero-tolerance invariant for double assignment or unbalanced journals.

Error budgets clarify the cost of a target. In a 30-day period, 99.9% availability allows 43.2 minutes of bad time; 99.95% allows 21.6 minutes; 99.99% allows 4.32 minutes; 99.999% allows 25.92 seconds. Request-based budgets differ from these time-based illustrations. A tiny market should not disappear inside a global average, and a dinner rush should not be treated as equivalent to an idle minute.

Use multi-window burn alerts. A rapidly exhausting budget pages responders; sustained slower degradation creates a tracked incident. Stop risky releases when the budget is depleted, but permit a reviewed reliability fix. Record dependency budgets: three independent mandatory services each meeting 99.95% do not automatically compose into a 99.95% journey. Correlated zone, configuration, and identity failures make simple multiplication optimistic.

Failure test. Return fast 200 responses while dropping order persistence. The availability monitor must count the journey as failed, not healthy. Use independent synthetic checks plus sampled reconciliation of accepted operation identifiers.

CHAPTER 06

APIs, idempotency and the boundaries of a request

The question. What should a caller do when a network response is lost?

Published evidence

Drive uses caller-provided external delivery identifiers and documents duplicate conflicts. Its JWT reference specifies an authenticated partner contract. These public APIs are useful examples, but the internal APIs below are original proposals. [S29] [S30]

The reference design

Every command that creates an obligation needs a stable operation identifier. Bind it to the caller, tenant, operation type, and a canonical request hash. A retry with the same identity and same payload returns the existing result or current operation state. Reuse with different content returns a conflict. Do not scope idempotency only to a human-readable order number that two merchants might share.

An operation record contains operation_id, principal_id, request_hash, status, resource_id, created_at, completed_at, and a retention policy. Creation of the operation and initial business state belongs in one transaction. A uniqueness conflict is a signal to reread the existing operation, not to generate a new key. Persist a stable response representation or enough fields to recreate it without making a second side effect.

Separate transport acceptance from business completion. 202 Accepted means work is durably scheduled, not that the restaurant agreed or the courier accepted. Return an operation URL and a stable resource identifier. Use explicit states such as PENDING, SUCCEEDED, REJECTED, and OUTCOME_UNKNOWN. A timeout after submitting a payment or merchant request should usually produce uncertainty requiring lookup or reconciliation, not a blind replacement request.

For updates, require the expected resource revision. An amendment to basket revision 4 cannot silently apply to revision 6. For plan acceptance, include the offer identity, route revision, authority epoch, and acceptance deadline. Server-side checks use trusted time and current state. A client timestamp is useful evidence but not authorization to revive an expired offer.

Keep retry responsibility at one controlled layer with a deadline, exponential backoff, jitter, and a retry budget. Nested retries multiply load. Retry safe reads and commands whose idempotency contract is complete; do not assume every POST becomes safe because a header named Idempotency-Key exists. External providers may have narrower key-retention windows or different payload-conflict behavior. Store their identifiers and align reconciliation procedures with those contracts.

CHAPTER 07

Consumer, merchant and Dasher applications

The question. How do three applications stay coherent without requiring perfect connectivity?

Published evidence

DoorDash has published a mobile-release process and, in 2026, a modular Dasher-onboarding platform with backend-for-frontend and server-driven interface elements. The onboarding article explicitly distinguishes current programmatic workflow definitions from future configuration-driven workflows. [S49] [S17]

The reference design

Give each client a task-oriented backend. Consumers need discovery, basket editing, price explanations, and delivery tracking. Merchants need new-order alerts, acknowledgment, preparation changes, substitutions, and reconciliation. Dashers need offers, navigation, step completion, evidence capture, and earnings explanations. Reusing a common domain API does not require returning the same payload or navigation model to all three clients.

Represent server responses as versioned resources with capability flags, not fragile assumptions about the newest application release. An older Dasher application may not support a new restricted-item handoff. The server must then exclude that capability from eligibility rather than sending an instruction the client cannot render. Support a documented minimum protocol version and a deprecation process that does not abruptly strand active deliveries.

The client maintains a small local database of active tasks, the last confirmed server revision, and a command queue. Every queued command has a stable ID, creation time, expected revision, and retry policy. Showing “pickup recorded locally, syncing” is more truthful than displaying a final server-confirmed state during an outage. Commands that change money, restricted-item outcomes, or route authority should require fresh server validation before being represented as final.

Use application sessions to order device telemetry. A reconnect may create a new session epoch so that an old buffered GPS packet cannot supersede the new device's location. A push notification is a wake-up hint; the application rereads authoritative state before acting. Notifications can be dropped, duplicated, or delayed. Long-lived streaming improves responsiveness but needs resumable sequence cursors and a snapshot fallback after gaps.

Accessibility and operational clarity matter as much as attractive screens. A driver-facing action must not require reading dense text while moving. Merchant rejection should have explicit reason codes and a visible outcome. A customer amendment should display the changed amount and items, not bury consent in a chat message. Localize currencies, decimal quantities, addresses, time zones, units, and right-to-left layout independently of business rules.

CHAPTER 08

Menus, catalogs and the product graph

The question. How do we represent a pizza with modifiers and a grocery product sold at many stores?

Published evidence

DoorDash distinguishes catalog attributes shared across stores from store-specific inventory such as price and availability. Its product knowledge graph work uses language models to enrich retail product information. The public Marketplace API exposes menu and store-management contracts. [S19] [S21] [S34]

The reference design

Separate canonical product identity from merchant-specific sellable listings. A product may have a brand, size, unit, ingredients, packaging, and taxonomy. A listing maps that product to a merchant SKU with local price, tax category, fulfillment constraints, and availability. A prepared menu item may not correspond to a globally standardized product at all. Keep uncertain mappings explicit rather than merging two items because their names resemble each other.

Model modifier groups as a constrained graph: minimum and maximum selections, required groups, option-specific price deltas, nested choices, mutually exclusive options, and availability windows. Validate graph cycles, unreachable required options, negative quantities, and inconsistent selection bounds during publication. A menu revision must be self-consistent. Atomically switch the store's published revision after the full graph passes validation; do not expose half an uploaded menu.

An accepted order stores a commercial snapshot: item and modifier identifiers, displayed descriptions, selected quantities, unit prices, currency, tax inputs, menu revision, and relevant policy versions. The snapshot remains meaningful when the merchant later renames the item or removes an option. Never calculate a historical refund by rereading today's price from the catalog.

Use a canonical ingestion envelope for POS systems, CSV feeds, merchant edits, and AI-assisted extraction. Preserve raw input, normalized output, validation errors, and provenance. A supplier-provided allergen declaration has different authority from a generated description. An AI model may suggest a category; it should not invent a safety guarantee. Route high-impact metadata through stricter verification and retain the source of each attribute.

The read path can serve denormalized store-menu documents and product search indexes. The write path owns revisions and validation. A content-addressed menu artifact improves caching and makes rollback precise. Publish the artifact reference and a change event together, then wait for downstream index progress before declaring a merchant launch complete. A successful upload is not the same as search visibility.

CHAPTER 09

Inventory is a stream of evidence, not perfect shelf truth

The question. How should the platform react when a merchant feed says an item exists but the shopper cannot find it?

Published evidence

DoorDash's May 2023 inventory platform article describes asynchronous ingestion, raw-feed persistence, catalog enrichment, price calculation, predictive availability, guardrails, and item-level observability. It also states that DashMart and the broader convenience-and-grocery platform had different architectures at that time. [S19]

The reference design

Store inventory observations with source, source sequence, observation time, ingestion time, confidence, and scope. A merchant's daily full feed, a real-time POS delta, a Dasher's item-not-found report, and a fulfillment-center stock count are not equivalent evidence. An older full snapshot must not erase a newer confirmed unavailability signal simply because its file finished processing later.

Use a per-source ordering rule and an explicit conflict policy across sources. For example, preserve the latest valid merchant quantity while maintaining a short-lived suppression after repeated recent item-not-found reports. A predictive availability score can influence ranking and customer warnings without being represented as an exact stock count. Owned warehouse stock, where receiving and picking are controlled, can support stronger reservation semantics than an external grocery shelf.

Ingestion runs through durable batches identified by merchant, store, source file hash, and version. Validate currency, decimal scale, unit-of-measure conversion, extreme price changes, item counts, and suspicious mass deletions. A full-feed importer needs a completion marker before treating missing rows as removed; a truncated file must not empty a store. Stage the new snapshot, compare it with the prior one, and publish only after guardrails pass.

For candidate c, estimate incremental travel, courier waiting, food dwell, customer lateness, acceptance likelihood, and uncertainty. Batching, route insertion and bounded optimization

The question. When is combining deliveries genuinely useful rather than merely cheaper on paper?

Published evidence

DoorDash's routing article discusses multithreading and ruin-and-recreate optimization for larger delivery batches. The dispatch publications also discuss batching as a tradeoff among efficiency, waiting, and delivery quality. [S08] [S07]

The reference design

A route is an ordered sequence of stops, not just a set of orders. Each order contributes pickup and drop-off requirements, time windows, service times, load changes, and possibly a return path. Pickup must precede drop-off. Vehicle load must remain feasible at every position in the route. Already completed stops cannot be moved, and a committed promise constrains how much delay a revision may add.

To insert a new order into a route with n remaining stops, evaluate feasible pickup/drop-off positions while pruning precedence violations and obviously excessive detours. Naive enumeration grows quadratically per insertion, before considering combinations of new orders. Restrict candidate orders geographically and by time window, cache travel matrices, and use cheap lower bounds before detailed stochastic evaluation.

A small illustrative example shows why nearest-first is insufficient. Courier A can reach store X in two minutes and store Y in three; courier B can reach X in three and Y in twelve. Assigning A to X greedily leaves B with Y, for fourteen approach minutes. Assigning A to Y and B to X uses six. Real objectives include preparation, acceptance, downstream stops, and uncertainty, but even this simple matrix demonstrates the cost of independent local choices.

For larger batches, start from a feasible plan, remove selected stops or orders, and rebuild with insertion heuristics. Run several bounded searches with diverse seeds, but ensure shared resources do not create unpredictable tail latency. Every candidate is checked against the same feasibility library used at commitment. The solver's objective and the validator's constraints must not drift into different interpretations of capacity or restricted-item compatibility.

Use uncertainty-aware quality gates. Summing independently predicted median leg times can underestimate correlated congestion or restaurant delay. Stress the route under plausible common shocks, and bound added lateness for orders already promised. Express or freshness-sensitive orders may be incompatible with a batch even if average travel cost improves. The user-facing product contract, not the optimizer's convenience, determines allowed degradation.

The question. Which data belongs in a relational database, a stream, an index, or object storage?

Transactions, contention and partition design

The question. How do we keep correctness when the busiest merchant or courier becomes a hot key?

Published evidence

CockroachDB documents serializable retry behavior and SQLSTATE 40001; PostgreSQL documents row locks and deadlocks. A database can supply strong primitives, but the application still needs a correct transaction boundary and retry policy. [S71] [S72]

The reference design

Keep transactions short, deterministic, and limited to authoritative state. Read and update the rows necessary to enforce the invariant, then commit. Do not wait for a payment provider, routing model, or human acknowledgment while holding database locks. Network calls inside transactions amplify tail latency, lock contention, and uncertainty.

For assignment, lock the courier plan and affected orders in a stable order. For refunds, lock or atomically update the refundable allocation while reserving the amount. For inventory, guard the stock position and reservation together. A read-then-write sequence outside a transaction is unsafe even if each individual query is fast. Uniqueness and check constraints provide a final line of defense but do not replace multi-row business validation.

Partitioning must preserve the common transaction shape. A market-local plan authority can keep courier and order assignment records together, while analytical copies cross boundaries asynchronously. Merchant catalog partitions may use store IDs. Money accounts may require legal-entity and currency dimensions. Avoid forcing every domain into the same key just to simplify infrastructure administration.

Hot counters need special treatment. Updating one global promotion budget row for every impression can serialize the system. Use hierarchical budget allocations or bounded per-partition leases with reconciliation where the business permits controlled approximation. For strict money bounds, retain an authoritative reservation path. Approximate counters are suitable for monitoring or candidate scoring, not for silently overspending a hard limit.

Serialization retries are expected under contention, but a rising retry rate is a capacity and data-model signal. Measure retries, lock wait, transaction duration, abort reasons, and hot-key distribution. Bound attempts by the caller's deadline. If retrying the same transaction repeatedly cannot succeed, reject or queue work rather than creating an endless retry storm. Distinguish a known aborted transaction from an unknown commit outcome after the connection drops.

DoorDash's feature-store and build-platform accounts describe Kubernetes-based infrastructure with workload-specific needs. The reference design

Capacity is the number of requests a service can finish within its latency and correctness contract, not the number of running containers. A newly started process may still be loading models, warming caches, downloading feature snapshots, establishing connections, or catching up state. Readiness must reflect ability to serve representative work rather than simply opening a port.

Separate startup, liveness, and readiness probes. A slow dependency should not necessarily trigger a liveness restart that creates more cold pods and load. Readiness can remove an instance from traffic while preserving it for recovery. Startup probes allow legitimate initialization without premature restarts. Test probes under real failure modes so they do not create a feedback loop worse than the original problem.

Choose scaling signals by bottleneck. CPU can work for compute-bound stateless services, while queue age, in-flight requests, model-specific throughput, or shard load may be better elsewhere. A feature-store shard with a hot key cannot be fixed by adding unrelated shards. A database-bound API may make the database worse when its frontend scales out. Coordinate pod scaling with node capacity and downstream admission controls.

Maintain warm headroom for predictable peaks and zone failures. Dinner rushes and planned events are not surprises. Pre-scale from forecasts, then use reactive controls for residual variation. Account for image pull time, artifact download, scheduling delay, and connection limits. Overly aggressive scale-down can create churn just before demand returns; use stabilization and minimum capacity.

Distribute replicas across failure domains and ensure disruption policies match quorum and serving requirements. A disruption budget protects only certain voluntary eviction paths; it does not make a zone outage safe. Verify that the remaining zones have real resource capacity and that storage leaders, caches, and network paths also survive. Reserve priority for active-delivery and financial-control workloads during contention.

The question. What can we safely learn before exposing a new policy to a real marketplace?

Published evidence

DoorDash's geospatial simulator article describes event-driven simulation of courier movement. Its dispatch publications also discuss simulation as part of iteration. A simulator is useful only within the behavior it represents; it is not automatically a causal model of all human responses. [S10] [S06]

The reference design

Build a discrete-event simulator with demand arrivals, merchant preparation, courier availability, travel, offer acceptance, pickup, delivery, cancellations, and failures. Separate the simulation clock from wall-clock time so experiments are reproducible and fast. Use deterministic seeds and store configuration, input snapshot, policy version, and output metrics for every run.

Start with invariants before realism. No order is picked up before acceptance under the chosen workflow; no drop-off precedes pickup; load stays within capacity; no task has two incompatible active assignments; money allocations remain conserved. These checks catch algorithm and integration errors even in a simple environment. Add realistic stochastic behavior only after the event model is coherent.

Replay historical observations carefully. The old policy influenced which courier accepted, how long food waited, and whether the customer canceled. Reusing those outcomes unchanged under a different policy can produce invalid counterfactual conclusions. Distinguish fixed exogenous inputs, modeled behavioral responses, and outcomes that cannot be identified from logged data alone. Use sensitivity analysis to show where conclusions depend on assumptions.

Calibrate travel and preparation distributions by relevant segments, including tails and correlated shocks. Test sparse markets, dense lunch peaks, storms, merchant outages, delayed GPS, and driver-device reconnects. A simulator trained only on successful normal deliveries underestimates precisely the exceptions that cause operational incidents.

Use simulation as one stage of evidence. It can screen infeasible policies, estimate capacity, and explore failure modes. It cannot replace controlled live experiments, safety validation, or partner certification. Maintain a simulator validation dashboard comparing predicted and observed distributions, not merely averages. When the marketplace changes, update and revalidate the model before trusting old results.

Capacity planning and an illustrative cost model

The question. How much infrastructure does a local-commerce platform need, and what drives its cost?

Published evidence

DoorDash reported 970 million consolidated orders in Q2 2026. Dividing by the 91 days in that quarter gives about 123 orders per second on average. This arithmetic is not a peak rate, an internal request rate, or a sizing recommendation; consolidated scope includes acquired businesses. [S01]

The reference design

Size a new platform from workload assumptions. An illustrative regional system might process 1 million orders per day, support 100,000 concurrently online couriers at peak, and receive one location observation every five seconds from each active courier. That implies about 11.6 average order submissions per second and 20,000 peak location updates per second. With an illustrative eight-times order peak factor, order submission reaches about 93 per second. These are constructed inputs, not DoorDash measurements.

Fan-out dominates many paths. If each order emits forty durable business events, one million orders create forty million events per day before telemetry. At an illustrative one kilobyte per encoded event, that is roughly forty gigabytes of raw payload daily, excluding replication, indexes, compression, and retention overhead. Search and ranking traffic depends on browse-to-order ratio; location volume depends on active sessions and sampling policy, not completed orders.

Use Little's Law as a consistency check: average concurrency is throughput multiplied by average time in system. A service handling 2,000 requests per second at 100 milliseconds average latency has about 200 requests in flight on average. Tail latency and burstiness require headroom. Do not size thread pools from this average alone, and do not confuse waiting concurrency with CPU parallelism.

Build cost by units: compute-hours, memory-hours, database storage/IO, stream retention, object storage and requests, network transfer, model inference, observability, and third-party services. Then allocate cost to orders, active courier-hours, merchant catalog size, and model decisions. Add redundancy and peak headroom explicitly. An illustrative compute line of 500 vCPUs at a hypothetical USD 0.04 per vCPU-hour is USD 14,400 for 30 days; this is arithmetic, not a current cloud quote or DoorDash spend estimate.

Capacity tests should cover peak skew, not uniform load. One large merchant, one dense cell, a hot promotion, or a popular model can exhaust a partition while global utilization looks low. Test a zone loss during peak, cold-cache recovery, model artifact rollout, and backlog catch-up. Cost optimization is successful only if the required correctness and recovery properties remain intact.

Worked example: grocery amendments during a regional failure

The question. Can a changing basket and a changing infrastructure owner remain consistent at the same time?

Scenario: illustrative

A customer approves a grocery basket revision 4 with a maximum authorized total of USD 60.00 and a restricted substitution policy. Courier D7 is shopping under plan revision 21, market epoch 8. One item is unavailable, and the customer is considering a substitute when the primary region becomes unreachable.

Walk through the boundaries

The shopper proposes amendment A2: a specific substitute, quantity, unit, price, and expiry. The customer approves exactly A2. Before the approval response returns, the region fails. The client preserves the operation ID and shows a pending state. It does not assume the approval failed and create a different amendment.

Recovery first fences the former writer and verifies the target's committed watermark. If the approved amendment was acknowledged before failure, the recovery design must preserve it under the chosen durability contract. If its commit outcome is uncertain, the API resolves by operation identity after authority transfer. The new owner uses epoch 9; delayed commands from epoch 8 cannot perform new authoritative writes.

Meanwhile, D7 has found another item and queued a scan offline. That evidence is not a new assignment. It is reconciled against the current task and basket revision when connectivity returns. Because D7 already has custody or active shopping responsibility, the platform does not automatically offer the same task to another courier merely because presence expired.

At store checkout, the final measured basket is USD 58.70. The system validates A2's consent, quantity bounds, tender eligibility, and authorization coverage. If the actual substitute differs from A2, the approval cannot be reused. The receipt upload is deduplicated, the final basket revision is frozen, and the capture operation uses one stable identity. A second receipt upload cannot produce a second capture.

The return of the old region is a recovery event, not permission to resume writing. Its processes are drained or remain fenced. Reconciliation compares task evidence, amendments, provider operations, and inventory observations across the outage window. Unresolved cases receive explicit ownership. The incident review measures customer confusion, duplicate-risk prevented, recovery time, and any remaining evidence gaps.

Incident runbooks and production-readiness gates

The question. What evidence is required before calling this platform production-ready?

Evidence boundary

The runbooks and gates below are original operational recommendations. They are not a claim that the teaching implementation is production-complete or that DoorDash follows these exact procedures.

The reference design

Classify incidents by the threatened invariant. An availability incident blocks useful work. A correctness incident may create duplicate assignments, incorrect prices, or money errors. A safety incident threatens people or custody. A privacy/security incident threatens data or authority. The same event can belong to several classes, and a low HTTP error rate does not reduce its severity.

For assignment corruption, stop the affected new-commit path, preserve current custody, fence suspect writers, identify conflicting plan revisions, and use task evidence to resolve ownership. Do not bulk-unassign active deliveries without understanding where goods are. For payment uncertainty, pause replacement submissions, preserve provider references, and reconcile before releasing reservations. For a bad catalog feed, stop publication, restore the last good generation, invalidate derived views, and review already accepted orders separately.

For model degradation, disable the affected policy/model combination, activate a tested fallback, and preserve decision logs. For a retry storm, reduce admission, disable uncontrolled retries, protect active obligations, and recover dependencies gradually. For a security event, revoke affected authority through a tested path, preserve evidence, and coordinate legal and operational response. Every runbook includes rollback limits and actions that must not be taken.

Production gates require evidence, not declarations. Demonstrate concurrency invariants under races, restore from backup, execute region/zone failure drills, verify partner duplicate handling, reconcile money against external records, validate privacy deletion, and rehearse staffed safety escalation. Test old clients, new schemas, late events, malformed feeds, and degraded model services. Record unresolved risks with an owner and a launch decision.

Relationship rules that deserve database enforcement

A merchant order belongs to exactly one purchase and store. A task references the commercial resource that justified it but can outlive a canceled purchase while goods are being returned. APPENDIX B

Transaction schema and critical algorithms

Purpose. The source bundle contains a deliberately small SQL core and a SQLite teaching implementation. This appendix explains the production contract the examples illustrate. Production SQL, isolation levels, key management, geospatial libraries and consensus mechanisms must be chosen and tested for the actual database and deployment.

A minimal authoritative core

The essential tables are operations, market authority, route plans, offers, offer tasks, assignments, captures, refund reservations, journals, journal entries, outbox events and consumer inboxes. An operations uniqueness constraint on (principal, operation_kind, key) is as important as the key itself. Hash a canonical request containing every parameter that changes the effect. Include the tenant or merchant boundary in authorization and in relevant natural keys.

Store amount_minor as an integer with a currency field. Keep conversion rates and rounding rules as separately versioned terms. Do not sum currencies or accept a currency mismatch because the numeric amounts balance. Retain the commercial revision, policy version and provider operation reference that explain an amount. Negative journal entries are unnecessary in the illustrative schema: use a side and a strictly positive amount, then reverse through a new journal.

A partial unique index can enforce one active assignment per task. A route's revision is a compare-and-swap guard against competing plan changes. A market epoch is a fencing value checked by the authoritative transaction, not merely supplied by a trusted-looking caller. After leadership changes, the old writer must be unable to satisfy the current epoch predicate.

Algorithm 1: accept an offer without partial assignment

BEGIN SERIALIZABLE TRANSACTION
  previous = lookup_operation(principal, "accept", key)
  if previous exists:
      require previous.request_hash == canonical_request_hash
      return previous.result
  authority = lock_market_authority(market_id)
  require authority.epoch == request.epoch
  offer = read_offer(offer_id)
  require offer.belongs_to(principal) and offer.not_expired(now)
  plan = lock_current_plan(offer.dasher_id)
  require plan.revision == offer.expected_plan_revision
  tasks = lock_affected_tasks_in_stable_id_order(offer.task_ids)
  require every new task is assignable
  require current eligibility and full revised-plan feasibility
  write all assignments and the new route-plan revision
  write operation(result) and outbox(accepted_plan_event)
COMMIT

The request fingerprint is checked even on a duplicate. The original successful result is returned before checking expiry, because a retry after a lost response is not a new acceptance. A serializable retry must rerun the whole validation against a fresh snapshot. Never call an external mapping, payment, or push endpoint while locks are held; precompute bounded advice, then revalidate its material assumptions.

Algorithm 2: reserve a refund under concurrency

BEGIN TRANSACTION
  return matching prior operation if it exists
  lock capture and its refund accounting projection
  available = captured - finalized_refunds - active_reservations
  require amount > 0 and currency == capture.currency
  require amount <= available
  insert refund reservation with stable provider reference
  record operation and outbox(refund_requested)
COMMIT
submit the same provider reference outside the transaction

On timeout, preserve UNKNOWN and the reservation. Poll or reconcile the same operation. A verified failure releases the reservation; a verified success converts reserved value into finalized refunded value. Both transitions are idempotent and mutually consistent. A late success after an apparently failed attempt requires an explicit exception workflow, not deletion of the original evidence.

Algorithm 3: emit and consume events

The producer writes state and outbox in one transaction. A relay publishes at least once, then records progress; a crash can repeat publication. The consumer starts a local transaction, claims a unique inbox key, validates aggregate version, updates its projection, and commits the marker with the effect. A crash before commit leaves neither. A crash after commit allows a duplicate delivery to be ignored.

This does not make an email, merchant submission or bank payout atomic with the local transaction. Use a stable connector operation reference, a connector-specific status machine and independent reconciliation. Kafka transaction semantics do not automatically encompass another database or an external API. [S74]

Algorithm 4: accept a location observation

Authenticate the device session and Dasher. Check its server-issued epoch against the active session. Within that epoch, compare sequence numbers atomically. Append acceptable evidence and update the current projection only if the sequence is newer. Keep measured time, received time and accuracy; reject implausibly future timestamps from freshness calculations. A late old-session packet may be retained for investigation without becoming the courier's current location.

Algorithm 5: commit a customer-approved amendment

Resolve the existing operation first. Lock the order revision and amendment. Validate the exact product identifiers, quantities, price limits, consent actor, expiry and expected revision. Check that the logistics and payment constraints still permit the change. Advance the basket revision, record consent and new totals, and emit a single amendment event. A stale agent proposal or shopper screen must receive a revision conflict, not silently overwrite the customer's later decision.

Algorithm 6: validate a journal

Require all entries to use the journal currency, all amounts to be positive integer minor units, and every account to be allowed for the event type. Sum debits and credits and reject inequality. Insert the journal and entries with the unique operation fingerprint in one transaction. A retry returns the prior journal. Corrections create compensating entries linked to the original; permissions must prevent update or deletion of posted entries.

Algorithm 7: publish a catalog or inventory generation

Validate the manifest and schema before changing a serving pointer. For a bulk source, require the expected partitions and completion marker. Apply source-specific sequence ordering so an old delivery cannot displace a newer observation. Apply an explicit precedence policy across sources, not a lexical comparison of unrelated version strings. Publish the generation atomically and retain the former one for rollback. Checkout still revalidates price and eligibility; a serving pointer is not a shelf reservation.

Algorithm 8: recover a market safely

Stop accepting new commitments into uncertain authority. Fence the previous writer at the authoritative data boundary. Verify the recovered watermark and enumerate in-flight offers, committed plans, goods in custody, unknown payment operations and pending amendments. Reconcile using durable events, partner records and device evidence. Raise the epoch only through the chosen authority mechanism, then admit bounded traffic. Any unresolved resource stays in a controlled exception state. Do not improve an uptime graph by double-assigning a delivery already in a Dasher's possession.

Test execution ladder

At the first level, property and transaction tests explore local interleavings, validation predicates and rollback. At the second, integration tests exercise real database isolation, message brokers, schema changes, partner simulators and deployment artifacts. At the third, fault tests introduce network partitions, clock skew, duplicate delivery and regional loss. Finally, controlled production experiments validate assumptions that a simulator cannot know, such as merchant behavior and model impact.

For each invariant, identify which layer can falsify it. A local lock test cannot establish cross-region fencing. An HTTP mock cannot establish partner deduplication. A historical replay cannot prove the causal effect of a new dispatch strategy on Dasher acceptance. Keep those limitations next to the evidence rather than burying them in a release checklist.

Runbook: unknown merchant submission

First stop automated retries that create new external identifiers. Resolve the original connector operation, inspect the documented partner lookup capability, and query by the stable external reference. Compare merchant acceptance, kitchen ticket and cancellation evidence. When the partner cannot establish a definitive outcome, hold the order in a visible exception queue and involve the merchant through an authorized workflow. Do not let the customer place an unnoticed duplicate while support is investigating.

Runbook: dispatch success without client acknowledgment

Look up the acceptance operation by the Dasher principal and original key. A committed result is returned even when the offer is now expired. Push the current plan revision and ask the client to replace its stale view. When no operation committed, fetch current authority and re-offer under the normal feasibility rules. Never infer non-commit solely from the timeout seen by the mobile application.

Runbook: negative or mismatched settlement projection

Quarantine the affected pay period or beneficiary scope while preserving unrelated settlement. Recompute expected obligations from immutable journals and versioned earnings/commercial rules. Compare bank or processor evidence by stable reference and amount/currency. Classify missing, duplicate, timing and allocation differences. Create approved corrective journals where justified. Release payout only after the decision has an accountable owner and evidence; editing the projection to match a spreadsheet is not reconciliation.

Runbook: stale feature publication

Identify the affected model, feature artifact and topology version. Stop advertising unready replicas. Check artifact completeness, checksum, loader failures and online freshness lag. Route to a known compatible version or a pre-approved fallback. Compare decision-quality guardrails, not only inference availability. Preserve the offending artifact and metrics for diagnosis, and avoid triggering a cold-load stampede across every replica simultaneously.

Runbook: regional data loss or isolation

Declare the affected market authority and admission boundary. Fence writers before promoting a new authority. Verify target durability and reconcile committed plans, merchant tickets, customer-approved amendments, goods custody and external financial operations. Reopen progressively, beginning with read access and bounded new work. The incident remains open while unresolved money or custody exceptions exist, even when endpoint uptime has recovered.

Recovery record template

Record detection time, first customer impact, authority epoch, last verified watermark, affected resources, external references, decision owner, containment actions, unresolved ambiguity, restoration gates and final evidence. Include what the system could not establish. This record supports a precise incident review: “requests resumed” and “every obligation reconciled” are separate milestones.

APPENDIX F

Capacity worksheet and architecture decisions

Equations with units

For a chosen planning interval, average order rate equals orders divided by seconds. Peak rate is a measured or explicitly assumed multiplier, not a universal property. Location ingest equals concurrent reporting devices divided by report interval. In-flight concurrency is arrival rate multiplied by average service time when the stable-system assumptions of the estimate hold. Durable storage equals event count times average serialized bytes, then adds replication, indexes, retention and operational overhead.

For queues, estimate the work that accumulates during a degraded interval and the spare capacity needed to drain it while serving new arrivals. A consumer that only matches normal arrival rate never clears a backlog. For model serving, measure distributions of batch size, feature fan-out, cold loading and tail latency. For database writes, count secondary indexes, outbox writes and retries; one business operation is not one disk write.

Launch gates by stage

The first launch needs a complete narrow journey including rejection, cancellation, support and payment uncertainty, not just a polished app. The next stage needs merchant integration certification, operational dashboards, pay-period reconciliation, incident rotations and repeatable releases. Retail expansion requires exact amendment consent, item-level outcomes, weighted quantities and substitution policies. A launch committee should review artifacts rather than slide claims: a successful failover exercise, journal reconciliation, replayed duplicate callbacks, capacity under overload, an accessible cancellation flow, a staffed safety escalation path and a rollback demonstration.
