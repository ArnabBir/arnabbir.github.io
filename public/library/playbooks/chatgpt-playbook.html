I. Foundations

01 A map of the product, not just a prompt box

ChatGPT is best approached as several interacting layers: a conversational interface, selectable models, contextual information, tools that retrieve or transform information, and permissioned actions. A subscription controls eligibility, but it is not a complete description of what an individual session can do. Device, workspace policy, region, connected accounts, rollout state, and remaining allowance also matter.

This publication is an independent field guide and engineering workbook, researched on 27 September 2026. It is not an OpenAI manual, a certification, or a claim to list private experiments. The source registry links 92 official documents. Product statements cite those documents; recipes, evaluation criteria, and the architecture in Part VIII are original recommendations.

Read through three lenses

Feature: What does the product expose? Workflow: How should a person use it to complete an actual task? Boundary: Which data, permissions, tools, or assumptions can make the result unsafe or wrong? Understanding all three prevents a common mistake: treating a convincing answer as proof that the relevant source was accessed or an external action completed.

A generated summary, a saved file, an approved email draft, and a sent email are different deliverables. State the required endpoint before starting. A good work brief says what must exist when the task is complete and what must never happen without approval.

A capability passport

Before a substantial assignment, record the account/workspace, selected mode and model, source locations, connected accounts, allowed actions, required output, and verification method. This is a recommended operating practice, not an additional ChatGPT setting. Keep credentials out of the record.

Use the chapters as a troubleshooting tree: first establish the right mode; then verify context and permission; only then investigate model quality. For a missing capability, check its dedicated help article and the account interface rather than assuming a screenshot from an older tutorial still applies.

Evidence labels used here

Documented describes official public behavior. Conditional identifies plan, region, device, or administrator dependencies. Historical identifies retired experiences. Planned means announced but not yet effective at the research date. Proposed marks our own engineering architecture. Neither diagrams nor example code represent a leaked implementation.

All diagrams are original conceptual illustrations, not screenshots of a particular account. Local companion tests validate only the supplied teaching model. They do not certify ChatGPT, a plugin, an enterprise tenant, or an external integration.

02 Choose Chat, Work, or Codex deliberately

Documented product split

Chat supports conversational help. Work is oriented toward multi-step tasks and deliverables. Codex is the software-development experience in the desktop application, with separate history. Work on web and mobile executes in the cloud; eligible desktop sessions can use local files or applications with permission. Local execution does not mean messages and context never reach cloud storage. Cloud Work conversations can continue across supported surfaces. Work Cloud, Work Local, and Codex Local are separately controlled in managed workspaces. [S002]

A decision method

For an explanation or a tightly scoped rewrite, begin in Chat. For an assignment involving several sources and a finished artifact, choose Work where available. For a repository change that must be tested, choose Codex and name the repository, branch, and acceptance tests. These are recommended choices, not claims that the other modes are incapable of related tasks.

Consider the request: "Prepare a board update from the approved operating data." In Chat, you might refine the narrative. In Work, you can specify source discovery, calculations, document generation, and final verification. In Codex, you might maintain a repeatable report generator. The business outcome is shared, but the execution environment and review burden differ.

Make completion observable

Replace "take care of this" with an explicit contract: produce a draft PDF and its editable source; preserve the numbers in the approved data; cite the relevant rows; do not send or publish anything. Review progress at irreversible boundaries rather than interrupting every harmless calculation.

Keep a distinction between conversation continuity and execution continuity. A conversation appearing on another device is not proof that a local process, file mount, or credential moved with it. Before continuing a local task elsewhere, confirm that the required host and files remain available.

Failure patterns

An excellent answer from the wrong environment can still be unusable. A cloud task cannot be assumed to see an unuploaded local folder. A desktop coding session should not be assumed to appear in ordinary Chat history. When a task says a tool is unavailable, first resolve the actual surface and permission boundary instead of repeatedly rephrasing the request.

A useful handoff includes completed outputs, open questions, source identifiers, the execution environment, and the next action requiring approval. It should not include reusable secrets or a claim that unfinished work has already succeeded.

03 Plans, entitlements, limits, and billing

What a plan does and does not buy

OpenAI publishes consumer Free, Go, Plus, and Pro offerings, and separate organizational offerings. Business has seat options; Enterprise is contract-based. Prices, taxes, currencies, rollout eligibility, and included quantities can change. Use the current pricing page and the actual checkout or contract rather than treating an old dollar amount as universal. A paid account does not remove all model, tool, storage, or abuse-prevention limits. [S004, S005]

Personal-plan credits can extend eligible metered usage under their published rules. Business credit purchasing and spend controls are a separate administrative surface. Credits and included allowances should be understood as different pools, not as one unlimited subscription. [S069, S085]

ChatGPT and the OpenAI API have separate billing systems. An API key does not automatically draw from a ChatGPT subscription, and a ChatGPT subscription is not prepaid API credit. [S079]

Build a practical entitlement table

For your own evaluation, create columns for the task, required surface, required model, connected source, action type, administrative approval, usage pool, and fallback. Fill them from the signed-in account, not from another person's screenshot.

For example, a research workflow may need search, files, and a report export but no write access to business systems. A coding workflow may need a repository and a test environment rather than the most expensive conversational model. Buying capability that the organization's policies disable will not fix the workflow.

Budget by completed work

Track cost per accepted deliverable rather than number of messages alone. A cheaper configuration that repeatedly produces unusable outputs may be more expensive operationally. Conversely, a high-capability configuration can waste allowance on repetitive formatting that a deterministic script could perform.

Recommended measurement: record the task class, duration, visible allowance consumption when available, human review time, and whether the output passed acceptance. Never fabricate exact token accounting from a consumer interface that does not expose it.

Buying and renewal controls

For a team, assign an owner to seat assignment, credit thresholds, renewal, and offboarding. Distinguish a spending alert from a hard stop; test the actual behavior with an approved small budget. Ensure that any overage approval is explicit. For personal use, inspect where the subscription was purchased before attempting cancellation or changing the billing channel.

The durable lesson is to evaluate a workflow's total requirements before choosing a tier. The table you maintain locally will be more reliable than a permanently frozen universal feature matrix.

04 Models, thinking levels, and choosing effort

Current documented controls

The model guide identifies GPT-5.6 Luna as the Free/Go default and GPT-5.6 Sol as the basis of paid Instant and supported thinking levels. Plus includes Medium and High; broader eligible tiers can expose Extra High and Pro options. GPT-6 Pro is powered by Astra on eligible plans. On Plus and Pro, asking an Instant conversation to "think harder" does not itself select a higher thinking level. Limits and fallbacks remain relevant; Pro subscription status does not mean unlimited Pro-model use. [S003]

GPT-6 Sol and Luna are documented for Work and Codex rather than ordinary Chat. Work/Codex model selection and usage are distinct from Chat. [S002]

Select effort using uncertainty

A recommended decision rule is to increase effort when the task contains interacting constraints, ambiguous evidence, complex code, or an expensive error. Do not increase effort merely because the output is long. A long transcription cleanup may need less reasoning than a short concurrency proof.

Prepare a small evaluation set of your real tasks. Compare correctness, source fidelity, completeness, time, and review burden under the configurations actually available to you. Avoid announcing a universally best model from one impressive answer.

Reasoning is not evidence

A longer internal computation cannot make a stale fact current or grant access to a missing file. For current facts, require retrieval. For arithmetic, require reproducible calculations. For a claim about a source, require a specific supporting passage. For an action, require a tool result or a verifiable external state.

Use this prompt pattern: "Identify the assumptions that would change the result. Show a concise derivation and an independent check. Cite the evidence for external facts." This requests an auditable explanation without pretending that hidden internal reasoning is a complete execution trace.

Reproducibility record

Record the visible model and effort selection, task date, source version, and output. When a model is unavailable, state the fallback before comparing results. Keep model selection separate from tool permission: stronger reasoning cannot authorize a restricted connector.

The residency and model documentation contain a specific UAE inference discrepancy discussed in Chapter 50. Do not convert regional model availability into a contractual data-residency guarantee.

05 Conversations, finding work, and sharing safely

The product provides ways to find chats, projects, and files rather than requiring every item to remain visible in the recent-history list. Search terms should describe the content you remember, not just an assumed title. Availability and navigation vary by surface. [S050]

Shared conversation links are a distinct distribution mechanism. Review exactly what the share contains. Removing a link is not a promise to erase copies already made by others. Sharing a conversation does not grant the recipient your connected-app credentials or turn the conversation into a secure document-permission system. [S067]

Organize around outcomes

A recommended naming convention is topic - decision or deliverable - date. Use one conversation for a coherent reasoning thread and a Project for an enduring body of work. Start a new thread when the assumptions, audience, or data authority fundamentally change; then include a compact handoff rather than relying on invisible history.

When editing an earlier request or taking a different direction, explicitly state which prior constraints remain binding. Preserve the accepted version of any artifact before experimenting with a replacement. Treat a regenerated answer as another candidate, not automatically a correction.

A safe sharing workflow

First remove confidential source excerpts and unnecessary personal information. Next review the actual preview, not merely the latest response. Then choose the smallest appropriate audience and record why access is needed. Finally, test access from a suitable separate account when organizational policy permits.

Do not share a transcript containing tokens, passwords, or private URLs merely because the answer is useful. Instead create a sanitized standalone artifact with the relevant conclusion and public evidence. Source access and output access are separate concerns: the recipient might be allowed to read the report but not the raw records.

Troubleshooting missing content

Check the account and workspace before assuming deletion. Search using a distinctive phrase and verify the relevant Project or Library. For local or Codex work, confirm the correct history surface. When reporting a problem, include the approximate date and non-sensitive identifiers, not a full export of confidential conversations.

A useful personal index lists accepted deliverables, where the authoritative copy lives, source dates, and review status. Chat history is helpful working context; it should not be your only records-management policy.

06 Prompting as an acceptance contract

A useful prompt is not a magic phrase. It is a compact description of a task, its evidence, the constraints that matter, and the test for completion. The following method is a recommended workflow rather than a product guarantee.

The six-field brief

Specify outcome, audience, inputs, constraints, allowed actions, and verification. For a design review, request a decision document for the engineering team, name the architecture files, state latency and cost constraints, prohibit production changes, and require a failure-mode table plus executable tests where applicable.

Separate instructions from source material. Introduce quoted emails, web pages, or documents as untrusted information to analyze, not as instructions to obey. This matters particularly when a source contains phrases such as "ignore the previous request" or asks the assistant to send data elsewhere.

Work in checkpoints

For a complex assignment, first ask for an outline of the approach and the critical assumptions. Correct misunderstandings before requesting the complete artifact. Then review substance before typography. Finally ask for a verification pass against the original acceptance criteria. This sequence reduces wasted polish on the wrong answer.

Examples help when the desired format is hard to describe, but label which properties to imitate. A sample report may demonstrate structure without authorizing invented data or copied confidential details. Supply a counterexample as well: "Do not present an inference as a confirmed finding."

Use executable evidence

For calculations, request the formula and a small test case. For code, request tests that fail before the fix and pass afterward. For research, request citations next to the claims they support and a statement of unresolved disagreement. For a file deliverable, request the actual file and verify that it opens.

Recover from a bad answer

Name the defect precisely: unsupported claim, missing source, wrong unit, ignored constraint, or incomplete action. Ask for a targeted revision and a regression check. Merely saying "make it better" gives little information about the acceptance test.

A strong final instruction is: "Do not claim a source was read, a command executed, or a file created unless you have evidence. Mark untested parts explicitly." It improves the task contract, but independent verification still remains your responsibility.

07 Custom instructions, personality, and characteristics

Custom instructions provide persistent preferences for how ChatGPT should respond. Personality presets influence style and tone rather than granting new tools or factual authority. Characteristics provide additional presentation adjustments where available. These controls work alongside the immediate request and other applicable instructions; they are not a substitute for task-specific requirements. [S017, S058, S059]

Separate stable preferences from project rules

Good persistent preferences are durable: use clear explanations, identify assumptions, show units, and provide runnable code when code is requested. Poor persistent preferences are temporary facts such as this quarter's revenue or a private access token. Put project-specific terminology and approved source material in the relevant project or task instead.

An effective preference might read: "For technical designs, distinguish requirements from assumptions, include failure cases, and state what has not been tested." Avoid demanding a fixed answer length for every situation or an artificially confident tone. Those preferences can make a simple factual answer unnecessarily long or conceal uncertainty.

Personalization is not an authorization layer

A friendly style does not authorize external actions. A preference to be decisive should not suppress a necessary approval step. A professional personality does not turn an output into qualified legal, medical, or financial advice. Keep these boundaries explicit in your own operating practices.

Test the combination

After changing settings, try a factual question, a creative task, a technical explanation, and a request to identify missing information. Check whether the result remains useful in all four. If a preference creates problems, revise the narrow instruction rather than accumulating contradictory rules.

For team use, maintain a small set of reviewed examples showing the desired voice. Compare outputs against those examples instead of assuming every member has identical personal settings. Organizational templates should encode required sections and checks, while leaving individuals enough flexibility for legitimate variations.

A recommended maintenance habit

Review persistent preferences periodically. Remove stale directives and consolidate overlapping ones. When troubleshooting, use a controlled conversation with a known settings state so that you can distinguish a source problem from a style instruction. Never describe custom instructions as an infallible policy-enforcement mechanism; they are guidance to a model, not a database constraint or access-control rule.

08 Memory: useful continuity without a perfect record

Memory features can use remembered information and relevant prior context where enabled. The memory summary is not an exhaustive audit of every influence. Turning memory off is not the same as deleting stored information, and deleting a chat does not necessarily remove a separately retained memory. To remove information comprehensively, review memory and its source conversations or files. Disconnecting an app does not erase information already included in a chat. Managed and regulated workspaces can have different availability and controls. [S012]

Choose what deserves continuity

Use memory for stable preferences and long-lived context that you are comfortable reusing. Do not rely on it as a transaction register, password manager, or exact archive. A phrase such as "remember this" does not establish a legally complete retention or deletion workflow.

For critical work, provide authoritative facts again or point to the maintained source. A current contract supersedes a remembered summary of an older contract. Ask the assistant to distinguish what comes from the supplied document from what it believes it remembers.

Avoid contamination between roles

A consultant may work with multiple clients, or a person may switch between personal and organizational accounts. Make the boundary explicit: identify the current client, allowed sources, and prohibited cross-use. Project-only context, workspace controls, and careful source selection are stronger operating practices than trusting the model to infer which remembered detail is appropriate.

Verify and correct

Periodically inspect the memory controls and remove inaccurate or unnecessary information. When correcting a fact, specify the new value and the scope where it applies. Do not assume the correction automatically rewrites historical artifacts or removes every old source.

A useful audit question is: "Which facts in this answer came from the files I supplied, which came from remembered context, and which are assumptions?" Treat the explanation as a helpful diagnostic, not a complete technical provenance log.

Recommended exercise

Run the same low-risk task in a normal conversation and in a deliberately non-personalized environment. Compare tone, assumptions, and source references. The goal is to observe whether personalization helps your workflow without making critical information implicit. Record the actual settings used so the exercise can be repeated after a product update.

09 Temporary Chat and intentional context isolation

The current Temporary Chat guide distinguishes a default non-personalized temporary conversation from an optional personalized variant chosen at creation. The default excludes memories, custom instructions, and plugins; the personalized variant can use them without creating new memory. Temporary conversations do not appear in ordinary history or train models unless converted into a saved chat under the applicable settings. Safety retention can still last up to 30 days. Saving changes the conversation's status and may save its files to Library. [S016]

What isolation is good for

Use a controlled temporary conversation to test a prompt without your normal personalization, explore an isolated topic, or avoid adding a transient exchange to ordinary history. It is not a guarantee that no processing or retention occurs anywhere. It does not exempt content from applicable safety or organizational requirements.

Before adding sensitive information, choose the correct account and confirm the actual mode. A temporary-looking title or an incognito browser window is not enough evidence that the conversation uses Temporary Chat.

A practical workflow

State the sources explicitly and keep the task self-contained. Ask the assistant not to fill gaps from assumed personal context. At the end, decide whether the result should remain temporary or become a retained artifact. Downloading or copying an answer creates another copy with its own lifecycle, even when the original conversation is temporary.

If you choose to save the conversation, review the consequences first. Information that was acceptable for a short isolated analysis may not be appropriate for a shared Project or a broadly accessible Library folder.

Testing prompts fairly

For comparisons, record whether personalization was enabled. Otherwise, you may attribute a difference to the model when it actually came from different context. Use a synthetic input with a known expected result, run the prompt in the two configurations, and inspect unsupported assumptions.

The right mental model is controlled context and retention behavior, not a universal privacy shield. For regulated work, use the approved environment and policy rather than substituting a consumer temporary session for contractual controls.

10 Projects as a durable work context

Projects group related chats, files, and instructions. Project instructions take precedence over global custom instructions within that project. Current memory options distinguish default from project-only context; shared projects use project-only memory. Work is not supported in project-only mode according to the current project guidance. Sharing introduces collaborator access and should not be treated as private personal storage. [S008]

Design a project deliberately

A recommended structure contains an objective, a short glossary, approved sources, output conventions, and a decision log. Keep the instructions compact. Large reference documents belong in sources, not copied repeatedly into the instruction field.

For an engineering initiative, include the current requirements, architecture decisions, and test strategy. Label superseded material. Ask for citations to the relevant source when a conclusion depends on an uploaded document. A project containing both old and new policies needs an explicit authority rule; mere presence does not tell the assistant which one governs.

Keep source ownership clear

Specify who maintains each source and when it was last reviewed. A project can coordinate work without replacing the original system of record. Where linked source access is used, confirm that each collaborator has the intended rights and that private data is not inadvertently copied into a broadly shared output.

A repeatable project routine

At the start of a work session, identify the current deliverable and newly changed facts. At the end, save a concise handoff: accepted outputs, decisions, unresolved questions, and next steps. This reduces dependence on reconstructing a long conversation.

Before inviting collaborators, review all included context and the practical visibility of files and chats. Use a test collaborator when allowed. Do not assume a person can access only the single message in which you mentioned them.

When not to use a project

A one-off question may not need a durable container. Highly restricted material may require a different approved environment. A workflow requiring Work must be checked against the project's memory configuration rather than discovering the incompatibility after extensive setup.

The project is most valuable when its content is curated. An unstructured pile of drafts can increase ambiguity rather than improve answers.

11 Library, folders, and connected file storage

Library manages uploaded and generated files independently of individual chats. Deleting a conversation does not automatically delete its Library files. Sharing can involve viewer or editor access, and files in a shared folder follow its ownership context. Connected storage support includes eligible providers and rollout-specific capabilities; a provider's original files remain in that provider. Some source types and collections, including particular drive configurations, have restrictions. Storage allowances and per-upload limits are separate concepts. [S011]

Treat files as managed assets

Use meaningful filenames, version dates, and a clearly identified authoritative copy. Distinguish an original input from a transformed output. A generated spreadsheet should not silently replace the source spreadsheet until someone reviews its formulas and assumptions.

Recommended metadata for important deliverables: owner, source date, intended audience, sensitivity, review status, and expiry or refresh date. This can be maintained in a companion index even when the product does not expose every field as a native property.

Sharing workflow

Choose the minimum permission necessary. A reviewer usually needs to inspect the artifact; an editor may change it. Confirm whether new uploads into a shared folder inherit an ownership or visibility rule that differs from personal storage. Test access before sharing sensitive material externally.

Retrieval versus direct access

A source being connected does not prove that every file was retrieved into the current task. Ask for the filenames or specific passages actually used. For a comprehensive analysis, enumerate the required inputs and verify coverage before accepting the summary.

Do not confuse a link to a provider file with an immutable snapshot. If the source can change, record its version or export a reviewed snapshot under the appropriate policy. A citation without version awareness may later point to different content.

Deletion and cleanup

Review copies in chats, Library, shared folders, downloaded files, and connected providers separately. Disconnecting a source, deleting a chat, and deleting a file are different operations. Build a lifecycle checklist for confidential projects so that cleanup does not depend on remembering every place an output was copied.

II. Context and knowledge

12 Uploads, extraction, and completeness checks

The upload FAQ documents separate constraints: a general 512 MB per-file ceiling, a two-million-token limit for text/document files, approximately 50 MB for spreadsheets depending on structure, and 20 MB for images. Other account, rate, and storage limits can apply. Successful upload does not establish that every page or cell was read or that every embedded image was understood. [S019]

Choose the right representation

For exact calculations, provide a structured spreadsheet or CSV rather than a screenshot. For layout interpretation, retain the PDF page or image. For code review, provide text files or a repository rather than a photograph of an editor. Preserve the original alongside extracted data so mistakes can be traced.

Ask for coverage before conclusions

For a long report, request a table of sections and the pages actually inspected. For a workbook, ask for sheet names, dimensions, hidden or filtered-data concerns, formula handling, and the units of important columns. These are recommended verification steps; not every tool exposes every property automatically.

Use a small checksum-like check appropriate to the data: row count, total amount, number of pages, a known phrase near the end, or an expected appendix. This catches incomplete retrieval much earlier than reading an eloquent summary.

Scans and embedded content

A scanned page may require visual interpretation or OCR. Tables can lose column alignment during extraction; footnotes can become detached from the statement they qualify. Ask for the original page to be inspected when the extracted text is ambiguous. Never infer an unreadable amount merely because the rest of the row suggests a plausible value.

A reliable analysis sequence

Inventory the files, identify which version is authoritative, inspect structure, extract a bounded section, compare against the original, and only then perform the full transformation. Keep corrections explicit. An error in ingestion propagates into every chart and conclusion downstream.

For sensitive data, remove fields unnecessary for the task before upload. Masking a visible column in a screenshot is not equivalent to removing hidden content from a source file. Use a proper sanitization process and verify the sanitized result.

II. Context and knowledge

13 Image inputs and visual reasoning

Image inputs support questions about visual content, but the official guidance notes limitations involving small text, rotated content, precise spatial interpretation, and counting. An image-capable interaction is not automatically a video-analysis workflow. The actual input type and interface matter. [S020]

Frame the visual task

State whether you need transcription, interpretation, comparison, or measurement. These are different problems. "Describe this dashboard" can tolerate an approximate summary; "extract every invoice amount exactly" needs a more rigorous verification process.

Provide a clear original, an orientation that preserves reading order, and context about relevant units. When helpful, attach both the full image and a close-up. Preserve enough surrounding material to avoid taking labels out of context.

Use coordinates carefully

For engineering drawings or UI reviews, use reference labels already present in the image. Ask the assistant to identify uncertainty rather than invent exact pixel coordinates. Where precise annotation is required, use a deterministic drawing tool or inspect the generated overlay against the original.

For a chart, distinguish observed data from inferred trends. Verify axis scales, zero baselines, date ranges, and whether a line is cumulative. A visually rising series does not by itself establish a causal explanation.

A useful comparison workflow

Ask for differences grouped into content, layout, and possible defects. Then request a second pass on the most consequential differences. For example, in a before-and-after diagram, confirm that no data flow disappeared and that an arrow's direction was not accidentally reversed.

Boundaries

Do not use visual plausibility as evidence of authenticity. A screenshot can be edited, and a generated image can look like a real document. Sensitive identity, medical, or security conclusions require the appropriate independent process. For accessibility, create alt text that conveys the meaningful content without pretending to reproduce every visual detail.

The most reliable visual workflow pairs the model's interpretation with the original source and a clear acceptance test.

14 Search: current evidence rather than remembered facts

ChatGPT Search retrieves web information and can provide citations. It is different from searching your own chat history or connected organizational files. Location-dependent results and shopping or reservation integrations depend on supported services and permissions. A citation is a route to evidence, not a guarantee that the attached claim is fully supported. [S015]

Start with a question that can be verified

Specify the relevant date, jurisdiction, product version, and source standard. "What changed in this API since version X?" is easier to verify than "tell me the latest." For time-sensitive work, compare publication date with the date of the underlying event.

Ask for primary sources where available. For a software feature, the official documentation and release notes are usually better evidence than an undated tutorial. For a disputed factual question, identify reliable sources for the relevant alternatives and explain what each actually establishes.

Inspect the evidence chain

Open important citations. Confirm that the page exists, supports the claim, and concerns the right time period. A search snippet may omit a qualification found in the full page. A citation to a broad homepage is weaker than a specific passage or release note.

Separate direct facts, calculations, and inferences. A factual observation can be sourced; an inference should be labeled and its premises shown. Do not ask the assistant to hide uncertainty for the sake of a more confident narrative.

Avoid irrelevant browsing

Search is valuable when freshness or verification matters. For rewriting a supplied paragraph, outside browsing can add unnecessary assumptions. Conversely, answering a current pricing or policy question from memory can create an avoidable error.

A repeatable source ledger

Maintain a small record with claim, source, date, supporting passage, and confidence or unresolved issue. This is particularly useful when a report will be reviewed by someone who did not participate in the conversation. Keep direct quotation short and distinguish it from paraphrase.

If an authoritative source cannot be accessed, mark that limitation. A plausible substitute should not be presented as if it were the unavailable original.

15 Deep research as a controlled investigation

Deep research can combine web sources, supplied files, and eligible connected sources into a cited report. Its setup supports a research plan and source choices, including prioritizing or restricting specified sites. Connected-app use in this research workflow is read-oriented; research should not be confused with permission to modify external systems. Availability and usage depend on the account. [S013]

Define a research brief

State the decision the research will support, the questions that must be answered, the time window, acceptable sources, excluded sources, and expected output. Include a stopping rule: for example, sufficient evidence to compare three technical approaches, not an unbounded attempt to read the entire internet.

Ask for the plan before a broad investigation. Look for missing dimensions, such as regulatory scope, implementation cost, failure modes, or contradictory evidence. A plan that only searches for benefits will predictably produce a one-sided report.

Distinguish discovery from synthesis

During discovery, collect and categorize evidence. During synthesis, explain agreements, conflicts, gaps, and implications. Keep observed facts separate from your own proposed strategy. A well-written synthesis must not turn a vendor's marketing statement into an independently measured result.

Review the finished report

Check the most consequential claims first. Verify source dates, definitions, and denominator choices. Look for evidence that contradicts the central conclusion. Confirm that charts and comparisons use consistent units and populations.

Request a residual-uncertainty section: what remains unknown, what would change the recommendation, and what requires a pilot or professional review. This is more useful than a generic disclaimer at the end.

Refresh rather than blindly reuse

A research report is a dated artifact. For a future decision, update unstable inputs such as pricing, availability, regulations, and release status. Preserve the original report and add a revision note instead of silently replacing its assumptions.

For confidential research, verify both source permissions and output audience. Reading an internal file legitimately does not imply permission to publish its contents in a public report.

16 Data analysis: from a file to a reproducible result

ChatGPT data analysis can execute Python in a stateful analysis environment and produce tables, calculations, and charts. The documented analysis environment does not itself provide arbitrary external network or API access. Other modes or tools may have different capabilities. A displayed chart should be evaluated together with the transformation that produced it. [S018]

Establish a data contract

Before analysis, define row meaning, keys, units, time zone, missing-value conventions, and the target metric. Ask for a schema inspection and a quality report. A column named revenue might mean gross sales, net revenue, booked revenue, or cash collected; the tool cannot resolve that ambiguity from the label alone.

Preserve raw inputs

Create derived columns rather than overwriting originals. Record filters, joins, aggregation rules, and exclusions. Check join cardinality: a many-to-many join can multiply values while producing a plausible-looking chart.

For monetary or scientific calculations, choose numeric representations appropriate to the task. Inspect rounding and units. Do not accept a summary merely because it was produced by executed code; incorrect code executes successfully too.

Verification ladder

Start with row counts and missing values. Next compare key totals against an independent source or a hand-calculated sample. Then test edge cases: empty groups, duplicates, negative values, time boundaries, and unexpected categories. Finally review the chart for honest scales and clear labels.

Request the code or a reproducible explanation alongside the output. When the environment cannot install a package or read a format, document the fallback. Avoid silently substituting synthetic data for missing real data.

Deliverable design

A strong analysis package contains the cleaned dataset, transformation code, a short methodology, the key chart, and a limitations statement. The narrative should distinguish descriptive associations from causal conclusions. If a result will drive an expensive decision, reproduce the calculation in the organization's approved environment before acting.

The companion exercises use synthetic records so the verification method can be practiced without uploading private business data.

17 The Data plugin and business-data questions

Treat supported sources and capabilities as account-specific rather than assuming every database is universally available. [S024]

The semantic problem comes first

A database connection answers where records live; it does not automatically answer what they mean. Define the metric, entity, grain, and time basis before asking for a query. "Active customers" is ambiguous without an activity event and a time window.

Maintain a reviewed metric glossary. For each metric, record the owner, formula, permitted dimensions, freshness expectation, and known exclusions. Give the assistant those definitions and ask it to identify conflicts rather than choosing silently.

Review generated queries

Use read-only credentials and a restricted dataset for exploration. Inspect joins, date filters, null handling, and aggregation before running an expensive query. Apply the organization's query budget and data-access controls independently of the natural-language request.

A recommended sequence is: identify tables, propose the query, explain its grain, execute a bounded sample, validate totals, and only then run the full analysis. Avoid broad extracts when an aggregate answer will suffice.

Data access is not output approval

An employee may legitimately query sensitive records for an internal task while still being prohibited from exporting them into a shared report. Apply output minimization: remove unnecessary identifiers, small groups, or confidential columns according to policy.

Trust but reconcile

Compare important results with an approved dashboard or a known reference query. Differences may be caused by time zones, delayed ingestion, deleted records, or competing metric definitions. A discrepancy is a reason to investigate, not automatically evidence that either system is wrong.

For production analytics, promote a reviewed query into version control and a governed reporting pipeline. Ad hoc natural-language exploration is valuable, but recurring business decisions deserve a stable, tested definition.

18 Documents, spreadsheets, presentations, and templates

A shared production pipeline

Use four stages: source inventory, content construction, rendering, and verification. Review the logic before investing in decorative polish. Then inspect the actual output, not just a textual description of what the file supposedly contains.

23 Study mode, quizzes, and flashcards

Study mode is designed around guided learning, questions, hints, and interactive explanation. Its availability differs from ordinary chat contexts; the guide identifies unsupported combinations such as Temporary Chat, GPTs, and Projects. The release notes also document quizzes and flashcards, including saved flashcards in Library on supported surfaces. These features are learning aids, not guarantees of pedagogical correctness. Design a learning loop

Begin with a diagnostic question. Ask the assistant to identify the specific misconception rather than immediately delivering a complete solution. Work through one example, solve a different example independently, and then explain the reasoning back in your own words.

State your level, goal, and time budget. Ask for one question at a time, hints before solutions, explicit correction of mistakes, and a final list of concepts to review. 24 Running multi-step Work with clear checkpoints

This chapter is a recommended operating method for multi-step agentic work, not a statement about an undocumented internal scheduler. The important shift is from asking for an answer to supervising a bounded process that gathers evidence, creates outputs, and may request permission to act.

Define the work envelope

Identify the deliverable, allowed sources, maximum scope, prohibited actions, and review checkpoints. A good envelope might permit reading a specified folder and creating a draft report while prohibiting external publication. Include a stopping condition for missing or contradictory inputs.

Ask for a source inventory and a plan proportional to the task. Avoid an elaborate plan for a trivial rewrite, but require one when the task crosses data systems or could create external side effects.

Separate preparation from commitment

A draft is reversible. Sending it to a customer, modifying a production record, or publishing a site may not be. Review the precise content, destination, and account before approving such actions. A generic approval early in the conversation should not be treated as unlimited authority for materially different later actions.

Keep progress evidence-based

Useful progress updates identify completed artifacts, discovered constraints, and decisions awaiting input. They should not claim that a step succeeded merely because the model intended to perform it. Ask for tool results or visible output where completion matters.

If the task changes direction, explicitly cancel obsolete planned actions. Reconfirm the remaining scope and preserve accepted outputs. For long work, request a compact checkpoint containing source versions, files created, tests run, and unresolved issues.

Recover from interruption

Resume by inspecting actual state. Is the file present? Was the external change applied? Is an action still awaiting approval? Do not automatically rerun an entire workflow when the last step's outcome is unknown. For consequential writes, recovery should use the same business operation identity where the integration supports it.

Final acceptance

Compare the result against the original brief. Verify content, sources, calculations, permissions, and file rendering. Archive the accepted artifact in the appropriate system. The chat is the workbench; the reviewed output and its evidence are the deliverable.

The architecture chapters later show how a company could implement these boundaries with explicit state machines rather than relying only on model-generated prose.

26 Plugins: reusable capability packages

Plugins package reusable capabilities such as skills, apps, and workflow resources. Installation, account connection, and permission to act are separate steps. A plugin can be installed while a required app remains disconnected or unavailable under workspace policy. Verification labels and directory availability do not replace an organization's own review. [S006]

Evaluate a plugin before adoption

Ask what data it reads, what systems it can change, which identities it uses, and what dependencies it requires. Review the publisher and update process. A useful description should explain when the plugin applies and what successful output looks like.

Start with a synthetic task and a low-privilege account. Observe whether the plugin retrieves the correct source and whether it asks for the expected approvals. Do not begin by granting broad access to the most sensitive production system.

Explicit versus implicit invocation

For early testing, explicitly select the capability so you know what is under evaluation. Once its behavior is understood, automatic selection can be convenient. If a result depends on a particular plugin, ask for the actual tool or source used rather than inferring it from the answer's wording.

Dependency hygiene

Keep the distinction between an instruction bundle and an executable integration. Instructions may teach a workflow; an app supplies access to external capabilities. A missing connection cannot be repaired by stronger prompting. Conversely, a connected app without appropriate workflow guidance can produce inconsistent results.

Team rollout

Maintain an approved catalog with an owner, intended use, data classification, permission profile, and review date. Stage updates and test representative workflows before organization-wide deployment. Revisit permissions when a plugin adds a new write capability.

When removing a plugin, inspect its related account connections, scheduled tasks, and published outputs separately. Uninstallation should not be assumed to revoke every external token or erase every previously generated artifact.

The durable design principle is composability with explicit authority: a package can simplify discovery and reuse without becoming a hidden source of unlimited access.

27 Skills: reusable instructions with concrete tests

Skills are reusable bundles of instructions and supporting material for a specific workflow. The public guide describes creating, managing, and sharing them through supported interfaces, with availability depending on the account and workspace. They can encode conventions and repeatable procedures, but they do not independently grant access to an external service. [S029]

Choose a good skill candidate

A skill is useful when the task recurs and the desired process is stable: reviewing an API specification, preparing a weekly report, or applying a house style. It is less useful as a container for a single enormous document that changes daily.

Define expected inputs, outputs, triggers, and failure behavior. A review skill should say which findings to report, how to rank severity, what evidence to include, and when to stop because a source is missing. Avoid broad instructions such as "be the best analyst" that cannot be tested.

Keep instructions compact

Put the core procedure in the entrypoint and supporting reference material in separate resources. Use deterministic scripts for fragile calculations or transformations where the environment supports them. A skill should direct the assistant to verify results, not merely produce them.

An illustrative specification might say: "For an API review, read the supplied schema, identify authentication and idempotency behavior, test examples where executable, and report unsupported assumptions separately." This is a workflow example, not an installed skill delivered by this guide.

Test triggers and outcomes

Create positive cases that should invoke the skill and negative cases that should not. Test missing inputs, contradictory requirements, and malicious instructions embedded in source material. Compare outputs against a rubric rather than one preferred sentence.

Version and share carefully

Record the skill version used for important outputs. Review bundled files for secrets and outdated policies. When sharing with a team, ensure supporting resources are appropriate for the same audience. An instruction that references a private folder is not useful to a colleague without access.

Skills improve consistency when they encode a clear process. They are not a substitute for authorization, exact arithmetic, or independent quality gates.

Account management supports connection-specific setup and, where available, multiple accounts. Connecting an app does not remove the provider's own access controls. Resolve identity before retrieval

Specify which account and workspace contain the source. A person may have personal and corporate accounts with the same provider. The wrong account can produce a confident "not found" or, worse, retrieve the wrong organization's data.

Retrieve only what is necessary for the task. A support summary may need ticket categories and trends, not every customer's personal details. Where a result will be shared externally, review the output separately from the underlying permission to read internal sources.

30 App permissions and the approval boundary

The permission guide distinguishes settings such as Always ask, Allow read, Allow low-risk actions, and broader permissions where supported. Not every option is available at every default or workspace level. Changing an action preference does not connect an account or grant underlying provider access. Live action controls also should not be mistaken for fine-grained filtering of already synchronized content. [S033]

Choose the least authority that completes the task

For research, begin with read access. For repeated low-risk updates, consider a narrowly scoped workflow after testing. Avoid granting broad write authority merely to reduce one approval prompt.

A useful approval must answer four questions: what action, on which account, affecting which resource, with what exact content? "Proceed" without those details is weak evidence that the person understood the effect.

Bind approval to a specific proposal

For consequential operations, the implementation should associate approval with an immutable action digest and an expiry. If the recipient, amount, attachment, or target resource changes, require renewed approval. This is a proposed engineering control, not a claim that every connected app exposes the same mechanism.

Consider the whole data path

A nominally read-only tool can still cause information to appear in a report or be transmitted to another tool later. Review source access, tool output, and publication separately. A low-risk classification is not a universal promise that every use of the tool is harmless.

Test denial and cancellation

A permission system is not proven by a successful approved action. Test that a denied action does not execute, that a canceled task stops pending writes, and that a user cannot switch accounts after approval to change the effect. Use synthetic data and approved test systems.

Operational habit

Before accepting a broad permission change, list the workflows that need it and the narrower alternatives. For organizations, maintain an owner and review date for exceptions. The goal is not to eliminate useful automation, but to keep authority proportional to the task and visible to the person responsible.

Searchable content can depend on indexing state and source access; connection success alone does not establish complete or instantaneous coverage. [S065]

Treat freshness as part of correctness

A policy updated minutes ago may not yet be reflected in a synchronized index. For a time-critical question, retrieve the authoritative current record through the supported direct path or verify it in the source system. Include the source version or last-updated time in the answer where available.

Understand the permission chain

A recommended model separates workspace eligibility, connector configuration, user identity, source permissions, retrieval filtering, and output sharing. Every layer must be considered. A broad service account can create a different risk profile from per-user authorization.

Test with a permission matrix

Create synthetic documents accessible to everyone, one team, and one individual. Confirm that authorized users can retrieve the right documents and unauthorized users cannot infer restricted content through summaries, search snippets, or citations. Repeat the tests after revocation and allow for documented propagation behavior.

Avoid stale-access assumptions

Removing a source permission may not erase information that was already included in an authorized conversation or exported artifact. Plan both access revocation and downstream retention. Do not describe an index as a magical single copy of the organization's knowledge.

Curate high-value sources

Deduplicate obsolete policies and mark superseded versions. A retrieval system can surface an old document accurately while still giving the wrong business answer. Source governance and retrieval quality are complementary responsibilities.

Engineering lesson

In a proposed retrieval architecture, store document identity, version, access metadata, and provenance alongside chunks. Recheck authorization at retrieval time and invalidate or refresh indexes when permissions change. Chapter 56 expands this into a concrete low-level design without claiming that ChatGPT uses that exact implementation.

Create test users for common combinations: default member, specialist, administrator, restricted user, and a user with overlapping roles. Verify the actual interface and tool behavior. Include negative tests for features that should remain unavailable.

Keep administration responsibilities distinct

Identity-provider administrators, workspace owners, plugin owners, and data-source owners may control different layers. Document who can approve each change. Avoid a workflow where everyone assumes another team reviewed the same permission.

Change safely

Record the intended change, affected users, expected behavior, and rollback plan. Allow for documented propagation and retest. A policy update should be treated like a configuration deployment, not a casual preference change.

Review exceptions

Time-bound broad access for a pilot or incident. Assign an owner and expiry. Do not let temporary exceptions become permanent because the original ticket was closed.

Measure control effectiveness

A control is useful only if it enforces the intended boundary. Test that disabled actions fail safely and that allowed tasks still work. Review audit evidence where available. For regulated workloads, confirm that the exact feature and data path are covered by the organization's contractual and technical controls.

Good RBAC is understandable and testable. Complexity without a clear effective-access model can create both unnecessary friction and unexpected privilege.

51 Cost controls, adoption, and organizational rollout

Business credit and spend controls provide mechanisms for managing eligible usage, while personal-plan credit rules differ. Administrative visibility and supported metrics depend on the offering. Verify whether a configured threshold is an alert, a cap, or another control before relying on it to stop spending. [S085, S069]

Pilot a small number of workflows

Choose tasks with clear inputs, low initial risk, and measurable outcomes. Examples include source-grounded internal summaries, draft documents, and code changes reviewed through existing CI. Avoid beginning with unrestricted external actions or highly sensitive data.

Measure useful work

Track accepted outputs, factual defects, human review time, cycle time, and total cost. Message count alone is not productivity. A workflow that produces more drafts but increases review effort may not be an improvement.

Build training around failure modes

Teach users how to verify sources, identify unsupported claims, inspect generated files, manage permissions, and recover from incomplete actions. Feature tours are useful, but they should not replace practical exercises with known expected results.

Roll out by risk tier

Start with read-only assistance. Next allow creation of local or private drafts. Then introduce narrowly scoped writes with approvals. Broader automation requires stronger observability, ownership, and recovery controls. This staged model is our recommendation, not a vendor-mandated sequence.

Avoid shadow production

A successful personal experiment can become a business dependency without anyone noticing. Identify recurring tasks, shared agents, and connected accounts that others depend on. Move them into an owned, reviewed operational process.

Exit and portability

Preserve important prompts, source references, accepted artifacts, evaluation cases, and workflow definitions in approved formats. Do not make the only copy of a critical process an undocumented conversation in one employee's account.

The objective is dependable adoption: better outcomes with known costs and controlled risks, not simply maximal feature activation.

This chapter provides a recommended diagnostic method. It does not claim access to private account state or replace the official support process.

A conceptual request lifecycle

A user supplies a request. The product assembles applicable instructions and context, invokes a model, may execute allowed tools, incorporates results, and returns text or artifacts. This is a useful conceptual model; the following chapters present an original implementation design rather than assert an exact private call graph.

Separate knowledge mechanisms

Training creates model behavior and learned patterns. Retrieval brings selected external information into a task. Memory supplies supported personalized context. Tools interact with systems or execute computations. These mechanisms have different freshness, provenance, and failure properties.

Why tools matter

A model can propose a calculation, but executed code can make the calculation reproducible. A model can describe an email, but a permissioned integration must actually send it. Build-versus-buy framing

A company can build a useful assistant product using model APIs, retrieval, execution environments, and strong application controls without recreating a frontier model training organization. The appropriate starting point is a narrow workflow with measurable value, not an attempt to reproduce every product surface at once.

VIII. Architecture and implementation

55 Proposed high-level architecture for an assistant company

Proposed design, not ChatGPT's private architecture. Build an organization-aware assistant around a model gateway, a durable task engine, permissioned tools, source retrieval, and an artifact service. Keep identity and policy enforcement outside the model's discretionary control.

Core components

The edge authenticates requests and assigns a tenant and trace identity. A conversation service stores messages and user-visible state. A context service retrieves authorized source material. A model gateway selects an allowed model and enforces usage budgets. A task engine coordinates tool calls. A policy service approves or rejects proposed actions. Sandboxed workers execute code or document transformations. An artifact store retains versioned outputs with access controls.

Separate the control plane from execution. Administrators configure allowed models, tools, data sources, and budgets; workers consume versioned policy snapshots but revalidate authority at critical boundaries. Do not make a natural-language instruction the only protection against cross-tenant access.

Trust boundaries

Treat user uploads, web pages, connector responses, and generated code as untrusted. Tools receive only the minimum context necessary. Never pass the entire conversation to every external provider by default. Credentials remain in a secure credential service and are injected only into the authorized executor.

Durability and observability

Persist task state before acknowledging durable acceptance. Record tool proposals, approvals, attempts, observations, and artifacts with stable identities. Emit audit events without unnecessarily duplicating sensitive content. A streaming answer is an interface behavior, not a replacement for durable state.

Initial implementation strategy

Start with a modular service and a transactional database for control state. Add isolated execution workers and object storage for artifacts. Use a queue for durable work scheduling, but make database state the authority for whether a business action is permitted. Add specialized services only when measured load or isolation needs justify them.

Deployment boundaries

Choose regions based on actual customer requirements and contracts. Establish backups, incident response, and data-retention policies before broad deployment. A multi-region diagram does not by itself prove availability, durability, or legal suitability.

The initial product should complete a narrow set of workflows reliably, expose its evidence, and fail safely when tools or sources are unavailable.

VIII. Architecture and implementation

56 Low-level context, conversations, and retrieval design

Proposed design. Store conversations and source references separately from retrieved context snapshots. A task should be reproducible enough to explain which source versions and policies influenced a result, without claiming deterministic model output.

Reference entities

Use Tenant, Principal, Conversation, Message, Task, SourceDocument, SourceVersion, Chunk, AccessGrant, ContextSnapshot, and Artifact. Every private row carries tenant scope. A context snapshot records selected source-version identifiers, retrieval scores where meaningful, and the policy version used. Do not store raw credentials in these tables.

Retrieval pipeline

Authenticate the principal, determine allowed source scope, perform candidate retrieval, apply access checks, rank and deduplicate results, and construct a bounded context package. Recheck access when source permissions changed or a long-running task resumes. Include document identity and page or section references so citations can be grounded.

The API's file-search documentation exposes hosted retrieval facilities such as vector stores and semantic/keyword search. Those are available building blocks, not evidence that ChatGPT uses the schema proposed here. [S078]

Context budgeting

Allocate space for instructions, the current task, critical source passages, tool results, and a compact history summary. Prefer high-value evidence over repeatedly appending every previous message. Summaries should preserve unresolved questions and action state, not just conversational themes.

Data integrity rules

A source version is immutable. A new upload creates a new version rather than silently changing old citations. Access grants have validity intervals and provenance. Artifacts identify their input snapshot and review state. A deleted source produces a tombstone and triggers the applicable retention workflow.

Common failure modes

A stale index can return an obsolete policy. A permissive service identity can leak another team's document. A compressed summary can lose a crucial exception. A citation can point to a document that no longer contains the quoted fact. Test each explicitly.

Practical implementation

Start with a relational metadata store and object storage. Add a search index as a derived projection. Rebuild the projection from authoritative source metadata and content where policy permits. Never make an eventually consistent search index the only enforcement point for sensitive authorization.

VIII. Architecture and implementation

57 Tool execution, approvals, retries, and unknown outcomes

Proposed design. A tool call is a typed proposal from a model, not an authoritative command. The server validates identity, arguments, scope, resource version, policy, and approval before execution.

The public function-calling guide describes a loop in which the model requests a tool and the application executes it, returning the result. The Responses API provides conversation and tool-oriented building blocks; it does not remove application responsibility for authorization or external side effects. [S076, S091, S092]

State machine

Use PROPOSED -> APPROVED -> DISPATCHING -> SUCCEEDED, with explicit REJECTED, FAILED, and UNKNOWN states. UNKNOWN means an external action may have happened even though the response was lost. It is not equivalent to safe-to-retry failure.

Bind authority to content

Canonicalize the tenant, actor, tool, target, arguments, and operation identity. Hash that representation. Approval records reference the hash, policy version, approver, and expiry. A changed recipient or payload invalidates the old approval. Revalidate current access immediately before dispatch.

Idempotency

Use a stable business operation ID and a unique effect record. A repeated ID with the same digest returns the existing result or state. A repeated ID with different content is a conflict. For an external provider, safe replay also depends on its actual deduplication contract; a local database cannot guarantee exactly-once behavior in an arbitrary remote system.

Transactional boundary

Persist the dispatch decision and attempt identity before invoking the provider. Execute outside the database transaction. Persist the resulting observation afterward. If the process crashes between those steps, recover using inquiry or the provider's documented idempotency behavior.

Sandbox execution

Run generated code with bounded CPU, memory, runtime, filesystem scope, and network access. Mount only required inputs. Scan and authorize exported artifacts. A model's statement that code is safe is not the isolation mechanism.

Companion example

The bundle includes a small local Python model that demonstrates tenant checks, approval binding, expiry, revocation, duplicate detection, and unknown-outcome recovery. It intentionally omits distributed persistence, real credentials, and network integrations. Passing its tests validates those modeled rules only.

VIII. Architecture and implementation

58 Threat modeling and red-team evaluation

Proposed security design. Begin with assets: credentials, private documents, conversation history, action authority, generated artifacts, and audit records. Identify adversaries who can control a user prompt, retrieved page, uploaded file, connector response, or dependency.

Prompt injection is a trust-boundary problem

An external source can contain instructions designed to redirect the assistant. The defense is not simply telling the model to be careful. Separate instruction authority from source content, minimize tool context, enforce server-side permissions, and require approval for consequential actions.

Test the data path

Place a harmless synthetic secret in a restricted document and verify that an unauthorized user cannot retrieve it. Put a malicious instruction in an otherwise relevant page and test whether the assistant ignores its attempt to redirect output. Use a benign test endpoint and synthetic content; do not expose real secrets during evaluation.

Test the action path

Attempt to change a recipient after approval, replay an expired approval, switch tenants, use a revoked grant, and submit conflicting content under the same operation ID. Every case should fail at a deterministic control boundary, regardless of the model's wording.

Supply-chain and artifact risks

Review plugin publishers, dependency updates, executable files, macros, and generated scripts. A document or spreadsheet can contain active content or external references. Keep artifact generation isolated and inspect outputs before distribution.

Logging without oversharing

Record enough evidence to investigate an incident, but avoid duplicating sensitive content unnecessarily. Protect logs as production data. Audit access to them and define retention and deletion policies.

Residual risk

No finite test suite proves that a model will never follow a malicious instruction. The goal is defense in depth and containment: a bad model decision should encounter an independent authorization or execution limit before causing disproportionate harm.

Run these tests continuously as models, prompts, tools, and policies change. An unchanged application binary can still behave differently after a model or integration update.

VIII. Architecture and implementation

59 Reliability, latency, capacity, and cost engineering

Proposed engineering targets, not published ChatGPT guarantees. Define separate indicators for request acceptance, first useful response, task completion, tool execution, artifact delivery, and source freshness. A fast acknowledgment is not a completed task.

Decompose the critical path

Measure authentication, context retrieval, model latency, tool latency, and rendering independently. Streaming can improve perceived responsiveness without shortening total work. Set bounded timeouts and cancellation behavior for each stage.

Capacity model

Estimate concurrency using arrival rate times average service time. For an illustrative workload of 20 tasks per second with 30 seconds of active execution, the mean active population is about 600 tasks. This is a sizing example, not a benchmark. Add headroom for bursts, slow providers, retries, and isolation limits.

Prevent retry storms

Give the overall task a retry budget. Use backoff, jitter, and per-tenant concurrency limits. Distinguish retryable reads from writes with uncertain outcomes. Do not let several layers independently multiply retries for the same external action.

Graceful degradation

When a preferred model is unavailable, use an explicitly allowed fallback or return a clear limitation. When search is unavailable, do not present remembered information as newly verified. When artifact rendering fails, preserve the content and report the rendering failure rather than claiming a finished file.

Durable work and recovery

Store authoritative task state and checkpoint completed steps. Use leases for worker coordination, but enforce action uniqueness at the business-operation boundary. Recover from persisted evidence, not from a model's recollection of what it intended to do.

Cost discipline

Track model input, cached input where applicable, output, tool costs, storage, and human review effort. Cache only where access, freshness, and privacy allow it. Reuse deterministic intermediate results rather than repeating expensive model work unnecessarily.

Reliability proof

Load tests, failure injection, restore drills, and production telemetry are required. A diagram showing multiple regions does not establish a service level. State the fault model and measure the user's actual outcome under those failures.

VIII. Architecture and implementation

60 Evaluation: prove usefulness, not just plausibility

OpenAI's evaluation guidance recommends task-specific evaluation and repeatable testing rather than relying only on informal impressions. Model and workflow changes should be assessed against representative cases. [S077]

Build a layered evaluation set

Include factual retrieval, calculations, document production, code changes, permission denials, and ambiguous external outcomes. Use synthetic or properly authorized data. Maintain expected facts and acceptance criteria separately from the model's answer.

Score the outcome

A recommended rubric evaluates correctness, evidence fidelity, task completeness, permission compliance, artifact usability, and disclosure of uncertainty. Do not reward verbosity or confidence by themselves. A beautiful report with an invented source should fail.

Separate model quality from system safety

A model can produce a poor draft without violating access controls. Conversely, a useful-looking result can expose unauthorized data. Track these as different failure classes with different release gates.

Use repeated trials where needed

Non-deterministic behavior means one successful run is weak evidence. Repeat difficult cases and inspect failure patterns. Keep the visible model configuration, tool versions, source snapshot, prompt, and policy version with each result.

Human review

Have qualified reviewers inspect consequential tasks. Resolve disagreements about the expected answer before using a case as a hard benchmark. For subjective writing tasks, use a rubric and multiple examples rather than a single exact-match string.

Release gate

Block release on unauthorized actions, cross-tenant leakage, fabricated completion, or broken artifacts. Set task-specific thresholds for other quality defects and document accepted residual risk. Roll out gradually and monitor real-world cases that the test set missed.

Regression discipline

When an incident occurs, add a minimized synthetic reproduction to the suite. A test is most valuable when it captures a previously missed failure mechanism. The companion package includes a rubric and local control tests to demonstrate this approach; it is not an official OpenAI benchmark.

62 Prompt recipes and a practical operating checklist

The downloadable prompt pack contains original templates for common workflows. Adapt them to the available tools and your organization's rules; a prompt cannot create capabilities or permissions the environment does not have.

Research brief

"Investigate the specified question using current primary sources. State the date and scope. Separate documented facts, inferences, and proposed recommendations. Cite each material claim. Preserve disagreements and explain what could not be verified."

File analysis brief

"Inventory the supplied files and identify the authoritative version. Inspect schema, units, missing values, and duplicates. Show the transformation and independent checks. Do not substitute invented data. Deliver the result and a reproducible method."

Artifact brief

"Create the specified editable artifact and a PDF using only the approved inputs. Preserve units and source references. Inspect the rendered output for overflow, missing content, and unreadable diagrams. Report untested assumptions separately."

Coding brief

"Inspect the repository and reproduce the issue. Make a minimal patch, preserve unrelated work, run relevant tests, and explain the diff. Do not deploy. List tests that could not run and remaining risks."

Action brief

"Prepare the exact proposed change and identify the account, target, content, and expected effect. Do not execute until the required approval is recorded. After execution, verify the outcome in the authoritative system. Treat an uncertain response as unknown, not as permission to repeat blindly."

Before starting

Confirm identity, workspace, mode, source access, allowed actions, output format, and the test for success. Use synthetic data when learning a new integration.

Before trusting

Open important citations, reproduce consequential calculations, inspect the actual artifact, review code changes, and verify external actions. Distinguish a drafted result from a committed change.

Before sharing

Review audience, confidential content, credentials, source permissions, and downstream copies. Preserve the accepted version and its source date.

Before automating

Assign an owner, define scope and failure handling, test permissions and duplicate events, monitor outcomes, and maintain a way to pause or revoke access.

The central discipline is simple: make the intended outcome explicit, keep authority bounded, and require evidence at the point where the result matters.

Appendix E. Glossary

Agentic workflow: a multi-step process involving model decisions and tools; not a guarantee of autonomy or success.

Artifact: a generated or edited file or interactive output with its own version and access lifecycle.

Context: information supplied to a particular model interaction, including instructions, messages, and retrieved evidence.

Entitlement: product eligibility arising from a plan or agreement; distinct from source authorization.

Grounding: tying an answer to specific evidence rather than relying only on learned patterns.

Idempotency: preventing repeat application of the same business operation under a defined identity and scope.

Inference: computation that produces a model response; distinct from training and from storage.

MCP: a protocol used by supported integrations to expose tools and resources; not an automatic trust grant.

Memory: supported personalized continuity, not an exact authoritative database of every prior fact.

Plugin: a reusable capability package that may include skills, apps, and resources.

Prompt injection: an attempt by untrusted content to redirect the assistant beyond the authorized task.

RAG: retrieval-augmented generation: selecting external evidence and supplying it to a model.

RBAC: role-based access control; its effective-access semantics must be checked for the actual product.

SCIM: a provisioning and identity-lifecycle standard, separate from interactive SSO authentication.

Skill: reusable workflow instructions with optional supporting resources.

Source version: an identifiable revision of evidence used to support a result.

Tool: an executable capability exposed under an application-defined contract.

UNKNOWN: a state in which an external effect may have happened but cannot yet be conclusively observed.

The companion's 37 Python unit tests passed. They cover exact synthetic-data analysis and an in-memory control model, including an in-process parallel retry case. The SQL schema was smoke-tested in a local SQLite database with foreign keys enabled. Neither test is a production certification.
