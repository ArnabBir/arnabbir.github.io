1. Executive thesis: build the financial operating system, not just the endpoints

1.1 The product hidden behind a simple POST

A developer sees a payment request, an identifier, a status, and a webhook. A production provider must also establish who is selling, who owes whom, which entity can legally provide the service, which credential can be used, whether the issuer approved the payment, when cash will arrive, when the merchant can withdraw it, and what happens when the customer disputes it months later. The API is the stable contract above these moving parts.

Our proposed implementation therefore has four enduring domains: commercial intent, external execution, financial accounting, and independent evidence. Commercial intent says what the customer authorized. Execution records what an acquirer or bank may have done. Accounting records assets, obligations, fees, reserves, and adjustments. Evidence records what outside parties actually reported. None is a safe substitute for the other three.

Stripe exposes payment collection through objects such as PaymentIntents, platform allocation through Connect, and third-party disbursement through Global Payouts. These products have different funding and eligibility contracts; a generic send_money() abstraction that erases those differences would misrepresent the platform. [S01] [S11] [S21]

1.2 Recommended company starting point

Northstar should initially choose one merchant segment, one legal-entity model, one acquiring relationship, a small set of currencies, and a tightly controlled payout corridor. Start as a provider using approved acquiring and banking partners rather than simultaneously attempting direct membership in every network, full banking infrastructure, global licensing, and a universal developer platform.

The recommendation is about sequencing, not an assertion that partnerships remove regulatory responsibility. Record who performs underwriting, settlement, safeguarding, sanctions controls, complaint handling, dispute representation, and loss absorption. A partner's technical API is only one part of that relationship.

Build the financial kernel, stable operation identities, connector recovery, reconciliation, merchant-facing APIs, and operational controls as core competencies. Initially purchase or partner for specialized identity verification, card-network access, bank connectivity, screening data, and secure infrastructure where that shortens the path to a controlled launch. Keep authoritative decision records and financial consequences in your own domain.

1.3 The five decisions that dominate the design

Decision Recommended starting position Reason
Product scope Merchant acceptance plus controlled merchant settlement; expand to marketplace and independent payouts separately Different products introduce different obligations and failure modes
Financial authority One serializable financial authority per approved cell Prevents duplicate spending and accounting dual writes
External execution Durable attempts, explicit UNKNOWN, contract-aware retries A network timeout does not prove a bank did nothing
Developer contract Intent-oriented resources, stable IDs, predictable errors, versioned schemas Developers need recovery semantics, not just endpoint coverage
Expansion Evidence-based corridor and capability gates API availability is not a global permission to move money

Success criterion: an engineer and a controller can trace one customer request through every attempt, journal, provider reference, settlement record, fee, correction, and payout without inventing missing history. That is more important than superficially matching the number of endpoints in Stripe's reference.

2. Research method, evidence boundaries, and unresolved internals

2.1 Four levels of confidence

The API documentation is the primary source for observable behavior. Published engineering articles explain selected infrastructure and operational practices. Our architectural deductions follow from those facts but remain deductions. Recommendations describe what Northstar should implement, not what Stripe necessarily implemented.

For example, Stripe has publicly described its DocDB platform and its Ledger system. Those publications do not establish that a particular PaymentIntent mutation, balance update, and event write are committed in a single publicly specified database transaction. Nor do they establish the precise replica placement, current internal schema, or every service involved in a live payment. [S40] [S41]

2.2 Evidence must preserve dates

The 2024 Ledger and DocDB publications describe systems at that time. Stripe's 2026 publications on its data-plane proxy, global treasury network, and API release pipeline are later evidence, but still selective disclosures. Historical throughput or explainability metrics must not be relabeled as today's production volume or online latency SLO. [S40] [S41] [S43] [S44] [S45]

This research checked official sources on 26 September 2026. Product pages are mutable. For preview APIs, even an official sample is not a promise that an account has access or that an older schema remains valid indefinitely. Save the accepted contract, schema version, test evidence, and entitlement decision for each production integration.

2.4 How to turn research into implementation evidence

Maintain an architecture decision record for each irreversible assumption. It should contain the source, date, interpretation, rejected alternatives, owner, test, and rollback or exposure limit. A useful record says, for example, that a partner guarantees deduplication within an account and endpoint for a specified period, and links the certification test that loses the response after execution.

Do not write only "provider supports idempotency." That sentence omits scope, lifetime, payload mismatch behavior, inquiry lag, cancellation semantics, and whether the guarantee survives a regional endpoint change.

The playbook uses source references next to externally sourced facts. Longer implementation algorithms, database boundaries, operational policies, and numerical scenarios are original proposals. Legal and accounting examples remain conditional on the chosen entity and product model.

3. Stripe's resource model: identities that survive individual requests

3.1 Payment resources are not interchangeable

A Customer is a reusable relationship and associated data, not a bank balance. A PaymentMethod is a payment-credential representation, not proof of customer consent for every future use. A SetupIntent prepares a credential for later use; a PaymentIntent manages a payment lifecycle. A Charge represents a payment attempt or charge outcome associated with that lifecycle. Stripe documents that a PaymentIntent can have multiple Charges when attempts are retried. [S01] [S05]

A BalanceTransaction explains an effect on a Stripe balance, including amount, fee, net, type, and availability information. It is not a public general-purpose double-entry journal API. A Transfer allocates funds between a platform and connected account under Connect's rules. A Payout moves eligible balance funds toward an external payout destination. [S11] [S20] [S51]

3.2 Resource families and your internal model

Stripe surface Developer question Northstar internal counterpart
Checkout Session How do I host or coordinate checkout? Checkout workflow, order, pricing snapshot
PaymentIntent What is this payment trying to accomplish? Payment plus stable business identity
PaymentMethod / SetupIntent Which instrument and future-use setup apply? Token, consent, mandate, credential policy
Charge What happened on a payment attempt? Processor attempt and normalized observations
Refund / Dispute What later claim or reversal exists? Independent adjustment lifecycle
Account / capabilities Who may use which financial function? Legal party, requirements, capability state
Transfer How are platform funds allocated? Liability reclassification with ownership evidence
Payout / OutboundPayment How is money sent externally? Disbursement, execution slot, evidence, cash accounting
Event What changed? Durable notification of committed state

The mapping is deliberately not one-to-one. Your legal books and bank reconciliation may need multiple entries for one public object. Conversely, one settlement batch can contain hundreds of charges and fees.

3.3 Business identity comes before API identity

A merchant's order should have a stable reference independent of request retries. Persist the resulting payment ID against that reference. Do not create a new payment every time the buyer reloads checkout. Stripe recommends reusing a PaymentIntent when a shopping session is resumed rather than treating each page visit as a new payment. [S01]

In Northstar, distinguish merchant_order_ref, payment_id, attempt_id, economic_effect_id, provider_reference, and bank_movement_id. Their uniqueness domains differ. Deduplicating on the amount and recipient is wrong because two identical-looking payments can both be legitimate.

3.4 Persist the terms, not just the latest status

Store the amount, currency, beneficiary version, merchant entity, fee policy, quote, capture mode, statement descriptor policy, and accepted terms version. Mutable profile data must not silently change a prepared payment. A beneficiary edit creates a new version; a payout prepared for the old version keeps its original destination until canceled through an approved process.

A status is a projection over facts. It cannot be the only financial record. A payment marked successful may later be disputed; a payout marked processing may already have debited cash; a payment marked canceled may still need a separate refund if capture actually occurred. Good resource design makes those distinctions visible rather than forcing operators to infer them from one enum.

4. Card payment mechanics: authorization, capture, clearing, and settlement

4.1 Message path versus money path

The conceptual authorization path is cardholder, payment provider, acquirer, network, and issuer, with a response traveling back through the chain. An approval indicates authorization under the applicable rules; it is not proof that merchant cash has reached its bank. Capture, clearing, settlement, merchant availability, and bank payout are separate stages. Stripe's manual-capture and payout documentation expose this separation. [S03] [S20]

Northstar should model the authorization request, processor response, capture instruction, clearing record, settlement advice, cash movement, and merchant availability release as separately identifiable facts. A reconciliation system can then explain a payment even if a synchronous response and a later settlement report disagree.

4.2 Manual capture is a bounded right, not an indefinite reservation

Stripe supports separate authorization and capture on eligible methods. The actual capture deadline is available through the charge's authorization details, such as capture_before; do not hard-code a universal seven-day rule. Partial capture normally releases the unused authorized amount, while features such as multicapture require specific eligibility. [S03]

For Northstar, a capture command carries the authorization ID, requested amount, remaining capturable amount, deadline evidence, capture sequence, and provider capability version. The authoritative transition validates all of them. A scheduler can alert before expiry, but it must not capture merely to avoid losing authorization unless the merchant has explicitly authorized that behavior.

4.3 Four clocks and a longer risk tail

The buyer's experience often collapses these stages into "paid." Merchant operations cannot. Define separate timestamps for authorization, capture acceptance, settlement expected, cash observed, funds available, payout submitted, and beneficiary receipt. Store provider timestamps alongside received-at and recorded-at timestamps; they answer different questions.

A later dispute or refund is not evidence that the original capture never happened. It creates additional financial consequences. Cancellation before capture, refund after capture, reversal of a platform transfer, and return of a bank payout therefore need different commands and journal templates. [S09] [S10]

4.4 How to normalize processor responses

In the proposed adapter, map responses into a small canonical model while retaining raw evidence: approved, definitively declined, action required, retryable transport failure, and unknown execution. Keep detailed network codes separately. Do not collapse every provider error into a business decline; an outage disguised as "insufficient funds" corrupts both customer behavior and availability metrics.

Authorization routing may choose among approved acquiring paths before execution. Once a request might have reached the first processor, automatic rerouting becomes a duplicate-risk decision. A local timeout does not grant permission to authorize or capture through a second processor. Use inquiry or the first processor's documented deduplication contract before selecting a new execution identity.

4.5 Evidence required for settlement

A capture confirmation supports a receivable under an approved accounting policy. A settlement file supports clearing and fee allocation. A bank statement supports observed cash. None alone proves all three. Reconcile gross charges, refunds, disputes, processor fees, reserves, and adjustments to the net deposit. Preserve batch and line identifiers so that a net amount can be explained rather than merely matched by coincidence.

5. PaymentIntents: lifecycle, retries, and a safe integration

5.1 The public lifecycle

Stripe's PaymentIntent state machine includes states for missing payment method, confirmation, customer action, processing, manual capture, success, and cancellation. The following diagram is simplified; actual transitions depend on payment method and confirmation mode. A successful browser return is not the authoritative completion signal for every method. [S02]

5.2 A minimal lower-level integration

The following is an illustrative Stripe API v1 request, not a complete checkout implementation. Use a secret key from a managed environment, a version tested for your account, and a business-stable idempotency key. Current Stripe guidance recommends Checkout Sessions with the Payment Element for many integrations; the lower-level PaymentIntent API remains useful for understanding orchestration and specialized flows. [S68] [S65]

curl https://api.stripe.com/v1/payment_intents \
  -u "$STRIPE_SECRET_KEY:" \
  -H "Stripe-Version: $STRIPE_API_VERSION" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: order-184-create-payment-v1" \
  -d amount=10000 \
  -d currency=usd \
  -d 'automatic_payment_methods[enabled]'=true \
  -d 'metadata[order_ref]'=order-184

The backend stores the returned PaymentIntent ID. It supplies the client secret only to the authorized checkout session, and the frontend confirms using the selected Stripe client integration. Never put the client secret in a public URL, analytics event, general application log, or response for a different customer. [S67]

5.3 The merchant's durable state machine

Northstar's merchant integration guide should prescribe a transactional order record, not a sequence of unrelated API calls. On initiation, store the order and an operation key. On network retry, reuse that key. On response, bind the payment ID exactly once. On webhook, verify authenticity and idempotently update the order's fulfillment state. On ambiguity, retrieve the known payment rather than creating another one.

Shipping goods is itself an economic side effect. A duplicate payment.succeeded notification must not produce a second shipment. The merchant should have a unique fulfillment occurrence for an order, just as the PSP has an economic-effect identity for a capture.

5.4 Attempts and customer action

A payment can require a new method after a decline or a customer authentication step before completion. [S66] Preserve the intent while recording each attempt. Do not mutate the amount or beneficiary during a customer challenge without restarting the appropriate confirmation and consent flow. If the cart changes, record a new terms version and determine whether the provider permits updating that intent in its current state.

Northstar's public response should contain status, an explicit next_action when required, the stable payment_id, and an operation ID for uncertain asynchronous work. It should not expose internal bank credentials, unrestricted raw processor responses, or another tenant's object existence.

5.5 What a 200 response does not mean

A successful HTTP request can create a payment that still requires customer action. A completed payment can still have unsettled funds. A settled balance can still be subject to future disputes. A payout can still be awaiting bank receipt. API reference examples must teach these distinctions; otherwise integrators will use transport success as a universal financial finality signal.

5.6 Asynchronous capture changes data availability

Stripe documents automatic_async capture, enabled by default in the latest API behavior described by the guide. A successful confirmation can precede availability of the Charge's balance transaction and, for some Connect flows, transfer or application-fee references. Integrations must tolerate these fields being temporarily null and consume the appropriate later events rather than treating missing expansion data as a failed payment. [S69]

For Northstar, separate payment outcome from enrichment completeness. A reconciliation task may wait for financial references without changing an already established customer-visible outcome. Persist the expected evidence and its deadline, and investigate overdue enrichment. Do not substitute a fabricated balance transaction merely to make a response object appear complete.

6. Credential vault, tokenization, 3DS, and future-use consent

6.1 Keep the sensitive-data boundary small

Stripe-hosted collection components can reduce the merchant's exposure to raw card data, but they do not erase all PCI responsibilities. PCI guidance prohibits retaining card verification codes after authorization, including encrypted retention. Those are constraints on the proposed architecture, not optional optimizations. [S38] [S39]

In Northstar, browser collection goes directly to a restricted vault domain. General payment services receive opaque tokens. A token-use grant is bound to tenant, operation, purpose, destination connector, and expiry. Detokenization is available only to a tightly controlled connector path. Production logs store token references and redacted metadata, never raw card numbers or verification codes.

Encryption keys, vault deployment permissions, and general API permissions belong to separate roles. Limit who can change vault code, who can access encrypted storage, and who can invoke decryption. Separation is valuable only when enforced by identity, network policy, and audited access paths rather than by naming two services differently.

6.2 Tokens need ownership and lifecycle rules

Treat a token as a reference under authorization, not as an unrestricted bearer asset. Each token has a tenant or explicitly approved sharing scope, instrument status, fingerprint policy, consent evidence, creation source, and revocation state. Cross-merchant sharing requires a deliberate product contract and security review.

Card updates and network tokens can change the underlying credential while preserving an application relationship. Your data model should distinguish the stable payment instrument from its current processor or network representation. Record which representation a particular attempt used so that disputes and inquiries remain explainable.

6.3 3DS is authentication, not payment authorization

Stripe documents frictionless and challenge-based 3DS flows. Passing authentication does not by itself guarantee issuer approval of the payment. The integration must carry the authentication result into the later authorization path and handle both outcomes independently. [S04]

Northstar's authentication session should persist the payment terms digest, protocol version, provider transaction references, device context needed under the applicable rules, expiry, and final authentication evidence. Never let a browser callback directly mark the financial payment successful. Validate the callback and obtain the authoritative provider result before advancing execution.

6.4 Future use requires a different contract

A SetupIntent is designed to set up a credential for future payments; it does not collect the payment amount. Off-session use must preserve the relevant consent and mandate context, not merely a saved token. [S05]

The proposed consent record identifies the merchant, customer, instrument, permitted use, agreement text version, acceptance time, and revocation. Future payment requests reference it. A risk or regulatory requirement may still require the customer to return and authenticate. The API must return a recoverable action-required state rather than endlessly retrying in the background.

6.5 Security failure examples

A leaked token should not permit arbitrary cross-tenant charging. A malicious webhook should not change beneficiary details. A developer's dashboard access should not imply vault decryption. A retry should not log a secret because the first call failed. These cases belong in integration and abuse tests, not only in a policy document.

7. Local payment methods: one intent, different capabilities

7.1 Why a universal card-shaped model fails

A card can support authorization followed by capture. An ACH debit is a pull from a bank account under authorization. A bank transfer is a customer-initiated push requiring funding instructions and reconciliation. SEPA Direct Debit depends on its mandate and return behavior. These methods do not share one universal finality or cancellation model. [S03] [S06] [S07] [S08]

Northstar should offer a common intent contract with a versioned capability model. The method implementation decides whether it supports separate capture, customer action, synchronous outcome, cancellation, partial execution, refund, and particular dispute or return paths. Reject unsupported combinations before attempting execution.

7.2 Capability model

{
  "method": "illustrative_bank_debit",
  "capability_version": "2026-09-01",
  "separate_capture": false,
  "requires_mandate": true,
  "completion": "asynchronous",
  "cancellation": "before_submission_only",
  "return_risk": "policy_defined",
  "currencies": ["usd"],
  "eligibility_policy_id": "policy_approved_17"
}

This is Northstar's proposed schema. It is not a Stripe object. Keep corridor rules and commercial eligibility in authoritative configuration rather than hard-coded frontend choices. A supported currency alone does not prove a method is available to the merchant.

7.3 Push funding requires a matching model

For bank transfers, issue a reference or account identifier under the relevant partner contract. On receipt, match the bank credit to customer and payment intent. Handle underpayment, overpayment, duplicate credits, missing references, and unsolicited funds. A received transfer can fund a customer balance without matching exactly one order; do not manufacture a payment success by guessing.

Unmatched cash goes into an approved suspense account with an owner and investigation deadline. The evidence record must survive manual resolution so that moving cash out of suspense has a traceable basis.

7.4 Pull methods require risk-aware availability

A debit submission may complete asynchronously and later return. Northstar can wait for the appropriate evidence before releasing merchant availability, or deliberately extend credit under an approved risk policy. Those are different products. Calling provisional funds "settled" hides credit exposure and contaminates treasury planning.

Maintain received, pending, available, reserved, and recoverable balances explicitly. Use limits and reserves when offering faster merchant access. The risk decision should state the exposure owner, limit, expiry, and accounting treatment.

7.5 Webhooks and retrieval are complementary

Use notifications to schedule state refresh and customer communication. Use the durable payment object and provider observations to resolve state. Repeated notifications, lost notifications, and late returns must all be safe. Periodic sweeps should inspect aged pending work and recover missed events without creating new financial identities.

8. Refunds, disputes, transfer reversals, and payout returns

8.1 Name the operation according to what it changes

Stripe exposes refunds for payments and separate transfer-reversal mechanisms for Connect. Refunding a separate charge does not automatically reverse its related transfers. Disputes have their own lifecycle and financial consequences. [S09] [S10] [S13]

Operation Changes Must not be confused with
Cancel uncaptured authorization Stops or releases an eligible authorization Refunding already captured cash
Refund Returns value associated with a payment Reversing every downstream allocation
Transfer reversal Reallocates Connect funds under its rules A bank receiving returned cash
Payout cancellation Attempts to stop an eligible outbound instruction Proof that an already sent payment cannot execute
Payout return Records money returned after execution Deleting the original payout
Dispute Handles an issuer/customer claim and possible debit An ordinary merchant-requested refund

8.2 A refund is an independent economic identity

Northstar creates a refund ID and effect identity scoped to the original capture and refund occurrence. The same refund request key replays the same operation. A new legitimate partial refund uses a new identity while the total refunded amount is guarded against exceeding the allowed captured amount.

Track requested, provider accepted, cash effect observed, completed, failed, and unknown independently where the rail requires it. A timeout after refund execution needs the same ambiguity handling as a payout. Do not issue a new refund just because the customer's support ticket repeats the complaint.

8.3 Marketplace loss allocation

When a platform refunds a buyer after transferring funds to sellers, the platform may need to recover seller funds, use a reserve, or extend credit. The financial system should post each consequence separately. A seller's empty balance does not erase the buyer refund or authorize an unrecorded negative balance.

Maintain a liability policy per platform and seller agreement. Record which entity bears chargeback principal, fees, FX changes, and unrecoverable losses. Public charge configuration and the actual legal/commercial arrangement must agree; neither should be inferred solely from a UI label.

8.4 Evidence and case management

Dispute handling needs a case deadline, evidence collection, document versions, submission receipts, and a final decision. Freeze the evidence package actually submitted. Later edits create new versions; they do not rewrite the prior submission. Treat duplicate decisions as repeated evidence, and contradictory decisions as a case to resolve rather than arbitrary last-write-wins status.

8.5 Why reversals are append-only

An original payment may have funded a merchant payout, tax record, invoice, and bank settlement. Deleting it after a refund destroys the explanation of those events. Append a reversing or corrective entry with a reason, authorization, reference to the original occurrence, and independent evidence. Corrections can restore an economic balance without pretending history never happened.

9. Onboarding, Accounts v2, capabilities, and the merchant of record

9.1 Identity is an ongoing state machine

Stripe's Accounts v2 model uses configurations and capabilities to represent supported account functions. Recipient onboarding for Global Payouts also uses account and capability concepts. Creating an account is not equivalent to all capabilities becoming active. [S14] [S22]

Northstar should maintain a legal party, its representatives and beneficial owners where required, business profile, verification evidence, product agreement, bank destination ownership evidence, and individual capability states. Separate collecting a document from verifying it, and verifying identity from approving a particular money flow.

A capability is keyed by party, legal entity, product, corridor or region scope, and policy version. States might include requested, information_required, under_review, active, restricted, and revoked. A single boolean kyc_passed is too weak: a merchant may be allowed to accept payments but temporarily unable to receive payouts, or eligible domestically but not for a cross-border program.

9.2 Requirements change after onboarding

Monitor evidence expiry, changes in ownership, new business activities, suspicious behavior, failed bank verification, and revised partner requirements. A requirement-change event should affect future execution according to an approved policy. It should not silently rewrite historic approval evidence.

At every irreversible boundary, validate the capability needed for that action. Cache only within explicit freshness rules. An unavailable verification service is not permission to ignore a restriction. Conversely, the availability of an external service should not be required for every already-approved low-risk read if a valid local decision remains applicable.

9.3 Merchant of record is not a decorative parameter

Stripe's guide distinguishes direct charges, indirect charges with on_behalf_of, and indirect charges without it when describing the merchant of record. It also distinguishes that role from platform loss responsibilities. [S12]

In Northstar, record the merchant of record and settlement merchant as explicit contractual facts, supported by business agreements, customer disclosures, and processor configuration. A parameter must implement the approved arrangement, not invent it. Test statement descriptors, receipts, support contact, dispute ownership, and tax/business records against that arrangement.

9.4 Hosted versus direct onboarding

A hosted partner onboarding flow can reduce the amount of sensitive information your platform collects and shorten integration work. Direct onboarding can improve product control but increases maintenance and security obligations. Both require state synchronization, signed redirects or callbacks, correlation IDs, and a recovery path for an abandoned flow.

Avoid embedding secret account-creation permissions in a browser. Create the onboarding session server-side after authorization. Store only required evidence and provider references. Design support tooling that explains which requirement blocks a capability without exposing unnecessary personal data.

9.5 Company-level control

The risk and compliance teams own eligibility policy; engineering owns reliable enforcement and evidence. Operations can request review but should not have a generic "force active" switch. Exceptions need scope, expiry, approver, documented reason, and exposure limits. A production exception that cannot be audited becomes an undocumented financial product.

10. Connect: charge ownership, allocation, and downstream liability

10.1 Three payment patterns

Stripe supports direct charges on the connected account, destination charges on the platform with a configured destination, and separate charges and transfers for decoupled allocation. Their fee, balance, refund, and dispute consequences differ. [S11]

Pattern Charge location Allocation Northstar lesson
Direct Connected account Seller receives the charge; platform can collect an application fee Scope API ownership and evidence to the seller relationship
Destination Platform One configured destination transfer Keep charge and allocation related without conflating their liabilities
Separate charge / transfers Platform Explicit later transfers, potentially multiple sellers Preserve gross collection, allocation, fees, and reversal identities

10.2 Transfer is not payout

A Connect Transfer moves funds under the platform's account-allocation model; it is not proof that a bank has credited a beneficiary. A Payout has a distinct external destination and lifecycle. Designing both as one table named payments without typed semantics invites incorrect refunds and balance displays. [S11] [S20]

10.3 Separate charge and transfer example

This is a Stripe v1 example after a successful, eligible charge. The IDs and amounts are placeholders. source_transaction is the source Charge ID, not the PaymentIntent ID. Stripe documents transfer_group as correlation, not a mechanism that itself gates funding or deduplicates transfers. [S13]

  -H "Stripe-Version: $STRIPE_API_VERSION" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: order-184-seller-a-allocation-v1" \

  -d currency=usd \
  -d destination="$CONNECTED_ACCOUNT_ID" \

Northstar's equivalent creates a stable allocation occurrence. It validates seller capability, the source's eligibility, unallocated amount, legal-entity relationship, and currency. It records the liability reclassification and allocation state atomically. An allocation instruction that has not committed cannot be described as seller-owned available money.

10.4 Splitting an order safely

Suppose an illustrative USD 100 order leaves USD 97 after a USD 3 platform fee. Allocate USD 70 to seller A and USD 20 to seller B, leaving USD 7 under the agreed platform treatment. The allocation policy must define whether that remainder is retained revenue, customer credit, or another obligation. It is not automatically revenue merely because it was not transferred.

If the buyer later receives a USD 50 refund, compute each seller's contractual responsibility separately. Do not reverse a random transfer because it happens to have the same amount. Track original allocation, recovered amount, outstanding recovery, and platform-funded exposure.

10.5 Important implementation consequences

Resource ownership must match authentication context. A platform secret, a connected-account context, and a seller-facing credential do not grant identical rights. Queries, webhooks, pagination, and exports must consistently apply the same ownership rules.

Keep platform fees and processor fees distinct. Keep cash owned by one legal entity separate from another's obligations. When balances cross entity boundaries, use an approved intercompany or settlement model instead of assuming software allocation alone transfers legal ownership.

11. Cross-border eligibility is a policy matrix, not a currency flag

11.1 Geography and currency are separate dimensions

A USD payment can be cross-border without an FX conversion. A conversion can occur within one country. A merchant's incorporation country, customer location, card issuer country, acquiring location, settlement currency, beneficiary country, and payout rail are different inputs.

Stripe's Connect cross-border guide describes supported programs and regional constraints rather than granting universal country-to-country transfers. Global Payouts has a separate eligibility model. Its public overview advertises broad recipient coverage while sender-country access and preview programs remain narrower. [S15] [S21]

11.2 The proposed eligibility key

Northstar should evaluate a route against a versioned policy whose inputs include:

sender legal entity and product agreement
merchant / platform / recipient configurations
sender and beneficiary country
business category and payment purpose
funding source and ownership
source, presentment, settlement, and destination currency
bank destination and rail
amount, frequency, cumulative limits
screening evidence and capability freshness
provider account, contract, and program version

The policy produces allow, deny, or review; a reason code; required evidence; applicable limits; and expiry. Persist the decision with the payment. Recheck relevant restrictions before execution when policy requires it.

11.3 Two separate gates

Product gate: is the company allowed to provide this service to this party under this contract? Execution gate: can the selected partner execute this specific instruction now, with available funds, active capability, valid destination, and required data?

A payment that passes the first gate can fail the second because of liquidity or a bank cutoff. A payment that passes a technical bank validation can still fail the product gate. Do not let an adapter's successful preflight override the company's eligibility policy.

11.4 India-specific caution without assuming an India entity

Stripe's India FAQ described new accounts as invite-only when checked. India-specific Connect documentation contains restrictions and supported exceptions that do not reduce to the general global guide. Treat the exact platform country, recipient agreement, charge model, and approved program as material. [S59] [S60]

For an India-based business building this platform, review the RBI's September 2025 consolidated payment-aggregator directions, including the cross-border framework, rather than assuming the 2023 document alone describes the current requirements. The actual authorization and compliance analysis belongs with qualified local counsel and the sponsoring institutions. [S61] [S62]

11.5 Product availability registry

Maintain CorridorPolicy, ProviderCapability, BankDestinationRule, and CurrencyRule as versioned configuration. Every rule has source evidence, owner, effective date, review date, and test fixtures. A support agent's informal statement should not silently become global production configuration.

Expose a merchant-facing eligibility explanation that is useful but does not disclose sensitive screening logic. For example: "This payout currency is not enabled for this destination account" is actionable. "Payment failed" is not.

12. FX: presentment, settlement, allocation, payout, and valuation

12.1 Four currencies, potentially multiple conversions

The customer sees a presentment currency. A platform and seller may settle into different balance currencies. A bank payout may require another destination currency. Finance also has a functional currency for reporting. Conversions at these boundaries must be modeled separately.

Stripe's Connect FX guide documents different conversion paths for direct charges, destination charges with or without on_behalf_of, and separate transfers. A quote attached to the charge does not automatically lock every later fee or transfer conversion. Multi-currency settlement can change the path where enabled. [S17] [S19]

12.2 Current FX Quotes API boundary

Stripe's FX Quotes API is documented as a preview. It offers informational rates and eligible time-limited locks, including five-minute, hourly, and daily choices depending on region and use. The quote can expire through time or significant market movement; delayed methods can have fallback behavior. Do not present a lock as unconditional protection for all future stages. [S16]

A production integration must pin the supported preview schema and account entitlement. The documentation currently includes samples using different dated preview versions. Copying one header from a page is not sufficient version management.

12.3 Adaptive Pricing is not your treasury engine

Adaptive Pricing provides a higher-level localized-pricing experience with its own supported integrations and limitations. It is not a generic API for hedging a company's bank liquidity. Its documented exclusions and availability must be checked for the intended flow. [S18]

Northstar should separate customer pricing from treasury execution. A customer quote commits commercial terms under the product policy. Treasury may already hold destination liquidity, hedge aggregated exposure, or use a partner conversion. Those choices affect the company's risk and cost without changing a previously accepted customer promise arbitrarily.

12.4 Exact arithmetic and direction

Define a rate as destination major units per source major unit. For source minor amount s, source exponent es, destination exponent ed, and rational rate n/d:

raw destination minor units = s * n * 10^ed / (d * 10^es)

Use integer arithmetic, a documented rounding rule, and checked limits. Persist both customer amounts. Never rely on binary floating point or a vague field named rate with no direction. Source-fixed and recipient-fixed quotes need different rounding policies: guaranteeing a recipient amount may require rounding the source requirement upward.

Record rate timestamp, expiry, fee currencies, minimum fee, spread treatment, quote provider, destination version, policy version, and the permitted acceptance action. A quote used for price display is not necessarily a booked conversion or a liquidity reservation.

12.5 Refund and return exposure

An original FX conversion and a later refund can happen at different rates. The resulting gain or loss belongs in an explicit account under the approved policy. Do not silently charge the difference to the customer or seller without a contractual basis. Stripe's FX documentation also describes current-rate behavior for applicable refunded or disputed conversions; verify the exact product contract before promising a fixed refund rate. [S16]

The proposed accounting examples use separately balanced journals per currency, with explicit FX-position accounts. Functional-currency valuation and hedge accounting are a separate controller-approved layer, not a reason to add USD and EUR debits together.

14. Treasury, FinancialAccounts, and stablecoin rails

14.1 Three concepts to keep separate

A merchant Payments balance, a legacy Treasury FinancialAccount, and newer financial-account types should not be treated as identical just because each has a balance. Stripe's current platform financial-account model includes different account types, including payments-related representations. Scope statements to the product generation actually being integrated. [S27] [S28]

Global Payouts distinguishes an outbound payment to another party from an outbound transfer involving an owned destination. The ownership semantics affect eligibility and controls, not merely the endpoint name. [S26]

14.2 Your treasury model

Northstar needs an inventory of actual bank accounts, their legal owners, currencies, restrictions, available rights, intraday limits, funding deadlines, and reconciliation status. Customer obligations and company operating cash must remain distinguishable. A balance on a provider API is evidence about one account relationship, not automatically cash freely usable across all company entities.

A treasury transfer has its own intent, authorization, execution attempt, fees, cash evidence, and counterpart accounting. It may rebalance a prefunded account without directly paying a customer. Preserve this separation so a treasury optimization cannot accidentally discharge the wrong customer obligation.

14.3 Stablecoins add a rail, not universal finality

Stripe documents stablecoin payment capabilities, and its 2026 engineering discussion describes extending its global treasury network with stablecoin infrastructure. These sources support treating digital-asset rails as part of an evolving network, not as proof that every existing payment product supports every chain or country. [S29] [S44]

For Northstar, an optional stablecoin adapter needs network and asset identity, address validation, custody policy, signing controls, fee funding, transaction broadcast identity, confirmation policy, chain observation, and conversion into the recipient's promised asset. A chain transaction becoming confirmed does not itself prove a fiat bank beneficiary has been paid.

14.4 New risks that must be explicit

The proposed risk model includes issuer and redemption exposure, custody compromise, chain disruption, address errors, irreversible transfer semantics, liquidity fragmentation, and jurisdictional restrictions. These are design considerations, not claims about a particular Stripe implementation.

Keep on-chain transaction hash, provider operation ID, customer disbursement ID, and bank payout ID separate. A single customer promise may span more than one execution system. If conversion or bank delivery fails after an on-chain step, the system needs an obligation and recovery plan rather than a false terminal success.

14.5 Decision rule for the initial company

Do not add a new rail solely because its happy path looks faster. Add it when the legal model, liquidity, unit economics, reversibility, support, and reconciliation are understood and tested. Reuse the common ledger, operations, and evidence framework; keep rail-specific finality and failure semantics inside a versioned capability contract.

16. Requirements, workload, and a defensible availability contract

16.1 State the promised operation

Northstar targets 99.999% availability for eligible intent acceptance, financial commands, and authoritative balance reads, measured separately. This is a proposed objective, not a measured result. Bank delivery has a corridor-specific promise because it depends on external institutions, cutoffs, and finality semantics.

Operation Good response Proposed deadline
Accept payment intent Durable identity and terms, or accurate deterministic rejection 2 seconds
Post financial command Committed result, identical replay, or accurate business rejection 2 seconds
Read spendable balance Authorized strong result 2 seconds
Dispatch eligible work Work progresses without avoidable internal queue delay Separate queue-age target
Complete external payment Rail-defined outcome and evidence Corridor contract

A 202 that promises durable acceptance can be correct. The same response cannot be counted as successful posting when the API promised immediate posting. Incorrect business rejections, outages disguised as declines, and throttling below agreed quotas count as service failures.

16.2 Error budget and dependency arithmetic

At complete outage, five nines permits 25.92 seconds in a 30-day month and 315.36 seconds in a 365-day year. A ten-second monthly outage consumes about 38.6% of that wall-clock equivalent. Request-weighted SLIs still need their own denominator and per-cell measurement.

Six independent serial dependencies each at 99.999% yield 0.99999^6, approximately 99.994%, before considering correlated failures. The arithmetic motivates removing banks, analytics, notifications, search, and event publication from the financial commit path. It does not predict real reliability.

Spanner's SLA lists five-nines availability for qualifying configurations. That contract does not automatically become the availability of an application built on it. [S53]

16.3 Reference workload, explicitly hypothetical

Assume 1,000 new payments per second on average and 10,000 at peak, with roughly five financial transactions and ten journal entries per payment. This yields 86.4 million payments and 864 million entries per day. At 6 KiB of total logical data per payment, the starting model is about 530.8 GB per day before indexes, replication, backups, and expanded evidence.

The purpose is to force capacity discussions. It is not a claim that a particular cluster or startup needs this scale. Replace it with an actual distribution of tenants, accounts, methods, amount sizes, refund rates, hot sellers, batch timing, and retention.

16.4 Fault model and non-negotiable correctness

Target process, machine, zone, temporary-network, duplicated-message, and one supported regional failure. Do not claim uninterrupted safe writes after quorum loss or arbitrary simultaneous compromise of all privileged authorities. When financial authority is unavailable, fail closed and count the unavailability.

Conservation, authorization, uniqueness, and no unauthorized overspending are invariants, not probabilistic objectives. A single duplicate financial effect is an incident even when every API responds quickly. Availability work should improve safe completion, never redefine incorrect execution as success.

17. Northstar high-level architecture and service boundaries

17.1 One cohesive financial authority

The Financial Core owns economic identities, journals, current balances, reservations, financial payment transitions, and outbox rows. It exposes typed business commands. These are deliberately kept inside one transactional authority per residency-approved cell.

Surrounding services handle checkout, credentials, identity evidence, risk, pricing, scheduling, provider translation, notifications, reporting, and reconciliation. They may be separate deployables, but they do not become alternative authorities for spendable money.

17.2 Why not a microservice for every noun?

Separating Payment, Balance, Journal, Reservation, and Idempotency into independent databases would make every ordinary payment require a distributed consistency protocol. The organization would then be solving coordination failures rather than product-specific financial problems.

Use modular code and clear ownership inside the Financial Core. Extract a module only when its independent scaling or security boundary is worth the additional coordination. The vault is a strong candidate for isolation; the journal and its authoritative balance are not a good first split.

17.3 Recommended storage choices

For the strict regional-resilience objective, use an approved multi-region Spanner configuration and explicitly serializable read-write transactions for financial commands. Spanner documents external consistency for serializable transactions. This is a proposed choice, not a claim about Stripe's stack. [S54]

Store immutable evidence blobs in controlled object storage with hashes and retention policy, while the database stores references, identity, digest, and processing state. Use the transactional outbox to feed an at-least-once event bus. Derived databases serve search and analytics with freshness metadata. A Redis-like cache can accelerate nonauthoritative configuration or reads; it must not decide whether money is spendable.

The reference SQL in the bundle uses PostgreSQL syntax to make the relational model inspectable. It is not a Spanner deployment schema and does not demonstrate regional safety. The implementation team must translate and test the chosen engine's types, constraints, transaction semantics, and operational behavior.

17.4 Cell boundaries

Choose the fewest cells needed for data placement, isolation, and measured scale. Each account and business payment has one home cell. A versioned directory routes requests. A stale route cannot create the same payment in a second cell; ownership is validated by the financial authority.

Cross-cell flows require an explicit protocol with durable decisions, local balanced entries, and spending gates. Avoid them for the initial product when one lawful transaction boundary is possible. A routing layer must never promote an asynchronously stale copy into an independent writable ledger.

17.5 Control plane and data plane

The control plane manages schemas, deployments, entitlements, routing, policy versions, credentials, and provider configuration. The data plane validates and executes already-configured operations. Preserve safe, bounded independence: a dashboard outage should not halt all valid payments, but an unavailable configuration service is not permission to ignore a newly authoritative freeze.

Deploy immutable policy bundles with explicit effective times, freshness rules, and emergency revocation paths. Persist the bundle version used by every irreversible operation.

18. API gateway, authentication, schemas, errors, and versioning

18.1 Request pipeline

Northstar's edge terminates transport, imposes request-size limits, assigns a correlation ID, and routes to the tenant's home cell. The API layer authenticates credentials, resolves account context, validates schema and quotas, and hands a typed command to the Core. The Core performs authoritative authorization and financial checks.

Do not trust a tenant ID supplied in a JSON body when the authenticated principal implies another tenant. Object IDs are identifiers, not authorization. Apply resource ownership checks to retrieves, lists, nested expansions, exports, events, and idempotent replays.

18.2 API v1 and v2 are materially different Stripe contracts

Stripe's API v1 primarily uses form-encoded requests, JSON responses, expansion, and snapshot-style events. API v2 uses JSON, explicit versioning, include-dependent fields, thin events, and different pagination and consistency behavior. v2 lists are eventually consistent by default; do not use a list result as a financial uniqueness check. [S30]

For Northstar, choose one clear public wire format initially, while preserving a versioning mechanism. JSON simplifies deeply nested money and beneficiary contracts. Represent potentially large minor-unit amounts as decimal strings, even when particular provider APIs use JSON numbers. Adapt at the connector boundary with checked conversion.

18.3 Error contract

{
  "error": {
    "code": "operation_outcome_unknown",
    "message": "The original operation is being resolved.",
    "retryable": true,
    "operation_id": "op_01_example",
    "request_id": "req_01_example"

}

Specify whether a response means no operation began, a durable operation exists, a deterministic business rejection occurred, or execution is unknown. Do not use retryable: true to imply that a new idempotency key is safe. The documentation must say which identity to reuse and which endpoint retrieves status.

Stripe's advanced error guide treats some server errors as indeterminate and warns that side effects may have occurred. A new key is not a safe generic remedy. [S32]

18.4 Stable versioning

Pin request semantics, enum behavior, event representation, canonicalization, and posting-template selection. A new response field can still break strict clients; test generated SDKs and typical handwritten clients. Unknown event types should be ignored safely rather than crashing a consumer.

For breaking changes, support explicit versions and an upgrade process with test environments, event replay fixtures, version-specific examples, and observability. Stripe documents versioned API upgrades; use that as a product-quality benchmark, not as a reason to copy every historical versioning decision. [S36]

18.5 Rate limits are part of the financial contract

Use per-tenant, per-operation, and hot-account admission controls. Return machine-readable retry guidance. Reserve capacity for inquiry, recovery, and reconciliation during overload. Rate limiting below contractual quota is an availability failure, not user misbehavior.

Stripe documents separate rate and concurrency limits. Northstar should similarly distinguish request volume from concurrent expensive work and expose understandable diagnostics. [S35]

19. Idempotency, concurrency, and unknown outcomes

19.1 Six identity layers

Identity Scope Protects against
API idempotency key Tenant + operation family + key Repeated HTTP submission
Merchant business reference Tenant + business action New key accidentally recreating the same order
Economic effect Business scope + effect type + occurrence Duplicate financial mutation
Provider execution identity Provider account + endpoint scope + attempt Duplicate external submission where contract supports it
Event identity Producer + event; consumer inbox Repeated message delivery
Actual cash-movement identity Bank account + statement identity + movement reference Repeated observation of one cash movement

These keys are not substitutes for one another. An actual second debit must be represented even when it violates the intended payment's uniqueness. A duplicate callback is not the same thing as duplicate cash.

19.2 Stripe's two idempotency models

For API v1, Stripe stores the initial result after execution begins, including saved error responses; keys can be pruned after at least 24 hours. Validation failures or concurrent conflicts before execution starts are not the same as a stored executed result. [S31]

For v2, the documented scope includes the same key, API, account or sandbox, and a 30-day window. Successful replay can return an updated representation; failed or partly completed work can be recovered rather than merely replaying one frozen response. Do not implement v2 clients by assuming v1's response-cache semantics. [S30]

These are provider contracts, not a sufficient retention policy for your company's economic history. Northstar retains economic-effect identities through every supported replay, dispute, restore, migration, and correction horizon under its approved retention policy.

19.3 Fingerprints and stable terms

Create a canonical representation containing account ownership, amount, currency, beneficiary version, quote, fee mode, legal entity, business reference, API semantic version, and canonicalization version. Exclude trace IDs and transport timestamps. Hash that representation with a standard cryptographic digest and store the canonical version alongside it.

Same key and same terms returns the existing operation. Same key and different terms returns a conflict. A new legitimate business occurrence gets a new economic identity. Reauthorize the caller before returning an old result; an idempotency key is not an access token.

19.4 Concurrent requests

Uniqueness must be enforced by the authoritative database, not a read-then-write check in application memory. Two duplicate requests race on the same key. Two different payments race on the same protected balance or spending-rights row. The losing transaction retries and reevaluates the new state.

Aggregate all deltas for an account before checking the final balance. Otherwise a transaction with several entries might incorrectly fail or pass based on an arbitrary loop order. Apply a deterministic lock or access order when the engine and implementation require it, while still relying on the database's transactional guarantees.

19.5 Unknown is a real state

A transport timeout can occur before execution, during execution, after commit, or after the response was sent. The caller cannot infer which from the timeout alone. Query or resubmit under the original identity. A temporarily empty lookup does not prove an earlier in-flight operation can never commit.

At the external boundary, safe replay depends on the provider's actual contract. The company must be willing to stop an ambiguous payout rather than execute it twice for the sake of a low latency graph.

20.2 Balance semantics

For a debit-normal account, posted_net = debits - credits; for a credit-normal account, posted_net = credits - debits. A protected spendable account uses spendable = posted_net - reserved_out. Require spendable >= approved_floor.

The approved floor may be negative only for a deliberately configured credit facility. Asset, liability, revenue, expense, and suspense purposes need controller-approved templates.

20.3 Constraints and checks

Foreign keys preserve ownership and book/currency relationships. Row checks enforce positive bounded amounts, valid sides, and reservation conservation. Unique constraints enforce economic and business identities. But an ordinary row check cannot prove a multi-row journal balances. The typed writer validates the whole posting plan, and an independent verifier reconstructs it later.

The bundled DDL is a reference skeleton. It intentionally does not claim that merely applying table definitions creates a secure ledger. A production deployment needs restricted write roles, the transaction implementation, migration controls, independent checks, evidence retention, and tested recovery.

20.4 Time and history

Keep created_at, recorded_at, provider_effective_at, received_at, and accounting_period separate. Late evidence can be economically effective earlier than its arrival. Never rewrite the fact of when the system learned it.

Use immutable events and controlled current-state projections. A historical query should specify whether it asks what was economically effective on a date or what the system knew at that time. This distinction matters during reconciliation, disputes, and incident reconstruction.

20.5 Index and partition design

Distribute independent IDs to avoid a single append hotspot where the database's key layout would make it one. Partition scanning work by stable buckets and status, not only a global timestamp. A leading monotonically increasing key can concentrate writes in range-partitioned systems, so validate the chosen physical layout under realistic skew.

Do not shard a protected balance by copying it. Split explicit spending rights, or serialize competing operations on the account. Table partitioning changes throughput; it does not eliminate the need for conservation.

21. Atomic financial command execution

21.1 The transaction boundary

The command's stable identity, journal entries, balance updates, reservation changes, financial state transition, and outbox notification must become visible together. The database transaction does not include the bank call, email, message-broker publication, or external screening request.

21.2 Reference algorithm

# Design pseudocode: persistence and engine details are not implemented here.
def apply_command(command, principal):
    prepared = canonicalize_and_assign_stable_ids(command)

    def body(tx):
        existing = tx.get_effect(prepared.effect_key)
        if existing:
            authorize_replay(principal, existing)
            require_same_fingerprint(existing, prepared)
            return existing.result

        state = tx.read_authoritative_state(prepared.references)
        authorize_command(principal, prepared, state)
        require_expected_version_and_valid_transition(state, prepared)
        require_current_capabilities_and_admissible_evidence(state)

        plan = build_versioned_posting_plan(state, prepared)
        validate_each_journal_per_book_and_currency(plan)
        deltas = aggregate_account_and_reservation_deltas(plan)
        next_state = checked_apply_deltas(state, deltas)
        validate_spending_floors_and_reservation_conservation(next_state)

        result = make_stable_result(prepared, next_state)
        tx.insert_effect(prepared.effect_key, prepared.fingerprint, result)
        tx.insert_journal_groups_and_entries(plan)
        tx.update_balances_reservations_and_payment(next_state)
        tx.append_reservation_events(plan)
        tx.insert_outbox_events(plan.events)
        return result

    return database.run_serializable(body, bounded_retry=True)

Persist deterministic business rejections under the operation's documented semantics where appropriate. Do not freeze an infrastructure timeout into a permanent financial failure. The financial-effect key and API operation key can have different result contracts while remaining linked.

21.3 Why callback purity matters

Spanner transaction runners can retry callbacks. A bank call inside a retried callback can execute twice even when the database commits once. Build a durable authorized attempt in the transaction, then let a separate worker perform the external action. [S55]

Generate stable IDs before the retry loop, or derive them deterministically from the original command. Do not include wall-clock readings, random pricing, mutable beneficiary lookups, or nonrepeatable external calls inside the posting plan.

21.4 Reservations are spending rights

For a customer with 500 units and a 102-unit hold: posted is 500, reserved is 102, spendable is 398. Capturing the hold changes posted to 398 and reserved to zero; spendable remains 398. Releasing an unused hold increases spendable without changing posted money.

After funding has been booked, releasing a reservation does not refund the customer. A refund requires a separate approved financial effect. Similarly, a reservation that protects a possibly executed payout cannot expire merely because a timer fires. The system must first establish the applicable non-execution or cancellation evidence.

21.5 Limits of the guarantee

Within the transaction boundary, the design can enforce at-most-one committed financial effect for an economic identity and serializable spending checks. It does not by itself guarantee exactly-once bank execution, correct partner evidence, freedom from privileged compromise, or that every accounting policy is correct. Those require separate controls and tests.

22. Acquiring connectors: from authorization to settlement evidence

22.1 Adapter contract

A proposed acquiring adapter exposes typed operations such as authorize, inquire_authorization, capture, cancel_authorization, refund, and fetch_settlement_report. Each capability specifies scope, request identity, response semantics, timeout behavior, safe-retry conditions, partial-operation support, and evidence requirements.

The connector transforms a prepared immutable instruction into the provider format. It cannot alter amount, merchant entity, credential scope, or capture policy opportunistically. Any material change requires a new approved command and terms version.

22.2 Execution slot and dispatch lease

Before dispatch, reserve an execution slot for the payment action. Store provider account, endpoint, payload digest, idempotency key, attempt state, and authorization evidence. A worker lease controls which local worker should act, but the provider's own deduplication or inquiry contract controls whether a delayed request can execute externally.

The lease duration is therefore not a safe bank-side fencing mechanism. A worker paused before a network send can wake after another worker took over. Without a safe provider contract, the replacement worker must resolve uncertainty rather than assume expiry proves non-execution.

22.3 Capture and clearing correlation

One authorization can lead to a capture under a specific permitted model. Store the provider's authorization reference, capture reference, merchant account, currency, amount, and settlement identifiers. If partial captures are supported, each has its own economic occurrence and remaining capturable amount check.

A clearing file can include fees, reversals, adjustments, and multiple settlement dates. Normalize the file with immutable line identities and a checksum. Reprocessing the same file must not duplicate postings; receiving a corrected file requires explicit supersession or correction handling, not blind replacement.

23. Payout adapters and the UNKNOWN execution problem

23.1 Three outcomes, not two

Every external instruction resolves to proven execution, proven non-execution, or unknown. A local HTTP error is not itself any of those financial proofs. The adapter must retain the original evidence rather than overwrite it with a simplified status.

23.2 Durable instruction before network access

Persist the disbursement identity, recipient version, source account, exact amounts, currencies, fee mode, provider scope, key, payload hash, capability contract, and approval evidence. The send worker obtains this immutable instruction from the financial authority.

On response, persist an observation with the original request reference and raw-evidence digest. A guarded Core command advances the financial state. The adapter does not directly update customer balances.

23.3 Safe retry policy

When a provider guarantees deduplication within a documented scope and lifetime, replay the identical instruction inside that contract. After its lifetime or outside its scope, do not assume the same string still prevents duplication. When no adequate guarantee exists, use inquiry, callbacks, statements, and controlled investigation.

A "not found" inquiry can be stale. Treat it as conclusive only if the provider contract establishes the required completeness and time boundary. A cancellation acknowledgment must also be interpreted precisely: accepted cancellation work is not always proof that execution is impossible.

23.4 No blind provider failover

Routing to provider B because provider A timed out can pay the beneficiary twice. Safe failover requires proof that no potentially executing attempt exists, a conclusive non-execution result, or a cancellation guarantee that excludes late execution. A local lease or internal state update cannot recall a packet already sent to a bank.

If a business chooses to compensate a customer while the original payout remains unresolved, record a separately approved compensation effect and exposure. Do not relabel the original payment as nonexistent to make the dashboard cleaner.

23.5 Cash and delivery are different evidence

A bank debit can precede beneficiary delivery. Record cash leaving into an outbound receivable or clearing account under the accounting policy. Clear the beneficiary obligation on the appropriate finality evidence. A later return creates a new event and journal, preserving the original execution.

UNKNOWN operations need exposure totals, aging, an owner, escalation deadlines, and customer communications. A queue with no owner is not a recovery mechanism. Measure both count and value; one high-value unresolved payment can dominate risk even when the event count is small.

24. FX engine: pricing, acceptance, risk, and exact amounts

24.1 Quote lifecycle

Northstar's quote is an immutable commercial offer with a unique ID, source and destination currencies, amount mode, fee breakdown, accepted rounding policy, expiry, and eligible execution context. A preview price is not yet a booked FX trade. A quote acceptance is not necessarily a hedge execution. A hedge does not discharge the customer payout obligation.

Separate indicative, executable, accepted, expired, canceled, and consumed states. Enforce the permitted usage count and scope. Some commercial products allow a quote for one payment only; others support an explicit capacity. Do not let a retry consume the capacity twice.

24.2 Source-fixed and recipient-fixed contracts

For a source-fixed quote, the sender commits a source amount and the system calculates the recipient amount after applicable fees. For a recipient-fixed quote, the system must collect enough source currency to deliver the promised destination amount. Rounding direction and fee placement differ.

Persist final integer amounts rather than recomputing them during payout. Validate exponent metadata and bounds. The bundled money helpers demonstrate rational arithmetic and explicit rounding, but they do not implement commercial pricing, executable market data, or provider risk.

24.3 Acceptance transaction

Within the financial authority, read the quote, payment terms, customer funds, destination liquidity rights, and relevant capabilities. Require the quote to be usable under its terms at the defined acceptance boundary. Reserve source and destination rights as the product contract requires. Record acceptance and the economic identity atomically.

If the external FX provider separately commits a trade, that action needs its own durable attempt and recovery protocol. Do not put it inside the database callback. The company must decide whether it accepts temporary market risk, uses prefunded currency, or waits for provider confirmation before guaranteeing the customer terms.

24.4 Market-data quality and circuit breakers

The proposed pricing service tracks source freshness, pair coverage, spread, outlier divergence, market-open assumptions, and provider health. Reject or review quotes when input evidence violates policy. A cached last-known rate is not automatically a safe executable price during a provider outage.

Circuit breakers should distinguish quoting new exposure from fulfilling already accepted obligations. Stopping new quotes can be prudent while continuing settlement of committed payments. Canceling existing promises requires the terms and customer-remediation process to support it.

24.5 Exposure and hedging

Track accepted but unhedged exposure by currency, maturity, entity, and counterparty. Net only compatible obligations. Treasury can hedge aggregates, but the ledger retains the gross customer and merchant facts. Record hedge execution, fees, valuation, and realized differences separately.

The engineering system supplies exact positions and evidence. Finance and treasury approve risk limits, valuation methodology, hedge accounting, and escalation. A mathematically optimized route cannot override those constraints.

25. Treasury routing, prefunding, netting, and liquidity rights

25.1 Model an eligible route graph

Represent accounts as nodes with currency, owner, available rights, restrictions, and reconciliation freshness. Represent rails as edges with supported amounts, destination rules, fees, timing distributions, cutoffs, finality, and provider contract version. The routing engine searches only the eligible subgraph.

The proposed optimization first enforces legal, operational, and liquidity constraints, then considers cost, expected delivery, risk, and reliability. A weighted score can compare valid paths; it must not trade away mandatory constraints for a lower fee.

25.2 Prefunding makes promises possible, at a cost

Destination prefunding avoids waiting for an international treasury transfer before every local payout. It ties up capital and creates bank, currency, and concentration risk. Maintain target and emergency buffers based on observed demand, replenishment time, volatility, returns, and bank operating windows.

Do not use the latest cached bank balance as the only spending budget. Subtract reservations, observed in-flight debits, protected obligations, uncertainty buffers, and other authorized consumers. If another system can debit the bank account outside Northstar's control, an absolute no-overdraft guarantee cannot be derived from Northstar's ledger alone.

25.3 Scale explicit rights

A EUR 100 million authorized pool might allocate EUR 30 million, EUR 40 million, and EUR 30 million to three disjoint buckets. Each bucket spends only its rights. Rebalancing moves rights atomically and preserves the total. Giving every worker a cached EUR 100 million balance would create EUR 300 million of apparent spending capacity.

Separate a rights rebalance inside one financial authority from moving actual bank cash between accounts. The first changes control allocation; the second creates an external money movement and reconciliation obligation.

25.4 Netting and batching

Batching reduces execution overhead, while netting reduces required movement between compatible obligations. Neither should erase the gross ledger. A batch contains stable item identities and a batch identity. Partial execution must identify exactly which items executed, remained pending, or failed.

Eligibility for netting depends on entity, currency, value date, counterparty agreement, and applicable rules. Do not net customer assets across unrelated legal owners merely because the arithmetic cancels. Preserve an audit trail from each gross obligation to the net settlement and its cash evidence.

25.5 Intraday operations

Treasury needs a live dashboard of available rights, expected inflows, scheduled outflows, pending bank work, unknown exposure, funding deadlines, and aged unreconciled cash. Alerts should have actionable playbooks: fund an account, restrict a corridor, reduce an exposure limit, or investigate a discrepancy.

A routing failover can be allowed for new instructions while existing unknown instructions remain pinned to their original provider. That distinction protects continuity without creating duplicate payments.

26.1 Assumptions and sign convention

These are illustrative operational entries for approved accounting templates, not Stripe's private ledger or fee schedule. Amounts are in major units for readability; implementation stores minor-unit integers. Debit and credit have accounting meaning, not a universal increase/decrease meaning. Controllers must approve recognition, safeguarding, revenue, tax, functional-currency valuation, and intercompany treatment.

26.3 Marketplace allocation and payout

Allocate USD 70 to seller A and USD 20 to seller B from the USD 97 available amount. Debit platform-held distributable liability and credit each seller liability under the approved ownership model. The remaining USD 7 needs an explicit contractual purpose.

For seller A's payout, reserve USD 70, then debit seller available liability and credit payout payable when funding is booked. On cash debit, debit outbound settlement receivable and credit bank cash. On delivery evidence, debit payout payable and credit outbound settlement receivable. This separates allocation, cash movement, and obligation discharge.

26.4 Cross-currency prefunded payout

Assume the customer has USD 500, pays USD 100 principal plus USD 2 fee, and the recipient receives EUR 90. The entity is approved to hold both currency books and destination liquidity is prefunded.

Reserve USD 102 and EUR 90. Customer spendable becomes USD 398. On booking, debit USD customer liability 102; credit USD FX position 100 and fee revenue 2. In the EUR journal, debit FX position 90 and credit payout payable 90. Commit both balanced journals in one journal group where the lawful database boundary permits.

Consume the customer's source reservation. Keep destination liquidity reserved until the relevant cash debit is recorded. On EUR bank debit: debit outbound receivable 90 and credit bank cash 90. On delivery: debit payout payable 90 and credit outbound receivable 90.

The USD and EUR FX positions require valuation and treasury management; they are not balanced by adding unlike currencies together.

26.5 Refund and intercompany examples

If an original EUR obligation cost USD 110 and a later required EUR refund costs USD 115, the extra USD 5 is an explicit FX expense under the illustrative policy, not a hidden balance adjustment. Fees and customer entitlements are separate.

When two legal entities participate, each book remains locally balanced using approved due-to/due-from or settlement accounts. Their intercompany balances reconcile. A cross-cell protocol cannot make two independent legal entities one accounting entity, and a shared database does not remove their obligations.

27. Events, webhooks, and a developer platform that survives retries

27.1 Transactional outbox and inbox

Write the event publication intent in the same transaction as the financial effect. The relay publishes and then records progress. A crash between those steps causes duplicate delivery, which consumers must tolerate. A consumer commits its inbox identity and business effect together before acknowledging the message.

An event ID prevents repeated processing of that event. The economic-effect identity still prevents two distinct events describing the same occurrence from double-posting. The merchant's own fulfillment system needs the same pattern for shipping or service activation.

27.2 Stripe's event contracts

Stripe documents retry behavior, signature verification, and non-guaranteed event ordering for webhooks. API v2 thin events and traditional snapshot events have different payload and retrieval expectations. Pin the event contract rather than assuming every notification contains a complete current resource. [S33] [S34]

Northstar should expose a stable event envelope containing event ID, type, tenant/account context, resource ID, resource version, occurrence time, schema version, and payload or retrieval reference. Do not promise global order across unrelated accounts or resources.

27.3 Secure webhook delivery

Sign the exact transmitted bytes and include a timestamp and key identifier under a documented scheme. Support signing-key rotation with overlap. The receiver verifies before parsing business effects and persists the observation before acknowledging it.

Protect the delivery service against server-side request forgery: validate destinations, block disallowed internal ranges, control redirects and DNS rebinding, and isolate egress. A tenant-configured URL must not become access to cloud metadata or internal services. Apply payload limits, timeouts, and retry budgets.

27.4 Operator and developer experience

Provide a delivery log with event ID, attempt timestamps, redacted request/response metadata, status, next retry, and a replay control. Replay keeps the original event identity. It must not create a new financial occurrence just to make a failed delivery look new.

Offer test events and realistic fixtures, but clearly label whether they test signature handling, resource state, or actual workflow semantics. A sample webhook payload cannot prove the entire payment integration is correct.

27.5 API factory

Use one reviewed schema and semantic contract to generate SDK models, reference documentation, CLI commands, and examples. Add behavior tests for retries, errors, unknown enum values, pagination, account scoping, and old API versions. Stripe's public release-pipeline and OpenAPI materials demonstrate why developer surfaces should move together. [S45] [S50]

Keep generated output reproducible and diffable. A release includes server changes, schema, SDK compatibility evidence, migration notes, and operational dashboards. A breaking error-code change is a product migration, not a harmless refactor.

28. Reconciliation, explainability, and data-quality controls

28.1 Three independent views

Compare internal expected obligations, provider execution/settlement evidence, and actual bank cash. A match requires compatible identity, amount, currency, account, and timing, with an explicit model for fees, net batches, partials, and FX. Do not match solely by amount and nearest timestamp when better references exist.

Stripe exposes balance transactions and payout reconciliation surfaces for explaining its balance and settlements. Its published Ledger work emphasizes financial explainability and data quality. Those ideas inform, but do not replace, Northstar's own bank reconciliation. [S40] [S51] [S52]

28.2 Immutable ingestion

For each statement or report, persist source, account, period, retrieval time, content digest, format version, and line identity. Parse into a normalized evidence model without discarding the original. A parser upgrade can reprocess evidence under a new parser version while preserving what the old system believed.

Handle duplicates, corrections, and reversals explicitly. The same provider event ID with different content is a conflict. Two actual cash movements with similar references are not automatically duplicates.

28.3 Matching hierarchy

Start with strong identifiers, then aggregate relationships such as batch-to-items or fee-to-principal. Use controlled heuristic matching only for cases where exact identities are absent, and record the confidence and reviewer decision. Never let a probabilistic match silently authorize a second payout.

SWIFT's UETR is a cross-chain tracking reference. It can improve correlation, but it is not a universal deduplication guarantee or proof of beneficiary receipt across every API and rail. [S58]

28.4 Exception management

An unmatched credit, missing debit, duplicate actual debit, fee difference, wrong currency, or missing report creates a case with exposure, owner, deadline, and allowed resolution actions. Approved adjustments are new idempotent effects linked to evidence. Operators do not edit prior journal lines or current balances directly.

Age cases by both calendar time and relevant bank operating windows. Report count and value, expected versus overdue evidence, concentration by provider, and materiality. A zero-balance suspense account obtained through unexplained adjustments is not successful reconciliation.

28.5 Independent invariant checker

Reconstruct account balances from immutable entries and reservations from reservation events. Check each journal's per-currency conservation, effect uniqueness, valid ownership, and consistent transitions. Compare independent results with the online authoritative state.

Use separately controlled signed manifests or hash trees to make historical tampering detectable. This is evidence protection, not a claim that privileged compromise becomes impossible. Backups, audit storage, database roles, and signing keys need independent controls.

29. Fraud, compliance, security, and operational authority

29.1 Independent permission gates

Credential authorization, payment fraud, identity verification, sanctions or prohibited-activity controls, and treasury limits answer different questions. An issuer approval does not replace them. A low fraud score does not permit a prohibited corridor. A verified business can still exceed its exposure limit.

29.2 Risk system design

Begin with transparent rules, velocity controls, merchant underwriting, exposure limits, and human review. Add models after building reliable labels and point-in-time features. Stripe's Radar publication describes sophisticated learned systems and latency constraints; it does not transfer Stripe's data advantage to a new company. [S47]

Persist the feature and model versions used for a decision, its reason codes, threshold policy, and exposure owner. Avoid training leakage from facts learned after authorization. Distinguish fraud loss, issuer decline, merchant dissatisfaction, and customer abandonment; optimizing one metric blindly can worsen another.

Run new models in shadow mode before enforcement. Shadow mode must not produce bank calls or spending authority. Compare false positives and delayed loss outcomes, and use bounded rollouts with rollback.

29.3 API and service security

Use managed secrets, workload identity, narrow service permissions, encrypted transport, key rotation, and tenant-scoped authorization. Stripe's key documentation distinguishes key types and restricted access; Northstar should offer similarly constrained credentials and avoid one universal production secret. [S37]

Store a digest rather than the plaintext of API secrets where possible. Show a new secret once. Audit use by tenant, environment, role, source, and operation. Separate sandbox and live resources cryptographically and operationally, not only by a boolean in the frontend.

29.4 Operational commands and approvals

Support typed actions such as restrict capability, approve adjustment, release a reviewed hold, rotate provider credential, or resume a corridor. Each has an authorization policy, reason, evidence, scope, and expiry. High-risk actions require dual approval from distinct principals and remain idempotent.

Do not provide a generic "set balance" or "mark payment succeeded" button. A support agent can record customer information and request investigation, not manufacture bank evidence.

29.5 Regulatory workstream

Create a jurisdiction-by-product matrix identifying required permissions, partner obligations, safeguarding, record retention, customer disclosures, reporting, privacy, and dispute handling. This is a required launch workstream, not a universal legal conclusion from this document. For India, the researched RBI 2025 framework should be reviewed alongside the actual business model and approvals. [S61] [S62]

Engineer evidence and controls so qualified teams can enforce their decisions. Software alone cannot grant a license, prove a beneficial owner, or establish a bank's commercial willingness to carry a corridor.

30. Multi-region architecture and five-nines operations

30.1 One synchronous authority, warm application capacity

Use a supported database topology. In Spanner, replica roles and witness behavior are specific to configuration; a witness is not a full readable replica. The application should not invent its own replica layout and assume equivalent durability. [S56]

Run stateless APIs and workers in warm serving regions with provider credentials, connection pools, routing, bank allowlists, quotas, and observability prepared before failure. Application failover is incomplete if the surviving region cannot authenticate to the bank or exhausts its database connection budget.

30.2 Partition behavior

When quorum is unavailable, stop financial writes. Read-only historical views may remain available with explicit freshness labels. They cannot authorize new spending. A stale replica with a plausible balance is not an alternative financial authority.

Existing external instructions may continue executing at banks during your outage. Recovery must reconcile those outcomes. Do not assume a database outage freezes the outside world.

30.3 Availability measurement

Measure per cell, critical API, tenant tier, and region. Count logical operations alongside HTTP attempts so retries do not inflate the denominator. Separate business declines from infrastructure failures, and periodically verify that supposedly deterministic declines are accurate.

Use multiple burn-rate windows for alerts and release decisions. The Google SRE workbook provides a primary reference for multiwindow error-budget alerting. [S57]

30.4 Capacity under failure

As an illustrative starting point, two serving regions each provisioned for 1.5 times platform peak run at roughly one-third of that capacity under an even healthy split and two-thirds after losing one region. This is application arithmetic, not a benchmark or database-sizing formula.

Load tests must include failover, retry amplification, hot accounts, reconciliation bursts, and provider slowness. Reserve capacity for inquiries and recovery. An overwhelmed system that prioritizes new work while starving uncertainty resolution can increase both downtime and financial exposure.

30.5 Release safety

Use schema expansion before behavior changes, canaries by cell or tenant cohort, financial invariants as automatic stop conditions, and immediate traffic rollback where compatible. Database migrations require a forward recovery plan because schema and posted money cannot always be rolled back like stateless code.

Never declare five nines from a topology diagram. Demonstrate the eligible-operation SLI over time and show failure drills, incident budgets, and a documented fault model. Correct fail-closed behavior preserves money but still consumes availability budget.

31. Scale, hot accounts, online migration, and cross-cell protocols

31.1 The real bottleneck is often shared state

Ten thousand requests per second spread across ten thousand independent accounts differs from the same volume spending one treasury pool. Test skew, not just uniform random IDs. Record transaction conflict rate, retry latency, maximum account fan-in, and throughput under settlement batches.

Split purpose-equivalent revenue accounts, explicit liquidity rights, and independent tenant cells when justified. Do not split an indivisible customer balance into eventually consistent copies. Coordination is part of the semantics of spending limited funds.

31.2 Migration without duplicate financial authority

Stripe's online-migration publication offers staged backfill, comparison, and cutover lessons. Apply them carefully: ordinary dual-write patterns are not permission to have two independent financial systems both authorize spending. [S46]

A safe financial migration establishes one authority at each moment, stable economic identities, deterministic backfill, reconciliation, a fence or epoch for cutover, and a recovery plan. Shadow writes may validate a nonauthoritative target, but the target cannot dispatch bank work or expose spendable balances before ownership transfers.

31.3 Cross-cell transfer protocol

When one transaction cannot span the lawful boundary, use durable prepare, decision, apply, and release stages. Each participant stores the transfer identity and terms digest, reserves required rights, and records readiness. A coordinator records an irrevocable COMMIT or ABORT. Participants idempotently apply their local balanced journals. Destination spending is released only after the required commit evidence.

A participant cannot expire its prepared hold blindly while a COMMIT may still arrive. A recorded COMMIT does not turn into ABORT because of a timeout. This protocol can block during a partition; that is an explicit liveness cost, not an implementation accident to hide.

31.4 Ownership directory and fencing

Account migrations use a durable ownership epoch. Old writers reject new commands after the epoch changes. In-flight external attempts remain discoverable under their original identities and are not recreated in the new cell. Directory cache expiry alone is not fencing.

If a provider does not enforce your internal epoch, it cannot fence a delayed packet at that provider. External ambiguity therefore survives even a perfectly fenced internal migration and still requires inquiry and reconciliation.

31.5 Retention and archival

Archive immutable history under approved retention policy while preserving query and recovery ability. Keep the identity index needed to prevent replay from recreating old effects. Deleting a short-lived API response does not imply the underlying economic identity can safely be reused.

Test archive restore and data-quality reconstruction, not only backup creation. Storage savings that make a disputed payment impossible to explain can be more expensive than the retained data.

32. Incident runbooks and disaster recovery

32.1 Recovery principle

The bank does not roll back when Northstar restores a database. Before restoring an older financial snapshot, fence every bank egress path, including scheduled jobs, surviving workers, operator tools, and delayed consumers. Restore into an isolated environment, recover post-snapshot identities and evidence, reconcile actual bank outcomes, and resume only a proven-safe scope.

32.2 Runbook: provider timeout surge

Detect elevated UNKNOWN count and value, provider latency, and inquiry lag. Stop new routing to the provider where appropriate, but keep existing attempts pinned. Reuse original identities for permitted inquiries or safe replays. Check whether the provider is accepting work but losing responses. Inform operations and treasury of exposure. Resume new traffic through a bounded canary after the provider contract and observed behavior agree again.

Never bulk-create replacement payouts with new keys. The runbook's objective is to resolve uncertainty, not merely drain a queue.

32.3 Runbook: balance reconstruction mismatch

Freeze affected spending scopes, preserve evidence, and compare immutable journals, reservation events, and current-state versions. Identify whether the issue is a projection bug, a posting defect, a migration error, or unauthorized modification. Rebuild projections only when journals are authoritative and validated. If the financial history itself is wrong, use approved corrective effects and an incident ledger, not silent balance edits.

32.4 Runbook: bank settlement discrepancy

Determine whether cash, provider report, or internal expectation is missing or incorrect. Preserve original statements. Check fees, FX, reserves, partial batches, returns, and value-date differences. Create a case with materiality and owner. A second real debit is recorded separately, with recovery action against the bank or provider as appropriate.

32.5 Runbook: regional failure

Fail traffic to warm capacity while preserving home-cell financial authority. Monitor quorum health, transaction latency, API error budget, credential access, provider connectivity, and queue age. Do not promote an asynchronous replica into a writable ledger to restore a green dashboard. After recovery, reconcile all external attempts that overlapped the incident.

32.6 Three replay modes

Projection replay rebuilds derived views and has no external side effects. Financial recovery reapplies missing economic facts under their original identities and approved evidence. External resubmission can move real money and requires a separately controlled decision. Make these different tools and permissions, not flags that an operator can easily confuse.

Resume operations through an explicit incident commander decision, finance sign-off for affected balances, and SRE verification of safety gates. Retain a timeline and the exact commands used.

33.3 Suggested code boundaries

api/          authentication, schema, account context, errors
core/         commands, templates, invariants, transaction interface
storage/      engine-specific transactions and repositories
workflow/     durable tasks, leases, sweepers, execution slots
adapters/     provider contracts, serializers, inquiry, observations
reconcile/    statement ingestion, matching, cases, corrections
risk/         versioned decisions and evidence
money/        integer amounts, currency metadata, rational FX
ops/          audited administrative commands and runbooks

Keep pure posting-plan construction separately testable from persistence. But test the real transaction implementation too: a correct pure function does not prove the database applies it atomically under concurrency.

33.4 First end-to-end test

Create a tenant and two test accounts. Fund the source with an approved journal. Submit one disbursement under a stable business reference. Reserve and book it. Let the simulator execute the payout and drop the response. Retry the same API call, then run inquiry. Assert one customer debit, one provider execution, one cash posting, one delivery effect, and one merchant-visible completion despite duplicate messages.

Next, change the request amount under the same key and assert a conflict. Then submit a genuinely new payment with the same amount and recipient under a new business reference and assert it is allowed if funds permit. This distinguishes real idempotency from crude duplicate detection.

33.5 Instrument before adding breadth

Emit request, operation, payment, attempt, effect, journal, event, provider, and bank identifiers into structured redacted traces. Operators should traverse these links without database shell access. Add a dashboard showing pending and unknown value, not just request success rate. A small transparent system is a better foundation than a wide but unexplainable API surface.

34. Testing, certification, chaos, and release gates

34.1 Different tests prove different properties

Pure unit tests check arithmetic and local state transitions. Model tests check conservation and identity rules over generated sequences. Real-database tests check uniqueness, isolation, lock conflicts, callback retries, and unknown commit recovery. Provider simulators check execution uncertainty. Bank certification checks the actual contract. Load and chaos tests check availability under failure. None substitutes for all the others.

Stripe provides test-mode guidance and public tooling such as stripe-mock. These help integration testing, but a mock does not establish live bank settlement, commercial eligibility, or financial correctness of your own system. [S48] [S49]

34.2 Adversarial simulator requirements

The simulator must retain its own state independently of the application and support execute-then-timeout, duplicate and reordered callbacks, stale inquiry, expired deduplication keys, region-scoped identity, partial batch execution, bank debit before delivery, cancellation races, returns after success, and duplicated actual cash movements.

A simulator that reads the application's status to decide whether a payment executed cannot test the boundary correctly. The two systems need independent histories that sometimes disagree.

34.3 Core assertions

Every journal balances per book and currency. No economic occurrence is applied twice. Protected spendable balances stay within approved limits. Reservations conserve their original amount. Actual cash movements are represented exactly once per movement identity. Unknown external execution never becomes non-execution merely through elapsed local time. Unauthorized tenants cannot read or mutate each other's data.

Add metamorphic tests: replay events in a different order, duplicate every message, pause workers before send, lose responses after commit, restore old projections, and compare the final economic explanation. The system should converge without inventing or deleting money.

34.4 Performance and failure evidence

Measure p50, p95, p99, and tail latency under realistic contention and region loss. Test the failover path with production-like credentials, limits, and network policies. Include database contention, retry storms, slow bank inquiry, delayed reports, and a large settlement batch overlapping a release.

Evaluate the logical-operation SLI, not only eventual recovery. A system that takes ten minutes to fail over safely has preserved correctness but has not achieved a five-nines synchronous availability target for that incident.

34.5 Launch gates

Require approved product and entity scope, signed partner contracts, finance-approved templates, security review, completed certification, independent daily reconciliation, tested restore, operator training, customer communications, and limited exposure. Launch one corridor with caps and a stop switch before broadening coverage.

The verification report in the bundle states exactly which local tests ran. It does not claim that the proposed database, cross-region deployment, or live adapters were tested.

35. Building the company: teams, partners, economics, and stage gates

35.1 The company is part of the architecture

A payment API company needs engineering, product, underwriting and fraud, compliance, finance and reconciliation, treasury, security, SRE, customer support, and bank/partner operations. These are responsibilities, not a fixed headcount prescription. Initially people may cover multiple roles, but high-risk approvals still need separation of duties.

The ledger team cannot decide licensing. Compliance cannot establish database isolation by policy. Treasury cannot treat an unreconciled balance as available because a customer is waiting. The operating model must define who can make each decision and what evidence they need.

35.2 Stage-gated roadmap

Stage Deliverable Exit evidence
Product and legal definition One merchant segment, entity, corridor, and loss model Approved permissions and partner responsibilities
Financial kernel Commands, identities, journals, balances, reservations Model and real-database invariants
Partner integration One acquiring or payout path with inquiry and reports Certification and adversarial failure tests
Controlled launch Low-volume real money with strict exposure caps Daily reconciliation and trained incident response
Resilience Warm regions, tested recovery, measured capacity Failure drills and sustained SLIs
Expansion More providers, products, entities, or countries New capability approval and end-to-end evidence

Do not attach a guaranteed calendar duration to these stages. External approvals, partner contracts, product scope, and team capability dominate schedule uncertainty.

35.3 Build versus buy

Build the product contract, authoritative ledger integration, durable operation identities, orchestration, reconciliation model, developer experience, and operational audit trail. Partner for network access and regulated services where appropriate. Buy specialized infrastructure only after evaluating recovery, exportability, correctness, and support, not just happy-path API latency.

A vendor can replace a component without replacing your responsibility to understand it. Require documented idempotency, inquiry, retention, incident communication, data ownership, and exit behavior. Test what happens when the vendor returns an ambiguous response or changes a preview contract.

35.4 Unit economics

Use an explicit per-transaction model: merchant revenue and FX spread, less acquiring and network costs, payout fees, fraud and dispute loss, funding and liquidity cost, partner charges, operational effort, and infrastructure. Add fixed compliance, security, engineering, and support costs separately.

Illustrative numbers are not Stripe prices. Stripe's published Global Payouts pricing distinguishes payout and cross-border/FX-related charges; the company's negotiated contracts and actual traffic mix determine its own economics. [S63]

Measure contribution by method, merchant cohort, corridor, amount band, and risk segment. A route with a lower nominal fee can be more expensive after failed payments, support contacts, chargebacks, and prefunding.

35.5 Distribution and trust

Developer adoption depends on predictable APIs, realistic examples, clear errors, stable SDKs, transparent fees, reliable dashboards, and support that can explain money movements. Build a sandbox that teaches the real failure model, not a demo that always succeeds.

Trust also requires honest status pages, incident communication, and statements about what is and is not guaranteed. Do not advertise instantaneous global settlement when the actual contract is durable instruction acceptance followed by bank execution.

36. End-to-end case study and final production decision

36.1 Scenario

A marketplace collects a card payment from a buyer, allocates earnings to a seller, converts an approved amount, and pays the seller's foreign bank account. This is a conceptual sequence for an eligible, approved product configuration, not a claim that every Stripe account can combine these flows in every country.

36.2 Step-by-step execution

First, the platform onboards the seller and verifies the required capabilities, legal role, and destination. It creates an order with immutable commercial terms and a stable business reference. The buyer submits credentials through a controlled collection boundary and consents to the payment.

Second, the payment intent is created and confirmed. Any required authentication completes, and the issuer authorization is recorded independently. A lost response triggers retrieval or the original-key recovery path, not a new payment identity.

Third, the merchant captures according to the authorized product terms. The ledger records the appropriate receivable and merchant pending obligation. Provider reports and bank evidence establish settlement and fee allocation. The platform releases merchant availability under its approved risk policy.

Fourth, the marketplace allocates seller earnings with independent transfer identities. Refund and dispute responsibility remains explicit. A seller allocation is not yet a bank payout.

Fifth, the seller accepts an eligible FX quote. The financial core validates current capability, exact terms, source availability, destination liquidity, and quote scope. It books separately balanced currency legs and creates an immutable payout instruction.

Sixth, the payout adapter sends the instruction. Suppose the bank executes but the response is lost. Northstar marks execution UNKNOWN, keeps required rights and obligations protected, and queries using the original identity. It does not route a replacement payout through another bank.

Seventh, a bank debit and delivery observation arrive. Each is authenticated, stored, deduplicated by the correct evidence identity, and applied through a stable economic effect. The ledger distinguishes cash leaving from the beneficiary obligation being discharged. The outbox publishes a completion notification; repeated delivery does not duplicate merchant-side fulfillment.

Finally, reconciliation matches the gross collection, fees, allocation, FX, payout, and bank movements. A later return or dispute creates a new linked case and journal. The original payment history remains intact.

36.3 What the example proves conceptually

The same stable commercial promise survives multiple API requests, database transactions, external attempts, events, and settlement records. Each boundary has an authority and recovery rule. An external timeout is not silently converted into a decline, a queue acknowledgment is not advertised as settled money, and a bank debit is not deleted because the application expected only one.

36.4 Final production checklist

The launch decision must answer: can every amount be explained; can every operation be safely retried; can every external unknown be investigated; can every account's ownership and spending authority be proven; can every relevant region fail without creating a second ledger; and can an old database be restored without paying again?

The company's strongest asset is not a large endpoint catalog. It is a dependable contract between developers, customers, finance, banks, and regulators, supported by an implementation whose behavior remains understandable under failure.

SELECT j.tenant_id, j.book_id, j.currency, j.journal_id
FROM journal AS j
LEFT JOIN journal_entry AS e
  ON e.tenant_id = j.tenant_id
 AND e.book_id = j.book_id
 AND e.currency = j.currency
 AND e.journal_id = j.journal_id
GROUP BY j.tenant_id, j.book_id, j.currency, j.journal_id
HAVING COUNT(e.line_no) < 2

         CASE WHEN e.side = 'D' THEN e.amount_minor::numeric
