PART I / Foundations and the architecture map

01. Uber as a real-time coordination system

The essential product is not a dot moving on a map. It is a promise to coordinate people, vehicles, goods, time, and money in a world the software only partially observes. This book explains that coordination from the customer experience down to transactions, network protocols, infrastructure, and operating teams.

Published evidence. Uber's Q2 2026 reporting separates Mobility, Delivery, and Freight. Its company-level operating measures are business aggregates, not measurements of API throughput, database transactions, or simultaneous drivers. They should not be used interchangeably for capacity planning. [S01]

Three businesses, different clocks

A ride request usually needs a decision within seconds. A restaurant order adds preparation, ingredient availability, and a handoff between independent participants. A freight load may remain available for days, with equipment constraints, appointments, and contractual obligations. Their interfaces can share geographic search, notifications, identity, experimentation, and accounting primitives. Their state machines and optimization objectives should remain different.

The proposed platform therefore has three product domains above shared capabilities. Mobility owns passenger transportation intent. Delivery owns a basket and its fulfillment plan. Freight owns a shipment and its execution commitments. Shared services do not erase those meanings. A generic status=completed field cannot describe whether a passenger arrived, food was handed over, a trailer reached a dock, or an invoice was paid.

How to read the evidence

Every chapter distinguishes a short published-evidence passage from a reference design developed here. A 2018 engineering article proves that an approach was described in 2018; it does not prove that every component still runs that way. Source dates are part of the architecture. Announced products, geographic availability, contract terms, and deployed infrastructure are separate claims.

The reference design is intentionally implementable but is not a leaked Uber specification. It supplies contracts, data ownership, failure behavior, and alternatives where public information stops. Numbers marked illustrative are assumptions for engineering exercises. The book does not invent a universal customer availability SLA or claim access to private source code, internal runbooks, production configurations, or all regional offerings.

The learning path

First understand the request, supply session, offer, assignment, and fulfillment plan. Then learn location and routes, because matching without travel-time semantics is misleading. Next study the commit boundary: a recommendation is not an assignment. Only after this foundation should pricing, machine learning, streaming analytics, and multi-region recovery enter the design.

A useful engineering question is: What does this component know, how fresh is that knowledge, and who can change the authoritative fact? Ask it of a map tile, a driver acceptance, a recommendation score, an account balance, and a Kubernetes controller. Many apparently unrelated failures reduce to confusing an observation with authority.

What production adds

The software must support operations when the happy path fails: a rider loses connectivity after entering a vehicle; a restaurant prepares an order that is subsequently canceled; a courier is delayed; a regional database is unavailable. Safe operation requires explicit uncertainty states, accountable human intervention, and evidence that survives retries. Success is not simply an HTTP 200. It is a consistent and recoverable commitment that people can understand.

02. Architecture evolution: keep the dates attached

Learning Uber's engineering requires a timeline rather than one giant diagram assembled from articles written a decade apart. Different generations often solve the same problem with different consistency and operational tradeoffs.

Published evidence. The 2021 fulfillment account describes replacing an earlier cache-heavy, application-coordinated architecture with a transactional platform. The 2025 compute account says the stateless shared-workload migration from Mesos to Kubernetes completed in 2024. A December 2025 account describes the cloud journey involving OCI and Google Cloud. These are scoped, dated accounts. [S02][S24][S25]

A timeline is not a current inventory

Early systems optimized for rapid growth and operational familiarity. Later systems paid down the coordination costs that appeared as products multiplied. This is not evidence that an early technology is inherently unsuitable. It is evidence that the required invariants, team structure, and scale changed.

In this book, historical Ringpop, Cassandra-centered fulfillment, early routing engines, and the original Michelangelo are discussed as architectural lessons. They are not placed indiscriminately beside newer Spanner fulfillment, Docstore, Kubernetes, and feature-logging descriptions as though one source verified that combination today.

Why a rewrite becomes rational

Consider a system that keeps authoritative-looking copies of trip state in an owning process, a cache, a database, and several secondary indexes. Every additional copy creates invalidation rules. Every cross-entity change adds a failure window. When a feature requires changing a rider request, a driver plan, and several jobs, application-level repair can become harder than using a database that expresses the invariant directly.

A rewrite is justified when the cost of maintaining the existing correctness model exceeds the cost and risk of migration. Evidence should include incidents, reconciliation backlog, engineer onboarding time, rollout friction, and inability to extend product semantics. A fashionable database or orchestration system is not sufficient justification.

The migration design is part of the new architecture

Build a compatibility layer that translates old requests into a canonical model. Shadow reads and decisions without issuing external side effects. Compare semantic outputs, not incidental field ordering or timestamps. Route complete sessions to one authority so a trip is not half migrated. Persist the selected version and ownership epoch. Rollback must know which state representation is authoritative.

Data translation needs a losslessness test. An old state that has no exact new equivalent should be represented explicitly, not silently coerced. During a transition, incompatible writes may need to be disabled for a cohort. This is preferable to pretending bidirectional replication makes two independently evolving schemas equivalent.

An architecture decision record

For every major choice, record the problem, invariant, alternatives, measurements, failure modes, migration plan, and exit strategy. Revisit decisions when workload shape changes. The correct starting architecture for a company operating in one city is usually much smaller than the published infrastructure of a global platform.

The practical lesson is to copy reasoning before copying scale. A modular service with a well-chosen transactional database can be a better starting point than hundreds of services whose ownership rules are unclear.

03. The canonical journey and its invariants

Start with a rider requesting a car. The request contains intent; the system must find feasible supply, obtain acceptance, record a commitment, observe execution, and settle the economic result. Each step creates different evidence.

Published evidence. Uber's fulfillment example separates an order, preparation work, and delivery work into distinct entities. [S02]

The proposed objects

A request is a customer's intent with an immutable service specification. A quote is a time-bounded commercial proposal. A supply session says that a driver or courier is eligible to receive work. An offer invites that participant to take a proposed plan. An assignment is the durable agreement after acceptance. A job is executable work. A plan is an ordered set of stops and tasks constrained by capacity and time.

Keep observations separate: GPS pings, app acknowledgments, inferred arrivals, photos, and support notes. They inform state transitions but do not all have equal authority. A vehicle appearing near the pickup does not establish that the correct rider entered it.

Invariants before service boundaries

For a single-capacity ride product, no driver may hold two conflicting committed assignments. For pooled rides or delivery batches, the invariant is stronger and more general: the committed plan must remain capacity- and time-feasible. A blanket rule of one active trip per driver would incorrectly prohibit valid batching.

A request cannot be fulfilled by two independently committed assignments unless the product explicitly models multiple jobs. An expired quote cannot silently become a different accepted price. A cancellation and completion race must resolve against one versioned state machine. The system must not acknowledge a commitment before its authoritative transaction is durable.

Three clocks

Store the event's observed time, the server's received time, and the authoritative commit time. A phone's wall clock can be wrong. Network delay can reorder observations. Database commit order governs state changes, while event time is useful for reconstructing the physical journey.

For example, a driver may start a trip while briefly offline. On reconnect, the start observation can arrive after several location updates. The server validates the observation against the assignment, allowed transition, time plausibility, and evidence. It does not blindly sort all business events by client timestamps.

State, evidence, and compensation

Once a car has moved or food has been prepared, database rollback cannot undo reality. A failed downstream notification is retried. A canceled accepted ride may require a new economic adjustment. A disputed pickup becomes a case with evidence. These are different responses, not one generic rollback operation.

Design each API to say whether it reports an observation, requests a transition, or reads current authority. That distinction prevents client code, analytics jobs, and support tools from acquiring unintended powers to change fulfillment state.

04. Availability, latency, freshness, and safety objectives

A usable platform must be available, but availability alone is an incomplete contract. A fast response containing an already-assigned driver is not a successful match. A fresh map with an incorrect commitment is worse than a temporarily unavailable booking button.

Published evidence. The 2021 fulfillment article states a 99.99% platform availability requirement. It is not a universal Uber customer SLA. [S02]

Define the denominator

For the reference system, measure accepted, valid requests at the service boundary. Report customer-visible success separately from infrastructure acceptance. A booking rejected because no eligible supply exists is a business outcome; a booking lost because a database timed out is a technical failure. Neither should disappear from product reporting.

A service-level indicator should include numerator, denominator, measurement point, exclusions, aggregation window, and owner. Segment by city, device network, product, and infrastructure region. A worldwide average can hide a complete failure in a small market.

Error budgets and dependencies

In a 30-day window, 99.9% permits 43 minutes 12 seconds of unavailability; 99.99% permits 4 minutes 19.2 seconds; 99.999% permits 25.92 seconds. These are arithmetic budgets, not suggested incident durations. A short total outage may affect more people than a long low-volume degradation.

Serial dependencies can consume the budget quickly. Multiplying component availability assumes independence, which is often false because regions, networks, configuration systems, and databases are shared. Model common-cause failures explicitly. Do not multiply optimistic vendor figures and label the result an end-to-end guarantee.

Fail safely and selectively

When recommendations fail, show a simpler ranking. When traffic calibration is stale, widen ETA uncertainty. When authoritative assignment storage is unavailable, stop creating commitments rather than risk duplicates. Continue read-only trip guidance and safety contact channels where possible.

Recovery objectives are separate: RTO is elapsed recovery time; RPO is potential data loss relative to the failure. Restore throughput in terabytes per hour is neither. Maintain a distinct objective for reconciling physical journeys after service resumes. The platform can be reachable before its operational state is trustworthy.

05. Reference architecture and failure domains

The reference architecture has a short, authoritative path for commitments and longer asynchronous paths for discovery, analytics, and optimization. A bounded outage in the latter must not corrupt the former.

Published evidence. Uber's domain-oriented microservice architecture describes grouping services into domains with explicit gateways and layers to control dependencies. That published organization pattern motivates clear contracts here; it does not specify the exact services or deployment topology proposed below. [S03]

Follow one booking

The edge authenticates the caller, applies abuse controls, attaches a request identifier, and forwards to a product gateway. The gateway validates the quote and creates an idempotent request. Marketplace services obtain eligible candidates from a geographic view, evaluate pickup travel times, and produce offers. Acceptance is committed by the fulfillment authority, not by the geographic index or matching worker.

The commit writes the assignment, affected plan, state version, and outbox record together. Notification delivery, payment preparation, audit projections, and model-training events consume the outbox asynchronously. Each consumer handles duplicates and can reconcile against authoritative state.

Control plane and serving plane

The control plane manages city configuration, product eligibility, models, price rules, routing data releases, deployment policies, and ownership assignments. Serving processes use validated, versioned snapshots. A control-plane outage should not immediately prevent a driver from finishing an existing trip.

However, not all stale configuration is acceptable. A critical safety or legal disablement may require an expiry policy or dedicated revocation channel. Define which settings can use last-known-good values, which have a maximum age, and which force restricted operation when freshness cannot be established.

A cell is a blast-radius boundary

A proposed cell contains gateways, matching workers, hot location partitions, and isolated capacity budgets for a collection of markets. Durable storage may span failure domains according to its replication contract. A cell is not automatically identical to a cloud region or city. Large cities may need multiple partitions; small markets may share infrastructure without sharing authorization or business state.

Routing resolves an entity's home authority. Requests for an existing trip follow that authority even if a client reconnects through another edge. Changing ownership requires an explicit epoch and fencing, not simply updating DNS. Global discovery can be eventually consistent; conflicting authoritative writers cannot.

Avoid a synchronous dependency web

Keep optional recommendations, marketing, receipts, experimentation exports, and analytics off the commit path. Place hard deadlines on route and risk calls. Cache validated reference data, not unbounded mutable authority. When a dependency times out, the caller must know whether it is safe to retry, degrade, or return an uncertainty response.

This architecture is deliberately asymmetric: reads can be widely replicated and approximate, while the small set of economically and operationally binding writes is carefully serialized. That asymmetry is the key to scaling without treating every piece of data as equally critical.

06. API contracts, retries, and durable intent

Mobile clients retry because networks disappear. Servers retry because dependencies fail. An API that treats every retry as a new intent will eventually create duplicate work. Idempotency belongs in the product contract, not in an optional middleware convention.

Published evidence. Uber Direct documents signed webhook delivery, and the Eats integration guide describes an order lifecycle involving notifications, retrieval, and explicit acceptance or denial. These public contracts demonstrate asynchronous integration; the internal APIs in this chapter are proposed, not copies of undocumented Uber endpoints. [S54][S55]

A proposed command envelope

Every mutating command contains a stable command ID, authenticated actor, entity ID, expected version where relevant, schema version, and trace context. The server records an idempotency key scoped to actor and operation, plus a canonical payload hash and durable result. Reuse with a different payload returns a conflict. Retrying an identical command returns the stored result or a queryable in-progress resource.

Do not delete deduplication records earlier than the maximum supported replay window. A short cache lifetime cannot enforce a long-lived business guarantee. Stable external references are especially important for partner operations and money movement.

Errors must reveal commitment status

A transport timeout means the caller did not receive an answer. It does not mean the server rolled back. The client queries using the same command ID before generating a replacement. A server that cannot yet determine outcome returns a durable pending reference, not a fabricated failure.

Distinguish validation failures, version conflicts, business infeasibility, throttling, and unknown outcomes. Include retry-after information only when it is meaningful. Never make clients interpret arbitrary error strings to decide whether another vehicle might already be assigned.

Notifications are hints plus evidence

A push message should carry entity ID, version, event ID, and a small display payload. The client discards obsolete versions and can fetch a snapshot after reconnecting. A notification does not become the sole surviving copy of an assignment.

For partner webhooks, verify the signature over the exact received bytes before parsing, enqueue durably, acknowledge quickly, then process idempotently. Delivery retries are bounded by the product contract; reconciliation reads are still necessary. Network-level deduplication and business-level deduplication are different layers.

Compatibility and privacy

Use additive fields, explicit enum handling, and versioned semantic changes. Old clients should receive an intentional unsupported-state response rather than rendering an unknown state as success. Keep exact location and personal information out of generic errors and tracing baggage. API usability includes safe debugging, not merely convenient endpoint names.

07. Data model, ownership, and transaction boundaries

The data model is a map of responsibilities. It tells engineers which service can establish a fact, which representations are projections, and which updates must succeed together. Without that map, a microservice architecture merely distributes ambiguity.

Technical basis. PostgreSQL documents that serializable transactions can abort and require a retry of the whole transaction. Spanner documents external consistency under its default serializable isolation. Neither database automatically defines the application's business invariants. [S74][S75]

Proposed aggregate ownership

The fulfillment authority owns requests, jobs, assignments, plans, and their transitions. Marketplace workers own temporary optimization inputs and offer proposals but must go through the authority to bind resources. Location ingestion owns observations and freshness metadata. Pricing owns immutable quote versions. Commerce owns economic obligations and adjustments. Support owns cases, not arbitrary database edits.

A practical core uses globally unique identifiers, per-aggregate monotonic versions, and an ownership epoch. Secondary indexes include rider-to-active-request, driver-to-current-plan, order-to-jobs, and external-reference-to-command. Indexes used to enforce uniqueness belong in the authoritative transaction boundary, not only in a search engine.

What is atomic

Accepting an offer validates the request version, driver plan version, eligibility snapshot, offer expiry, and ownership epoch. It writes the assignment and revised plan, marks the request committed, and appends an outbox event in one transaction. If the database aborts, retry the full deterministic transaction with the same command identity. Do not send notifications or perform external calls from inside a retryable database callback.

Some product workflows are too broad for one transaction: a restaurant accepts an order, a courier later agrees to delivery, and a customer may change instructions. Model these as explicit durable steps with compensations and deadlines. Transaction boundaries protect local invariants; workflows coordinate time and independent parties.

Schema patterns

Store money as integer minor units with currency and policy version. Store locations with coordinate system, accuracy, observation time, and provenance. Store immutable plan revisions rather than overwriting stop sequences without history. Separate personally identifying details from operational identifiers so access and retention can differ.

A generic event has event_id, aggregate_id, aggregate_version, event_type, schema_version, occurred_at, committed_at, and a minimized payload. Event time is not a universal ordering key. Consumers order within the aggregate or fetch a consistent snapshot when a version gap appears.

Contention and evolution

The hot object is often a driver plan or a high-demand request, not an entire city. Partition around authority and access patterns, and avoid monotonically increasing hot keys. Keep transactions short, precompute expensive route scores outside them, and revalidate the facts that determine feasibility at commit.

When adding a new field, define its behavior for old rows and old events. A migration that backfills data must not emit new real-world commands. A database copy is not a replay instruction. The appendices expand this model into a logical schema, API envelopes, and executable teaching examples.

PART II / Clients, location, maps, and routes

08. Rider and driver applications under unreliable networks

A mobile application is a distributed-system participant with a battery, intermittent connectivity, and an operating system that may suspend it. Treating it as a thin, continuously connected browser produces fragile trip experiences.

Published evidence. Uber's 2018 driver-app account describes RIBs: Router, Interactor, and Builder components that organize business logic and navigation. It also distinguishes essential trip functionality from features that can fail independently. This is a dated architectural account, not a claim that every current screen uses an unchanged implementation. [S13]

Separate the local experience from authority

The proposed app has an authenticated session layer, local entity store, command queue, real-time channel, map renderer, and feature modules. The local store contains a versioned snapshot of the current assignment and pending commands. It may optimistically animate a button, but it must not show an unconfirmed acceptance as a durable commitment.

Represent command states explicitly: queued, transmitted, outcome unknown, confirmed, or rejected. Persist command identity before sending. After process death, recover the same command rather than create a new one. Use encrypted local storage for sensitive state and minimize retention after a trip.

Offline execution needs product rules

Some observations can be collected offline: GPS samples, arrival evidence, or a driver's declaration that a trip started. Whether a binding action can proceed offline is a product and safety decision. The app should make uncertainty visible and preserve evidence for reconciliation. It must not manufacture a server acknowledgment.

On reconnect, first obtain the server's current assignment version. Upload pending observations with their original sequence numbers and device-session identity. The server validates transitions against the actual assignment and applies them only if permitted. A cached offer that expired while the device was offline cannot be accepted by replaying its button tap later.

Performance is part of reliability

Keep map rendering, image loading, analytics, and promotional content out of the critical interaction loop. Budget startup, memory, battery use, and network bytes by screen and device class. Degrade optional visual layers before degrading assignment display. Test low-memory process termination, permission revocation, airplane mode, and network changes during every important transition.

Location frequency should depend on activity and confidence, not a permanently aggressive timer. Use OS-supported background execution rules and expose the consequences of disabled permissions. A silent failure that leaves an old location looking current is more dangerous than an explicit stale-location indicator.

Release discipline

Mobile rollouts are slower to reverse than server rollouts. Maintain backward-compatible command envelopes and server-side kill switches for optional features. Test a matrix of old app versions against new entity states. Avoid protocol changes that require every rider and driver to upgrade simultaneously.

The key invariant is simple: the app can explain and observe the journey, but the authoritative service decides whether a commitment exists. Good offline design preserves that distinction without making the experience unusable.

09. Location ingestion, sequencing, and freshness

Location is a stream of uncertain observations, not a perfect track of reality. GPS noise, clock skew, delayed packets, duplicated samples, tunnels, and background restrictions all affect what the platform sees.

Published evidence. Uber's May 2026 traffic publication describes high-volume GPS-derived processing and real-time road-segment prediction. Its reported scale applies to that traffic pipeline; it should not be treated as the number of ride requests or active drivers. [S08]

The proposed ingestion contract

A sample includes actor ID, device-session ID, increasing sequence number, coordinates, horizontal accuracy, speed and bearing when available, observed time, and optional sensor provenance. The edge attaches received time and authenticates the device's permission to report for that actor. Validate coordinate bounds, payload size, implausible jumps, and rate limits before routing to a keyed partition.

Maintain the latest accepted sample per actor, but also retain an appropriately governed raw stream for debugging and model training. A late sample can be useful historically without replacing the current position. Sequence numbers order samples within a device session; a new device session requires an explicit generation transition so old uploads cannot overwrite it.

Freshness has multiple dimensions

Compute age from server receipt and observation plausibility. Track uncertainty separately from age: a fresh location with a 200-meter accuracy radius may be less useful than a slightly older precise sample. Surface both to candidate generation and ETA estimation.

For a reference dispatch policy, exclude supply beyond a configured freshness limit, reduce confidence in borderline samples, and request a refresh before commitment when needed. These limits are product-specific and measured in field tests. Do not hard-code one universal timeout for dense downtown roads, airports, and rural areas.

Partitioning and write amplification

Key the ingestion stream by actor to preserve local ordering. Materialize a latest-location view and a geographic membership view. When an actor changes cells, update both memberships with an actor version; queries deduplicate by actor and choose the newest valid version. Otherwise, a driver can appear twice during cell movement or disappear during a non-atomic transfer.

For illustrative sizing, one million active devices sending every four seconds create 250,000 samples per second before retries. At 200 payload bytes each, that is 50 MB/s of raw payload, excluding transport overhead, replication, indexing, and retention. The model shows why location traffic must be budgeted separately from bookings.

Loss and overload behavior

During overload, coalesce obsolete location samples where the downstream purpose permits it. Never coalesce business transitions such as pickup confirmation into the same lossy queue. Backpressure policies should identify which data may be sampled and which must be durable.

Monitor freshness distributions, rejected jump rates, out-of-order arrivals, device-session resets, and geographic coverage. A healthy ingestion HTTP success rate can coexist with unusable location data. The consumer's ability to make a safe decision is the real measure.

10. H3 and geographic candidate discovery

A spatial index answers where to look. It does not determine which driver can legally and quickly reach a pickup. That second question requires road topology, direction, restrictions, and travel-time estimates.

Published evidence. Uber introduced H3 as a hierarchical hexagonal index. H3 documentation distinguishes exact logical parent-child relationships from approximate geographic containment across resolutions, and its grid includes pentagons. These details matter when converting cell sets into business boundaries. [S05][S06][S76]

The proposed geographic view

Map each sufficiently fresh supply location to a cell at a chosen resolution. Maintain cell-to-actor membership with version and last-seen metadata. A pickup query begins with its cell and nearby cells, filters eligible supply, and expands until it has enough useful candidates or reaches a time and distance budget.

The expansion policy should adapt to density. A fixed number of neighboring cells may be excessive in a city center and insufficient in a sparse area. Candidate count limits protect the routing service from unbounded fan-out. A coarser aggregate view can estimate where to expand next without scanning every fine cell.

Exact boundaries need exact checks

A service zone, airport queue, restricted curb, or pricing boundary should not be enforced solely by approximate hierarchical containment. Use the grid as a fast prefilter, then evaluate the authoritative polygon or road-access rule. Handle points on boundaries consistently and version the geometry.

Cells near pentagons or geographic distortions require library-supported traversal rather than assumptions copied from a flat, perfect hexagon drawing. A diagram in this book is conceptual, not a mathematically exact rendering of the global H3 grid. Test high latitudes, the antimeridian, and resolution changes.

Discovery and ownership are separate

A candidate may be visible in several cell-query responses because memberships update asynchronously. Deduplicate by actor ID and latest valid location version. Then check current supply eligibility and plan state through the authority. A geographic view is allowed to be slightly stale because it does not commit assignments.

Do not partition durable driver authority solely by the cell the driver currently occupies. Vehicles move frequently; moving database ownership on every cell crossing would create needless coordination. Geographic indexing can change independently of an actor's stable home partition.

Choosing resolution

Estimate expected actors per cell, membership churn, expansion depth, cache efficiency, and road-network ambiguity. Run replay experiments with historical-like synthetic traces before selecting a resolution. A finer grid reduces initial candidate volume but increases cell transitions and may miss useful candidates unless expansion is robust.

The final pipeline is therefore: spatial prefilter, eligibility filter, approximate travel-time ranking, exact route evaluation for finalists, and authoritative feasibility validation. H3 accelerates the first stage; it does not replace the rest of dispatch.

11. Map matching and map quality

Raw coordinates often fall beside the road, on a parallel street, or inside a building. Map matching estimates which road segment a traveler most likely used. Map quality systems then distinguish repeated map problems from ordinary sensor noise.

Published evidence. Uber's CatchME account describes using trace matching and aggregated anomalies to detect map issues; it includes HMM/Viterbi-style reasoning and S2-based processing. This is a useful reminder that Uber's published geographic systems do not all use H3 for every task. [S61]

The inference problem

For each GPS sample, generate plausible nearby road segments. An emission score measures how compatible a segment is with the observed location, accuracy, and heading. A transition score measures whether movement between candidate segments is plausible given elapsed time and the road graph. Dynamic programming chooses a likely sequence rather than independently snapping every point to the nearest line.

The proposed matcher should preserve alternatives and confidence. Parallel roads, elevated highways, and tunnels may remain ambiguous. If evidence is insufficient, do not invent certainty. A location useful for a map animation may not be suitable for calculating a disputed distance-based charge.

Build a quality feedback loop

Aggregate repeated mismatches by road segment, map version, device class, and time. A single impossible turn may be a noisy sample; many independent traces taking the same path may suggest a missing connection or incorrect restriction. Rank suspected defects for automated checks and human review.

Keep the original observations, matched sequence, matcher version, and map release identifier within their retention policy. Otherwise, a map correction can make historical debugging irreproducible. Ground truth may require surveyed data or validated partner information, not just consensus among noisy traces.

Release maps like software

A road-graph update affects routing, ETA, pickup placement, and potentially pricing. Validate connectivity, turn restrictions, ferry and toll metadata, and legal access. Replay representative journeys on both versions. Canary the update in bounded areas with rollback support.

Map version compatibility matters for cached routes. A route containing edge IDs from one graph version cannot automatically be interpreted against another. Include graph version in route artifacts and translate or recompute explicitly when necessary.

Privacy and misuse boundaries

Exact traces can reveal homes, workplaces, and sensitive visits. Restrict access, minimize exported detail, and aggregate before broad analysis. A quality-debugging tool should not become an unrestricted employee location browser. Use purpose-bound access and auditable queries.

What the system must not infer

A likely matched route is not proof of intent, fault, or misconduct. Road closures and unexpected detours can make correct travel look anomalous. Use map inference as evidence with confidence and context. Human-facing decisions that affect earnings or safety need an appeal path and access to the underlying uncertainty.

12. Routing, pickup access, and time-dependent travel

The nearest driver by straight-line distance may be on the wrong side of a divided road. A route engine translates geography into feasible movement. Dispatch quality depends on that distinction.

Published evidence. Uber's 2015 routing account discusses evolution from earlier routing approaches toward a specialized engine and traffic-aware computation. It is historical evidence about design pressures, not a verified description of every current routing component. [S62]

The proposed road graph

Represent directed road segments and legal transitions. Edge costs include expected traversal time, while constraints encode turn restrictions, vehicle eligibility, access rules, and closures. A pickup is not merely a latitude and longitude: it may refer to a permitted curb, terminal door, walking approach, or designated meeting zone.

Use hierarchical routing or preprocessed shortcuts for fast queries, with a customization stage for changing traffic weights. Preserve a slower correctness path for unusual constraints. A cached shortest path under yesterday's weights may be a useful candidate but is not necessarily optimal now.

Time-dependent costs

For a route with edges e1 through en, arrival at each edge depends on the time spent on previous edges. Evaluate travel time at the estimated entry time, not with one timeless weight for the whole journey. Enforce reasonable properties in the cost model; nonphysical discontinuities can make route selection unstable.

For dispatch, many-to-many travel-time estimation can dominate cost. Begin with cheap geographic and historical approximations. Compute more accurate routes only for a bounded candidate set. Batch requests sharing nearby origins or destinations, but maintain per-request deadlines so batching does not add unacceptable waiting.

Curb and walking time

Pickup ETA includes driving to a reachable curb, parking or stopping, and potentially walking or finding the rider. Delivery ETA includes entrance access, elevators, and handoff. A route engine that only measures road travel systematically underestimates these tasks.

Store access instructions separately from the road graph and govern their privacy. Customer-provided instructions should be sanitized and treated as untrusted content. They cannot override road legality or safety constraints.

Failure behavior

If live traffic is unavailable, use a versioned historical profile and widen the estimate. If the graph cannot establish a feasible route, exclude the candidate or request a corrected pickup rather than fabricate a straight-line path. Log the fallback reason for later quality analysis.

Use route stability controls to avoid repeatedly switching between nearly equivalent paths. Drivers need understandable guidance, not oscillation caused by tiny model updates. The optimization objective should include practical route quality and confidence, not only the lowest predicted number.

A route is a proposal grounded in a model. The real vehicle may take another safe route. Preserve observed execution separately so navigation, ETA calibration, and commercial adjustments do not overwrite one another's evidence.

13. ETA: road traversal, whole-trip prediction, and uncertainty

An ETA is a conditional prediction, not a timer counting down with certainty. Pickup time, journey duration, food preparation, parking, and handoff are different prediction targets and should be evaluated separately.

Published evidence. DeepETA's 2022 account describes learning corrections around routing-based estimates. The May 2026 DeepETT account describes road-segment traversal prediction using spatial-temporal modeling and real-time calibration. Segment travel prediction and complete-trip ETA are related but not interchangeable systems. [S07][S08]

A layered reference model

Start with a route and its time-dependent segment estimates. Add calibrated residuals for context that the route engine does not fully explain. For pickup, include the driver's approach and expected meeting delay. For delivery, include preparation, courier arrival, waiting, transport, and handoff. Some stages overlap, so blindly adding their averages is wrong.

For example, departure from a restaurant occurs approximately after both food readiness and courier arrival: departure = max(food_ready, courier_arrival) + handoff. The distribution of that maximum depends on both variables and their dependence. A simple sum double-counts parallel waiting.

Train on the right label

Define exactly what the label means: wheels moving, arrival at curb, passenger pickup, restaurant departure, or customer handoff. Use observed evidence and confidence. Exclude or separately model corrupted GPS, missing transitions, and exceptional events. Avoid training labels that include information unavailable at prediction time.

Maintain model version, feature schema, route version, and prediction timestamp. Evaluate by market, route length, time of day, weather-like disruption categories, and device confidence. Global mean absolute error can hide severe failures for a small but important segment.

Calibration and presentation

A reference serving API returns point estimate, uncertainty interval, freshness, and fallback reason. Product design can show a range when uncertainty is high. Evaluate interval coverage and bias, not only point error. A systematically optimistic pickup ETA damages trust even when its average absolute error looks acceptable.

Calibrate online only with safeguards against delayed labels and feedback loops. A traffic incident can shift the distribution abruptly. A model should degrade toward conservative, interpretable estimates when inputs are missing rather than extrapolate confidently from out-of-distribution features.

Latency and load

Batch inference where it reduces cost without violating deadlines. Cache reusable segment features, not final predictions beyond their freshness budget. Separate training pipelines from online serving capacity. Model or feature-store failure must have a documented fallback.

Test the entire chain: geographic candidate selection, route generation, feature lookup, inference, calibration, and user presentation. An accurate model behind a stale cache or mismatched road graph can produce poor ETAs. The service-level objective is a timely, well-calibrated prediction for the actual decision, not a good offline benchmark alone.

14. Real-time channels, acknowledgments, and reconnects

Push delivery keeps rider and driver views aligned, but a network connection is not a durable workflow. The system must survive dropped connections, duplicated messages, app suspension, and changes in transport.

Published evidence. Uber's 2022 RAMEN account describes a move from SSE toward bidirectional gRPC with its QUIC/HTTP3 integration, acknowledgments on the stream, and fallback support. The 2019 QUIC account discusses mobile-network performance. This does not imply that ordinary gRPC deployments universally use HTTP/3. [S14][S15]

The proposed channel model

A connection gateway terminates authenticated streams and subscribes to the actor's authorized entity updates. A durable event pipeline feeds the gateway. Messages include event ID, aggregate version, connection generation, and priority. The gateway can coalesce old map updates but must not discard a new assignment or cancellation as though it were just another coordinate.

Distinguish transport acknowledgment, application receipt, and business acceptance. A driver device receiving an offer is not the driver accepting it. An app rendering an assignment is not proof of pickup. Each acknowledgment has its own schema and meaning.

Reconnect without guessing

The client reconnects with its last applied entity versions and a cursor when supported. The server sends missing events if the retained history is sufficient; otherwise, it sends an authoritative snapshot plus a new cursor. Apply a snapshot atomically before newer deltas. Reject obsolete connection generations so a delayed old connection cannot overwrite the new session's state.

A cursor is scoped to its stream and retention contract. It is not a global timestamp for every service. If multiple aggregates appear on one screen, the client may need a server-composed snapshot to avoid impossible combinations.

Backpressure and priorities

Bound per-connection queues. Classify messages into critical state transitions, operational updates, and replaceable telemetry. When a slow client cannot keep up, replace queued location samples with the latest version, preserve essential transitions, and eventually require resynchronization.

Use heartbeats to detect dead channels, but do not equate a missed heartbeat with a canceled trip. The authority continues to own the assignment. Fallback push notifications can alert the user to reopen the app; they are not a substitute for durable state retrieval.

Security and observability

Authorize each subscription, not just the initial connection. Remove access when the actor's relationship to an entity ends. Avoid exposing another rider's exact location in pooled or shared contexts beyond the product's intended visibility.

Measure connection establishment, reconnect frequency, server-to-client lag, application acknowledgments, dropped replaceable updates, and snapshot recovery. A gateway can have excellent uptime while delivering stale state. Real-time quality is the age and correctness of what the user sees.

15. Supply sessions, eligibility, and feasible plans

A vehicle visible on a map is not necessarily available for a new job. Availability is a derived business state involving the participant, vehicle, current plan, product eligibility, connectivity, and local operating rules.

Reference design. Supply availability is modeled as a lifecycle with explicit eligibility and plan constraints, not an online Boolean.

Model a session, not a flag

The reference supply session progresses through offline, starting, eligible, offered, committed, temporarily unavailable, and ending states. The session has a generation, participant ID, vehicle ID, capabilities, and current plan version. A driver changing vehicles or restarting a session creates an explicit transition, not an unnoticed overwrite of metadata.

Eligibility combines slowly changing facts, such as vehicle class, with rapidly changing facts, such as capacity and active restrictions. Cache the slow facts with a version. Validate the facts that can invalidate a commitment again during acceptance. A matching worker's snapshot is only a proposal input.

Capacity is a plan constraint

For a single-rider product, one active commitment may consume all capacity. For pooled rides, the plan must track occupancy after every stop. For delivery, feasibility may include volume, temperature constraints, pickup readiness, and maximum detour. Capacity is not simply the number of jobs assigned.

A proposed insertion algorithm evaluates every allowed pair of pickup and drop-off positions in the current stop sequence, preserving pickup-before-drop-off ordering. It checks capacity along the resulting route, time windows, existing customer promises, and driver eligibility. Expensive route estimates are computed before the commit; plan version is checked at commit.

Leases versus business ownership

A short offer lease can reserve an opportunity while a driver decides. It does not mean that a distributed lock alone establishes the assignment. Durable authority must reject an acceptance for an expired or superseded offer. A lease expiry should be evaluated using trusted server-side time and persisted offer state.

The current trip continues even if the driver's heartbeat stops. Connectivity loss changes observability, not automatically the physical assignment. Escalation may involve contacting the participant, restricting new offers, and reconciling on reconnect.

Eligibility changes mid-trip

Some changes prevent new work but do not safely terminate an ongoing journey. Other changes require immediate operational intervention. Represent the policy category explicitly. Do not let a generic account-state update strand a passenger without a defined safe process.

Audit why supply was excluded from a candidate set. Debugging should reveal an explainable reason code and relevant policy version without exposing unnecessary personal information. Monitor stale sessions, conflicting vehicle bindings, infeasible plan attempts, and sessions that remain committed long after expected completion.

The durable invariant is not that the marketplace's view is always perfectly fresh. It is that a stale view cannot create an invalid committed plan.

16. Quotes, prices, and marketplace balance

Pricing has two different responsibilities: communicate a commercial offer and influence marketplace behavior. Mixing them in one mutable number makes auditing, experimentation, and customer support unnecessarily difficult.

Published evidence. Uber's upfront-pricing material describes showing a fare before requesting. Its March 2025 US driver-fare account explains that driver offers can reflect factors beyond a simple time-and-distance formula. Rider pricing and driver earnings should therefore not be assumed to be the same formula or a fixed percentage split. [S10][S11]

The proposed quote object

A quote contains product, pickup and destination references, currency, component amounts, applicable taxes or fees, policy version, model version, expiration, and constraints under which adjustment is allowed. Store the accepted quote immutably. A later change in destination creates a new agreed revision or a documented adjustment, not a silent edit to history.

Keep the customer's price, driver's offered earnings, platform-funded incentives, and third-party charges as distinct objects. Accounting connects them later. The platform's economic result cannot be inferred by subtracting two headline numbers without understanding taxes, incentives, refunds, and partner obligations.

A reference pricing pipeline

Validate geography and product. Estimate route and time. Read current supply-demand aggregates with freshness metadata. Evaluate a versioned pricing policy within configured bounds. Produce a quote and explanation components. Record which inputs were unavailable and which fallbacks were used.

Dynamic adjustments should be bounded, monitored, and reviewed for market-specific requirements. The policy service owns permitted behavior; a learned model supplies estimates or recommendations. A model deployment must not accidentally remove a legal restriction or create unreviewed price discrimination.

Prevent unstable feedback

Higher prices can change demand and supply. Those responses arrive with delay. An overly reactive controller may oscillate, especially during events or outages. Smooth noisy aggregates, limit rate of change, monitor uncertainty, and evaluate the effect on fulfillment and participant outcomes rather than optimizing immediate revenue alone.

A reference model can use elasticity estimates, but those estimates are causal assumptions requiring evidence. Historical correlations between price and demand are confounded by time, weather-like disruptions, and supply shortages. Run carefully designed experiments and maintain guardrails.

Quote failure and expiry

If critical route or policy inputs are unavailable, return a deliberate no-quote outcome or a conservative approved fallback. Do not generate an arbitrary number just to keep API availability high. A request submitted after expiry should receive a new quote for explicit confirmation.

Measure quote latency, expiration before booking, adjustment frequency, explanation quality, conversion, fulfillment, and error by market segment. Prices are part of a trust contract. A technically sophisticated model that repeatedly surprises users is not a successful engineering system.

17. Candidate generation: fast, bounded, and explainable

Dispatch should not evaluate every driver against every request. It needs a cascade that spends cheap computation broadly and expensive computation only where it can change the decision.

Published evidence. H3 was introduced in the context of spatial analysis and marketplace needs. Uber's reinforcement-learning account describes matching decisions that consider longer-term marketplace balance. The candidate pipeline below is a proposed implementation, not a disclosed production algorithm. [S05][S09]

Stage one: discover

Query nearby geographic cells using fresh supply observations. Expand adaptively until a bounded number of candidates is found. Remove duplicate actor IDs and retain location version, uncertainty, cell provenance, and last-seen age. Keep a reason trace for exclusions so a missing driver can be diagnosed.

Do not let a dense airport or event area create unlimited routing fan-out. Candidate budgets should depend on request urgency and system load. A separate admission controller can reduce optional exploration under pressure while preserving already accepted work.

Stage two: filter

Check product eligibility, vehicle capability, active plan compatibility, pickup access, and relevant operating constraints. Apply hard constraints before scoring. A very attractive predicted travel time cannot compensate for an ineligible vehicle or impossible capacity.

Use cached eligibility for speed, but annotate the version and revalidate at commitment. The pipeline must tolerate candidates becoming unavailable between discovery and offer creation. This is expected concurrency, not an exceptional system failure.

Stage three: estimate and rerank

Use a cheap travel-time approximation to reduce hundreds of candidates to a small set. Then request more accurate route and pickup estimates. Compute a score from wait time, detour, acceptance likelihood, existing commitments, and marketplace opportunity cost. All coefficients and objectives require product review and experiments; the book's examples are illustrative.

A stale feature should not silently look like a favorable value. Include missingness and confidence, or use a safe fallback. Log score components and model versions for sampled decisions, subject to privacy controls.

Stage four: produce feasible proposals

For each finalist, generate a concrete proposed plan revision rather than merely a driver ID. The proposal includes route assumptions, stop sequence, capacity checks, deadline, and expected plan version. A downstream assignment service can then validate a meaningful object.

The output should include an explicit no-feasible-candidate result. Repeatedly expanding forever increases user waiting and infrastructure load without guaranteeing service. Escalation may offer another product, revise pickup, or clearly report unavailability.

Quality evaluation

Measure candidate recall against a more expensive offline evaluator, routing calls per request, time to first feasible proposal, and exclusion reason distribution. Evaluate not only the chosen driver but also whether the pipeline systematically fails to consider good alternatives.

Candidate generation is a recall-versus-cost tradeoff. Matching cannot recover an excellent candidate that the earlier stages incorrectly discarded.

18. Matching, batching, and optimization objectives

The closest available driver is not always the best global assignment. When several requests compete for supply, independent greedy choices can create avoidable waiting and leave difficult requests stranded.

Published evidence. Uber's 2025 marketplace account describes reinforcement-learning value estimates used to model longer-term balance in matching. It is evidence of a richer objective than nearest-neighbor selection, not a public specification of all production constraints or weights. [S09]

A small example

Suppose pickup times in minutes are A-to-1 = 2, A-to-2 = 3, B-to-1 = 3, and B-to-2 = 9. Greedy selection of the shortest edge chooses A-to-1, leaving B-to-2: total 11 minutes. Choosing A-to-2 and B-to-1 totals 6 minutes. These are illustrative costs, not Uber measurements.

This motivates a bipartite assignment model. Requests and feasible supply plans form vertices; valid pairings form edges. Minimize total cost subject to each request and resource being used within its capacity. Include an explicit unassigned option with a penalty so the optimizer is not forced into an unacceptable match.

Time is also a cost

Batching requests creates more opportunities for global optimization but makes early arrivals wait. Choose a short decision window bounded by the user experience and current load. Compare the improvement from waiting against the additional delay. Under sparse demand, immediate matching may be preferable.

A reference objective can combine pickup delay, existing-passenger detour, cancellation risk, and a future-supply value term. Hard safety, legal, and capacity constraints remain outside the weighted score. Otherwise, a sufficiently large positive score could mathematically justify an invalid action.

From solver output to commitment

The solver operates on a snapshot. By the time it returns, a driver may have accepted another offer or a rider may have canceled. Treat the result as a proposal set. Commit each compatible assignment through version checks and resource ownership. If a batch cannot be committed atomically, define how partial success changes the remaining optimization problem.

Never replay an old solver output after recovery without revalidating every affected plan. Store the snapshot generation, objective version, and decision deadline. A late optimal answer is often less useful than an on-time feasible answer.

Algorithms and scale

Start with a small, well-tested min-cost assignment implementation for a bounded candidate graph. For richer pickup-and-delivery plans, use insertion heuristics, decomposition, or specialized optimization with a strict time limit. Exact global optimization may be computationally impractical and operationally unnecessary.

Evaluate solver timeout rate, feasible coverage, total waiting, tail waiting, cancellation, and distribution across participant groups and neighborhoods. A global average improvement can conceal concentrated harm. Report tradeoffs explicitly rather than compressing every outcome into one opaque score.

The important engineering boundary is between optimization quality and commitment correctness. A mediocre but feasible proposal can be safe. A brilliant proposal committed twice is not.

19. Offers, acceptance races, leases, and fencing

An offer is a temporary invitation. An assignment is a durable commitment. The transition between them is one of the most important correctness boundaries in the platform.

Technical basis. Serializable transactions provide a way to enforce multi-row invariants, but callers must handle transaction aborts and ambiguous network outcomes. The reference protocol below uses that property and explicit idempotency rather than claiming that a distributed cache lock guarantees exactly-once assignment. [S74][S77]

A proposed offer record

Store offer ID, request ID, actor ID, proposed plan, expected request and plan versions, authority epoch, expiry, and state. The invitation payload references that record. The client submits an acceptance command with a stable command ID and the offer ID; it does not send an arbitrary driver-plan replacement.

Inside one authoritative transaction, read the offer and affected aggregates, verify eligibility and versions, check expiry using trusted time, and ensure the authority epoch is current. Then write the assignment, new plan revision, request state, offer outcome, command result, and outbox event. Commit before acknowledging acceptance.

Competing acceptances

Two workers may attempt to bind the same driver. Both can have valid-looking snapshots. The transaction must make one lose by version conflict or an enforced uniqueness constraint. A retry re-reads current state and returns a conflict; it must not reinterpret the old offer as permission to create another assignment.

Two requests can also race to consume the same pooled capacity. Lock or serialize the plan aggregate and recompute feasibility against the current revision. Counting active rows without protecting the predicate can admit more work than capacity allows.

Why leases alone are insufficient

A worker can pause longer than its lock lease, resume, and attempt a stale write after another worker takes ownership. A fencing token solves this only if the authoritative resource checks it. Merely attaching a token to logs does nothing.

The proposed authority row stores an increasing epoch. All commitment transactions verify the epoch in the same consistency domain as their writes. Failover advances the epoch only after the prior writer is fenced. Geographic caches and notification gateways may lag; their stale messages cannot pass the authoritative check.

Lost acknowledgment

If the database commits and the response is lost, the client repeats the same acceptance command. The server returns the stored assignment result. It does not create a new assignment or report failure merely because the original offer is now marked accepted.

Offer-expiry timers are also idempotent. They may expire only an offer still pending at the expected version. A late timer cannot undo an accepted assignment. Measure acceptance conflicts, expired taps, ambiguous outcomes, and late timers; these reveal network and concurrency problems that a simple success rate hides.

20. Trip state machines and business transactions

A trip is not one linear status field with arbitrary transitions. It is a coordinated set of states: customer intent, driver assignment, physical execution, commercial obligation, and operational exceptions.

Published evidence. Uber describes statecharts, coordinated entity transactions, and at-least-once post-commit work in its 2021 fulfillment framework. [S02]

A proposed execution model

A simplified ride progresses through requested, searching, assigned, approaching, arrived, in_progress, and completed, with cancellation branches defined by policy. Each transition specifies allowed source states, actor permissions, expected version, evidence requirements, and side effects.

Hierarchical states avoid duplicating rules. For example, assigned, approaching, and arrived may share a pre-pickup cancellation policy, while in_progress requires a different process. Exceptions such as safety intervention or disputed pickup should be explicit substates or case links, not undocumented flags.

Keep the transaction deterministic

The transition function takes current authoritative state and a validated command, then produces new state and events. It should not perform network calls, read mutable external configuration without a pinned version, or send notifications inside a database retry loop.

Precompute route estimates and policy decisions outside the transaction when possible, then verify their validity constraints inside it. If the relevant plan version or policy epoch changed, reject and recompute. Short transactions reduce contention and make retries easier to reason about.

Completion versus cancellation

Suppose a rider cancels while the driver reports pickup. Define which evidence and version order governs the outcome. The server should not use whichever message arrived last to overwrite the other. One transition commits; the other receives a conflict or enters a reviewed exception path.

A completed trip cannot be made nonexistent by changing its status to canceled. A later dispute creates an adjustment and case record. Preserve the original execution history and the reason for any correction.

Timers and durable work

Arrival-wait timers, offer deadlines, and no-show processes carry the aggregate version they expect. On firing, they re-read state. Timers are wake-up hints, not authority to mutate whatever happens to exist at that time.

Post-commit tasks include notification, receipt preparation, accounting, analytics, and partner updates. Each task has a stable effect identity. A worker crash after sending but before recording completion can cause a retry; the receiver must deduplicate or the workflow must reconcile the outcome.

Verification

Test every legal transition, every illegal transition, duplicate commands, stale versions, actor mismatches, delayed observations, and concurrent terminal transitions. Property tests should assert that completed work retains a valid assignment history and that capacity is never exceeded by committed plans.

A state machine is valuable because it makes impossible states rejectable, not because it makes a diagram look tidy.

21. Reservations, airports, shared rides, and specialized products

Specialized ride products change constraints rather than merely changing a label. Scheduled requests introduce future uncertainty; airports introduce access and queue rules; shared rides introduce coupled passenger commitments.

Published evidence. Uber Health's public trip API distinguishes multiple scheduling modes, including product-specific scheduled and reservation behavior. Public availability and semantics depend on the integration and market; the general mechanisms below are a proposed design rather than a promise that every feature exists everywhere. [S66]

Reservations are future commitments

A reservation stores a time window, pickup requirements, accepted commercial terms, and fulfillment policy. It may reserve supply immediately or create a future dispatch obligation. Those are different guarantees and must be communicated clearly.

The proposed scheduler evaluates coverage risk as the departure approaches, confirms participant readiness, and escalates if expected supply deteriorates. A background timer alone cannot guarantee pickup. Weather-like disruptions, traffic, and driver cancellations require operational contingencies and customer communication.

Airport access and queues

Model airport operating zones, permitted pickup points, product eligibility, and queue policy as versioned local configuration. Geographic presence may be necessary but insufficient for queue membership. GPS uncertainty near boundaries requires hysteresis or additional evidence to prevent repeated enter-exit churn.

Queue position should be auditable. Prevent duplicate sessions from multiplying eligibility. When an airport changes access rules, existing assignments need a deliberate transition plan rather than a sudden route failure.

Shared rides

Represent a vehicle plan with ordered pickup and drop-off stops. Every insertion checks occupancy, detour promises for existing riders, pickup windows, and compatibility constraints. A new rider's low fare or short pickup time cannot justify violating an existing commitment.

If one rider cancels, recompute the plan and commercial consequences without erasing other assignments. Expose only the information each participant needs. A shared-ride map must not disclose unnecessary personal details or historical locations of another rider.

Accessibility and specialized vehicles

Capabilities should be explicit and verified: vehicle equipment, loading space, assistance requirements, or product-specific training where applicable. Do not infer a participant's needs from sensitive attributes. Let the product capture necessary service requirements with consent and appropriate privacy controls.

Specialized supply may be sparse. Candidate expansion, reservation horizons, and support escalation should reflect that scarcity. Reporting a generic technical success while repeatedly failing to find eligible supply obscures the actual product outcome.

Test by market configuration

Create a product-capability matrix and validate each enabled combination. A new city launch must not accidentally inherit an airport rule or vehicle category from another jurisdiction. Maintain effective dates and rollback semantics for configuration. The platform should refuse unsupported combinations explicitly instead of improvising behavior from missing fields.

22. Navigation, trip changes, no-shows, and execution evidence

After matching, the platform still has substantial work to do. Pickup instructions change, roads close, riders are delayed, and drivers may lose connectivity. Execution needs a coherent record even when the original plan changes.

Published evidence. Uber's Verify My Ride documentation describes a PIN-based check before beginning a ride. This is one product mechanism for confirming the intended pairing; it does not turn GPS proximity into proof of pickup. [S73]

Plan revisions, not silent overwrites

The reference system stores each accepted plan revision. A destination change creates a command with the current version, revised destination, and any required commercial confirmation. The server validates feasibility, produces a new plan, and communicates the change to affected participants.

Separate route recalculation from contractual change. A traffic detour may update navigation without changing the destination or quote contract. Adding a stop may require explicit agreement. The user interface should explain which type of change occurred.

Arrival and pickup

Use multiple signals for arrival: location, route progress, participant action, and product-specific confirmation. Inferred arrival can trigger a prompt, but should not automatically establish that the correct person entered the vehicle. Preserve confidence and source for each observation.

If a PIN is used, bind it to the correct assignment and enforce attempt limits. Do not expose it in routine logs or broad support views. A bypass or exception process must be narrow, auditable, and safe rather than a generic administrative override.

Waiting and no-show rules

A wait timer starts from a defined event and policy version. The event may require both plausible arrival and driver declaration. Do not let a client-controlled clock determine billable waiting. Delayed uploads require reconciliation against server evidence and the applicable policy.

A no-show decision can affect charges and earnings. Preserve the reason, timing, communication attempts, and relevant location confidence. Support should be able to review the case without modifying the original trip history.

Offline completion

A driver may report completion after connectivity returns. The server checks assignment identity, previous state, observation plausibility, and whether another terminal transition already committed. If evidence conflicts, enter an exception path. Do not resolve all disputes by trusting the latest packet.

Receipts and economic adjustments follow the authoritative outcome. A navigation service outage should not erase a completed journey. Conversely, an app showing the destination should not by itself trigger completion and charging.

Operational metrics

Track pickup-location corrections, repeated route changes, no-show disputes, unconfirmed starts, and delayed completion uploads. Segment by map confidence and network quality. These metrics often reveal real product problems that generic request-latency dashboards miss.

Execution engineering is the bridge between digital plans and physical evidence. Its goal is not to force reality into an idealized sequence, but to preserve a coherent, explainable account when reality differs.

23. Reinforcement learning and supply repositioning

A matching decision changes the future marketplace. Sending the only nearby vehicle on a long journey can improve one request while making the next several requests harder to serve. This creates a sequential decision problem.

Published evidence. Uber's July 2025 account describes reinforcement-learning value functions for marketplace balance in matching. It discusses learning future value rather than optimizing only the immediate pairing. The specific model, constraints, and policy proposed here are illustrative. [S09]

State, action, and delayed value

A reference state summarizes supply and demand by geography and time, current commitments, travel conditions, and uncertainty. An action may be a feasible assignment or a suggested repositioning opportunity. A reward must represent the intended product outcomes over time, not merely immediate gross bookings.

A value estimate can act as an opportunity-cost term in the matching objective. For example, using a driver in a scarce area may incur a larger future-supply cost. The deterministic feasibility layer still enforces capacity, permissions, and operating constraints before the model influences ranking.

Offline learning is not a free experiment

Historical logs reflect the policy that generated them. Actions rarely chosen in the past have weak evidence. Counterfactual estimates may be biased when action probabilities are unknown or important state is missing. Use held-out time periods, policy-aware evaluation, and conservative deployment gates.

A simulator can explore policies but inherits assumptions about rider demand, driver behavior, cancellations, and travel times. A model that exploits simulator errors may look excellent offline and fail in the real marketplace. Validate simulated distributions against observed-like data and stress cases.

Repositioning is advisory, with human response

Drivers may decline or ignore repositioning suggestions. Model acceptance and actual movement separately from the recommendation. Avoid treating displayed incentives as guaranteed supply. Track whether repositioning creates excess movement, worsens other areas, or produces unstable herding toward the same hotspot.

Bound the number of simultaneous suggestions into a region and account for already-en-route supply. Use uncertainty-aware forecasts so a temporary noisy spike does not redirect a large fleet unnecessarily.

Safe deployment

Start in shadow mode, then small cohorts with strong guardrails and rollback. Monitor waiting time, fulfillment, cancellations, participant earnings distributions, and geographic coverage. Do not evaluate only the reward used in training; that invites reward misspecification.

Version state features, reward definitions, model artifacts, and policy constraints together. A changed feature scale can alter decisions even when the model binary is unchanged. Keep a deterministic fallback policy available.

The useful role of reinforcement learning is to estimate consequences that simple local heuristics miss. It does not remove the need for causal evaluation, explicit constraints, or accountable product choices.

24. Forecasting demand, disruptions, and cold starts

Forecasts support capacity, incentives, reservations, and operational staffing. They do not guarantee that a specific driver or courier will be available at a particular time.

Published evidence. Uber's Michelangelo publications describe a shared platform for training and serving predictive models and its later expansion into deep learning and generative AI. The forecasting pipeline in this chapter is a reference application of those platform concerns, not a disclosed Uber demand model. [S46][S47]

Forecast the decision-relevant quantity

Separate requested demand, completed demand, available supply, and unmet demand. Completed trips alone understate demand when the marketplace is constrained. A model trained only on successful outcomes can incorrectly predict low demand in an area where users repeatedly fail to find service.

Choose spatial and temporal resolution based on the decision. Capacity planning may use hourly regional forecasts; short-horizon dispatch support needs finer intervals. Hierarchical reconciliation should make city totals and neighborhood estimates coherent without pretending each small cell has enough data for an independent model.

Inputs and uncertainty

Use calendar patterns, recent request rates, supply sessions, route conditions, known events where appropriately sourced, and product changes. Record freshness and provenance. Avoid treating an external event feed as infallible. Missing or delayed inputs should widen uncertainty or trigger a fallback.

Produce prediction intervals and scenario forecasts. Operations may care more about a high-demand tail than the average. A reservation coverage plan should consider adverse supply and traffic scenarios, not just the most likely forecast.

Cold-start markets

A new market lacks local history. Start with conservative rules, analogous-market priors, and deliberate operational observation. Measure differences in geography, trip length, pickup behavior, vehicle supply, and device connectivity. Do not transplant another city's model without calibration.

Use gradual product enablement and explicit uncertainty budgets. Human operations can fill gaps while data quality improves, but their interventions must be logged; otherwise, the model learns from outcomes without seeing the actions that produced them.

Detect drift and feedback

A promotion changes demand, which changes wait time, which changes conversion. A forecast should know about planned interventions or it will misinterpret them as unexplained behavior. Separate demand shocks from telemetry failures and product experiments.

Monitor residuals by cohort, interval coverage, input freshness, and forecast usefulness for the downstream decision. A lower statistical error is not automatically valuable if it arrives too late to change staffing or dispatch.

Operational use

Publish forecasts as versioned artifacts with issue time, target horizon, confidence, and supported uses. The control plane can use them to prewarm compute or staff support, but authoritative commitments still require real-time checks. Forecasting helps prepare the system; it does not replace current eligibility and feasibility.

25. Earnings, incentives, memberships, and commercial rules

Commercial products affect participant behavior and financial obligations. They require transparent versioning and accounting, not just feature flags scattered across checkout and payout code.

Published evidence. Uber's 2025 US driver-fare explanation separates upfront offers from additional earnings such as tips and certain incentives. It should not be generalized into one worldwide compensation formula. The membership and incentive architecture below is a proposed design. [S11]

Separate offer, accrual, and payment

An earnings offer is what the participant is shown for accepting work under stated conditions. Accrued earnings are the amount established after execution and approved adjustments. A payout is the movement of funds. These states can differ in time and amount without being inconsistent, provided the rules are explicit.

Store commercial policy version, eligibility facts, accepted offer, execution evidence, and adjustments. An incentive should have an immutable definition for the participant's enrolled period. Changing the rule midway must not silently reinterpret already completed work.

Incentive state machines

A reference incentive can progress through eligible, enrolled, accumulating, qualified, credited, and expired. Progress events must be deduplicated by qualifying work identity. A canceled or corrected trip may require a compensating progress event, not deletion of history.

Guard against duplicate credit when events arrive both from real-time processing and reconciliation. The credit operation needs a stable business key such as participant, program, period, and qualification version. Financial posting remains separate from a promotional UI counter.

Membership entitlements

A membership service owns subscription state and benefit entitlement. Checkout reads a versioned eligibility result and records the benefit used. Recurring billing failure, cancellation, grace periods, and refunds need explicit rules. A cached entitlement can be allowed briefly for some benefits, but its freshness policy must be deliberate.

Benefits can span products without forcing all products to share one order model. A ride discount, delivery-fee benefit, and customer-support entitlement have different application points and accounting consequences.

Explainability and experimentation

Provide a participant-facing breakdown that reconciles to the ledger. Internal support tools should show why an incentive did or did not apply, with evidence and policy version. Avoid vague error categories that require manual arithmetic to resolve every case.

Experiments must preserve accepted commitments. Assign the relevant cohort before generating an offer and store the assignment. Re-evaluating a flag during settlement can produce a different rule from the one the participant accepted.

Controls

Monitor duplicate credits, negative unexpected adjustments, delayed accruals, enrollment mismatches, and benefit leakage. Review product economics and user understanding alongside system latency. A commercial feature is operationally complete only when its exceptions can be explained and corrected without rewriting history.

26. Business, Health, guest rides, and accessibility

When an organization arranges transportation for someone else, the platform must distinguish the payer, requester, traveler, administrator, and support contact. One account identifier cannot safely represent all of them.

Published evidence. Uber Health's API supports organization-arranged rides for guests who may not have an Uber account. Its trip-creation documentation warns against putting protected health information in driver notes. These are scoped product contracts, not automatic compliance guarantees for a newly built application. [S65][S66]

The proposed delegated journey

An organization creates a request under a policy and budget. The traveler receives appropriate trip information through supported channels. The driver receives only operationally necessary details. Billing goes to the organization or authorized payer. Each actor has a different view and set of permitted actions.

Store requester, traveler, payer, and organization references separately. Verify that the requester is authorized to act for the organization and that the request fits its policy. Do not expose organization-wide trip history to a traveler or driver.

Scheduling and guest communication

A guest may not be able to use the standard app. Provide a clear communication and support path, including how to identify the vehicle and what to do if the pickup changes. Avoid assuming a smartphone, continuous data connection, or a particular language.

Scheduled rides need explicit timing semantics and escalation rules. An organization may need to change or cancel a trip, but that permission must be constrained once a traveler is already in transit. The system should avoid conflicting instructions from the traveler and administrator.

Sensitive information boundaries

Collect only service-relevant accessibility requirements. Avoid putting diagnosis, appointment details, or other unnecessary sensitive data into free-text driver instructions, notifications, analytics, or logs. Use structured fields with purpose-limited visibility where possible.

A compliance program requires contracts, governance, access review, retention policies, incident processes, and jurisdiction-specific assessment. Selecting a database encryption feature does not establish legal compliance by itself.

Business controls

Implement budgets, cost centers, approval workflows, travel policies, and invoice references as versioned rules. A policy denial should explain the actionable reason without leaking another employee's data. Audit who booked, changed, approved, and canceled each trip.

Invoice reconciliation must map charges and adjustments to the original organization-authorized request. Avoid charging a guest's personal payment method merely because an enterprise integration temporarily fails.

Accessibility testing

Test screen readers, large text, contrast, nonvisual vehicle identification, alternate contact methods, and support escalation. Specialized vehicle requirements must participate in matching as hard constraints. Accessibility is part of the end-to-end service contract, not a cosmetic layer added after dispatch works.

27. Safety detection and human response

Safety systems operate under uncertainty and high consequences. Their purpose is to help identify concerning situations and connect people with appropriate assistance, not to claim perfect detection or replace emergency services.

Published evidence. Uber's RideCheck announcement describes using trip signals to identify unusual events and prompt a check-in. Uber's US safety material describes additional safety features and support processes. Availability and integrations vary by location and product. [S23][S67]

Detection is not diagnosis

The proposed safety pipeline consumes minimized trip-state and sensor-derived signals. It detects patterns such as an unexpected extended stop or route anomaly, assigns confidence, and selects a response policy. A traffic jam, phone issue, or planned stop can resemble an incident.

Use a tiered response: quiet verification, participant check-in, support escalation, and emergency guidance where appropriate. The policy must account for false positives, missed events, and the danger of distracting a driver. Do not let a model directly make unsupported accusations.

Dedicated operational paths

Safety contact should not depend on optional commerce, recommendations, or promotional systems. Maintain dedicated capacity and tested fallback channels. Clearly state what the product can and cannot do when connectivity is unavailable.

A case record links relevant evidence, participant communications, actions, and timestamps. Access is tightly controlled and audited. Operators need enough context to act, but not unrestricted browsing of unrelated trips or personal data.

Human-in-the-loop design

Define escalation ownership, response expectations, handoff procedures, and regional integrations. Train staff on uncertainty and appropriate communication. A model score should never be the only explanation shown to an operator.

Test with tabletop exercises and controlled simulations, not by creating unsafe real-world situations. Measure time to acknowledgment, successful contact, escalation completeness, and quality-review outcomes. A high alert volume is not evidence of better safety.

Evidence and privacy

Sensitive recordings or location traces require explicit policy, access controls, retention, and legal review. Different participants may have different rights and consent requirements. Do not assume one global recording rule applies everywhere.

Preserve relevant evidence under authorized holds while preventing unnecessary distribution. Export tools need redaction and audit trails. A support attachment can be as sensitive as a production database row.

Failure modes

Plan for a false alarm, unavailable participant, overloaded support queue, delayed sensor data, and a regional service outage. The app should offer clear, locally appropriate guidance rather than imply that an automated alert guarantees immediate help.

Safety engineering combines software, operations, policy, and human judgment. Its reliability cannot be reduced to classifier accuracy or API uptime alone.

28. Autonomous partners, fleets, and electric vehicles

An autonomous vehicle integration adds a new fulfillment partner, but it does not make the marketplace responsible for generating steering or braking commands. Commercial orchestration and the vehicle's driving safety system are distinct boundaries.

Published evidence. Uber announced Autonomous Solutions in February 2026, describing capabilities around commercialization, rider experience, and fleet operations for autonomous partners. Announcements and planned deployments should not be interpreted as proof of universal live availability. [S59]

The proposed partner contract

The marketplace requests a mission with pickup, destination, rider requirements, and commercial references. The partner validates its operational design domain, vehicle readiness, route feasibility, and local permissions. Only then does it accept a mission. The partner's autonomous-driving system retains responsibility for vehicle control and its safety behavior.

Track mission ID, partner ID, assignment version, capability snapshot, acceptance, execution events, and terminal outcome. Use stable idempotency keys for mission creation and cancellation. A timeout after acceptance requires status reconciliation, not an immediate duplicate mission.

Operational design domain

Eligibility depends on supported geography, road types, weather conditions, operating hours, and service constraints. Treat partner-reported capability as versioned and freshness-sensitive. A vehicle physically nearby may be unable to serve a particular route safely.

If conditions change mid-mission, the partner's safe handling procedure and the marketplace's rider-support procedure must coordinate. The reference platform does not prescribe vehicle-control actions. It does require clear responsibility, communication, and evidence.

Fleet readiness

Fleet availability includes cleaning, maintenance, charging or fueling, inspection, connectivity, and passenger readiness. A vehicle can be mechanically functional but not service-ready. Model these as explicit states that gate assignment.

For electric vehicles, route feasibility may include conservative energy reserve and access to charging. Charging plans consume time and physical capacity. Do not use the battery percentage alone as a universal range estimate; vehicle characteristics, conditions, and uncertainty matter.

Partner certification

Use sandbox missions, duplicate and delayed event tests, cancellation races, communication-loss scenarios, and controlled operational exercises. Validate that neither party mistakes a notification for a commitment. Test the rider experience during partner failure, not just normal dispatch.

Rollout boundaries

Enable integrations by partner, market, product, and supported scenario. Maintain the ability to stop new assignments without disrupting the handling of active missions. Report partner health separately from core platform health.

The engineering challenge is reliable coordination across an organizational boundary. Strong APIs, explicit ownership, and rehearsed operations matter as much as the matching algorithm.

29. Catalogs, menus, inventory, and INCA

A delivery marketplace cannot treat every purchasable thing as a restaurant menu item. Grocery, retail, and other categories introduce variants, bundles, weighted goods, store-specific availability, and frequent updates.

Published evidence. Uber's August 2025 INCA account describes a catalog and inventory platform designed for broader commerce categories, with ingestion, enrichment, publication, and downstream serving. The proposed implementation below focuses on correctness at those boundaries rather than assuming access to INCA's private schema. [S17]

Separate product identity from store availability

A reference catalog distinguishes a canonical product, a store-specific listing, an option or modifier, a price version, and an inventory observation. The same product can have different prices, availability, and fulfillment rules at different locations.

A restaurant modifier may be required, optional, or constrained by another selection. A grocery item may be sold by estimated weight. A retail variant may have a distinct stock-keeping unit. Model these relationships explicitly; flattening them into free text makes validation and substitution unreliable.

Ingestion and validation

Merchant feeds arrive through APIs, files, and integrations. Assign every update a source, merchant sequence or version, received time, and schema version. Deduplicate repeated submissions. Reject malformed prices, impossible option constraints, and unauthorized store changes before publication.

Use a staging area where validation and enrichment run without changing customer-visible state. Publish an immutable catalog version or a coherent delta. A partial failed import must not leave half the menu on a new schema and half on the old one.

Availability is often uncertain

An inventory observation is not always a guaranteed stock reservation. Store systems may lag shelf reality. Expose confidence and timestamp to the fulfillment policy. A product can remain searchable while checkout performs a stronger availability check, but the user experience should not imply certainty the system does not possess.

For partners supporting reservations, reserve with an expiry and stable order reference. Release on cancellation or timeout. If a reservation response is lost, query or retry idempotently rather than reserve a second unit.

Serving and indexing

Publish catalog changes to search, recommendations, cart validation, and analytics through versioned events. Each consumer records its applied version. Search may lag briefly, but checkout validates against the authoritative listing and accepted price policy.

Monitor missing categories, zero or negative unintended prices, broken modifier groups, duplicate listings, and merchant-wide disappearance. A syntactically valid import can still be commercially disastrous.

Governance

Content moderation, restricted-item eligibility, tax classification, and local sale rules require specialized policy ownership. The engineering platform should support those decisions without hard-coding unreviewed assumptions into a generic catalog service.

The core invariant is that a customer purchases a validated, versioned offer—not whatever a mutable search document happens to contain at the moment of payment.

30. Search, semantic retrieval, and tail latency

Search must understand intent, retrieve relevant inventory, respect geographic feasibility, and respond quickly. A semantically similar product that cannot be delivered is not a useful result.

Published evidence. Uber's April 2026 delivery-search account describes two-tower semantic retrieval and ANN infrastructure with model/index coordination. Its September 2026 search-latency article discusses several serving optimizations; some end-to-end microbatching ideas are described as future work, not universally deployed behavior. [S18][S19]

A reference retrieval pipeline

Normalize the query and identify language, location, and relevant intent. Run lexical retrieval for exact terms and identifiers alongside semantic retrieval for meaning. Merge candidates, apply serviceability and policy filters, then rank with current availability and user context.

Use metadata-aware retrieval where possible. Fetching a large ANN list and filtering almost all of it afterward can destroy recall and latency. Keep a bounded refill strategy for selective filters and a lexical fallback when embeddings or model serving are unavailable.

Model and index compatibility

An embedding model defines the vector space. Query vectors from one version cannot safely be compared with document vectors from an incompatible version. Build the new index, validate coverage, warm it, and switch query-model and index versions together through a controlled alias.

Store corpus version, embedding version, feature schema, and index build lineage. A rollback must restore a compatible pair, not merely redeploy an old model binary. Detect missing or stale listings before increasing traffic.

Hydration and fan-out

Search indexes often contain compact candidate fields rather than complete product details. Hydration fetches the display and availability data needed for results. Batch these reads, deduplicate repeated keys, and use clear freshness budgets. Do not turn one query into hundreds of independent slow calls.

A shard deadline should leave time for merging and response serialization. Hedging can help isolated tail latency, but indiscriminate duplicate requests worsen overload. Enable it only with capacity-aware limits and cancel unnecessary work when possible.

Relevance and integrity

Evaluate query cohorts: exact restaurant names, misspellings, dietary requests, generic categories, and long natural-language queries. A language model can interpret a request, but deterministic policy checks must enforce restricted-item and serviceability constraints.

Keep sponsored placement distinguishable from organic relevance. Search evaluation should measure successful fulfillment and user satisfaction, not only clicks. An unavailable item can generate clicks while producing a poor outcome.

Performance measurement

Break latency into query understanding, retrieval, filtering, ranking, hydration, and rendering. Track p50, p95, and p99 plus timeout and partial-result rates. A lower average can conceal a worse tail. The fastest search result is not valuable if it is stale, ineligible, or inconsistent with checkout.

31. Recommendations, advertising, and ranking contracts

A recommendation system decides what to show before a user has expressed a precise query. Advertising adds commercial objectives, but neither should bypass availability, policy, or customer-experience constraints.

Published evidence. Uber's 2026 restaurant-recommendation account discusses richer interaction modeling, while its ads-personalization account describes heterogeneous experts and multitask learning. These publications concern model architecture; they do not disclose a complete auction or all ranking-policy weights. [S71][S72]

A reference recommendation stack

Generate candidates from several sources: previous preferences, local popularity, contextual similarity, new listings, and exploration pools. Apply eligibility and availability filters, then score candidates. A final page composer enforces diversity, layout constraints, repetition limits, and business rules.

Log the candidate source, model version, score, final position, and whether the user actually saw the item. A score generated for an offscreen candidate is not an impression. Training labels need the exposure context to avoid learning from events that were never observable to the user.

Advertising has separate accounting

An ad campaign includes budget, schedule, targeting constraints, creative status, and billing policy. Ranking can estimate relevance or conversion, but budget consumption and charge creation require authoritative deduplication. A repeated click event must not create repeated billable charges.

Pacing controls distribute spend over time. They must handle delayed conversion signals and reporting lag. A campaign that appears under budget in a stale analytical view can overspend if serving uses that view as authority.

Exploration and feedback

Purely exploiting past behavior can suppress new merchants and amplify position bias. Introduce controlled exploration with measurable exposure probabilities where appropriate. Store enough policy context for evaluation, but minimize personal data and respect consent and purpose limitations.

Multitask learning can share useful representations across click, order, and longer-term outcomes. It can also create conflicts among objectives. Monitor each task and important cohort rather than trusting one aggregate loss.

Guardrails

Hard constraints include serviceability, merchant status, restricted-item rules, and accepted user settings. Soft ranking objectives should not override them. Clearly identify sponsored content in the product experience according to the applicable design and legal requirements.

A reference fallback can show a cached, geographically valid popular list when personalized serving fails. The fallback must still filter closed or unavailable stores. Cached recommendations are not exempt from current operational constraints.

Evaluate the whole page

Measure conversion, repeat satisfaction, cancellation, merchant exposure distribution, and latency. A locally better item score can produce a worse page if all items are similar or mutually redundant. Page composition is a constrained decision problem, not merely sorting a column of predictions.

32. Cart, checkout, merchant acceptance, and order state

An Eats-style order coordinates a customer, merchant, courier, and payment system. These parties do not act simultaneously, and one party's acceptance does not imply that every other stage has succeeded.

Published evidence. Uber's Eats integration guide describes receiving order notifications, retrieving details, and explicitly accepting or denying orders, followed by lifecycle updates. The reference design below preserves those separate commitments and does not invent undocumented API deadlines. [S55]

The proposed order model

The cart is editable intent. Checkout creates a versioned order proposal with item selections, listing versions, prices, fees, delivery address, and substitution preferences. The accepted order is immutable except through explicit amendments.

Separate customer confirmation, payment authorization, merchant acceptance, preparation, courier assignment, pickup, delivery, and financial completion. A single paid flag cannot represent the fulfillment lifecycle. Some steps happen in parallel; others require a prior commitment.

A practical transaction boundary

The order service atomically records the order, commercial snapshot, idempotency result, and outbox event. External payment authorization and merchant communication happen as durable workflow steps. Each step has a stable reference and a deadline.

If payment authorization succeeds but the response is lost, reconcile it using the same reference. If the merchant rejects the order, cancel or reverse the appropriate payment obligation according to policy. Do not issue a second authorization because the first workflow attempt timed out.

Merchant acceptance and preparation

A merchant acknowledgment of a webhook is not acceptance of the order. The order remains pending until the business response is recorded. If no response arrives within the product's configured policy window, escalate or expire deliberately. Retrieve current state when notifications are missing.

Once preparation begins, cancellation has physical and economic consequences. The compensation policy may differ by stage and reason. Preserve the original order and append an adjustment; do not erase prepared work from history.

Cart validation under change

A listing may change between browsing and checkout. Validate current eligibility, required modifiers, price rules, and availability. Present material changes for confirmation. Do not silently replace an item or increase the accepted total because a background catalog update occurred.

For estimated-weight goods, record the allowed range and finalization rules explicitly. Final quantity evidence and customer consent determine the adjustment, not an arbitrary merchant payload.

Race testing

Test duplicate checkout, merchant acceptance after expiry, cancellation during preparation, courier assignment before merchant readiness, and delivery confirmation after a reported issue. Every race should resolve through a versioned state transition or exception process.

The order platform succeeds when all parties can understand what they have committed to and what remains uncertain.

33. Preparation time, courier dispatch, and batching

Delivery dispatch is not passenger dispatch with a restaurant as the pickup point. The goods may not be ready, couriers may need to park and walk, and batching creates dependencies across several customers.

Published evidence. Uber's 2018 Eats optimization account describes inferring stages such as driving, parking, walking, and waiting from trip signals. Those inferred stages help understand delays; they are not the same as authoritative merchant readiness or proof of handoff. [S16]

Align readiness and arrival

The reference planner predicts food-ready time and courier arrival separately. If the courier arrives too early, utilization suffers and waiting rises. If too late, food waits and customer experience worsens. The objective balances these costs with uncertainty rather than matching two point estimates exactly.

Use a preparation distribution conditioned on merchant, basket, time, queue, and available signals. A merchant's updated readiness estimate should be treated as evidence with source and confidence. Repeated optimistic updates may need calibration, but not punitive assumptions without review.

Courier plan insertion

A courier can have several pickup and drop-off tasks. Evaluate each proposed insertion for pickup-before-drop-off order, capacity, temperature or handling constraints, promised time windows, and existing-customer detour. A geographically close restaurant may still be a poor batch partner if preparation times differ sharply.

The plan is versioned. A solver proposes an insertion against version N; the authority commits only if N remains current and feasibility still holds. Concurrent order assignments cannot independently consume the same capacity.

Handoff evidence

Merchant-ready, courier-arrived, picked-up, and delivered are separate events. Use product-appropriate confirmation and evidence. An inferred departure from the restaurant is not sufficient proof that the correct order was collected.

If the courier reports an unavailable order, open an exception flow that can contact the merchant, update customer expectations, and adjust assignment. Repeated blind retries of the same pickup task waste time and may duplicate work.

Batching tradeoffs

Batching can improve utilization and reduce cost while increasing complexity and tail delay. Evaluate per-customer outcomes, not only average delivery time. A second order should not quietly violate the first customer's accepted service promise.

When one order is canceled, recompute the remaining plan and financial consequences. The cancellation may free capacity but does not erase already completed pickup or handling work. Keep each order's accounting and evidence distinct even when they share a courier journey.

Operational signals

Measure restaurant wait, food wait, courier idle time, batch detour, missed pickup windows, and handoff disputes. Diagnose which stage causes delay before changing the dispatch policy. A better driving ETA cannot fix a systematically underestimated kitchen queue.

34. Grocery, substitutions, weighted items, and stock uncertainty

Grocery fulfillment often discovers the real inventory only when a shopper reaches the shelf. The system must manage that uncertainty without silently changing what the customer agreed to purchase.

Published evidence. INCA's broader catalog model and Uber's 2026 Cart Assistant publication reflect a commerce surface beyond fixed restaurant menus. Cart Assistant is described as a staged, reviewable shopping flow; it is not evidence that an agent may make unrestricted purchases or substitutions. [S17][S20]

A proposed item lifecycle

An order line progresses through requested, located, unavailable, substitution_proposed, approved, picked, and finalized states as appropriate. Preserve the requested item separately from the fulfilled item. Record quantity, unit, price basis, and customer preference version.

Substitution policy can be no replacement, a customer-selected alternative, or a bounded proposal requiring approval. Define price and quantity limits. A similarity model can suggest alternatives, but it cannot override allergy-related preferences, restricted-item rules, or explicit customer rejection.

Inventory is evidence, not omniscience

A store feed may report stock that has already been sold or misplaced. Use timestamps and confidence. Do not overpromise exact availability unless the partner provides a reliable reservation mechanism and its contract supports that promise.

If reservations are supported, use a stable line-item reference and expiry. A retry must not reserve twice. Release reservations on cancellation, and reconcile ambiguous outcomes with the partner before creating replacement obligations.

Weighted goods

Store both estimated and final quantity with explicit units. Calculate the final amount using deterministic rounding rules and approved bounds. For example, a customer may authorize a weight range and maximum total; exceeding it requires a defined confirmation process.

Do not use floating-point arithmetic for final money amounts. Preserve the measurement evidence and pricing version. A correction creates a revised line and financial adjustment, not an unexplained overwrite.

Shopper workflow

The shopper app should work through a durable task list with item-level progress, barcode or product confirmation where supported, and clear exception actions. Offline scans can be buffered with sequence numbers, but final commercial changes require authoritative reconciliation.

Prevent two shoppers or devices from independently finalizing the same line. Task ownership and version checks must survive reconnects and reassignment. A shelf observation from a previous shopper can inform the next attempt without being treated as current guaranteed inventory.

Customer communication

Group related decisions to avoid a flood of notifications, but preserve deadlines and consent. If no response arrives, follow the accepted fallback policy. The system should clearly show requested items, replacements, unavailable items, and the final charge.

The engineering goal is controlled amendment under uncertainty. It is not to force every order to look complete by making hidden substitutions.

PART V / Delivery, commerce, and fulfillment

35. Uber Direct and merchant-facing delivery APIs

A merchant using a delivery API retains its own checkout and customer relationship while delegating physical delivery. The integration boundary must make responsibility and state visible on both sides.

Published evidence. Uber Direct provides delivery APIs and documents webhook verification using HMAC-SHA256 over the raw body. Webhook delivery behavior is contract-specific and bounded; integrations still need retrieval and reconciliation rather than assuming every notification arrives exactly once. [S54][S56]

A reference delivery lifecycle

The merchant requests a quote with pickup, drop-off, package characteristics, and required service options. After customer confirmation, it creates a delivery using a stable merchant reference. The provider returns a delivery ID and current state. Subsequent updates communicate courier assignment, pickup, progress, and terminal outcome.

A quote is not a delivery commitment. A successful create response is not proof of pickup. A delivered webhook is not automatically the merchant's business acceptance of every item. Define each boundary in the integration contract.

Idempotency and external references

Store merchant order ID, delivery creation key, provider delivery ID, and payload hash. If a create request times out, query or retry using the same business reference according to the API contract. Do not generate a new delivery for the same package merely because the response is missing.

Cancellation is also a workflow. The provider may have already assigned a courier or collected the package. The merchant must handle an outcome that requires return, support intervention, or a commercial adjustment rather than assuming cancellation always erases the task.

Webhook receiver design

Read and retain the exact request bytes long enough to verify the signature. Compare signatures in constant time. Validate content type, payload size, and event schema. Persist the event or enqueue it durably before returning success, then process asynchronously with event deduplication and aggregate-version checks.

Rotate secrets using the product's documented mechanism. Do not assume a timestamp or replay-protection header exists unless the contract provides one. Business deduplication and current-state checks remain necessary even after cryptographic verification.

Reconciliation and support

Run periodic checks for deliveries stuck in nonterminal states, missing expected transitions, or disagreeing with merchant state. Link support cases using both parties' references. A dashboard should show the last verified provider state and its age.

Use sandbox and certification scenarios for duplicate callbacks, invalid signatures, delayed pickup, address changes, failed delivery, and cancellation races. Test partner rate limits and downtime. The integration is complete only when it can recover from missed messages without sending a second courier for the same parcel.

36. Freight load marketplaces and carrier matching

Freight matching operates on a different time scale and constraint set from passenger rides. A load may involve equipment, weight, appointments, contractual terms, multiple facilities, and a carrier organization rather than one nearby individual.

Published evidence. Uber Freight's developer material describes controlled API onboarding, and its load-recommendation account discusses matching carriers with relevant loads. These sources support a partner-integrated marketplace model, not an assumption that every endpoint is anonymously available or that passenger dispatch algorithms transfer unchanged. [S57][S63]

The proposed load object

A load contains shipper reference, origin and destination facilities, pickup and delivery windows, equipment requirements, cargo attributes, service constraints, commercial terms, and version. Separate the shipper's transportation order from the carrier's accepted commitment.

A carrier profile includes verified capabilities, operating eligibility, equipment, preferences, and available capacity. A driver location can inform execution but does not establish that the carrier can legally or operationally accept a load.

Candidate generation

Filter by hard constraints before ranking: equipment, appointments, route feasibility, required qualifications, and contractual eligibility. Then estimate relevance using lane preferences, deadhead distance, follow-on opportunities, and historical behavior where appropriately governed.

A recommendation is not a tender acceptance. The carrier must accept the current version of the load and terms. If a shipper changes the appointment or cargo requirements, prior recommendations may no longer be valid.

Booking correctness

Use a durable booking command with a stable reference. Atomically ensure the load is still available, the version matches, and the carrier commitment is recorded. If multiple carriers accept concurrently, one authoritative transaction wins. The others receive a clear conflict rather than an ambiguous success.

A carrier organization may have several users and dispatch systems. Deduplicate across those channels. Do not assume that one browser session represents the carrier's entire decision process.

Operational exceptions

Carrier cancellation, facility delay, missing equipment, and appointment changes require explicit workflows. Some problems can be rematched automatically; others require operations staff. Preserve the original commitment and the reason for replacement so financial and service consequences can be reconciled.

A proposed load state machine includes available, tendered, booked, dispatched, at_pickup, loaded, in_transit, at_delivery, delivered, and closed, with exception states. Closing the load is not identical to receiving payment or approving every accessorial charge.

Evaluation

Measure coverage before pickup, carrier acceptance, deadhead, on-time performance, cancellations, manual intervention, and dispute rates. A recommendation model that maximizes clicks on loads can still worsen actual coverage. The marketplace objective is a feasible, executed transportation commitment, not merely digital engagement.

PART VI / Freight, obligations, risk, and support

37. Freight pricing, bidding, and network optimization

Freight pricing is a sequential decision under a deadline. The platform may have several days to cover a load, but the cost of remaining uncovered rises as pickup approaches.

Published evidence. Uber's 2021 freight-pricing article models carrier pricing with a controlled Markov decision process and a finite pickup horizon. It explicitly simplifies to a single-load case for part of the explanation, setting aside competition among loads and limited operations capacity. Those limitations matter. [S64]

A reference finite-horizon model

Let state include load attributes, remaining lead time, market conditions, current offer, and uncertainty. An action changes the offered carrier price within policy bounds. A transition represents booking, continued availability, or cancellation. The terminal cost reflects manual coverage, rescheduling, service penalties, and relationship impact as defined by the business.

The Bellman idea is to choose today's offer by comparing its expected immediate cost with the future value of waiting. A higher offer may improve booking probability but reduce margin. Waiting may preserve margin or create an expensive last-minute problem.

Estimation is the difficult part

Historical loads disappear from the available population once booked. This creates censoring and selection bias. Short-lead-time observations are not a random sample of all loads; they may disproportionately contain difficult or underpriced shipments.

Estimate booking response with careful time-at-risk modeling and validation. A model should respect plausible monotonic behavior where justified: all else equal, a more attractive carrier offer should not arbitrarily reduce predicted booking probability. Correlation alone does not identify price elasticity.

Network effects

Single-load optimization ignores shared carrier capacity. Several similar loads can compete for the same carriers, while a sequence of loads may form an attractive tour. A reference network optimizer can account for lane balance, repositioning, and appointment compatibility, but must remain bounded in complexity and explainable to operations.

Manual operations capacity is also a resource. A policy that leaves many loads uncovered until the final hour may overwhelm staff even if its average modeled cost looks low. Include operational constraints and stress scenarios.

Bids and contracts

A carrier bid is a proposal with validity and terms. Acceptance creates a versioned commitment. Contract rates and spot offers may follow different workflows. Preserve provenance so later invoicing can identify which agreement governs the movement.

Do not let a late price update change a carrier's already accepted compensation. Amendments require explicit rules and agreement. Pricing services may recommend values, but the authoritative booking record fixes the accepted commercial terms.

Rollout

Begin with offline replay, then shadow recommendations, then bounded markets and human review. Monitor coverage, tail cost, service quality, carrier behavior, and manual workload. A statistically strong price model can still produce an operationally unstable policy if the surrounding workflow is ignored.

38. Freight execution, tracking, documents, and accessorials

A booked load becomes a physical operation involving facilities, equipment, appointments, and documents. The execution system must combine telemetry with human and partner evidence without treating every signal as equally authoritative.

Published evidence. Uber Freight's transportation-management onboarding material describes a staged integration process, including testing before production access. Its developer surface includes shipment and tracking workflows. The execution model here is a proposed design for those operational needs. [S57][S58]

Milestones and observations

Separate planned appointments, estimated arrival, geofence arrival, checked-in confirmation, loading completion, departure, and proof of delivery. A truck entering a large facility's geofence does not prove that loading began. Store the source and confidence of each milestone.

Partner updates can arrive late or out of order. Apply them through a versioned event model, preserving observed time and received time. A late pickup event can enrich history without moving a delivered load backward to an earlier state.

Documents as governed evidence

Bills of lading, proof of delivery, photographs, and invoices need secure upload, malware scanning, content-type validation, retention rules, and access controls. Store immutable originals and derived extraction results separately. OCR or an AI extraction is not the original document and may be wrong.

Link every document to the load, party, document type, and version. Detect duplicates by content hash where appropriate. Human review should be able to correct extracted fields without altering the original evidence.

Accessorial charges

Detention, additional stops, and other extra charges require policy-specific evidence and approval. A timer alone should not create an unquestionable charge. The reference workflow records the request, supporting milestones, contract rule, reviewer, decision, and financial adjustment.

Disputed charges remain separate from the delivered state. A load can be physically complete while commercial closure is pending. Conflating these states causes misleading dashboards and premature payouts or collections.

Integration reliability

Use stable references across shipper, carrier, and transportation-management systems. A webhook or EDI-like message can be duplicated, delayed, or corrected. Maintain an inbox, deduplicate by source identity, and reconcile against periodic snapshots or authoritative queries.

A partner outage should queue updates without allowing unlimited backlog growth. Track oldest unprocessed milestone and provide an operations view for urgent loads. Recovery order should prioritize time-sensitive execution over historical reporting.

Closing the loop

Compare planned and actual milestones, document completeness, approved charges, and invoice state. Reconciliation should explain differences rather than force all systems to the same status string. The operational record is complete when the physical journey, evidence, and commercial obligations can be traced coherently.

39. Entity fares, Gulfstream, and the money core

A mobility or delivery platform creates many economic relationships: a customer owes a charge, a driver earns compensation, a merchant is owed proceeds, and the platform may fund an incentive or adjustment. These relationships should be modeled explicitly.

Published evidence. Uber's August 2026 payments-platform account describes Gulfstream, entity fares, immutable money orders, ledger changes, and asynchronous processing, with money-order and balance stores serving distinct roles. The reference ledger below is an original teaching design, not a reproduction of Uber's private accounting schema. [S21]

Separate commercial calculation from posting

A fare calculation establishes what each party owes or is owed under a policy version. A posting command translates approved obligations into balanced accounting entries. Payment collection and payout execute external movement. These are separate state machines.

For an illustrative tax-free ride, a customer charge of 28 units might establish 21 units payable to a driver and 7 units of platform revenue. This simplified example is not an actual Uber fare split and excludes taxes, fees, incentives, and jurisdiction-specific accounting treatment. Real designs require finance-approved accounts and recognition policies.

A reference journal

Store an immutable journal transaction with business event ID, currency, posting rule version, and entries. Debits and credits must balance within each currency. Corrections use reversing or adjusting entries linked to the original transaction. Never edit an old amount merely to make a dashboard match a new expectation.

The posting service deduplicates on the business event and posting purpose. A trip-completed event replay cannot create a second driver payable. A later approved adjustment has a new explicit adjustment identity and its own entries.

Balance projections and authority

A balance is a projection of postings, potentially with reservations or pending obligations. If a balance is used to authorize spending or payout, its consistency requirements are stronger than those of a reporting dashboard. Update the authoritative balance and journal atomically, or use a rigorously defined equivalent protocol.

Do not treat a Kafka offset as proof that external money moved. The event log records platform processing; the bank or payment provider has its own outcome evidence.

Money and execution can disagree temporarily

A trip may complete while collection is pending. A delivery may be refunded after the courier has earned compensation. These are valid business situations, not reasons to reverse the physical state. Model who bears the obligation and how it is resolved.

Currency conversion requires explicit rates, timestamps, rounding, and accounts. Do not balance different currencies by adding their integer amounts together. Maintain per-currency invariants and record conversion obligations separately.

Controls

Run trial-balance checks, duplicate-posting detection, orphan-obligation reports, and event-to-journal reconciliation. Restrict posting-rule changes and require review. Financial correctness is an independently tested subsystem, not a side effect of successfully completing a trip API.

40. Collection, disbursement, reconciliation, and uncertainty

External payment systems can complete an operation even when the platform loses the response. The safe design preserves uncertainty and resolves it using stable references and independent evidence.

Published evidence. Gulfstream's published account distinguishes durable money-order processing and balances from the broader movement of funds. That separation motivates the reference workflows here; provider-specific contracts and local financial obligations require separate validation. [S21]

A reference collection workflow

Create an internal payment intent linked to an approved obligation. Submit authorization or collection through a provider adapter using a stable external key. Record request identity, provider reference, state, and last verified outcome. A timeout becomes unknown or pending, not automatically failed.

Before creating a replacement attempt, query the provider or follow its documented idempotent retry contract. A browser error is not evidence that a charge did not occur. Customer-visible status should explain pending confirmation without inviting duplicate payment.

Disbursement

Driver, courier, merchant, and carrier payouts may have different schedules and eligibility rules. Determine available payable balance, reserve the intended amount, and create a payout command atomically. Submit it externally with a stable reference. Finalize or release the reservation only after a verified terminal outcome.

A bank rejection and a lost response are different. Releasing funds after an ambiguous response can allow the same obligation to be paid twice. Escalate unresolved cases with evidence rather than repeatedly trying new references.

Reconciliation layers

Compare business events to internal obligations, obligations to journal entries, journal entries to provider transactions, and provider transactions to bank settlement. Each comparison answers a different question. A balanced ledger can still contain an incorrect duplicated business event; a provider success can still be absent from the bank statement until settlement.

Use matching keys, amounts, currencies, dates, and controlled tolerances. Unmatched items enter a case queue with ownership and aging. Do not automatically force near matches that could conceal a real discrepancy.

Refunds and adjustments

A refund command references the original collection and approved amount. Concurrent partial refunds must not exceed the refundable amount. Reserve the requested refund amount under a serialized invariant, then execute externally and reconcile uncertainty.

A customer refund does not automatically cancel a participant's earned compensation. The commercial policy determines who funds the adjustment. Preserve that reasoning in the journal and support case.

Operational controls

Monitor unknown outcomes, aged reservations, reconciliation breaks, duplicate references, and negative unexpected balances. Define manual actions as controlled commands with dual review where appropriate, not direct SQL edits.

The system should be able to explain every unit of money from the originating ride, order, or load to its final external evidence. That traceability is more important than making every asynchronous process appear instantaneous.

41. Fraud, abuse, risk challenges, and appeals

A large marketplace must protect accounts, promotions, payments, and physical operations from abuse. Risk controls should be proportionate, privacy-aware, and designed to recover from false positives.

Published evidence. Uber's 2024 risk-challenges account describes combining rules and learned risk signals with adaptive challenges. The reference architecture here focuses on decision integrity and operational safeguards, not on revealing or bypassing detection thresholds. [S22]

A layered decision service

Collect authorized signals with clear purpose and retention. Compute features, evaluate deterministic rules and models, then select an allowed action: permit, limit, request verification, hold for review, or deny. The product policy owns the permitted actions; the model provides evidence or a score.

Keep feature freshness, model version, rule version, and explanation codes with the decision. Missing data should be an explicit condition, not silently converted into a benign or suspicious value. Use fallback policies when a dependency fails.

Protect the challenge flow

A challenge is itself a state machine with identity, expiry, attempt limits, and completion evidence. Bind it to the intended operation so success cannot be reused for unrelated actions. Do not expose sensitive risk reasoning or internal thresholds in client responses.

Repeated challenges can burden legitimate users. Measure completion, abandonment, false-positive review outcomes, and accessibility impact. Provide a clear recovery path when automated verification is unsuitable.

Physical marketplace abuse

Trip and delivery systems may receive suspicious location, execution, or incentive signals. Treat anomaly detection as evidence, not automatic proof of intent. Device faults, map errors, network delays, and operational exceptions can produce similar patterns.

High-impact actions affecting access or earnings need appropriate review and appeal mechanisms. Preserve the relevant evidence and uncertainty so a reviewer can make an informed decision rather than merely repeat the model's output.

Reliability and separation

Risk checks on the booking path require strict latency budgets. Some decisions can be deferred; others cannot. Document the behavior for unavailable risk services instead of making every call indiscriminately fail-open or fail-closed.

Separate model training, feature computation, online decisions, and case management. A data-pipeline failure should not silently change every user's risk profile. Rollouts need cohort limits and rapid rollback.

Evaluation

Measure prevented harm, false positives, user friction, review workload, and delayed outcomes. Fraud labels are often incomplete and arrive late. Avoid declaring success solely from immediate rejection rates or offline classification accuracy.

The objective is a trustworthy marketplace with accountable decisions. A system that blocks more activity is not necessarily safer or more accurate.

42. Support platforms, cases, and controlled corrections

Support is part of the product's recovery architecture. When automated workflows cannot resolve uncertainty, people need tools that reveal evidence and permit safe, bounded action.

Published evidence. Uber's public safety materials describe support processes alongside product features. The case-management and correction mechanisms below are a reference design; no private support tooling or internal response-time contract is asserted. [S67]

A case is not a database edit

The proposed case record links participant, trip or order, issue category, evidence, communication history, assigned team, and decision state. Corrections are typed commands: approve adjustment, update contact preference, initiate investigation, or reconcile an external outcome. Each command has permissions, preconditions, and audit requirements.

Do not give general support tools unrestricted mutation access to trip, ledger, or eligibility tables. Direct edits bypass invariants and make later reconciliation impossible. A high-privilege emergency path should be rare, time-limited, and independently reviewed.

Assemble a coherent timeline

A support view combines authoritative transitions, relevant observations, commercial versions, and external references. Label each item's source and timestamp type. A client-observed event and a database commit should not appear as equivalent facts merely because both have a time field.

Redact unnecessary personal information by role and case purpose. Exact location, recordings, documents, and payment details should require stronger access than a generic order summary. Log access as well as changes.

Resolve without rewriting history

A customer reports a wrong charge after a completed ride. The operator reviews the accepted quote, execution evidence, and policy. An approved correction creates an adjustment linked to the original obligation. The trip remains completed and the original record remains visible to authorized auditors.

A delivery status is disputed. The operator can initiate investigation or a defined correction workflow, but cannot simply set delivered because a dashboard needs closure. Corrections need reason, evidence, reviewer, and version.

Automation and AI assistance

An assistant can summarize a case, retrieve policy, or draft a response. Retrieved messages and attachments are untrusted content. The assistant should not execute refunds, change eligibility, or expose sensitive information without a constrained tool contract and appropriate approval.

Evaluate summaries for omitted evidence and incorrect certainty. Human review is especially important when decisions affect safety, access, or significant money.

Operational measurement

Track time to first meaningful action, resolution quality, repeat contacts, correction reversals, and unauthorized-access attempts. A low average handling time can hide poor resolutions. Support tooling is successful when it helps people make accurate, explainable decisions while preserving system invariants.

43. Storage portfolio: choose by invariant and access pattern

A global marketplace needs several storage behaviors. Trying to make one database serve live location, transactional assignment, full-text search, model features, and historical analytics usually creates poor compromises.

Published evidence. Uber's public accounts describe Docstore, Spanner-backed fulfillment, Hudi, Pinot, and other specialized systems in distinct contexts. These publications support a heterogeneous portfolio, not a claim that every named system stores every kind of Uber data. [S04][S02][S34][S36]

The reference storage map

Use a transactional store for assignments, plans, accepted commercial terms, and idempotency records. Use a keyed low-latency view for recent location and supply discovery. Use a search index for catalog retrieval. Use an event log for distribution and replay. Use object storage and a lakehouse for governed history, and analytical engines for aggregation.

The important distinction is authority. A search result can be stale if checkout revalidates it. A location view can be approximate if acceptance revalidates supply. A ledger or assignment authority cannot simply choose the newest-looking cached value.

Consistency is scoped

Ask whether a guarantee covers one key, one partition, one table, or several shards. A database advertised as strongly consistent may still require additional design for a cross-partition invariant. A replica's read mode may differ from its write semantics.

Document which APIs require current authority and which permit bounded staleness. Carry a minimum required version after a write so subsequent reads can avoid moving backward. A generic cache abstraction should not hide those choices.

Partitioning

Choose keys around access and transaction patterns. Driver-plan authority often benefits from stable actor-based partitioning; location discovery benefits from geography. They need not use the same key. Avoid forcing physical movement to trigger durable ownership movement.

Large merchants, cities, and campaigns can become hot partitions. Introduce virtual shards and explicit routing metadata where needed. A rebalancing operation must preserve ownership and consistency, not just copy rows and update a hash function.

Operational cost

Every additional storage system adds backup, schema, observability, security, and on-call requirements. Start with fewer systems and introduce specialization when measurements justify it. An early company can use one transactional database and a small number of projections without imitating global-scale infrastructure.

The right portfolio makes guarantees visible. The wrong portfolio hides them behind a common API and leaves application teams to discover the differences during incidents.

PART VII / Transactional data and event infrastructure

44. Docstore, sharding, replication, and SQL access

A distributed database platform can reduce application burden, but its guarantees must be understood at the correct scope. Partition-level consistency is not automatically a global transaction across every entity.

Published evidence. Uber's 2021 Docstore account describes a MySQL-based storage layer, Raft replication, query and control-plane components, and strict serializability at the partition level. The partition scope is important; the article should not be read as promising arbitrary global transactions everywhere. [S04]

A reference layered design

A query layer accepts SQL-like requests, authenticates the caller, resolves schema and shard routing, and sends work to storage partitions. A control plane manages placement, schema evolution, membership, and rebalancing. Storage replicas maintain the data and replicated log.

Separating query from storage allows independent scaling, but creates routing and metadata-coherence problems. A query node using stale shard ownership must receive a redirect or retryable error that preserves command identity. It must not write to an old owner after migration.

Replication and leadership

A replicated partition elects or designates a leader according to its consensus protocol. Writes become durable under the configured quorum and failure assumptions. A follower may serve reads only under the consistency mode the API promises.

Replication protects against certain failures; it does not replace backups or prevent an authorized bad write from being replicated. Logical corruption and accidental deletion can spread perfectly through a healthy replication system.

Cross-partition operations

If an assignment touches a request and driver plan in different partitions, the application needs a supported cross-partition transaction or a redesigned ownership boundary. A best-effort sequence of writes is not equivalent to atomicity. Do not infer unsupported guarantees from the presence of SQL syntax.

Where workflows genuinely span independent domains, use explicit reservations and compensation. Keep the invariant that must be immediate within a supported transaction boundary. This is a schema and ownership decision, not merely a database tuning parameter.

Rebalancing and schema changes

Move a partition through copy, catch-up, validation, ownership switch, and cleanup. Fence old writers at the transition. Retain enough metadata to diagnose requests that crossed the handoff. Verify row counts, checksums, and recent-write coverage where appropriate.

Schema evolution should support rolling application versions. Add fields with safe defaults, backfill in bounded batches, validate, then enforce stronger constraints. A large backfill can compete with serving traffic and replication; schedule and throttle it as production work.

What to measure

Track per-partition latency, leader changes, replication lag, hot keys, query fan-out, transaction aborts, and rebalancing progress. Global database health can look normal while one hot participant or merchant experiences severe contention. The operational unit of analysis should match the unit of consistency and load.

PART VII / Transactional data and event infrastructure

45. Spanner-style fulfillment transactions

A database that supports cross-row and cross-shard transactions can simplify fulfillment correctness, but only if the application uses those transactions carefully and keeps external effects outside retryable callbacks.

Published evidence. Uber's 2021 fulfillment publication identifies Google Cloud Spanner, including a North America multi-region configuration, as its primary fulfillment storage engine at that time. Google's documentation describes external consistency under serializable isolation. This is not a claim that all Uber data or all current deployments use that configuration. [S02][S75]

The reference acceptance transaction

Read the offer, request, driver plan, and authority epoch. Verify their expected versions and eligibility. Write the assignment and revised plan, advance the request state, and append an outbox record. Return only after commit. This makes the customer-visible commitment correspond to a durable database fact.

The transaction callback must be safe to rerun. Generate stable command and effect identifiers outside it or deterministically from the command. Do not send a push message, charge a card, or call a partner from inside the callback; an abort followed by retry could repeat the external effect.

Latency and contention

Distributed transactions involve communication and coordination. Minimize the read/write set and avoid unnecessary secondary-index work. Precompute expensive route and model results outside the transaction, then validate the versions that make those results applicable.

A hot driver plan can experience repeated conflicts when many offers compete. Control concurrency upstream rather than relying on infinite transaction retries. Apply bounded backoff within the request deadline and return a meaningful conflict when the proposal is no longer valid.

Read semantics

Operational screens that follow a successful write should request a sufficiently current view. Historical analytics and some discovery queries can use stale reads for efficiency. Make this choice explicit in the API rather than assuming all reads have identical semantics.

A multi-entity snapshot can prevent impossible UI combinations, such as a request showing assigned while the displayed plan still lacks that assignment. Versioned client reconciliation is still necessary because notifications can arrive out of order.

Failure interpretation

An aborted transaction is known not to have committed and can be retried as a whole. A lost commit response is different: the client may need to resolve the command using its idempotency record. A generic timeout handler should not treat both cases as the same failure.

Database durability does not guarantee that the driver saw the assignment, that the car moved, or that payment settled. Those are separate facts with separate evidence. The value of strong transactions is that the platform's own commitments remain coherent while those external processes unfold.

Starting smaller

A new company can implement the same logical invariant on a well-operated relational database before needing a globally distributed system. Choose based on measured latency, failure domains, transaction scope, operational skill, and cost. The lesson is to preserve the invariant, not to purchase a particular product prematurely.

46. CacheFront-style acceleration and freshness contracts

Caching can make a read-heavy platform economical, but every cache introduces a question: how old may this answer be, and what happens after a write?

Published evidence. Uber's 2025 CacheFront account describes caching in the Docstore query path, Redis-based storage, and change-driven invalidation mechanisms. It also discusses cases where invalidation failure does not fail an already committed write. A cache hit therefore must not be interpreted as an unconditional linearizability guarantee. [S39]

A reference cache contract

Classify reads into immutable, bounded-stale, read-your-writes, and authoritative. Immutable catalog versions are easy to cache. Recent location may tolerate a small age budget. A user's immediate post-write view may require a minimum version. Assignment acceptance must read authority, not a convenient stale projection.

Include schema version, entity version, and expiry in cached values. Cache keys need all relevant policy and authorization dimensions; otherwise, one user's permitted view can leak into another's response.

Invalidation strategies

Write-through or synchronous invalidation reduces some stale windows but adds dependencies to writes. Asynchronous invalidation improves write isolation but permits temporary staleness. Time-to-live is a backstop, not a complete correctness protocol.

A delayed invalidation can race with a cache fill and delete a newer value, or an old read can repopulate stale data after invalidation. Version-aware writes and tombstones can prevent moving backward. The exact protocol should be tested under reordered events.

Session consistency

After a client commits version 12, subsequent reads can carry min_version=12. A cache with version 11 is bypassed or refreshed. This provides a practical read-your-writes contract without forcing every unrelated read to use the strongest mode.

Do not confuse a version number with global time. Versions are scoped to an aggregate or explicitly defined snapshot. Comparing unrelated aggregate versions is meaningless.

Stampedes and hot keys

Use request coalescing, bounded refresh concurrency, jittered expiry, and admission limits. A popular merchant or event can create a hot key whose expiry floods the database. Negative caching can help repeated misses, but must expire appropriately when a new entity appears.

Cache failure should degrade toward controlled database load, not unlimited direct reads. Reserve capacity and shed optional queries. A cache is a performance layer; treating it as an infinite protection barrier makes outages worse.

Verification

Test stale fills after writes, reordered invalidations, key-version collisions, authorization changes, cache outages, and schema rollout. Measure hit rate together with stale-read age and correctness violations. A very high hit rate is not a success if users repeatedly see old assignments or prices.

PART VII / Transactional data and event infrastructure

47. Kafka, uForwarder, outboxes, and consumer correctness

An event log decouples producers and consumers, but it does not make every downstream effect exactly once. The application must define what duplication means and where deduplication is durable.

Published evidence. Uber's February 2026 uForwarder account describes pushing Kafka messages to gRPC consumers and handling consumption concerns in shared infrastructure. This changes the integration model, not the need for application-level idempotency and outcome reconciliation. [S33]

The transactional outbox

Write the business state and an outbox record in the same database transaction. A relay publishes the record to the log and marks progress. If the relay crashes after publication but before marking completion, it may publish again. Consumers must expect that duplicate.

Use stable event IDs and aggregate versions. Partition by the ordering key that matters, such as assignment or order ID. Global ordering is expensive and usually unnecessary. Cross-aggregate workflows should use explicit dependencies rather than assuming topic order defines every business relationship.

Consumer inbox and side effects

A database-writing consumer can atomically record the event ID in an inbox and apply its local effect. On a duplicate, it returns the stored outcome. The inbox retention must cover the supported replay window.

For an external effect, such as a notification or partner request, use a stable effect key and the receiver's idempotency contract. If no such contract exists, maintain uncertainty and reconcile. Kafka delivery guarantees cannot prevent an external system from executing twice after an ambiguous response.

Ordering and version gaps

If event version 15 arrives before 14, the consumer can buffer briefly, fetch a snapshot, or process according to a documented commutative rule. It must not blindly overwrite state with whichever payload arrives last.

A compacted topic retains a latest keyed value, not every historical transition. Choose retention and compaction based on the consumer's recovery needs. Audit history may require a separate immutable stream or store.

Backpressure and poison messages

Bound retries, apply exponential backoff with jitter, and separate transient failures from invalid payloads. A poison message should not permanently block unrelated keys. A dead-letter queue needs ownership, alerts, replay controls, and a way to repair the underlying problem.

Track consumer lag in both offsets and elapsed event age. A small offset lag can still be operationally serious if the topic is quiet and one critical event is stuck. Prioritize time-sensitive work during recovery.

Schema evolution

Use explicit event versions and compatibility tests. Consumers should tolerate added fields but reject semantic changes they cannot interpret. Replaying old events through new code requires version-aware behavior. A replay must never accidentally reissue physical or financial actions that already happened.

PART VII / Transactional data and event infrastructure

48. Cadence and durable workflow orchestration

A durable workflow coordinates steps that span time, failures, and independent services. It is especially useful when a business process waits for a participant, timer, or external system.

Published evidence. Cadence documents durable workflows and activities. Its January 2026 review discusses multi-region capabilities, including active-active domain work. That should not be misread as permission for two regions to execute conflicting writers for the same workflow simultaneously. [S41][S42]

Workflow state and replay

The reference workflow records a history of decisions and activity outcomes. After a worker failure, the engine reconstructs state from that history. Workflow code must behave deterministically under replay; direct wall-clock reads, random choices, or uncontrolled network calls belong behind supported workflow primitives or activities.

An activity performs an external action such as contacting a merchant or querying a payment provider. Activities can be retried. Their effect identity must remain stable across attempts. A successful external action followed by a lost activity acknowledgment is a normal failure case to design for.

Time and signals

Use durable timers for order acceptance deadlines, reservation preparation, and escalation. A timer firing means it is time to re-evaluate state, not that the workflow may ignore a concurrently received acceptance.

Signals carry external observations or commands into the workflow. Deduplicate them and validate their relationship to the current entity version. A late signal may be recorded for history without changing a terminal state.

Compensation

Compensation is a new business action, not a time machine. If a merchant has prepared food, canceling the order may require payment or support handling. If a courier has collected a package, compensation may mean return delivery. Define those actions explicitly.

Do not put every microsecond-scale database transaction into a workflow engine. Use transactions for immediate invariants and workflows for long-lived coordination. The two mechanisms complement each other.

Versioning and operations

Long-running workflows can outlive several deployments. Use supported versioning patterns so old histories replay correctly. Test upgrade and rollback against recorded histories. A code change that reorders activity calls can break deterministic replay if not managed deliberately.

Monitor stuck workflows, activity age, timer backlog, retries, and unresolved external outcomes. Operators need safe actions such as retry, cancel, or escalate, each with preconditions and audit trails.

Regional recovery

Moving workflow execution requires a clear ownership protocol and consistent history. The destination must know which activities completed and which remain uncertain. Replaying history must not resend every completed action. Active-active infrastructure is useful only when per-workflow authority remains coherent.

PART VII / Transactional data and event infrastructure

49. CDC, schemas, retention, and data contracts

Change data capture connects transactional systems to caches, analytics, and search. It is powerful precisely because many consumers depend on it; a poorly governed schema change can spread errors widely.

Published evidence. Uber's IngestionNext account describes a streaming path using Kafka, Flink, and Hudi, with recovery coordination between streaming checkpoints and table commits. This is evidence of explicit consistency engineering across the ingestion boundary, not a claim that CDC is automatically lossless under every configuration. [S35]

A reference data contract

Every published dataset or event declares owner, schema, key, ordering scope, freshness objective, retention, privacy classification, and supported uses. Consumers should know whether a field is authoritative, inferred, nullable, or eventually corrected.

CDC rows are not always ideal business events. A database update may expose implementation detail without explaining the business reason. Use domain events for stable semantic contracts and CDC for replication or analytical needs where row-level change is appropriate.

Snapshots and incremental changes

Bootstrapping a consumer requires a consistent snapshot and a change position that avoids gaps or double application. Copying rows while independently starting a stream can miss updates or apply them twice unless the protocol defines the boundary.

Carry source versions and tombstones. A deleted row must not reappear after a late snapshot chunk or a restored consumer. Reconciliation checks should compare counts, key coverage, and selected values at consistent positions.

Schema evolution

Additive fields are easier than renamed meanings. A field called completed_at cannot quietly change from driver-declared completion to server-verified completion. Introduce a new field or version and document the distinction.

Backfills should be distinguishable from new business events. A historical correction may need to update analytics without sending a new customer notification or reissuing payment. Include processing purpose and effect restrictions in replay tooling.

Retention and deletion

Retention is dataset- and jurisdiction-dependent. Define legal holds, operational needs, and deletion propagation rather than choosing one universal duration. Derived indexes, features, exports, and backups need a documented treatment.

A deletion event should be versioned and durable enough to survive restore. If a backup reintroduces a previously deleted record, reapply the deletion ledger before broad access resumes. Privacy correctness is part of recovery correctness.

Observability

Monitor source-to-sink lag, dropped or quarantined records, schema incompatibility, checkpoint progress, and tombstone propagation. A pipeline can be running while silently omitting a partition. Data consumers need freshness and completeness indicators, not just a green job status.

The contract is complete when a downstream team can explain what the data means, how current it is, and how it will change without reading the producer's private implementation.

PART VIII / Analytics, machine learning, and experiments

50. Flink, event time, and streaming ingestion

Streaming systems must process events that arrive late, out of order, or more than once. The challenge is not simply reading a queue quickly; it is defining what a result means before all possible evidence has arrived.

Published evidence. Uber's IngestionNext publication describes a move toward streaming ingestion using Kafka, Flink, and Hudi, with checkpoint and table-commit coordination. The reference examples below explain the correctness questions such a pipeline must answer. [S35]

Event time versus processing time

A trip observation may occur at 10:00 but arrive at 10:03. Processing-time aggregation counts it when received; event-time aggregation associates it with the physical event's time. Both can be useful, but they answer different questions.

A watermark expresses the pipeline's estimate of event-time progress. It is not proof that no older event will ever arrive. Define allowed lateness, correction behavior, and a side path for very late events. A delayed device upload should not silently disappear from all reports.

Stateful joins

Joining a prediction to a later impression or a trip to a payment outcome requires keyed state and a time window. Choose the window based on the business delay distribution and memory budget. Expiring state too early loses valid matches; retaining it forever is not scalable.

Use stable event identities and aggregate versions. Deduplicate before counting where the metric requires it. A replayed completion event should not increase completed-trip counts twice.

Checkpoints and sinks

A checkpoint captures recoverable operator state and input positions. End-to-end consistency also depends on the sink. If output was committed before a crash but the checkpoint was not finalized, recovery must avoid duplicating the visible result.

Use a sink protocol that coordinates commit identity with checkpoints, or make writes idempotent by deterministic keys and versions. The phrase exactly once must identify its boundary. It does not automatically include arbitrary HTTP calls or every downstream consumer.

Backpressure and state growth

A slow sink can propagate backpressure through the pipeline. Measure per-operator throughput, busy time, state size, checkpoint duration, and skew. Increasing parallelism may not help a single hot key or a bottlenecked external service.

Partition large keys carefully and use aggregation strategies that preserve semantics. Splitting one key's state changes how ordering and final combination work; it is not a free performance optimization.

Operational use

For dispatch-support aggregates, publish freshness and completeness with the value. A supply-demand estimate based on half the partitions should not look equivalent to a complete current snapshot. Use fallbacks when lag exceeds the decision's budget.

Test restart from checkpoint, replay after sink failure, late corrections, schema changes, and retention expiry. The pipeline is correct when the same accepted input history produces the intended result under failures, not merely when it runs without exceptions.

51. Hudi and the lakehouse lifecycle

Historical data is not always append-only. Late trip events, corrected order details, privacy deletions, and updated business facts require a table format that can manage changes without repeatedly rebuilding entire datasets.

Published evidence. Uber's January 2026 Hudi account describes large-scale lakehouse operation, including indexing and metadata concerns. Its published volume metrics describe that data platform, not ride-request throughput. The storage layout and operational choices below are a reference explanation. [S34]

Records, files, and table versions

A lakehouse table groups records into files while maintaining metadata about commits and record placement. A record key identifies logical identity; a partitioning scheme supports pruning. A commit timeline determines which changes are visible in a table snapshot.

For mutable trip summaries, use a stable business key and a version or ordering field that reflects the chosen update semantics. A late stale record must not overwrite a newer authoritative correction simply because it arrived later.

Copy-on-write and merge-on-read tradeoffs

A copy-on-write style rewrites affected base files to produce read-friendly snapshots. A merge-on-read style can append changes to logs and merge them during reads or compaction. The latter can reduce immediate write cost but moves work to readers and background maintenance.

Compaction consumes CPU, I/O, and cluster capacity. Schedule it with serving and ingestion objectives in mind. An unbounded compaction backlog can make queries increasingly expensive even while ingestion appears healthy.

Indexes and metadata

Record lookup is needed to route updates to the right file group. Metadata tables and indexes can avoid repeatedly listing large object-store directories or scanning unrelated files. Their own consistency and recovery must be part of the design.

Partition by stable analytical access patterns, not a field that changes frequently without a migration plan. Small files increase metadata overhead and scan inefficiency; overly large files can amplify rewrite cost. Tune using measured workload distributions.

Incremental consumption

Downstream jobs can consume changes between commits rather than reread the entire table. Preserve delete and correction semantics. A consumer that only appends incremental rows may double-count updated records or retain deleted data indefinitely.

Track source offsets, table commit identity, schema version, and data-quality results. Recovery should know whether a commit is complete, pending, or rolled back. A directory of files alone is not an adequate transactional contract.

Governance and reproducibility

A model-training run should reference a reproducible table snapshot and transformation version. Privacy deletion and legal holds may constrain which snapshots remain accessible. Define those policies explicitly rather than assuming immutable history can be retained forever.

The lakehouse is a governed record of evolving facts. Its value comes from making updates, lineage, and visibility manageable at scale, not from storing the largest possible collection of files.

PART VIII / Analytics, machine learning, and experiments

52. Pinot, Presto, and operational analytics

Operational analytics needs fast answers over large histories, while transactional services need precise updates to a small number of authoritative entities. These are different workloads and should not be confused.

Published evidence. Uber has published accounts of operating Pinot, using Pinot for inventory and catalog analytics, and accelerating Presto workloads. These sources describe analytical systems in specific contexts, not alternatives to the assignment or financial authority. [S36][S37][S38]

A reference analytical path

Domain events or CDC flow through validation into real-time and historical analytical tables. Dashboards query aggregates by market, product, merchant, time, and outcome. The response includes data freshness and known completeness issues.

A support dashboard can use analytics to find suspicious cases, but a correction command must re-read current authority. An aggregate showing available balance or driver availability may be too stale to authorize action.

Why columnar segments help

A columnar analytical segment stores values by column rather than as a row-oriented transactional record. Forward indexes, dictionaries where used, and optional inverted or other indexes accelerate filtering and aggregation. Different indexes trade storage and ingestion cost for query speed.

For a query such as completed deliveries by merchant and hour, the engine can read relevant columns and prune segments. It need not reconstruct every full order. However, index choices should follow real query patterns; adding every index increases build, memory, and maintenance cost.

Real-time and historical boundaries

A streaming table handles recent data while immutable historical segments support efficient scans. Define how late events, updates, and deletions are represented. The boundary between real-time and historical data must avoid both gaps and double counting.

A dashboard comparing today's live data with yesterday's finalized data should disclose the difference in completeness. Otherwise, ingestion lag can look like a sudden business decline.

Query isolation

Interactive dashboards, ad hoc investigation, and large analytical joins need different resource budgets. Protect latency-sensitive queries from exploratory scans. Apply admission control, query limits, tenant quotas, and cancellation.

Presto-like distributed SQL is useful for broader joins and exploration, but expensive cross-source queries can create unpredictable load. Materialize repeated computations where justified and document their refresh interval.

Data correctness

Check row counts, duplicate business keys, aggregate reconciliation, and schema drift. A fast query over incorrect data is not an operational success. Alert on freshness and completeness alongside query latency.

Analytics should help humans and models understand the platform. It should not quietly become the source of binding decisions merely because its SQL interface is convenient.

PART VIII / Analytics, machine learning, and experiments

53. Michelangelo, features, and training-serving consistency

A machine-learning platform is more than a model server. It must connect data, features, training, evaluation, deployment, monitoring, and ownership so that predictions remain reproducible and useful.

Published evidence. Michelangelo's 2017 and 2024 accounts describe the evolution of Uber's ML platform. A September 2026 publication describes logging selected features actually used at inference and joining them with impressions to reduce training-serving mismatch. This is a specific feature-consistency approach, not a guarantee that every model uses identical data paths. [S46][S47][S48]

The feature contract

A reference feature definition includes name, type, unit, entity key, event-time semantics, default behavior, freshness, owner, privacy classification, and transformation version. A value called recent_orders is incomplete without a window, timezone, and treatment of cancellations.

Training data must reflect what was knowable at prediction time. Joining a trip with a feature computed after completion leaks future information and produces misleading offline performance. Point-in-time joins and source lineage are essential.

Online and offline consistency

The same conceptual feature can be encoded differently in training and serving. Language formats, missing values, category vocabularies, and units can silently diverge. Validate schema and distributions at the serving boundary.

Logging the actual inference feature vector can reduce this mismatch, but creates volume and privacy costs. The reference design uses an allowlist, compact feature identifiers, sampling where appropriate, and retention controls. Never log every input indiscriminately merely because it helps debugging.

Exposure and label joins

A scored candidate is not necessarily displayed. Join prediction records to actual impressions and later outcomes using stable identifiers. Define late-arrival windows and missing-event behavior. If only exposed candidates are logged, recognize the resulting selection bias and preserve the policy context needed for evaluation.

Training labels may arrive days later or be corrected. Version datasets and distinguish preliminary from finalized labels. A model trained on incomplete outcomes can learn the behavior of the logging delay rather than the intended task.

Serving contracts

A prediction response includes model version, feature schema, confidence or diagnostic metadata where meaningful, and fallback status. Critical consumers should know whether they received a current model output or a safe default.

Separate feature-store availability from model-serving availability in observability. A healthy inference process can still return poor results when features are stale or missing.

Ownership

Every production model needs an owner, supported use, evaluation suite, rollback artifact, and retirement plan. A model without ongoing monitoring becomes a hidden policy decision. The platform should make that responsibility visible rather than treating deployment as the end of the lifecycle.

PART VIII / Analytics, machine learning, and experiments

54. Training, serving, explainability, and model reliability

Model quality depends on the full system around the model: data selection, training reproducibility, serving infrastructure, calibration, and the product decision that consumes the prediction.

Published evidence. Uber's integrated-gradients account discusses explainability for deep models, while its responsible-AI publication describes organizational and technical practices for scaling governance. These are complementary concerns; an explanation method alone does not establish fairness, causality, or safety. [S49][S50]

Reproducible training

Record dataset snapshot, feature definitions, code revision, dependencies, hyperparameters, random seeds where applicable, and evaluation results. Reproducibility may be approximate for some distributed training operations, but the provenance should still be complete enough to investigate differences.

Separate training, validation, and test periods to reduce leakage. Include geographic and product cohorts. For time-dependent marketplaces, random row splits can leak closely related events across sets and overstate generalization.

Evaluation beyond one metric

A pickup model needs calibration and tail-error analysis. A ranking model needs exposure-aware evaluation. A risk model needs false-positive and delayed-label analysis. A model's offline metric should map to a real decision and known failure cost.

Stress missing features, distribution shift, extreme demand, and corrupted inputs. Compare to a simple baseline. Complexity is justified only when it produces robust value under the actual serving constraints.

Serving architecture

Deploy a versioned artifact behind an inference contract. Use bounded queues, batching where suitable, resource isolation, and explicit timeouts. GPU acceleration can improve throughput but introduces scheduling and cold-start concerns. A fallback must be available when the model or feature dependency fails.

Canary by a controlled cohort and compare business guardrails before expanding. Shadow predictions can reveal technical differences without influencing users, but cannot fully measure behavioral effects because the shadow policy does not change the marketplace.

Explainability

Feature attribution can help debug unexpected predictions and identify reliance on questionable inputs. It does not prove a causal explanation. Correlated features, baselines, and model structure affect attribution results.

Provide explanations appropriate to the audience. Engineers may need detailed diagnostics; users need clear, actionable reasons without exposing sensitive data or internal abuse defenses. High-impact decisions require review and appeal mechanisms beyond a colorful attribution chart.

Retirement and rollback

Keep compatible feature and model versions for rollback. Retiring a feature can break an older fallback model. Test the rollback path before an incident. Remove unused models and access permissions rather than accumulating an ungoverned registry.

The model is one versioned component in a decision system. Reliability comes from the contract around it, not just the accuracy of its weights.

PART VIII / Analytics, machine learning, and experiments

55. Experimentation, interference, and switchbacks

A marketplace experiment can affect both the treated user and everyone competing for the same supply. Ordinary independent-user assumptions may fail when one group's treatment changes another group's waiting time.

Published evidence. Uber has published its XP experimentation platform and later work on faster local evaluation. Research on switchback experiments studies time-based treatment assignment and carryover effects. The experimental designs here are methodological proposals, not a claim that every Uber experiment uses one particular scheme. [S31][S32][S69]

Choose the unit of randomization

A cosmetic screen experiment may randomize users. A dispatch or pricing policy may require geographic or time-based clusters because participants share supply. Randomization should match the mechanism of interference.

A switchback alternates treatment across time blocks for a market or cluster. It can reduce some cross-user contamination, but introduces temporal confounding and carryover. Use randomized schedules, sufficient replication, and a planned analysis rather than simply comparing Monday with Tuesday.

Carryover and washout

A repositioning policy can move vehicles into an area whose effect persists after the treatment changes. A short block may mix both policies' consequences. Estimate plausible carryover and consider washout periods or analysis that models it.

Longer blocks reduce rapid contamination but provide fewer independent units and may be more exposed to time trends. There is no universally correct block size. The design must reflect the product's dynamics and available statistical power.

Assignment integrity

Persist the experiment assignment or relevant policy version when generating an accepted quote or offer. Do not re-evaluate treatment during settlement and accidentally apply another policy. Log exposure separately from assignment: a user assigned to a feature may never see it.

Local evaluation can improve latency and reduce dependence on a central service, but configuration freshness and deterministic bucketing must be controlled. Different services must not assign contradictory treatments for the same decision.

Guardrails and stopping

Predefine primary outcomes, safety and reliability guardrails, analysis populations, and stopping rules. Avoid repeatedly peeking at noisy results and declaring success at the first favorable moment. Account for multiple comparisons and delayed outcomes.

Evaluate waiting, fulfillment, cancellation, participant outcomes, and operational burden. A revenue increase during a short experiment can coexist with worse long-term retention or unequal geographic impact.

What experiments cannot prove

A result applies to the tested population, period, and implementation. Scaling can change network effects and operational load. Revalidate after major product or market changes. Experiments support decisions; they do not eliminate the need to understand the system's causal structure.

56. Generative AI, Cart Assistant, and bounded agents

Generative AI is useful for interpreting requests and assisting workflows, but it should not become an unbounded authority over purchases, trips, accounts, or sensitive data.

Published evidence. Uber's GenAI Gateway account describes shared infrastructure for model access. Its June 2026 Cart Assistant article presents a staged shopping assistant with focused model calls and reviewable cart construction. The reference agent architecture below keeps consequential actions behind deterministic validation and approval. [S20][S51]

Interpret, retrieve, validate, review

A shopping assistant can turn natural language or an image into structured intent. Retrieval finds candidate products. Deterministic checks enforce store availability, item constraints, price limits, and user preferences. The system presents a draft cart for review before checkout.

Do not let generated text invent product identifiers or assume inventory. Every selected item must resolve to a current catalog entity. A model-generated substitution must pass the same rules as a human-proposed substitution.

Tool contracts

Give each tool a narrow schema, explicit permissions, bounded output, and an idempotency model where it mutates state. Separate read tools from write tools. A request to summarize an order should not grant permission to cancel it or issue a refund.

External documents, merchant descriptions, and support messages are untrusted inputs. Their text cannot override system policy or authorize actions. Treat tool outputs as data, not instructions.

Gateway responsibilities

A reference gateway manages provider routing, quotas, model versions, request policy, cost tracking, and redaction. It should not indiscriminately log sensitive prompts. Define retention and access separately from ordinary application telemetry.

Provider fallback can change behavior and latency. Evaluate compatibility before switching models. A cheaper model is not interchangeable if it fails structured-output or safety requirements.

Evaluation and observability

Build task-specific tests: correct item resolution, unsupported request handling, prompt-injection resistance, price-bound compliance, and refusal to execute unapproved actions. Measure human correction rate and end-to-end success, not just fluent output.

Record a minimized action trace with model and tool versions. For consequential operations, preserve the user's approval and the exact structured command. A natural-language conversation alone is a poor audit record.

Failure behavior

If the model is unavailable, fall back to ordinary search and manual workflows. If a tool outcome is uncertain, reconcile it before retrying with a new identity. An agent should inherit the platform's transaction and permission rules, not bypass them because it can reason in language.

The safest useful architecture gives models flexibility in interpretation and gives deterministic systems authority over commitments.

PART IX / Compute, networks, and regional resilience

57. Clouds, regions, cells, and authority placement

Global infrastructure must balance latency, failure isolation, operational cost, and the location of authoritative state. Running services in several regions is not enough; the platform must define what each region is allowed to decide.

Published evidence. Uber's late-2025 cloud account describes its journey involving OCI and Google Cloud. Its September 2026 compute account describes a fleet spanning multiple environments and active-active regional operation. These sources do not establish a complete private inventory or prove that every workload has identical failover semantics. [S25][S26]

The proposed topology

Use global edge entry points for connection termination and request routing. Assign product entities to explicit authorities within cells. Replicate durable state according to its transaction and recovery requirements. Keep regional caches, location views, and stateless workers close to the traffic they serve.

A cell contains bounded serving capacity and operational dependencies. It may cover several markets or a partition of a large market. City boundaries are business boundaries, not necessarily database or cloud-region boundaries.

Active-active does not mean unrestricted multi-writer

Different cells or entities can be active in different regions at the same time. For a particular assignment or workflow, authority must still be unambiguous. A globally replicated database may provide that coordination; another design may use explicit ownership and fencing.

A request arriving at the wrong edge should be routed to the entity's current authority. During failover, stale routers can continue sending to the old region, but the old writer must reject writes after its epoch is revoked. DNS changes alone do not establish this guarantee.

Latency placement

Put high-volume approximate reads near clients where practical. Keep invariant-sensitive transactions within a topology that meets latency and durability objectives. A worldwide transaction for every location ping is unnecessary; a purely local unreplicated commitment may violate recovery requirements.

Model cross-region bandwidth, database round trips, and partner endpoints. The geographically nearest cloud region may not be nearest to the authoritative storage or external dependency. Measure complete request paths.

Shared failure causes

Cloud diversity can reduce some risks while introducing others: network interconnects, identity and configuration dependencies, tooling differences, and operational complexity. A shared deployment system or global configuration error can affect every region regardless of provider.

Inventory dependencies that remain global. Test their failure explicitly. Regional isolation is incomplete if every booking synchronously requires a single global policy service or secrets endpoint.

Evolution

Start with a topology the team can operate reliably. Add regions when user latency, scale, or failure requirements justify the cost. Each added region needs data ownership, rollout sequencing, observability, failover, and reconciliation plans.

The design goal is not the largest possible footprint. It is a topology whose failure behavior is understood and whose commitments remain trustworthy when part of it disappears.

58. Kubernetes, Up, scaling, and controller correctness

A compute platform translates desired service behavior into running workloads. At large scale, the control plane itself becomes a distributed system with stale observations, competing writers, and irreversible workflow decisions.

Published evidence. Uber's 2025 account describes its Kubernetes migration. Its September 2026 scaling article introduces ServiceScale and a separate controller for reconciling scaling intents, including failover needs. It highlights stale-cache status and multi-writer problems rather than presenting autoscaling as a simple replica-count update. [S24][S26]

A reference compute abstraction

Service owners declare image, resources, placement constraints, criticality, rollout policy, and scaling expectations. A platform layer converts these into cluster-specific workloads. This reduces repeated operational work while preserving explicit escape hatches for unusual services.

Separate normal deployment intent from temporary failover or emergency scaling intent. A reconciler combines them according to documented precedence. Store the contributing intents so failback can restore steady state without reconstructing decisions from logs.

Stale status is dangerous

A controller's cached view may lag its own writes. If an upstream workflow treats a stale healthy status as permission to advance a rollout, it can make an irreversible decision too early. Use generation-aware status and verify that observed resources reflect the required generation before declaring success.

Optimistic concurrency and field ownership reduce conflicting writes but do not eliminate semantic races. Two controllers can each make individually valid patches that combine into an invalid desired state. Define which controller owns each field and how temporary overrides are resolved.

Autoscaling signals

CPU is only one signal. Queue depth, request concurrency, latency, memory pressure, and downstream capacity may matter more. A service blocked on a database can show low CPU while being overloaded. Scaling it up may worsen the database problem.

Use minimum capacity, rate limits on scaling, warmup periods, and stabilization windows. Ensure new instances receive compatible configuration and warmed dependencies before taking full traffic. Cold caches can turn a scale-out into a load spike.

Failover capacity

A reference platform can reclaim low-priority batch capacity for critical serving during a regional failure. That requires pretested priority rules, bounded eviction time, compatible hardware, and enough surviving network and storage capacity. Compute availability alone is insufficient.

Do not promise recovery based on theoretical empty cores. Measure how long the whole path takes to become healthy under failure load, including image pulls, scheduling, cache warmup, and database connections.

Platform operations

Test controller restarts, informer lag, conflicting updates, partial rollouts, and stale status. Observe desired versus available capacity by generation and region. The compute platform should make intent and convergence visible, not hide them behind a single green deployment label.

59. Service mesh, discovery, and load balancing

A service mesh can standardize routing, telemetry, and transport policy, but it does not remove application semantics. A proxy cannot decide whether retrying a payment or assignment command is safe without a contract.

Published evidence. Uber's dependency-analysis account describes infrastructure that correlates inbound and outbound failures, including its Muttley proxy context. Its load-balancing publication discusses heterogeneous hardware. These are scoped infrastructure accounts, not proof that one algorithm is optimal for every workload. [S28][S29]

The reference request path

A caller resolves a logical service name to healthy endpoints with locality and version information. A proxy or client library applies deadlines, connection pooling, telemetry, and allowed retry policy. The receiving service authenticates the caller and validates the command.

Discovery data is eventually updated. An endpoint removed from the registry may still receive traffic from stale clients. Servers need graceful draining, explicit readiness, and a shutdown sequence that preserves in-flight commitments.

Load balancing under heterogeneous capacity

Equal request counts do not imply equal load. Hosts may differ in CPU generation, core count, memory, or background work. Requests also differ in cost. Use measured capacity and outstanding work rather than assuming every endpoint is interchangeable.

A latency-aware balancer can react to congestion, but overly aggressive feedback can oscillate and herd traffic. Smooth signals, cap changes, and distinguish a slow endpoint from a slow shared dependency.

Locality and failure

Prefer local healthy capacity when appropriate, but avoid trapping traffic in an overloaded zone. Cross-zone or cross-region spillover needs a budget and awareness of downstream authority. A stateless request may move easily; a stateful command may still require a particular authoritative store.

Connection pools and DNS caches have their own lifetimes. Test failover with existing long-lived connections, not only fresh requests. A deployment can be removed from discovery while still serving substantial traffic.

Transport policy

Propagate deadlines so a downstream service does not spend seconds on work whose caller has already timed out. Limit request and response sizes. Use backpressure rather than allowing unbounded queues at every hop.

Transport encryption and service authentication protect the channel, but authorization remains application-specific. A valid internal caller should not automatically have permission to mutate every trip or access every location record.

Diagnostics

Trace service, endpoint, region, version, and error category without including sensitive payloads. Correlation can identify likely dependency failures, but coincident errors are not always causal. Preserve enough context for investigation and avoid automated actions that assume perfect attribution.

The mesh is most useful when it makes common behavior consistent while leaving business correctness visible to the services that own it.

60. Retry storms, deadlines, overload, and graceful degradation

Retries can recover from transient failures, but coordinated retries can turn a small outage into a much larger one. The system needs a policy for who retries, how much, and under which evidence.

Published evidence. Uber's September 2026 retry-storm article describes context-aware error ownership to limit retry amplification across dependency chains. It also notes that correlated failures weaken optimistic assumptions about retry success. The reference controls below retain explicit business idempotency. [S27]

Amplification

If each layer makes two attempts and failures propagate through five retrying layers, a leaf can receive up to 32 attempts for one original request in a simplified worst-case chain. This is an illustrative arithmetic model, not a measured Uber incident. Fan-out can make the situation worse.

A retry budget limits additional attempts relative to original traffic. Shared context can prevent every upstream layer from retrying an error that originated deep below it. However, error attribution is imperfect; the policy should be tested under coincident and partially observed failures.

Deadlines first

Assign an end-to-end deadline and divide it among dependencies with time reserved for response handling. Do not start a retry that cannot complete within the remaining budget. Cancel abandoned work where supported, while recognizing that cancellation may arrive after an authoritative commit.

A timeout after a mutating operation remains ambiguous. Resolve by command identity rather than creating a new operation. Retry safety is a business property, not merely an HTTP status-code property.

Admission control

Reject or defer work before queues become unbounded. Protect critical execution and safety traffic from optional recommendations, analytics, and batch work. Use per-caller, per-market, and global budgets to prevent one hot tenant or event from exhausting shared capacity.

Queue length is not the only signal. Measure age of queued work, service time, and deadline misses. A short queue of very expensive requests can be worse than a long queue of cheap ones.

Degradation ladder

A reference ladder might disable expensive personalization, reduce candidate exploration, use approved ETA fallbacks, defer noncritical notifications, and pause new commitments when authority is unavailable. Each step has an owner and a clear user-visible consequence.

Do not degrade by skipping safety, permission, or financial invariants. A system that remains fast by accepting invalid assignments has not preserved availability in a meaningful sense.

Recovery

After an outage, avoid releasing every queued retry simultaneously. Use jitter, rate limits, and priority. Reconcile expired work before execution. A ride request that is no longer wanted should not suddenly produce a vehicle after a backlog drains.

Test overload with realistic dependency failures and cold caches. The goal is bounded damage and predictable recovery, not maximum throughput until collapse.

61. Observability: metrics, traces, logs, and business invariants

Observability should explain both infrastructure health and the state of customer commitments. A CPU dashboard cannot tell whether a rider has two drivers assigned or whether a completed delivery lacks a financial obligation.

Published evidence. Uber's observability account describes Jaeger, M3, and sampling, while its September 2026 M3 account discusses operational isolation through subclusters. These publications show that telemetry itself has scale and reliability concerns. [S44][S45]

Four layers of signals

Infrastructure metrics describe hosts, containers, networks, and storage. Service metrics describe latency, errors, saturation, and deadlines. Domain metrics describe matching, acceptance, pickup, delivery, and reconciliation. Invariant checks detect impossible or unexplained states.

For example, monitor assignments without corresponding plan entries, requests with conflicting active commitments, aged unknown payment outcomes, and orders whose terminal states disagree across authorities. These are not ordinary availability counters; they are correctness signals.

Trace the decision

Propagate request and command IDs through gateways, matching, route calls, transaction commits, and asynchronous events. Connect traces across queues using explicit links rather than pretending asynchronous work is one continuous synchronous call.

Sample strategically. Rare errors and important transitions may need higher retention than routine location traffic. Sampling introduces bias; do not estimate all business counts from traces without understanding the method.

Cardinality and privacy

User IDs, exact coordinates, and arbitrary URLs can create enormous metric cardinality and privacy risk. Keep high-cardinality detail in access-controlled logs or traces where justified, not in every metric label. Use bounded dimensions such as market, product, error category, and service version.

Redact secrets and sensitive payloads before telemetry leaves the process. A debugging system should not become a second uncontrolled copy of production data.

Alerting

Alert on user impact and impending saturation, not every transient fluctuation. Combine fast burn-rate alerts with longer-window checks. Route alerts to the team that can act, with runbooks and relevant context.

A location freshness alert should identify affected markets and likely ingestion stages. A transaction-conflict alert should distinguish expected competition from a runaway retry loop. Good alerts narrow the decision space for responders.

Telemetry failure

Monitoring pipelines can fail during the same incident they are meant to explain. Maintain independent synthetic checks, bounded local buffers, and clear indicators of missing telemetry. Absence of errors is not evidence of health when the collector is down.

The best observability system lets an engineer move from a customer symptom to a specific violated contract, then to the responsible component and recovery action.

PART IX / Compute, networks, and regional resilience

62. Reliability, failover, and error-budget operations

Reliability is a property of complete journeys under a stated failure model. Redundant servers help, but correctness during partial failure depends on ownership, data durability, capacity, and tested procedures.

Published evidence. Uber's 2026 compute account describes failover-related scaling and reclaiming lower-priority capacity for important workloads. This illustrates that regional recovery includes capacity orchestration, not merely traffic rerouting. [S26]

A reference failure model

Define which failures the system must tolerate: process, host, zone, region, network partition, control-plane outage, logical corruption, and partner outage. State whether simultaneous failures are included. A zero-data-loss objective for one-region loss does not imply protection from every possible multi-region catastrophe.

For each critical service, specify availability, latency, durability, freshness, RTO, RPO, and degraded behavior. The targets must fit together. A booking service cannot promise stronger durability than its authoritative store and commit protocol provide.

Failover sequence

Detect and classify the failure. Stop or fence affected writers. Confirm the surviving authority and data position. Ensure capacity and dependencies are ready. Shift traffic gradually. Reconcile in-flight and ambiguous operations. Only then declare full recovery.

A database failover and a product failover are not the same milestone. The database may be reachable while drivers still hold old assignments, notifications are delayed, and external outcomes remain unresolved.

Capacity assumptions

If two equally loaded regions each run at 70% of their individual capacity, one cannot absorb all traffic from the other without exceeding capacity. In the simplest symmetric model, steady-state utilization must be at most 50% for full loss absorption without scaling or shedding. Real systems need additional headroom and account for bottlenecks beyond CPU.

Low-priority workload reclamation can reduce idle capacity cost, but only if it completes quickly enough and does not share a nonreclaimable bottleneck. Test storage, network, and partner limits as well as compute.

Error-budget policy

When a service consumes its reliability budget, prioritize remediation, reduce risky changes, and review dependency assumptions. Do not treat an error budget as permission to cause avoidable outages. It is a mechanism for balancing delivery and reliability with measured evidence.

Exercises

Run zone-loss, region-isolation, stale-route, expired-credential, and control-plane-unavailable drills. Include operators and product support. Record actual recovery time and unresolved-state backlog. A successful drill should prove that commitments remain coherent, not merely that a health endpoint returns 200 in another region.

63. Backup, restore, replay, and the physical-world gap

Restoring a database returns digital state to an earlier point. It does not return vehicles, food, people, or bank transactions to that point. Recovery must reconcile the gap between restored records and events that continued in the real world.

Published evidence. Uber's 2025 backup-and-recovery account describes automated backup and restoration work for its database platform. Backup throughput and restore throughput are capacity measures; they must not be confused with elapsed RTO or a universal RPO for every Uber service. [S40]

Backups are not replication

Replication preserves availability under certain failures and can replicate bad writes. Backups provide historical recovery points, but may be stale and require lengthy restoration. Maintain both according to the data's criticality and threat model.

A backup is only useful if it can be restored with keys, schemas, metadata, and dependencies intact. Test restores into isolated environments and verify logical invariants, not just successful file extraction.

The restored-state hazard

Suppose a backup says driver D is available, but D accepted and completed a trip after that backup. Replaying old assignment logic could create a conflicting commitment. Likewise, a payout may have succeeded externally even though the restored database lacks its final status.

Before reopening writes, establish a recovery epoch, fence previous writers, replay durable logs where valid, and reconcile external references. Treat uncertain operations as unknown until evidence resolves them. Do not infer failure from absence in the restored snapshot.

Replay classes

Classify consumers into pure projections, idempotent local effects, and external side effects. Pure projections can often be rebuilt. Local effects require deduplication. External effects need stable references and reconciliation; replay should not automatically reissue completed missions, messages with consequences, or money movement.

Include tombstones and privacy deletion records in recovery. Otherwise, restoring a backup can reintroduce data that should no longer be broadly accessible.

Recovery validation

Check assignment uniqueness, plan capacity, request-to-assignment links, order lifecycle consistency, journal balance, and external-outcome coverage. Compare recovered state to surviving device and partner observations with appropriate trust levels.

Bring the system back in stages: read-only access, reconciliation tools, limited new work, then full operation. The system may need to complete existing journeys before accepting additional demand.

Operational evidence

Record backup age, restore duration, replay position, unresolved command count, and reconciliation progress. A recovery report should state what is known, what is uncertain, and which operations remain restricted.

Disaster recovery is complete when the platform can safely make new commitments without contradicting commitments that already happened—not when the database process merely starts.

64. Flipr-style configuration and controlled rollouts

Configuration can change production behavior faster than code deployment. That makes it powerful and dangerous. A malformed city rule or globally enabled experiment can create a wider incident than a single service release.

Published evidence. Uber's Flipr publication describes dynamic configuration and contextual evaluation. Its later experiment-evaluation work emphasizes efficient local decisions. The reference control plane below adds explicit governance and recovery contracts rather than assuming every flag is harmless. [S30][S31]

Configuration as a versioned artifact

Store schema, owner, scope, effective time, validation rules, and rollback version. Separate feature exposure from critical policy such as product eligibility or safety restrictions. The latter may need stronger review, expiry, and revocation semantics.

A serving process loads a validated snapshot and reports the version it uses. Invalid updates are rejected before publication. Last-known-good behavior is useful, but the maximum acceptable age must be defined for each setting.

Scope and precedence

Rules may apply globally, by country, city, product, participant cohort, or application version. Define precedence explicitly and provide an explanation tool that shows why a value was selected. Hidden interactions among flags create behavior no single team understands.

Avoid free-form executable code in ordinary configuration. Use a constrained expression language or typed policy representation with bounded evaluation cost. Treat all configuration inputs as potentially erroneous.

Rollout sequence

Validate statically, run simulations, apply to internal or test markets, canary a small cohort, then expand with guardrails. Record the decision and metrics at each stage. A kill switch should have a narrow purpose and tested propagation time.

Accepted quotes and assignments retain the policy version under which they were created. A later flag change should not silently rewrite existing commitments. New requests may use a new policy while old journeys complete under compatible rules.

Control-plane failure

Serving should continue with approved snapshots for settings that permit it. For freshness-critical restrictions, use explicit expiry or a dedicated revocation path. Do not make every request synchronously depend on the configuration service merely to avoid thinking about staleness.

Audit who changed what, why, and which cohorts were affected. Support tools should be able to reconstruct the effective policy for a historical decision.

Testing

Test conflicting scopes, missing fields, invalid values, delayed propagation, rollback, and old-client compatibility. A configuration change deserves a deployment-level safety process when it can alter pricing, matching, or access.

The control plane's job is to make change deliberate and observable. It should not become a shortcut around the engineering review required for consequential behavior.

65. Monorepos, Bazel, GitFarm, and the software factory

Developer infrastructure determines how safely thousands of changes can reach production. Build speed matters, but so do reproducibility, dependency visibility, and confidence that a change has been tested against what it can affect.

Published evidence. Uber's Bazel account discusses monorepo build engineering. Its July 2026 GitFarm article describes a centralized Git client executing commands in isolated, prewarmed sandboxes, not a replacement repository-hosting system. Its August 2026 software-factory account discusses increasing automation in development. [S52][S70][S53]

A reference build pipeline

A change enters code review, dependency-aware tests, security checks, reproducible build, artifact signing, and staged deployment. The build records source revision, toolchain, dependencies, and generated artifacts. A production binary should be traceable to reviewed inputs.

Hermetic builds reduce dependence on a developer machine or mutable external download. Remote caching helps only when cache keys include all relevant inputs and artifacts are trustworthy. A poisoned or incorrectly keyed cache can distribute errors quickly.

Monorepo tradeoffs

A monorepo can simplify coordinated changes and dependency discovery, but increases repository size and CI complexity. Ownership rules, test selection, and build graph quality become essential. A small change should not require every unrelated test, yet must run the tests for its actual dependency impact.

Use a merge queue or equivalent protection for the integrated branch. Two individually passing changes can fail together. Test the combination that will actually merge, not only isolated feature branches.

Shared Git operations

A GitFarm-like service can reduce repeated cloning by maintaining warm checkouts and executing operations in isolated environments. It must enforce repository permissions, sandboxing, quotas, and cleanup. Freshness is explicit: a warm checkout may require a fetch before an operation that depends on the latest upstream state.

Do not confuse shared client execution with the source-of-truth repository server. They have different durability and authorization responsibilities.

AI-assisted development

Agents can draft code, tests, migrations, and documentation. They still need constrained credentials, isolated execution, review, and evaluation. Generated tests may repeat the implementation's mistake rather than independently validate the requirement.

Do not grant an agent production mutation rights merely because it can complete a coding task. Keep code generation, test execution, merge approval, and deployment authority separate. Preserve provenance without treating attribution metrics as proof of software quality.

Measures that matter

Track lead time, build reliability, flaky-test rate, change failure rate, rollback success, and escaped defects. Faster code generation is useful only if the system can validate and operate the resulting changes. The software factory should increase trustworthy throughput, not just the number of pull requests.

66. Simulation, model checking, testing, and chaos

A physical marketplace cannot safely test every failure by experimenting on real trips. Simulation and controlled fault injection provide a way to explore behavior before deployment, but their limitations must remain visible.

Published evidence. Uber has published work on marketplace simulation. The test program below is an original reference plan, not a reproduction of its internal certification suite. [S12]

Test at several levels

Unit tests validate deterministic transition functions and calculations. Property tests explore invariants across many command sequences. Concurrency tests force conflicting accepts, cancellations, and refunds. Integration tests exercise databases, queues, and partner adapters. End-to-end tests validate the user journey under realistic connectivity.

A local SQLite teaching test can demonstrate an invariant, but cannot prove a multi-region production database protocol. State the boundary of every test result. The included teaching suite is deliberately limited in this way.

Marketplace simulation

A reference simulator generates demand, supply movement, acceptance behavior, travel times, cancellations, and disruptions. Compare policies using common random seeds or other variance-reduction methods where appropriate. Include the cost of waiting and operational intervention.

Validate the simulator against observed-like distributions. If simulated drivers always accept offers or roads never become congested, the resulting policy evaluation will be misleading. Test sensitivity to uncertain assumptions.

Shadowing

Run new matching or pricing logic without applying its decisions. Compare outputs, latency, feasibility, and resource usage. Shadowing is valuable for technical validation, but cannot reveal all behavioral feedback because users experience only the live policy.

Never allow shadow traffic to send real partner requests, create charges, or notify participants. Use explicit side-effect suppression and isolated credentials.

Chaos and fault injection

Inject process crashes, queue duplication, delayed messages, stale caches, clock skew, network partitions, and regional unavailability. Define a bounded blast radius and abort conditions. Begin in isolated environments, then controlled production exercises only with appropriate operational readiness.

The success criterion is not merely that services restart. Verify assignment uniqueness, plan feasibility, preserved command identity, and reconciled external outcomes.

Model-based testing

Represent allowed state transitions as a small executable model. Generate commands and compare the implementation's behavior to the model. Explore terminal-state races and replay. For especially critical protocols, a formal state-machine specification can expose counterexamples before implementation.

Testing should target the ways the system can lie about reality: duplicate commitments, lost accepted work, stale authority, and falsely certain outcomes. Those failures deserve more attention than superficial endpoint coverage.

67. Security architecture and privileged operations

A marketplace handles location, communications, financial references, and operational control. Security must protect both data and the ability to create or alter real-world commitments.

Published evidence. Uber's public privacy and safety materials describe sensitive data uses and participant protections. The controls in this chapter are a proposed security architecture; they are not an assertion about undisclosed internal systems or a certification claim. [S67][S68]

Trust boundaries

Separate public clients, partner integrations, internal services, production operators, and analytical users. Authenticate every boundary and authorize each action against the relevant entity relationship. A valid employee or service identity is not permission to view every trip or change every assignment.

Use short-lived credentials where practical, scoped service permissions, transport protection, and secret rotation. Keep secrets out of mobile binaries, logs, and source repositories. A leaked debug token should not grant broad production access.

Protect commands and data

Mutating APIs require actor binding, idempotency, input validation, and rate limits. Object identifiers should not substitute for authorization. A user who guesses another trip ID must not retrieve its route or issue a cancellation.

Encrypt sensitive data at rest with controlled key access and rotation procedures. Separate operational identifiers from direct personal information where possible. Encryption is not a substitute for access control; an overprivileged application can still decrypt data improperly.

Privileged access

Use time-bound elevation, purpose recording, approval for high-risk actions, and immutable audit trails. Support and engineering tools should expose typed operations rather than unrestricted SQL consoles. Emergency access needs an explicit break-glass process with review after use.

Restrict exports and bulk queries. A read-only credential can still cause serious harm if it can extract every location trace. Detect unusual access patterns without creating another uncontrolled store of sensitive data.

Software supply chain

Pin and review dependencies, scan artifacts, sign builds, and verify provenance before deployment. Isolate CI workers and untrusted code execution. A pull request from an untrusted source must not automatically receive production secrets.

Maintain vulnerability response ownership and tested patch rollouts. Security fixes can cause availability regressions, so emergency procedures need both urgency and validation.

Threat modeling

Review account takeover, unauthorized trip access, forged partner callbacks, malicious attachments, abusive automation, insider misuse, and compromised service credentials. For each, identify prevention, detection, containment, and recovery.

The security objective is not only confidentiality. It is also preserving the integrity of assignments, commercial obligations, and operational actions that affect people outside the software system.

68. Privacy, deletion, residency, and governance

Location-rich services need explicit data-purpose boundaries. Collecting a field for trip execution does not automatically justify retaining it forever or using it for every future model.

Published evidence. Uber's March 26, 2026 privacy notice for riders and order recipients describes data collection, uses, sharing, and retention/deletion topics. The notice is a source about Uber's stated practices, not a universal legal rule for a new company. Local obligations require current professional review. [S68]

A reference data inventory

Classify operational identifiers, direct personal data, exact location, communications, financial references, documents, and derived features. Record purpose, legal basis where applicable, owner, retention, recipients, and access roles.

Map data lineage from collection through streams, databases, caches, search, analytics, model features, exports, and backups. A deletion request is incomplete if it only removes the profile row while leaving easily linked traces everywhere else.

Minimize by design

Use coarse or aggregated geography when exact coordinates are unnecessary. Avoid copying full payloads into logs. Retain precise operational evidence only for defined purposes and periods, subject to applicable holds and requirements.

Separate identity resolution from analytical datasets where feasible. Pseudonymous identifiers reduce some exposure but do not make data automatically anonymous, especially when detailed movement patterns remain linkable.

Deletion workflow

A proposed deletion coordinator verifies scope and authority, determines applicable holds, emits durable deletion tasks, tracks acknowledgments, and records completion evidence. Each downstream system implements its supported deletion semantics.

Backups may require access restrictions and reapplication of deletion records on restore rather than immediate physical rewriting. That policy must be explicit and legally reviewed. A restored environment should not broadly expose data before deletion obligations are reapplied.

Residency and transfers

Define where each data class is stored, processed, backed up, and accessed. A cloud region setting does not fully determine residency if support tools, telemetry, or model providers export the data elsewhere.

Regional policy should influence routing, storage, and access without fragmenting the core state model. Use policy-as-data with effective dates and review, not ad hoc country checks scattered throughout code.

Governance and evidence

Run access reviews, retention audits, data-quality checks, and incident exercises. Give users understandable controls and explanations. Document exceptions and their approval rather than silently extending retention.

Privacy engineering is a lifecycle discipline. It changes schemas, event design, observability, testing, and recovery—not just the wording of a notice displayed at signup.

69. Safe releases and data/model migrations

A platform release can change code, configuration, schema, maps, models, and mobile behavior. These changes interact, so rollback must restore a compatible system rather than merely an older container image.

Published evidence. Uber's public accounts of configuration, search model/index evolution, and software development describe different parts of this change surface. The release protocol below is a reference synthesis, not a claim of one universal internal deployment procedure. [S30][S18][S53]

Compatibility first

Use expand-and-contract schema changes. Add compatible fields, deploy readers and writers that tolerate both versions, backfill safely, validate, then remove old behavior only after dependencies have migrated. A rollback during the transition must still understand the data already written.

For event schemas, test old consumers against new producers and replayed old events against new consumers. Unknown enum values should fail deliberately or use a documented fallback, not silently map to a success state.

Session-aware rollout

A long-running trip or workflow can cross several deployments. Persist the relevant policy and state-machine version where needed. Route existing sessions consistently when compatibility requires it. Avoid splitting one business transaction between incompatible implementations.

Shadow new behavior with side effects disabled. Compare semantic outcomes and invariants, not just response bytes. Differences may be acceptable if intentional and reviewed; unexplained differences should block expansion.

Model and map releases

Deploy a model with its feature schema and compatible index or vocabulary. Deploy a road graph with route-cache invalidation and edge-ID compatibility. A rollback artifact is a bundle of compatible dependencies, not one file.

Data backfills need rate limits, checkpoints, and clear replay semantics. A backfill of historical completion records must not issue new receipts, charges, or driver assignments.

Canary gates

Gate on latency, errors, transaction conflicts, data freshness, business outcomes, and invariant checks. A release can pass infrastructure health while creating malformed plans or incorrect prices. Use cohort-specific dashboards and an explicit owner for the rollout decision.

Automated rollback is useful for well-understood signals, but some data migrations are not automatically reversible. Document the recovery plan before execution. Restoring an old binary may not undo a destructive schema change.

Release evidence

Record versions, approvals, test results, rollout scope, guardrail observations, and rollback actions. During an incident, responders should quickly identify what changed for the affected cohort.

Safe delivery is the ability to change the platform without losing track of which rules govern each existing commitment.

70. Capacity planning, cost models, and performance budgets

Capacity planning begins with workload decomposition, not a single company-level transaction count. Location, search, dispatch, notifications, analytics, and model inference have very different traffic shapes.

Published evidence. Uber's operating reports and engineering publications expose metrics at different scopes. Business trips, model predictions, data rows, and pod launches are not interchangeable units. The numbers and charts in this chapter are explicitly illustrative planning assumptions. [S01]

A reference workload model

Assume one million active devices report location every four seconds: 250,000 samples/s. Assume 2,000 new requests/s at peak and 20 accurately routed candidates per request: up to 40,000 route evaluations/s before reuse or batching. These are examples, not estimates of Uber's actual current traffic.

For each workload, model average, peak, burst duration, payload, fan-out, service time, storage writes, and replication. Include retries and failure-mode traffic. A system sized only for average normal traffic may collapse during recovery.

Concurrency and queueing

Little's Law gives average in-flight work as arrival rate multiplied by average time in the system under stable conditions. At 2,000 requests/s and 0.2 seconds average service time, the mean is 400 in-flight requests. Tail latency, burstiness, and downstream variability require additional headroom.

A simple M/M/1 illustration shows delay rising sharply as utilization approaches one, but real services rarely match its assumptions exactly. Use it to understand the shape, then measure the actual distribution and bottlenecks.

Storage and retention

At 50 MB/s of raw location payload, one day is about 4.32 TB in decimal units before compression, indexes, replicas, or protocol overhead. Multiply by the chosen retention and replication policy, then add metadata and derived views. Do not retain high-resolution data indefinitely merely because compression makes it affordable.

Model event logs, hot databases, lakehouse data, backups, and model features separately. Compaction and backfills need their own capacity budget.

Cost per useful outcome

Track cost per completed ride, order, or load alongside cost per API call. Include idle failover capacity, network egress, storage maintenance, model inference, observability, and operational staff. Reducing one service's CPU can increase another service's I/O or support burden.

Use current vendor quotes and measured usage for a real business case. This book does not provide a current cloud-price estimate or claim a private Uber cost structure.

Load-test design

Replay realistic distributions with hot markets, slow clients, skewed merchants, and dependency failures. Test the maximum sustainable rate under the latency and correctness objectives, not merely the highest rate before processes crash.

Capacity is sufficient when the platform can absorb expected peaks and stated failures while preserving its contracts and recovering without an uncontrolled backlog.

71. Build from one market to a global platform

A new company should not begin by reproducing every published Uber system. It should begin with a narrow product, explicit invariants, and an operating model that can safely handle exceptions.

Reference roadmap. The stages below are proposed milestones, not a reconstruction of Uber's founding timeline or an estimate of its current staffing and costs. Each stage is gated by evidence rather than a promised calendar date.

Stage one: one coherent journey

Build rider and driver applications, basic account and vehicle eligibility, a transactional request-and-assignment service, location ingestion, simple candidate ranking, notifications, and an external payment adapter. Use a modular codebase and a well-operated database. Keep a small number of clear service boundaries rather than creating dozens prematurely.

Launch only a constrained product and geography with trained operations. Before real use, prove duplicate-command handling, assignment uniqueness, legal transitions, safe cancellation, and reconciliation. A demo that moves a car icon is not launch readiness.

Stage two: operational reliability

Add observability, on-call, support cases, automated reconciliation, backup restoration, controlled configuration, and partner-failure handling. Build simulators and replay tools. Improve map and ETA quality using measured data.

Do not postpone these capabilities until after growth. Without them, every exception becomes a manual database edit and every incident consumes the same engineers needed to improve the product.

Stage three: marketplace optimization

Introduce bounded matching batches, better route estimates, forecasting, experiments, and more sophisticated pricing only after the basic outcome data is trustworthy. A complex model trained on inconsistent trip states produces sophisticated errors.

Add specialized products through explicit capabilities and plan constraints. Avoid copying one ride state machine into many divergent variants. Preserve a common commitment model with product-specific rules.

Stage four: multiple businesses and regions

Delivery adds catalog, inventory uncertainty, merchant acceptance, and courier plans. Freight adds longer horizons, carrier organizations, equipment, appointments, and documents. Reuse infrastructure where semantics match; do not force identical workflows where they do not.

Add regional cells, durable ownership transfer, and tested failover when business requirements justify them. Expand analytical and ML infrastructure based on measured bottlenecks. A global platform is an operational capability, not a deployment flag.

Build versus buy

Initially buy commodity maps, messaging, payment processing, cloud infrastructure, and workflow or data services where suitable. Build the product's differentiating marketplace logic and the integration contracts that preserve correctness. Revisit choices when cost, latency, control, or scale changes.

Every replacement needs migration and exit plans. Owning more infrastructure is not automatically strategic if it consumes the team needed to serve customers reliably.

Launch gates

Require tested recovery, reviewed commercial rules, current legal and insurance assessment, partner certification, safety operations, and an incident communication plan. The company is ready to expand when it can explain and recover its failures, not only demonstrate its successes.

72. Teams, ownership, on-call, and engineering governance

Architecture and organization reinforce each other. A service without an accountable owner becomes a shared risk; a team responsible for an outcome but unable to change its dependencies becomes a permanent escalation queue.

Published evidence. Uber's domain-oriented architecture describes grouping services and exposing clear domain interfaces. The team model below is a proposed organization aligned to those kinds of boundaries, not an internal Uber organization chart. [S03]

Outcome-aligned domains

A reference organization includes Mobility fulfillment, Delivery commerce and logistics, Freight execution, maps and routing, marketplace science, money systems, trust and safety, data and ML platforms, infrastructure reliability, developer productivity, and customer operations.

Small companies combine these responsibilities within fewer teams. The important property is clear ownership of invariants and operating outcomes, not a particular headcount. Avoid creating a platform team for every technology before there are enough users to justify it.

Service ownership contract

Each service declares owner, on-call rotation, SLOs, dependency contracts, data classification, runbook, dashboards, escalation path, and lifecycle status. A service cannot be considered production-ready merely because it has a repository and deployment pipeline.

Cross-domain changes need explicit reviewers for the affected invariant. For example, adding pooled rides requires fulfillment, pricing, maps, safety, and support input. A local code change can have global business consequences.

On-call design

Provide primary and secondary coverage, escalation, incident command, and handoff. Limit alert noise and track toil. Repeated manual reconciliation should create engineering work with an owner rather than become an accepted permanent burden.

Train responders using realistic exercises. An on-call engineer should know when to pause new work, preserve evidence, and ask for operational support. Restoring throughput is not always the first safe action.

Decision governance

Use architecture decision records for consequential choices. Define a decision owner, review scope, and escalation mechanism. Avoid requiring every team to approve every detail, but do not let shared invariants be changed without relevant expertise.

Security, privacy, finance, safety, and legal review should participate at design time. Treating them as last-minute launch checklists creates avoidable rework and risk.

Learning culture

Post-incident reviews should identify technical and organizational contributing factors, corrective actions, and verification. Avoid explanations that stop at an individual's mistake. Systems should anticipate human error through safer interfaces, review, and recoverability.

Measure customer outcomes, reliability, change quality, and operational burden. A team that reduces incident frequency or reconciliation work may create more value than one that ships a larger number of features.

The organization should make it easy to answer: who owns this commitment, who can change it, and who responds when it is uncertain?

74. Worked journey: one ride from quote to settlement

This example connects the reference design into one trace. All identifiers, timings, and amounts are illustrative. The commercial example excludes taxes and other real-world complexity to make the state and accounting boundaries visible.

Quote and request

Rider R receives quote Q7 for 28 currency units, policy P12, expiring at a specified server time. The app persists command C1 before sending a booking request. The order gateway validates Q7 and atomically creates request T9 at version 1 with an idempotency result and outbox event.

The response is lost. The app retries C1 with the same payload. The server returns T9 rather than creating a second request. The rider sees searching, not an unconfirmed assignment.

Candidate and offer

The geographic view returns drivers A and B with location versions and freshness. Eligibility and route checks identify A as feasible. The matcher proposes plan revision 18 based on A's current plan version 17 and creates offer O4 under authority epoch 6.

A receives O4 over the real-time channel. Transport receipt is recorded separately from acceptance. The driver taps accept, creating command C2. The authority verifies O4, T9, plan version 17, expiry, and epoch 6.

Atomic commitment

One transaction writes assignment A55, plan version 18, request version 2, O4 accepted, C2's stored result, and an outbox event. Only then does the server acknowledge. A competing acceptance based on plan version 17 fails without creating another assignment.

Notifications update rider and driver views. If a notification is missed, a snapshot fetch returns the committed state. The assignment exists independently of either phone's connection.

Execution

The driver approaches and reports arrival. The system records location evidence and the relevant arrival transition. Pickup confirmation advances execution to in_progress. A destination change would require a new plan and commercial revision; none occurs in this example.

The driver completes the ride. The authoritative transition produces a completed-work event. Repeated completion commands return the existing result. A delayed location packet does not reopen the trip.

Economic record

For the simplified example, the approved fare establishes 28 units receivable from the customer, 21 payable to the driver, and 7 of platform revenue. The journal records balanced entries with a stable posting identity. Replaying the completion event does not post again.

Collection creates an external provider reference. A timeout is resolved using that reference. Provider settlement and driver payout are separate events, each reconciled to the journal. The driver can be owed money before payout occurs; the trip can be completed before collection is finally settled.

What the trace proves

The trace preserves one customer intent, one accepted assignment, one execution history, and deduplicated financial obligations. It does not claim the network delivered exactly once. Correctness comes from stable identities, authoritative transactions, version checks, and reconciliation across every unreliable boundary.

This example follows a grocery-style delivery order with a substitution and courier-plan conflict. All amounts and times are illustrative, and payment behavior depends on the chosen provider contract.

Checkout

A customer confirms two items totaling 42 units: item X at 18 and item Y at 24. The order records catalog versions, substitution preferences, and a maximum approved amount. The payment workflow obtains an authorization under a stable reference. The order is not marked delivered or financially settled merely because authorization succeeds.

The merchant accepts. The preparation or picking job begins. Courier dispatch uses a readiness estimate and current supply rather than assuming the goods are already waiting at the door.

Unavailable item

The shopper reports Y unavailable with an item-level command. The customer's policy allows a proposed replacement but not an unapproved price increase. A candidate replacement costs 22 units and passes the required product constraints.

The customer approves the amendment. The order service records a new commercial revision totaling 40 units while preserving the original 42-unit request. If the customer does not respond, the accepted fallback policy governs; the system does not silently choose a different item.

Courier batching conflict

Courier D already has a committed delivery plan at version 8. A matcher proposes inserting this order, estimating a small detour. Before acceptance, the merchant updates readiness substantially later. The revised plan would now violate the existing customer's delivery constraint.

The authority rejects the stale proposal or requires recomputation. It does not accept merely because the earlier score was attractive. Another courier or later dispatch is considered. The existing commitment remains protected.

Pickup and handoff

The merchant marks the amended order ready. A courier accepts a feasible plan, arrives, and confirms pickup using the supported evidence. Inferred movement away from the store does not substitute for that confirmation.

The customer receives an updated ETA that accounts for the delay. Notifications include order version so stale messages do not restore the earlier item list or time estimate. The courier sees only the information needed to complete delivery.

Finalization

Delivery confirmation advances the order's execution state. The payment workflow finalizes the approved 40-unit amount using the provider's supported authorization/capture behavior and releases or adjusts the unused amount as required. If the external outcome is uncertain, the order's financial state remains pending confirmation.

Merchant proceeds, courier compensation, customer charge, and any platform-funded adjustment are separate obligations. A delay compensation does not require rewriting the original order or erasing the courier's completed work.

Lessons

The order has several versions and several independent commitments, but one coherent history. Inventory uncertainty, customer consent, courier feasibility, and external payment outcomes are resolved through explicit transitions rather than hidden field edits.

76. Worked incident: regional failure after an acceptance commit

This scenario tests the most dangerous gap: the platform committed an assignment, but the client did not receive the answer before the region failed. The safe response is to recover the existing commitment, not create a replacement blindly.

Before failure

Driver D accepts offer O9 using command C77. Under authority epoch 30, the transaction commits assignment A90, plan version 14, the request state, C77's stored result, and an outbox event. The response is lost as the region becomes unreachable.

The driver app shows confirmation pending and retains C77. Another device or edge may still hold stale discovery data that lists D as available. That view has no authority to commit a new assignment.

Establish the surviving authority

The recovery process fences the failed writer and establishes epoch 31 under the storage system's supported protocol. The destination confirms the committed data position before accepting writes. Traffic routing changes only after that authority is ready.

A resumed process from epoch 30 cannot create new commitments. The database-side epoch check rejects it. A stale gateway can route incorrectly, but cannot override the fence.

Resolve the command

The driver retries C77. The server finds the stored successful result and returns A90. It does not reject the historical success merely because the current authority epoch is newer. Epoch checks govern new writes; idempotent retrieval can report an already committed outcome.

If the command result is not yet known because the recovery point is uncertain, the system returns an explicit pending status and reconciles. It does not assume absence means failure and offer the driver to another rider.

Recover side effects

The outbox relay may republish the assignment event. Consumers deduplicate by event or effect identity. A rider who missed the original notification receives a current snapshot. A delayed offer-expiry timer sees that O9 is accepted and does nothing.

External actions are checked by their stable references. Completed provider calls are not reissued under new identities simply because their local worker state was lost.

Reconcile physical execution

The driver may already be approaching the pickup or carrying the rider. Device observations help reconstruct execution but do not replace the authoritative assignment. Support can contact participants if state remains uncertain.

New booking traffic is ramped only after capacity, consistency checks, and critical backlogs are acceptable. Read availability can return before full write admission. The incident commander reports both service recovery and unresolved operational cases.

The invariant

At every step, the system prefers a temporarily uncertain visible outcome over a contradictory committed outcome. The cost is a bounded pause in new work; the benefit is avoiding duplicate drivers, stranded commitments, and financial confusion.

77. Runbooks, production gates, and the final engineering checklist

A master design becomes operational only when teams can detect failure, choose a safe action, and verify recovery. Runbooks should be executable decision aids rather than long documents nobody can use during an incident.

A useful runbook format

State the symptom, customer impact, authoritative dashboards, immediate safety actions, diagnostic branches, permitted mitigations, rollback conditions, and recovery checks. Identify the incident owner and escalation contacts. Keep dangerous actions behind explicit confirmation and audit.

For an assignment-conflict incident, first stop new invalid commitments in the affected scope, preserve evidence, and identify the authority/version failure. Do not repair by deleting one assignment arbitrarily. Existing physical journeys require coordinated operational handling.

Three example response paths

Stale location across a market: verify ingestion age and partition coverage; inspect device/network patterns; reduce candidate confidence or exclude stale supply; preserve active assignments; use controlled fallback estimates; recover the stream and confirm freshness before restoring normal matching.

Partner delivery or payment uncertainty: stop creating replacement operations under new references; query existing references; prioritize aged cases; communicate pending status; reconcile verified outcomes; release holds only when failure is established.

Regional overload: protect critical execution traffic; shed optional work; constrain retries; reclaim approved lower-priority capacity; verify downstream limits; shift traffic gradually; drain backlogs by priority and expiry rather than all at once.

Production gates

The core invariants must have executable tests. Every mutating API needs an idempotency and ambiguity contract. Every external integration needs reconciliation. Every critical service needs SLOs, owner, on-call, dashboards, and a tested recovery path. Every sensitive dataset needs purpose, access, retention, and deletion treatment.

Commercial rules require finance and product approval. Market launches require current professional review of applicable obligations and partner readiness. Safety operations need rehearsed escalation. A software load test cannot substitute for these requirements.

Evidence for readiness

Collect results from concurrency tests, migration rehearsals, backup restores, regional drills, partner certification, security review, privacy review, and support exercises. Record limitations. A teaching model or a successful staging run is not proof of production readiness at global scale.

Define the maximum unresolved backlog and uncertainty duration acceptable before restricting new work. Reliability includes the ability to stop safely when the system no longer knows enough to make commitments.

The engineering principles to retain

Use approximate data to discover possibilities and authoritative transactions to establish commitments. Keep optimization separate from feasibility. Preserve stable identity across retries. Treat physical outcomes and external money movement as independently evidenced facts. Version models, policies, maps, and schemas. Make recovery and human operations first-class parts of the design.

Those principles remain useful whether the company serves one neighborhood or operates a global marketplace. Scale changes the machinery; it does not remove the need to know what was promised, who owns it, and how to recover when the answer is uncertain.

C. Critical algorithms and transaction pseudocode

The algorithms are explanatory designs. They omit transport setup, production authorization integration, observability plumbing, and database-specific retry APIs. The executable teaching model in the source bundle tests selected local invariants only. Its 36 local tests pass; the 72-scenario catalog in Appendix D is a broader specification, not a claim of completed validation.

C1. Commit an offer acceptance

accept(actor, command_id, offer_id, payload_hash):
  begin serializable transaction
    prior = read command(actor, "accept", command_id)
    if prior exists:
      require prior.payload_hash == payload_hash
      return prior.stored_result

    offer = read offer_id
    request = read offer.request_id
    plan = read offer.plan_id
    authority = read authority_for(plan)
    require offer.actor_id == actor
    require offer.state == PENDING
    require trusted_now < offer.expires_at
    require offer.epoch == authority.epoch
    require plan.version == offer.expected_plan_version
    require request.version == offer.expected_request_version
    require current_eligibility(actor, offer)
    require feasible(plan, offer.proposed_revision)

    assignment = create stable_assignment(command_id)
    update request, plan, offer with new versions
    insert outbox(stable_event_id(assignment), committed_event)
    insert command(..., payload_hash, assignment_result)
  commit
  return assignment_result

All invariant-sensitive reads and writes must lie in a supported consistency boundary. External calls cannot occur inside the retryable transaction. A cached successful command result is returned before attempting to re-execute an already completed command.

C2. Apply a versioned location observation

apply_location(actor, device_session, seq, sample):
  require authenticated device belongs to actor
  require valid coordinates and plausible payload
  current_session = read registered_session(actor)
  require device_session == current_session.id
  if sample is implausibly future-dated: quarantine or reject
  atomically:
    prior = latest_location(actor)
    if prior.session == device_session and seq <= prior.seq:
      return DUPLICATE_OR_OLD
    write latest_location(sample, seq, server_received_time)
    emit versioned geographic membership update
  append permitted historical observation separately

A stale sample may remain useful for history without replacing the latest view. Device-session enrollment, not a client-chosen large sequence number, establishes a new generation.

C3. Generate a bounded candidate set

candidates(request, deadline):
  cells = initial_neighborhood(request.pickup)
  seen = map actor_id -> newest_location
  while time_remaining(deadline) and below_search_budget:
    merge fresh membership(cells) into seen
    eligible = hard_filter(seen, request.product_constraints)
    rough = cheap_route_rank(eligible)
    if enough promising candidates: break
    cells = bounded_adaptive_expansion(cells)
  finalists = rough.top(K)
  exact = batch_route_and_plan_check(finalists, deadline)
  return feasible proposals with expected plan versions

The budget limits fan-out and waiting. The final authority revalidates plan and eligibility. A geographic result never binds a driver by itself.

C4. Insert pickup and drop-off into a plan

best_insertion(plan, new_job):
  best = NONE
  for pickup_position in allowed_positions(plan):
    for dropoff_position after pickup_position:
      candidate = insert(plan, new_job, positions)
      if any stop violates capacity: continue
      if pickup does not precede dropoff: continue
      if route or time windows are infeasible: continue
      if existing promise is violated: continue
      score = cost(candidate) - cost(plan)
      best = lower_cost(best, candidate, score)
  return best with original plan.version

For large plans, use pruning and strict computational budgets. An optimal insertion computed after its deadline is not operationally useful.

relay(record):

  mark_relay_progress(record.event_id)

C6. Reserve a partial refund

reserve_refund(original_payment, command_id, amount):
  require integer amount > 0 and correct currency
  begin serializable transaction
    return stored result for identical prior command
    payment = read original_payment
    committed_or_reserved = sum active refund obligations
    require committed_or_reserved + amount <= refundable_amount
    create refund reservation with stable external key
    insert command result and outbox
  commit
  execute externally using the stable key
  finalize only from verified outcome; unknown remains reserved

This is a money-safety example within the Uber-like commerce design. Real refund eligibility, accounting, and provider behavior require reviewed policies and contracts.

C7. Switch authority safely

promote(partition, recovery_plan):
  restrict new writes in affected scope
  establish old-writer fencing through supported storage protocol
  verify destination data position and quorum requirements
  atomically advance authority epoch
  validate invariant checks and critical dependency readiness
  publish new routing generation
  admit bounded traffic
  reconcile ambiguous commands and external effects
  expand only after recovery gates pass

A counter in a cache is not enough. The authoritative resource must enforce the fence. The protocol depends on the chosen storage system and failure model.

These are per-stage planning budgets, not a claim that adding measured p99 values produces the end-to-end p99. Dependencies, overlap, and correlated tails require end-to-end measurement. Driver acceptance occurs later and is not included in a booking acknowledgment target.

Capacity formulas

location_samples_per_second = active_devices / sample_interval_seconds
raw_bytes_per_second = samples_per_second * average_payload_bytes
raw_bytes_per_day = raw_bytes_per_second * 86400
mean_inflight = arrival_rate_per_second * mean_time_in_system_seconds
ideal_cpu_cores = requests_per_second * measured_cpu_seconds_per_request

route_calls = new_requests_per_second * exact_candidates_per_request

Use decimal TB and binary TiB consistently. Compression, indexing, metadata, protocol overhead, and backup storage are separate factors. Measure CPU cost on the actual hardware and workload; a synthetic microbenchmark may omit serialization, encryption, cache misses, and downstream waiting.
