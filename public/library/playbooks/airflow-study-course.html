Source and study guide

An original study companion

Data Pipelines with Apache Airflow

Bas Harenslak and Julian de Ruiter
Manning Publications, 2021
ISBN 9781617296901

This course follows the instructional sections of all 18 chapters and Appendices A-C. Each card teaches one small idea using original paraphrases and, where useful, original diagrams, comparison tables, or illustrative snippets. The practice questions and explanations are new teaching material based on the cited sections.

The supplied book tested its examples with Airflow 2.0 and includes substantial Airflow 1.x context. APIs, configuration names, defaults, cloud products, security examples, and limitations are historical. This is not an independently verified current installation or production guide. Source inconsistencies and gaps are called out rather than silently repaired.

How to study

Read one card, restate its takeaway, and move on. Mark an idea learned only when it makes sense to you. Bookmark useful references and flag difficult ideas for revisit. Practice checks reveal the answer after a choice, or when you deliberately ask to reveal it.

Finding the original passage

Every card includes printed book pages and corresponding uploaded-PDF pages. For this file, the uploaded PDF page is the printed page plus 26. Source references point into your original book; that full book is not embedded in this file. Coverage in the library maps every indexed instructional section to its course-card range.

Code and limits

Code blocks are short illustrations of the book-era interfaces, not complete tested programs. Complete runnable examples and their environments belong to the book's companion repository. This course does not reproduce every source-code line, console screenshot, or external resource template. Where the book leaves an algorithm or deployment detail outside scope, the cards say so.

Offline and private

The course contains its own content, diagrams, styles, and scripts. It makes no network requests. Progress is stored locally where the browser permits it. Export a backup before moving or renaming the HTML file, changing browser, or clearing browser data.
